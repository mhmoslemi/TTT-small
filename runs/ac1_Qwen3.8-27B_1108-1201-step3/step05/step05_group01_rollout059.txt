**Frequency-domain shaping:**

The idea is:
1. Compute A = FFT(a, L) where L >= 2n-1.
2. The power spectrum is P = |A|^2. The autocorrelation is b = IFFT(P, real).
3. We want b to be flat. In the frequency domain, "flat b" means P is concentrated at m=0.
4. shaping: P_new[m] = P[m] * (P[0] / (P[m] + ε))^α for some α.
   - This "boosts" the DC component and "suppresses" other frequencies.
   - Equivalently: |A_new[m]| = |A[m]| * (P[0] / (P[m] + ε))^(α/2).
5. Keep the phase of A: A_new[m] = A[m] / |A[m]| * |A_new[m]| (for |A[m]| > 0).
6. a_new = IFFT(A_new, real)[:n].
7. Project a_new onto the simplex: a_new = project_simplex(a_new).

This is similar to the Gerchberg-Saxton algorithm. The parameter α controls the aggressiveness of the shaping (α=1 is full shaping, α=0 is no change).

Let me also think about a variant: instead of shaping towards a delta at m=0, shape towards a "top-hat" or "Gaussian" in frequency. This might give better results because a perfect delta is unachievable.

Actually, let me think about what the optimal spectrum looks like. We want:
- b[k] ≈ c (constant) for all k.
- This means P[m] ≈ c * L * δ[m] (a delta at m=0).
- But P[m] = |A[m]|^2 and sum_m P[m] = L * sum_j a[j]^2 >= L/n.
- Also P[0] = (sum a)^2 = 1.
- So the "excess" energy in P is sum_m P[m] - P[0] = L*sum(a^2) - 1 >= L/n - 1 ≈ L(1/n - 1/L) ≈ L/n for L >> n.

We want to minimize the "excess" spread in P. The best we can do is to concentrate this excess in the lowest-frequency bins (m=1, m=L-1, m=2, m=L-2, ...), because low-frequency components in P correspond to slowly-varying components in b, which are easier to keep below the peak.
