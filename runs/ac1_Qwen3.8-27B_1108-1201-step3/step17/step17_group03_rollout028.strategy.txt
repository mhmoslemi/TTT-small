The goal is to minimize the evaluation function J = 2*n*max(conv(a,a)) / (sum(a))², which inherently compares the ratio of the maximum autocorrelation to the square of the sequence sum. This function relates to the problem of finding a sequence with the flattest autocorrelation spectrum, a well-known challenge in signal processing. - Operate on normalized sequences (sum=1) to maintain the scale dependence of J
- Find sequences that avoid sharp peaks and abrupt transitions in their autocorrelation
- Use polynomial representations to explore different decay behaviors
- Apply spectral regularization to prevent oscillatory artifacts that increase autocorrelation peaks

The solution involves iteratively adjusting pairs of coefficients based on their contribution to autocorrelation peaks, with directional search guided by the autocorrelation profile. 2. Mathematical Specification:
We work with normalized sequences `a` (sum(a)=1) of length `n`. The objective is:

J(n) = 2 * n * max(conv(a, a, mode='full')) 

where the convolution produces 2n-1 coefficients. Each iteration:
- Computes the autocorrelation `c := conv(a, a)`
- Identifies the maximum value in `c`
- Finds pairs (i, j) contributing to max_j c[k] (where k is the lag)
- Adjusts a via fraction `f` from `a[i]` to `a[j]`, ensuring nonnegativity and normalization

The mathematical framework is:

minimize J = 2n * (max_lag | (a * a_shifted)|) / (sum(a))²

Subject to:
0 ≤ a[i] ≤ 1000 for all i
