But wait, I need to check: is the maximum really at k=n-1 for p>1?

For a[i] = (i+1)^2 (unnormalized), the convolution is the convolution of the triangular sequence with itself, which is a "B-spline" of order 4. The peak is at the center.

For a[i] = (i+1)^p with p>1, the sequence is more concentrated at the right end (for i going 0 to n-1). The convolution peak might shift.

Actually, for a[i] = (i+1)^p (increasing), the product a[i]*a[k-i] is maximized when both i and k-i are large, i.e., when k is large. So the peak of conv should be at k = n-1 (the middle of the convolution where both i and k-i can be large).

Wait, for k = n-1: both i and k-i range from 0 to n-1, so both can be at their maximum.
For k > n-1: i must be at least k-n+1 > 0, and k-i must be at most n-1. So the effective range is smaller.

I think for monotonically increasing sequences, the peak is indeed at k = n-1.
