Subject to: a^T Q_k a <= t for all k (where Q_k encodes the k-th convolution)
           a >= 0
           sum(a) = 1

This is a QCQP (Quadratically Constrained Quadratic Program). These are generally NP-hard, but for our size (n up to a few thousand), we might be able to solve them with semidefinite relaxation or other techniques.

However, the number of constraints is 2n-1, which is large. And the matrices Q_k are specific structure (permutation-like).

Let me think of a more practical approach.

**Practical Algorithm: Gradient Descent with Subgradient for the Max**

1. Initialize a (e.g., from the previous best or a random smooth sequence).
2. Normalize: a = a / sum(a), a = max(0, a).
3. Iteration:
   a. Compute C = conv(a, a).
   b. Find the top-K indices where C is largest (K is a small number, e.g., 10).
   c. For each of these indices k, compute the gradient g_k[j] = 2*a[k-j] (with boundary handling).
   d. Compute the combined gradient: g[j] = sum_{k in top-K} w_k * g_k[j] where w_k are weights (e.g., proportional to C[k]).
   e. Update: a[j] = max(0, a[j] - eta * (g[j] - mean(g)))  [subtracting mean to maintain sum ≈ 1]
   f. Normalize: a = a / sum(a).
   g. Adapt eta.

The key insight is to subtract the mean of the gradient to maintain the normalization constraint approximately.
