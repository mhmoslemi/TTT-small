**Core Idea:**  
Optimize sparse structured sequences directly against the exact evaluation function, rather than relying on surrogates derived from – norm constraints or – inf-multiplying sprinkles. Minimize the value \( \frac{2 \cdot n \cdot \max(\text{full\_convolve}(a, a))}{\sum(a)^2} \) via gradient-driven hyperparameter tuning.

**Why It Matters:**  
The previous program used an upper bound evaluation (near zero) and a constrained simplex, but failed to lock in a significantly lower value than 1.5027. By using exact computations and acting on the specific landing pad points of the convolution, we can more efficiently drive down the peak-normalized cost.

**Analysis and Limitations:**  
Compute exactly for 2*n-1 convolution points avoids ordering errors. But because the objective is minimized, the optimization is better guided - by gradient projection steps on the score function. Random starting points are too agnostic; we should begin with a tuned double-tone sequence. Also, the previous approach didn't sufficiently prune invalid or near-zero sequences, so we will add that.
