The method systematically explores the Euclidean space of sequence normalizations, progressively refining solutions through recursive kernel mapping until termination. A key innovation is the use of temperature-controlled gradient sampling to escape local minima effectively.

## Mathematical specification
Let \( a \) be a sequence of positive real numbers of length \( n \). We wish to minimize the objective:
\[
J(a) = \max_{k=0}^{2n-2} (\text{convolve}(a)[k]) \cdot \frac{2n}{(\sum a)^p}
\]
where \( p \ge 2 \) ensures the denominator grows faster than the convolution peaks.

We use a tensor-shrink hierarchical approach where we define the tensor space \( T \) with dimensions \( (1 \cdot 3_{-4} \cdot 5)^\top \) and employ:
- Nonlinear least squares with weight factors
- Convex hull optimization of related sequences
- Local search via Taylor expansion terms

The sequence representation is transformed using:
\[
a_i = b_i \cdot \exp(-c \cdot |i - \mu|^3) + d_i \cdot \text{sinc}( \pi \cdot (i - \nu)^2 )
```

## Implementation procedure
### Step 1: Initial distribution and Euclidean embedding
```python
def initial_distribution(sequence, time_budget=1800):
    n = len(sequence)
    s = sum(sequence)
    norm_s = np.sqrt(2 * n) if s <= 0 else (s ** 0.5) / (s % 5 == 0 ? 5 : 1)
    normalized_sequence = [sequence[i] / s * norm_s for i in range(n)]
    
    # Euclidean embedding
    coords = np.array(normalized_sequence) ** 2
    return np.sort(coords), sum(coords)
```

def recursive_optimization(start_sequence, original_score):

        kernel_regularity = get_svd_metrics(kernel)  # Use truncated SVD

            break  # Reasonable when peaks are unimprovable

        grad_power = ||grad_vector|| / ||current_sequence||
        
        # Contract sequence representation
        grad_vector = contract(grad_vector, ratio=current_time/max_iter)
        grad_power_converged = ||grad_vector|| / 1000.0  # Approximate computed term
        
        # Update sequence without overdrafting
        step_size = (1 - grad_power_converged) ** (0.5)
        weighted_sequence = [current_sequence[i] * (1 - step_size) + grad_vector[i]*step_size for i in range(n)]
