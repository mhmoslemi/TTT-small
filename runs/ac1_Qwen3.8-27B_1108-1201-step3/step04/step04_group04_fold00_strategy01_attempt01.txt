of the step-function coefficients to accelerate the core minimization procedure. 
Instead of using dense linear algebra or gradient-based methods directly on the sequence, 
we project the sequence into the frequency domain using a discrete wavelet transform, 
solve for minimal energy in the transform domain (which corresponds to minimal peak autocorrelation), 
and then map back to the time domain. This allows efficient constraint-handling and 
gradient computation directly in the transform domain. 

We start with the parent sequence (n=768) and also consider other lengths via bi-scale 
initialization and perturbation. Our methods include:
1. Parent initialization with fallback in evaluation.
2. Bi-scale optimization: using wavelet coefficients to optimize a transformed objective.
3. Explicit handling of the coefficient cap [0, 1000] via element-wise clamping in results.
4. Time-limited exhaustive evaluation: we cannot assume the evaluator will accept surrogate derivatives.

Key novelty: the sparse optimization via wavelet transform leads to faster convergence 
during the initial descent phase, while retaining freedom for complex sequences.
