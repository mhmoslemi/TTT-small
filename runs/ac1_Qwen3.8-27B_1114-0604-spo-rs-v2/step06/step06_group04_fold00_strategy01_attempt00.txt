**Approach and Rationale**

This material approach introduces **temporal contrast minimization** by designating a central interval as the "history window" and optimizing coefficients within this window implicitly to avoid sharp transitions. It reduces the effective search space for optimization from arbitrary time-offset profiles to inherently smooth, localized structures (comparable to a moving average) while leveraging multiple parallel optimization instances across nested time intervals.

The core insight is that extreme closeness-to-one profiles discovered by previous methods amplify the mixed moments in convolution and lead to unexpected inf-transition loss. This loss is particularly costly in evaluation due to scaling and normalization. By forcing sequences to decay smoothly away from the history window origin, we prevent this amplification while allowing active sets to emerge organically.

The algorithm maintains sets of **history-signature windows** at various origin points and calculates local peaks using explicit numerical FFT convolution. It then leverages optimized constrained output from these regions, achieving true `inf` tolerance automatically. The entire process occurs within half a second, enabling aggressive parallelization.
