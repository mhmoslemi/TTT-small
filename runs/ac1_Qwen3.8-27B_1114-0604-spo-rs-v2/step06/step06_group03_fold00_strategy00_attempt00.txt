**Approach and Rationale**
The primary goal is to minimize the sequence evaluation metric \( \frac{2n \cdot \max(\text{convolve}(a, a)_\text{full})}{\sum(a)^2} \). We recognize that peak reduction and uniform profile are often at odds, so generate multiple initial sequences with diverse characteristics—broad and narrow, symmetric and asymmetric, etc. The central idea is **divide and conquer**: first find sparse candidate sequences, then refine them with mirrordescent and penalty-driven score minimization. This approach avoids local minima better than continuous optimization alone.

**Mathematical Specification**
For a non-empty sequence \( a \) of length \( n \):
1. \( S = \sum(a) \)
2. Compute convolution \( b = \text{convolve}(a, a, \text{mode}="full") \) of length \( 2n-1 \)
3. The objective is \( \mathcal{L} = \frac{2n \cdot \max(b)}{S^2} \)

Each objective-interpolating subgradient (e.g., \( \mathcal{A}_\text{spike} \), \( \mathcal{B}_\text{plateau} \)) preserves non-negativity. Penalties are used to enforce gradient constraints, integrated into scoring. Active subgradient sets are determined by maxima positions but dynamically adjusted by top-k selection in the domain and score space.
