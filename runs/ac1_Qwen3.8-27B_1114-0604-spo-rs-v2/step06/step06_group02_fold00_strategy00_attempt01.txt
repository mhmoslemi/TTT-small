### Approach and Rationale

The NEA approach infuses randomness into standard evolutionary methods to promote exploration, potentially escaping local minima that the deterministic operations (like L-BFGS) might get stuck in. The key idea is to replace mutation-specific coefficients with variance-controlled, statistical estimates optimized through Gaussian Expectation. For each fitness evaluation and convolution, we will use the previous program's `fast_conv` function for efficiency, but mutate each candidate sequence using algorithm parameters derived from the expected variance of the best candidates.

Steps:

2. **Selection**: Maintain the best parent solutions and periodically inject noisy variations.
3. **Mutation**: Replace with new sequences where the variance of the mutation coefficients is optimized relative to the improvement rate.
4. **Crossover**: Explore by combining sequences, weighted by their fitness scores.
5. **Termination**: After 2400 seconds, return the best candidate found.

This method's primary benefit is the ability to handle the stochastic search space effectively and is subsequently optimized by self-adapting the step size variance.
