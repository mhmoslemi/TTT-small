Approach and rationale
I will introduce a novel optimization strategy based on minimizing the L-infinity norm of the autocorrelation function. This differs from standard methods in that it directly targets the core of the objective function (max(convolve(a,a)) which is problematic for previous approaches. The L-infinity norm minimization is formulated using a linear transformation of the original problem, making use of Parseval's identity to relate the L-infinity norm of the autocorrelation to constraints on the signal domain. 

A key distinguishing feature is using stochastic expansion of the solution space through multiple lengths, coupled with deterministic gradient truncation. Oscillations in the initial search space are transformed to reduce their energy in frequency domain while maximizing peak power, represented by the form-shift operation. This method sacrifices exponential search in one dimension for structured multi-length exploration that inherently tracks improvements across different resolutions. A k-threshold pruning mechanism ensures we never waste evaluations on nonpromising candidates, leveraging smooth relaxation factors (gamma) up to 1.4.
