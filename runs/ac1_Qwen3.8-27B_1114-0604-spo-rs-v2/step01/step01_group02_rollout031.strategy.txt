# 1. Approach and Rationale

The overarching goal is to minimize the step function's autocorrelation energy evaluation, defined as `f(a) = 2 * n * max(convolve(a,a)) / (sum(a))**2`. This problem combines nonnegativity constraints with an evaluation metric involving both self-convolution and sum. The central idea is to use competitive formations:

- We explore two fundamental sequence patterns: two-cell clusters and three-cell symmetric building blocks.
- These patterns are designed to distribute signal energy evenly across autocorrelation peaks.

This contrasts with the Adam optimizer approach by considering sequences as combinations and artifacts of basic waveforms. The formation is generated by solving a simple optimization:

- For two-cell clusters, we find placements and values that minimize the coincident autocorrelation while maintaining total energy.
- For three-cell symmetric sequences, residue management is applied to distribute the values.

We then validate and score these candidates directly using `evaluate_sequence`. This method provides a coordinate-consistent alternative to freely moving parameters in optimization space.
