Unlike the previous spectral flattening and L-BFGS methods, I focus explicitly on minimizing peak autocorrelation by selectively amplifying or reducing geometric progressions within the sequence. Starting from a basic uniform sequence, it creates geometric progressions (GP) of various orders, positions, and ratios, then trains these on independent samples while tracking the minimal peak. The GSE leverages combinatorial methods to explore sequence configurations without requiring linear algebra or gradient descent. It computes the exact objective function for every test sequence rather than using expensive gradient calculations, and focuses optimization efforts on parameter combinations that demonstrably reduce peak values.

1. Precompute convolution for uniform base sequence
2. Generate parameterized geometric sequences based on parent sequence
3. Use combinatorial selection to produce candidate sequences
4. Calculate F for each candidate
5. Apply hill climbing to select candidates that minimally reduce F
6. All sequences are constructed from positive real numbers
