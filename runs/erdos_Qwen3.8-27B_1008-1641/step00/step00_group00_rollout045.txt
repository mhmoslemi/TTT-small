But at points where multiple f_j are equal maxima, the subdifferential is the convex hull of {∇f_j : j argmax}. Taking the average is one choice, but it might not be the best.

Actually, for minimizing, at a point where f_1 = f_2 = max, any convex combination of ∇f_1 and ∇f_2 is a valid subgradient. The "steepest" descent direction in the subdifferential would be the one with minimum norm.

Let me use the minimum-norm element of the subdifferential:
- At a point where k_1, k_2, ..., k_m are the argmax lags
- Subdifferential = conv{∇f_{k_1}, ..., ∇f_{k_m}}
- Minimum norm element = the one closest to 0

For 2 elements: a∇f_1 + (1-a)∇f_2, minimize ||·||². The optimal a is:
a* = (||∇f_2||² - ∇f_1·∇f_2) / (||∇f_1||² + ||∇f_2||² - 2∇f_1·∇f_2), clamped to [0,1]
