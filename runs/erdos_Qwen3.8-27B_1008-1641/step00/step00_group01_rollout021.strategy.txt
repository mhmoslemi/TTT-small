<strategy>Approach and rationale
The central idea is to combine Bayesian optimization with a physically inspired step function construction to minimize the Erdős discrepancy constant C5. Unlike the previous Differential Evolution approach that relied solely on expensive function evaluations, this method leverages the known structure of optimal solutions to the problem. We start with a step function optimized from existing literature and then fine-tune it using Bayesian optimization. This hybrid approach balances exploration of the solution space with physical intuition, potentially yielding better results faster.

The key improvement is the use of an initial solution derived from the known minimum configuration (a 3-point equidistant step function) and its extensions. This reduces the search space and provides a good starting point for optimization. We also incorporate an exact repair method to ensure all solutions strictly satisfy the constraints (0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1 and sum(h) = n_points/2).

The algorithm is optimized for speed by using a carefully constructed initial guess, reducing the number of expensive evaluations, and parallelizing where possible (via a Jax transformer). We target n_points=47 first before scaling to 94 if time allows.

Mathematical specification
The representation is a step function h(x) : [0,2] → [0,1] with integral = 1. The value at grid points is parameterized by a list h_values with length n_points and satisfies hx in [0,1] for all x. The integral constraint is ∑(h_values[i] * dx) = 1, with dx = 2/n_points.

The objective is to minimize:
    C5 = max_k ∫ h(x) (1 - h(x+k)) dx
In discrete form: max(np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode='full')) * dx

The repair method ensures h[i] ∈ [0,1] and ∑(h[i]) = n_points/2:
   1. Clip values to [0,1]
   2. Sort and scale to meet the sum constraint
   3. Check boundaries for failure to meet the sum

We use Bayesian optimization with a Matérn kernel for global optimization and employ a repair function that preserves feasibility.
