def pair_coordinate_value(h, i, j):
    q=h[i]+h[j]
    lo=max(0,Q-1); hi=min(1,Q)
    if hi-lo<=small: return None,None
    cand0 = h.copy(); cand0[i]=lo; cand0[j]=q-lo

1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea.  
The plan is a parent-preserving stochastic/speculative coordinate-line search. The present best construction is retained as the authoritative fallback. Candidate improvements are generated only in feasibility-preserving two-coordinate line searches. During a candidate search, two selected coordinates are moved together while their pair sum is held fixed, so the total sum constraint is maintained by construction. Every candidate is judged by the exact evaluator definition, not by a surrogate. If the exact evaluator does not show a strict improvement over the current best, the candidate is discarded. Therefore the output is never worse than the initial/fallback construction except for tiny final feasibility-repair drift, which is handled explicitly.

This addresses a major bottleneck of local continuous optimization for this problem: the integrand involves products h(x)h(y), which creates many narrow local optima and makes standard local solvers flatten or create small artificial feasible projections that degrade the parent. Exact two-coordinate line search respects the sum-partition constraint and searches an entire one-dimensional subspace in a way incompatible with many gradients that cancel exactly.

1.2 Mathematical reason for using the one-dimensional pair direction.  
For fixed outside coordinates and fixed q=h_i+h_j, define t=h_i, h_j=q-t. Each specific shifted-correlation component can include the two changing coordinates together in at most at most its diagonal term for their distance |i-j|, where it contributes a positive quadratic term t(Q-t contribution) after expansion. Therefore the directional component is either affine in t or convex quadratic in t; a pairwise overlap sum being the max of the altered components remains a homogeneous convex univariate function of t. Candidate acceptance uses a direct golden/regeneratable minimum and final direct exact evaluation. The acceptance rule is strict: accept only if exact objective strictly improvement. No feedback from surrogate solver.

1.3 What is validly claimed and what is not.
