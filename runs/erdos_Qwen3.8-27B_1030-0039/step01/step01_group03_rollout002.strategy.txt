1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The construction will be improved by a safeguarded tangent-minimax descent method. The current feasible vector is regarded as a piecewise-constant point on the product set

    H = {h in R^N : 0 <= h_i <= 1, sum_i h_i = N/2}.

For each admissible shift (lag) there is one correlation component. The objective is the maximum over these components. The nonconvexity comes from bilinear terms h_i h_j in these components. At the current vector I will form first-order tangent inequalities for the largest active correlation components, solve a small linear program for a descent direction in the feasible linearized tangent space, and then walk along that direction with a line search. Every accepted point must strictly beat the stored exact score using the original evaluator `max(np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")) * (2/N)`. The accepted point itself is then rechecked/unshook by an exact feasible repair before acceptance.

This is materially different from the earlier global smoothing/log-sum-exp/SLSQP family. Rather than replacing the max by a soft maximum and relying on smooth objective gradients alone, I treat the objective as a max of many nonconvex shifted-correlation polynomials and generate explicit feasible directions from the active tangent inequalities. Suppressed active lag constraints are handled only as tangent side constraints; the only decision boundary is the original nonconvex evaluator. Since the method accepts only strict exact-score improvements, it cannot degrade known feasible constructions except through numerical tolerance handling explicitly addressed by a fallback repair.

1.2 Why this addresses the bottleneck

The local maxima/vicinity problems are centered on a small active set of shifts attaining or nearly attaining the maximum. The other components provide basin constraints but, to first order, they have slack. A tangent LP searches exactly for a feasible direction that simultaneously lowers active shifted-correlation components as far as its first-order model permits while preserving the global sum constraint and box constraints. Nonconvex interaction effects that are invisible in that LP can appear for larger line steps; those are screened by exact all-lag evaluation. Inflation of inactive constraints for small steps is tolerated; the exact acceptance decides whether the full nonconvex objective lowers.

1.3 Search ensemble

The descent direction generator is not used alone as a certifier. It is combined with:

- parent preservation as the safe baseline;
- multiple safeguards by trust-region scaling and line search;
- a short exact coordinate-pair rescue if the tangent descent stalls;
- randomized diversity transfers/restarts and interpolation at different discretization resolutions.

The search does not prove global optimality. It is a heuristic nonconvex local-and-nearby search whose every accepted output is judged by the official objective.

1.4 Justified facts versus heuristics

- Correct: the shifted-correlation formulas, the scalar feasibility projection, and the gradient of each component.
- Correct: exact `np.correlate(..., mode="full") * (2/N)` computes all finite-support shift correlations and both signs for the output ordering checked by length and middle entry.
- Heuristic: tangent LP descent directions may be rejected or may miss improvement due to nonconvex terms.
- Heuristic: alternative resolutions or transfers can yield better feasible evaluations; each one is valid only if exact evaluation returns a strict lower objective.
- Not claimed: global optimality or any automatic guarantee that the parent will be beaten; the returned structure will never be worse than the best valid candidate found, with parent fallback.
