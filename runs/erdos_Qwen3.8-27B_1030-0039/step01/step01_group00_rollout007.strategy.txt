1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea  
The problem is a constrained nonconvex minimax problem: find a feasible vector h of length n with mass n/2 preventing all finite-support cross-correlations h_i (1 - h_j) from becoming too large. The previous approach was built mainly around a single log-sum-exp surrogate of the exact maximum, followed by SLSQP and coordinate transfers. That has two systematic weaknesses:

- a smoothed surrogate makes every lag contribute, even when it is far below the current maximum; this wastes descent capacity and can distort the active geometry, especially when several lags are nearly tied at the optimum;
- a generic nonlinear solver sees a dense constraint set only through the objective, while the true local geometry is determined by the active set of constraints reaching the maximum.

The proposed approach is materially different: it solves, repeatedly, a small linearized minimax subproblem over the current active constraints while preserving feasibility in the direction and accepting moves only after exact non-smooth evaluation. The core update is therefore a feasible trust-region active-set/LP descent direction for the exact min-max objective, not a temperature-smoothed SLSQP evolution. Only exact scores are used for acceptance and return.

At each current vector h, the lags whose correlations are at/near the maximum are selected. Their local linearizations are imposed and the subproblem minimizes the maximum of these linearized correlations subject to a zero-mean mass direction and bounded max-step size. This is a tiny linear program:
  minimize z
  subject to graduation g_lap^T delta - z <= -(correlation_lap - current_C) for active lags,
             sum_j delta_j = 0,
             box-bounded delta, trust-scale-controlled z.

After solving, a line-search along the resulting feasible direction is evaluated with the exact task metric. If the line-search gives an exact improvement, it is accepted; otherwise the trust radius is reduced and, if stagnation continues, simple mass-preserving pairwise moves are used as a fallback. This should be better at lowering a ceiling determined by several active lags than maximizing a smoothed aggregate.

1.2 Additional elements: distribution of difficult starting points and resolution ladder  
The construction is nonconvex and local methods can get trapped. Therefore the primary method is embedded in a finite set of complementary starting points:

- the supplied parent vector, plus exact transformations (reverse, complement, roll) and small feasible perturbations;
- Fourier/raw profile candidates after waterfill projection;
- pairwise moving descent under a bounded trust radius;
- a resolution transfer ladder. Starting from 45 or another supplied base, the best/found vectors are transferred to moderately higher resolutions and recalculated there.
