1. Approach and rationale
-------------------------

1.1 Central idea
----------------
The task is a finite-support nonconvex minimax problem:
    minimize over h 
        max_lag  dx * sum_{i : 0 <= i-lag < n} h[i] * (1 - h[i-lag]).
The exact objective is evaluated by the supplied numpy correlation rule. The previous/localized method is strong at fine coordinate-pair refinement but vulnerable to local minima and to finite-grid nonconvexity. The proposed method combines:
- an exact feasibility repair operator preserving 0 <= h <= 1 and sum(h) = n/2 without violating bounds, so every candidate used inside the search is a valid evaluator candidate;
- a population-based projected global search using differential evolution over raw feasible vectors;
- several deterministic local refinements that exploit the structure of the maximum: smooth maximum gradients, exact two-coordinate line search, and local block coordinate descent;
- optional symmetrization/complement/reflection proposals;
- a multiscale refinement that transfers the parent/candidate to higher grid resolutions by exact cell-area averaging and re-optimizes there, only accepting solutions with a better evaluated score.

This is not a convex relax-other: stochastic search and lower-level local descent are heuristics. It is not claimed that global optimality is certified. The evidence is that many different basin states are evaluated exactly and only exact evaluator-based scores are allowed to update the final upper bound.

1.2 What bottleneck it attacks
-----------------------------
The main bottleneck of the supplied type of previous method is:
- the local coordinate directions do not always escape the current nonconvex basin;
- the grid may be too coarse to represent the best structure discovered after smooth/deep optimizations;
- the current candidate might reflect an asymmetry or noise pattern that blocks final small reductions near 0.3808;
- one-dimensional pair moves alone do not create the discontinuities/interactions generated by small blocks of low-frequency/window-like shapes.

By adding a projected population search, potential basins outside current coordinate-pair descent are probed. By then refining with exact objective and repair after each update, invalid population proposals cannot contaminate the final published score. Multiscale exact transfer gives the search additional detail after a better coarser solution is found, and the vector is returned at the resolution proven to be exactly valid and better. Since the objective is evaluated with np.correlate * dx, larger grids can lower the discrete upper bound by approximating finer constructions.

1.3 Important limitations and hypotheses
----------------------------------------
- A lower exact score is the sole evidence of improvement; no convex problem or proof provides a global lower/upper match.
- The exact optimization is nonconvex due to product h[i] * h[j] terms. Therefore deterministic local stages are not outside global/heuristic loops.
- A multi-resolution vector at n_new is not guaranteed to match the same real step function semantically as the lower-resolution values under evaluator conventions. The shared and exact constraint is total integral: if coarse values are h[i] on cells of width dx_old, the transfer to n_new cell averages preserves ∑ h dx = 1; the discrepancy in objective is then evaluated exactly and accepted only if better.
- Symmetrization/complement/reflection are structurally motivated, not convex/at all-assuring methods. Every variation is re-evaluated exactly; if the score worsens it is not stored as the best. If the variation is introduced but later worsening moves occur, verifier monitors exact feasibility and best result.
