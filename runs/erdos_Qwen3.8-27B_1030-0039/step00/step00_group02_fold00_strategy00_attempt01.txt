1. Approach and rationale

The evaluator is an explicit discrete nonlinear problem: over a length-`n` vector `h` with independent entries constrained by `0 <= h[i] <= 1` and `sum(h) = n/2`, the score is

`C_5 = max_{full lags} np.correlate(h, 1 - h, mode="full") * (2 / n)`.

This is not a convex optimization: the expression contains products of optimization variables in the overlap counts. A direct single convex CVXPY model is therefore inappropriate because the objective is generally bilinear/minimax and the exact problem contains nonconvex products. The feasible set itself is a capped equality box. The returned vector must be judged exactly by the evaluator formula, not by a surrogate or circular convolution.

The central robust idea is a candidate-pool local optimizer with exact global bookkeeping:

- Start from the supplied current construction, never by hard-coding its entries, but by algorithmically reading, validating, and modifying it.
- Create a pool of feasible seeds: the parent, constrained neutrally projected perturbations, binaryized variants, deterministic smooth starts, stochastic starts, and simulated-annealing binary results at nearby dimension lengths.
- Optimize a stable exponential smoothing of the direct linear-correlation maximum with `'SLSQP'` or another derivative estimate. Use equality `sum(h) = n/2`, bounds `[0,1]`, and finite differences aligned to the actual `np.correlate` evaluator.
- Re-materialize every optimized vector in the feasible set using a separable fuwwaterfilling projection and exact residual correction.
- Accept candidates only by re-evaluating the original evaluator metric after final feasibility repair.
- Maintain the best globally valid candidate with a public upper bound equal to that exact score. Since the problem is nonconvex, this is explicitly a local/global candidate search, not a certification of global optimality.
- Allow multi-dimensional candidates (`n = initial_dimension` and slightly larger even dimensions or 48/50/60/64 styles may be used). A discrete grid may provide a cheaper representative construction if the discrete piecewise alignment is improved. The accepted score is the evaluator's exact discrete value for the returned `n_points`, so no silent monotonicity fallback is needed.

The bottleneck addressed is the combination of extreme constraints, exact linearity in boundary terms, and multiple near-maximal lag constraints in a nonmonotone discrete model. The search is randomized but deterministic under `seed`, so multiple sampled programs can vary the neighborhood schedule while always improving from or matching a valid fallback.

Justified facts versus heuristics:

- Facts: the problem is bilinear/nonconvex; exact objective is a full linear correlation integral/across finite support; constraints form a box plus equality; objective is globally nonnegative on feasible points; the evaluator uses full lags.
- Heuristic but reasonable: smoothed log-sum-exp approximation of the maximum is practical; local bounds stay near previous optimum and exact objective after repair gives valid upper-bound candidates; simulated annealing yields binary seeds; dimensional enlargement may increase expressive sufficiency but has no monotonic guarantee, so every candidate is scored by exact metric.
