Wait, I have a bug in the local_search. The line with `ap` is broken. Let me fix it. Actually, let me just remove that broken line and keep the simpler approach.

Let me also reconsider the time allocation. With 8 n-values and budget of 1300s, each gets about 140s. That's plenty for SA.

But actually, I realize that for larger n, the SA might not converge as well in the given time. And the theoretical advantage of larger n (smaller dx) might not materialize if the max correlation count grows proportionally.

Let me think about this more carefully. For a "well-optimized" binary sequence:
- The max correlation count should be O(√n) (based on results from low-autocorrelation sequence theory)
- So C₅ ≈ O(√n) * 2/n = O(1/√n)

If this holds, then larger n gives better results, and we should focus our time on the largest n we can optimize.

But in practice, the constant in O(√n) matters, and optimization might not achieve the theoretical bound for large n within our time budget.

Let me try a different strategy:
1. Spend most of the time on 2-3 well-chosen n values
2. Use a good initialization (from the parent + interpolation)
3. Use aggressive SA with multiple restarts

Let me also add a "perturbation" step: occasionally make a larger perturbation (swap multiple bits at once) to escape local optima. This is like a "large neighborhood search".
