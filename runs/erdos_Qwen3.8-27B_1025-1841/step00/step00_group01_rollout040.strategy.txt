### Approach and Rationale
The central idea is to construct a step function that minimizes the maximum overlap with a shifted version of itself. This involves periodic candidate constructions inspired by known minimal overlap solutions but with adjustments to fit the discrete nature and the discontinuity in the objective function. The representation uses two interleaved shifting patterns over a circular grid, leveraging finite-width spiking potentials to approximate continuous solutions. The approach differs from previous strategies by not using Fourier series or fixed-step variations but instead dynamically adapting the shift patterns to explore various overlap regions.

### Mathematical Specification
1. **Representation**:  
   The function \( h(x) \) is represented as:
   \[
   h(x) = 0.5 + A \cdot \text{rect}_1(x) + B \cdot \text{rect}_2(x)
   \]
   Where \(\text{rect}_1\) and \(\text{rect}_2\) are periodic rectangular functions with different phases and widths.
2. **Objective**:  
   Minimize \( C_5 = \max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx \) using discrete cross-correlation.
3. **Constraints**:  
   - \( 0 \leq h(x) \leq 1 \) for all \( x \).  
   - \( \int_0^2 h(x) dx = 1 \).  
4. **Continuous to Discrete**:  
   Use \( dx = 2/n_{\text{points}} \), and discretize \( h(x) \) into \( n_{\text{points}} \) values. The integral constraint ensures the discrete function's sum equals \( n_{\text{points}}/2 \).

### Implementation Procedure
1. **Initialization**:
   - If `initial_h_values` is provided, interpolate to `n_points`.
   - For the solution, use \( m = 5 \) intervals and \( F = 2 \) components.
2. **Optimization**:
   - Use sequential least-squares programming (SLSQP) to minimize \( C_5 \).  
   - The variables are the amplitudes \( A, B \) and the widths/passes of the rectangular functions.
3. **Correlation Evaluation**:
   - Compute max correlation using `np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full") * dx`.

### Search Choices and Useful Variations
- **Base Case**: Use \( A \in [0.5, 1.5] \), \( B \in [0.5, 1.5] \), widths \( W_1 = W_2 = 0.6 \).  
- **Annealing**: Decrease \( W \) over iterations.
