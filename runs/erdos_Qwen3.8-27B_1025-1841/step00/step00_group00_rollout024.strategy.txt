1. **Approach and rationale**
The central idea is to seek globally optimal solutions for the continuous relaxation of the Erdős minimum overlap problem, using a multi-resolution optimization with continuous variables. Unlike previous discrete strategies, this approach directly works with real-valued functions. It leverages three key innovations: (a) using a convex relaxation for the $\ell_1$-norm constraint by projecting step functions onto the simplex, (b) combining Fourier analysis with gradient-based optimization, and (c) iterative resolution doubling with directional search. The algorithm uses a two-stage Burer-Mathias style embedding with Hermite interpolation to reason about the behavior across different scales while maintaining continuity.

The main advantages are: (1) the optimization landscape is proven continuous, better than noisy discrete representations (2) we can use gradient-based methods for local refinement without getting trapped in discrete choose-which-pixel moves. The limitations include: (3) the problem is still non-convex but the continuous embedding makes local minima less elusive, and (4) Fourier projection does not guarantee preservation of the exact binary structure, which is needed to match the discrete checkerboard constructions.

2. **Mathematical specification**
- Representation: $h:[0,2]\to[0,1]$ represented using Fourier series truncated at $N$ modes. We assume $h \in \mathbb{R}^{N+1}$ with components $c_0,...,c_N$ and $h(x) = c_0/2 + \sum_{k=1}^N c_k\cos(2\pi k x) + \sin(2\pi k x)$
- Variables: Coefficients $c_k \in [-1,1]$ (normalized appropriately to ensure $h\in[0,1]+$ and $\int h dx = 1$
- Objective: Minimize $C_5 = \max_k\int h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx$, expressed as $C_5(h) = \frac{2}{n}\max_{k}\left|\langle h, \mathbf{g}_k \rangle\right|$ where $\mathbf{g}_k = (1-h_{\cdot+k})$
- Projection: $P\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{c} - \lambda\mathbf{1}$ where $\lambda$ adjusts the DC term to satisfy $\int h dx=1$, with $h\geq0$ enforced by a half-space constraint
- Relaxation: The.Interval/Box constraints are lifted to $\ell_1$ projection globally: $h^\star_{II} = \argmin \|h - \mathbf{p}\|_1 \,\text{s.t.}\, \int h dx=1$
- The optimization alternates between Fourier projection and gradient descent, projecting onto the feasible set after each iteration
