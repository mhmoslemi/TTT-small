1. **Approach and Rationale**
   - **Central Idea**: The minimum overlap problem is transformed into a continuous constrained optimization problem using harmonic analysis and projected gradient methods. Unlike previous methods relying solely on gradient optimization, this approach incorporates periodicity and spectral constraints to separate shape optimization from high-frequency domains.
   - **Key Insight**: By expressing h(x) as a Fourier series and penalizing high-frequency components, we create a smoother optimization landscape that escapes local minima while respecting domain constraints. The solution should exhibit high concentration and misalignment between h(x) and its shifted variants.
   - **Bottleneck Addressed**: The provided gradient-based methods struggle with boundary effects and non-convexity. This method introduces explicit period-2 constraints and regularization to mitigate these issues.

2. **Mathematical Specification**
   - **Variables**: h(x) defined on [0,2], with dx=2/n_points.
   - **Objective**: Minimize 
     \[
     C(h) = \max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx
     \]
     approximated by \((dx) \times \max \text{cross-correlations}\).
   - **Constraints**:
     - \(0 \leq h(x) \leq 1\)
     - \(\int_0^2 h(x) dx = 1\)
     - \(h(x)\) is periodic with period 2: \(h(x+2) = h(x)\).
   - **Representation**: Fourier series with coefficients \(a_k, b_k\):
     \[
     h(x) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{k=1}^\infty \left[ a_k \cos(k\pi x) + b_k \sin(k\pi x) \right]
     \]
   - **Objective Transformation**: Uses Parseval's identity to compute cross-correlations in Fourier space.
