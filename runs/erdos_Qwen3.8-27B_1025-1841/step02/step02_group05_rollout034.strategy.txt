1. Approach and Rationale
   The central idea is to employ a genetic algorithm (GA) based optimization with a population of step functions. Unlike gradient-based methods that can be trapped in local optima, GA explores the solution space through crossover and mutation, enabling discovery of global optima. We represent step functions as discrete grids fixed at 2000 points (dx = 2/2000) and optimize the grid values (h[i] in [0,1]) to minimize the maximum overlap integral (C5). The GA maintains population diversity through tournament selection and crossover (SPX), while mutation introduces perturbations. This approach is justified by its ability to navigate complex non-convex landscapes and avoid local minima that plague gradient-based methods.

2. Mathematical Specification
   The optimization problem:
      Minimize: C5(h) = max_{k} ∫ h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx
      Subject to: ∫ h(x) dx = 1 and 0 ≤ h(x) ≤ 1 for all x in [0,2].

   We discretize [0,2] with N=2000 points. The objective becomes:
      C5(h) = max_k (coefficient * sum_i h[i] * (1-h[i+k])), where coefficient = 2/N.

   Constraints are enforced by projection to the feasible set [0,1] with integral constraint 1000 (since sum(h)* (2/2000) = 1).

   The algorithm: Initialize population with 50 individuals derived from `initial_h_values` by random perturbation. Each generation:
   - Select parents via tournament selection
   - Perform Simulated Binary Crossover (SPX) for crossover
   - Apply Gaussian mutation (mean 0, std 0.05) with probability 0.1
   - Project all individuals to feasibility
   - Replace two worst individuals with two children
