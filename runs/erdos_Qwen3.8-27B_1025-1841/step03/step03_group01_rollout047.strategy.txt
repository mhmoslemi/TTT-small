### Approach and Rationale  

The core idea is to represent the function \( h(x) \) as a piecewise polynomial interpolant throughout the optimization process. This breaks \( h(x) \) into basis coefficients instead of point values, leveraging the power of \( \ell_1 \)-penalized optimization (LASSO) to encourage piecewise smoothness and sparsity. This approach combines benefits from harmonic analysis, convex optimization, and sparse representation, addressing the non-convexity of the problem with tailored relaxations.  

The key steps and their justifications are:  
1. **Piecewise Polynomial Basis**: Use B-spline basis functions parameterized by knots and coefficients. This allows \( h(x) \) to be reconstructed with minimal oscillations and directional jumps.  
2. **Convex Relaxation**: The \( \ell_1 \) penalty converts the basis coefficients into a sparse representation, effectively clustering similar segments of \( h(x) \) while retaining smoothness.  
3. **Iterated Reweighted Least Squares (IRLS)**: Solving the sparse-regularization problem iteratively ensures that peaks and valleys in \( h(x) \) are preserved while minimizing overlap, a hallmark of the Erdős Minimum Overlap Problem.  
4. **Hyperparameter Tuning**: Sweep the \( \ell_1 \)-parameter \( \lambda \) to balance sparsity and smoothness, ensuring the trade-off aligns with the global minimum known to be around 0.3809.  
5. **Tractable Computation**: By fixing resolution early and iteratively refining coefficients, focusing on higher-resolution grids avoids prematurely discarding optimal candidates.  

### Mathematical Specification  

We discretize the domain \([0, 2]\) into \( N = 2560 \) points for sufficient resolution. Piecewise polynomials are represented via B-spline basis:  
- **Basis Functions**: \( \{ \phi_j(x) \}_{j=1}^M \), constructed with uniform knots spaced \( 1/(N-1) \) apart.  
- **Function Representation**: \(\mathbf{h}(x) = \sum_{j=1}^M \beta_j \phi_j(x)\), where \( \beta \in \mathbb{R}^M \) are coefficients.  
- **Constraints**:  
  - \(\sum_{j=1}^M \beta_j \int_0^2 \phi_j(x) \, dx = \int_0^2 h(x) \, dx = 1\).  
  - Non-negativity: \(\beta \geq 0\) (enforced via a log-transformation or soft constraints).  
  - Point Bounds: \( 0 \leq h(x) \leq 1 \) discretized as \( h(x_i) \in [0, 1] \) for all \( i \).  

**Objective Relaxation**:  
The original objective \( C_5 = \max_k \int_0^2 h(x) (1 - h(x+k)) \, dx \) is approximated by:  
1. **Correlation Matching**: Optimize over both positions and coefficients by formulating the correlation as a function of basis weights, using the periodicity implied by the erdos-specific implementation.  
   - The correlation \( \tau_k = \sum_{i=1}^N h(x_i) (1 - h(x_i + k)) \) becomes \( \tau_k(\beta) = \sum_{i,j} \beta_i \beta_j (1 - \beta_j) \) if using a discrete matching correlation.  

**Algorithm Structure**:  
- **Constraint Handling**: Use projective penalization: \( |\beta|_1 + \) smoothing term to enforce bounds, controlled by a fixed \( \lambda \).  
- **Solver Input**: The optimization problem is:  
  \[
  \min_{\beta} \left( -\lambda \sum_{k} \tau_k(\beta) + (1 - \lambda) \|\beta\|_1 \right)
  \]  
  Truncated for high-value correlations.  

### Implementation Procedure  

**Steps**:  
1. **Basis Initialization**: Define \( M \) B-spline basis coefficients (\( M \) adaptive) uniformly over 2560 points. This implicitly includes \( \ell_1 \)-induced sparsity.  
2. **Precompute Basis Features**:  
   - Matrix \(\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{2560 \times M}\): Rows for each \( x_i \), columns for each \( \beta_j \). Each row is \(\mathbf{A}(i, j) = \phi_j(x_i)\).  
   - Binding constraints: \(\mathbf{c} \beta = \mathbf{d}\), where \(\mathbf{c}\) gathers integral or bound constraints.  

3. **Iterative Sparse Optimization (IRLS)**:  
   - **Initial Guess**: Start with parent solution (160 repeats) or uniform \( \beta \).  
   - **IRLS Iteration**:
     - Initialize residual weights \( w^{(t)} \).  
     - Form weighted \( \ell_1 \) loss: \( \sum_i w_i |\beta_i| \).  
     - Update \( \beta^{(t)} = (\mathbf{C} + \lambda_t \mathbf{C}^{\top} \mathbf{W}^{(t)} )^{-1} \mathbf{b} \), where \( \mathbf{C} \) factors the basis into the constraint matrix.  
     - Solve \( \min_{\beta} \left( \sum_i w_i |\beta_i| + \mathbf{\ell}(\beta) \right) \), where \( \mathbf{\ell}(\beta) \) models the correlation penalty.  
   - **Stopping**: Until \( \| \beta^{(t+1)} - \beta^{(t)} \| < \epsilon \) or time exceeds budget.  

4. **Scoring**:  
   - Evaluate \( C_5 \) via discrete-sum correlation at 2560 points.  
   - Coat the score with the parent's method for correlation at all lags, ensuring we capture the maximum overlap.  

**Key Helper Functions**:  
- `assemble_phi(M: int) -> Callable[[np.ndarray], np.ndarray]`  
- `compute_AandC(N_points, M)`: Build basis matrices.  
- `integral_constraint(beta, phi, N_points)`: Check \( \int_0^2 h(x) \, dx = 1 \).
