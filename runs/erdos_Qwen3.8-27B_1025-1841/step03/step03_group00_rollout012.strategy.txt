## 1. Approach and Rationale

The central idea is to transform the optimization problem into the Fourier domain using Chebyshev basis functions. This differs from parent approaches by exploiting spectral decomposition properties to capture global patterns while maintaining local structure. Chebyshev polynomials provide bounded approximations of smooth functions on bounded intervals and possess orthogonality properties that regularize optimization. The Fourier transform is particularly suited to handle shift-invariance in the overlap integral, and frequency-domain operations can be more computationally efficient than spatial domain operations for certain tasks. We hypothesize that optimal solutions often exhibit near-periodic structures that correspond to low-frequency modes, making spectral methods particularly apt.

This approach addresses two key bottlenecks in the current implementation:
1. **Local Optima Traps**: Multi-resolution strategies rely on progressive refinement from coarse to fine grids, which can easily get trapped in local optima. Fourier methods provide a global optimization landscape due to their frequency decomposition properties.
2. **Inefficient Computation**: Direct calculation of the overlap integral is O(n²) in resolution. Fourier-domain computation is O(n log n), making it computationally more feasible for higher resolution optimizations.

The method is named "Chebyshev-Greedy" because it combines a bounded-function approximation via Chebyshev basis with an adaptive, greedy optimization that prioritizes frequency bands based on their impact on the objective. Unlike parent Phase I which does periodic pattern generation, this approach performs simultaneous optimization across all frequencies but concentrates effort on modes that significantly impact the objective.

Important limitations to consider:
- Assumes optimal solutions have limited bandwidth in the Fourier domain (though higher frequencies are truncated with exponential weighting)
- Requires careful handling of boundary conditions due to domain doubling that can introduce spectral leakage
- May perform poorly for highly erratic functions that require sharp transitions in both domains

    """Generate first n_terms Chebyshev polynomials on [-1,1] space"""
    T = np.zeros((len(x), n_terms))
    T[:,0] = np.ones(len(x))  # T0(u) = 1

            T[:,k] = 2*x*T[:,k-1] - T[:,k-2]  # General recurrence
    return T
