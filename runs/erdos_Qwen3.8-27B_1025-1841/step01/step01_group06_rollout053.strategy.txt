### Approach and Rationale

I propose to reframe the Erdős minimum overlap problem as a **gradient constellation search** on the unit sphere. The core idea is to represent the step function as a vector on the (n-1)-dimensional sphere constrained by its integral (sum(h)=1). This allows us to leverage Riemannian optimization techniques to escape local minima.

**Key advantages**:
1. Gradient-based optimization will converge faster to better candidates than the subgradient method's 300 iterations
2. The spherical embedding naturally handles the constraints and periodic nature
3. We'll find stationary points with smaller curvature characteristics compared to the current approach

**Limitations and bottlenecks**:
1. The embedding ignores function space structure - we're not enforcing the step function property directly
2. Calculating the Hessian-free gradient-adjoint is costly for large n
3. No statistical theory guarantees convergence to global optimum

### Mathematical Specification

**Objective**: Minimize $$C_5=\max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx$$

**Constraints**:
$$0\le h_i \le 1, \sum h_i = n/2$$ where $n$ points uniformly spaced in $[0,2]$

**Representation**:
- Embed $h$ as a point on $\mathbb{S}^{n-1} \cap \Delta_n^{(1)}$ where $\Delta^{(1)}_n = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n: \sum x_i = 1/2, 0 \le x_i \le 1\}$
- Unit sphere: $\|h\|_2=1$
- Additional feasible set: $\Delta^{(1)}_n$

**Gradient Calculation**:
1. First component: $\nabla_J(\eta) = P(\nabla_H J^i(\eta))$ for each sampled correlation $i$
   where $J^i(h)=\langle h, (1-h+k)\rangle$
2. Projected gradient via retraction $R(u) = \frac{R_{\Delta}(\exp_{\eta} u)}{\|\cdot\|_2}$
3. Use the Moreau-Yosida approximation for non-smooth max penalty

**Algorithm**:
- Use SR1 quasi-Newton method on the spherical manifold
- Backtrack line search along the geodesic flow
- Stochastic Jacobian estimation every 10 steps
- Perform restarts (n restarting points per resolution)
