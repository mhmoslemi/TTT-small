Approach and rationale:
The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks to minimize the maximum overlap between a step function h and its shifted complement. The current record is 0.38085, and despite recent constructions showing potential for improvement, the problem remains challenging due to its non-convex nature. The proposed approach leverages continuous optimization guided by multi-dimensional features built from mixed-shift correlations. This considers correlations at three canonical lag types, normalized to handle variations from full-period and zero-shift evaluations. The method systematically explores feature space using a hypersurface-based parameter optimization, following convergence validation with Monte Carlo sampling to avoid premature stagnation. Unlike previous methods focused solely on maximizing correlations at discrete lags, this strategy adaptively expands dimensionality by interleaving lag-shifting and reaction-diffusion-inspired phase transitions to potentially escape local optima. Two-phase execution—dense feature sampling followed by sparse hyperparameter tuning—targets high-performance computation while respecting resource constraints.
