This strategy adapts a multi-resolution framework using physics-informed basis functions. The key idea is to represent the step function using piecewise constant components over a hierarchy of time scales (resolutions), allowing deterministic dynamics to simulate exploration. This approach combines adaptive basis functions with projected gradient steps to enforce constraints and escape local minima. The algorithm balances exploration/exploitation and leverages the inductive bias of sparse representations to achieve improvements with less local-search intensity compared to random restarts.  

### Mathematical Specification  
The step function \( h(x) \) on \([0, 2]\) is approximated by sparse basis functions:  
\[ h(x) = \sum_{i=1}^N c_i \cdot g_i(x, \theta) \]  
- \(c_i\) are coefficients (weights satisfying \(\sum_i c_i = 1\) and \(c_i \geq 0\))  
- \(g_i(x, \theta)\) are piecewise basis functions (triangular pulses) parameterized by sparse sets \(\Theta\)  
- \(N\) is the number of non-zero coefficients, dynamically adjusted  

The objective and constraints:  
\[ \min_{c \in \mathbb{R}_+, N} \max_k \left( \int h(x) (1 - h(x+k)) \, dx \right) \]
