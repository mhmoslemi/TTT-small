### Strategy Plan for Erdős Minimum Overlap Problem

#### 1. Approach and Rationale
**Central Idea:**  
The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks to minimize the maximum overlap between a step function \( h(x) \) (with integral 1) and the complement \( 1 - h(x+k) \) across all shifts \( k \). Our approach uses **constrained gradient projection** to iteratively refine a candidate solution by enforcing two key constraints: \( 0 \leq h[i] \leq 1 \) and \( \sum h[i] = N/2 \) (where \( N \) is the number of samples). Unlike domain transformation (e.g., from coarser to finer resolutions) or multi-start random search, this method leverages gradient **flow analysis** directly on the discrete objective, making it computationally efficient and locally rigorous.

**Objective Contribution:**  
We optimize the overlap integral:
\[
C_5 = \max_{k} \left( dx \cdot \text{np.correlate}(h, 1-h, \text{mode}=\"full\"\right)_\text{peak}
\]
subject to \( h[i] \in [0, 1] \) \( \forall i \) and \( \sum h[i] = N/2 \) exactly.

**Bottlenecks Addressed:**  
- **Non-convexity:** Gradient projection avoids local traps by projecting gradients into feasible sets.
- **Discretization:** Chebyshev interpolation guides limit progression to maintain smoothness.
- **Exploration-Exploitation:** Progressive resolution from \( N = 42 \) (parent) via simulated annealing ensures we don’t miss finer-grained improvements.

#### 2. Mathematical Specification
**Variables:**  
- \( h \): A real-valued \( N \)-dimensional vector, each \( h[i] \in [0, 1] \).
- \( dx = 2/N \): Spacing ensures integral \( \sum h[i] \cdot dx = 1 \) (i.e., \( \sum h[i] = N/2 \)).

**Constraints:**  
- **Box Constraints:** \( 0 \leq h[i] \leq 1 \) for all \( i \).
- **Integral Constraint:** \( \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} h[i] = N/2 \).

**Objective Function:**  
- Compute \( b = 1 - h \) (element-wise).
- Perform \( \text{np.correlate}(h, b, \text{mode}=\"full\") \) (O(N²) correlation).
- The peak value of this correlation, multiplied by \( dx \), is \( C_5 \).

**Gradient Formulation:**  
The gradient of \( C_5 \) (max operation) is the subgradient of the peak correlation component. For a non-differentiable point \( \text{argmax}_k \), we use the average ascent gradient for all \( k \) contributing to the maximum.

**Feasibility Projection:**  
Clip values to [0, 1] and scale to meet \( \sum h[i] = N/2 \) via:
\[
h_{\text{proj}} = \frac{h_{\text{clip}} \cdot (N/2)}{\sum h_{\text{clip}}}
\]
ensuring exact integral satisfaction (may hinge \( h[i] \) slightly beyond [0, 1] temporarily).
