### Approach and Rationale
The core idea is to reformulate the minimum overlap problem as a continuous optimization on the unit sphere with linear and box constraints. This allows leveraging manifold optimization techniques to escape local minima more effectively than gradient descent. The sphericał embedding ensures:
- Native handling of integral and sum constraints
- Dimensionality reduction (projected gradients)
- Geodesic search along the sphere with exponential maps

**Key advantages**:
- Escapes flat-space optimization traps
- Adapts to problem curvature through geodesic linesearch
- Theoretical foundation in Riemannian geometry

**Limitations**:
- Expensive curvature computations
- Doesn't enforce step-function structure directly
- Requires explict Jacobian estimation

**Algorithm**:
1. Project variables to unit sphere slice
2. Compute geodesic gradients using Ninomiya's formula
3. Perform exponential map line searches
4. Use Moreau-Yosida regularization for smooth optimization

This approach addresses local minima by:
- Hyperparameter tuning (temperature, sampling)
- Multi-step optimization (gradient → exponential → projected gradients)
- Scale invariance through spherical embedding

### Mathematical Specification
**Variables**: $h \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ representing step-function samples
**Constraints**:
- Box: $0 \leq h_i \leq 1$
- Sum: $\sum h_i = n/2$
- Sphere: $\|h\|_2 = 1$

**Objective**:
$\min \max_k [dx \cdot \langle h, (1-h) \circ S_k h \rangle]$

**Smoothed Objective**:
$\min \frac{1}{T} \log \left(\sum_{j=1}^T \exp(T \cdot J(h))\right]$

**Gradient**:
$\nabla J(h) = \sum_{i} \exp\left(\frac{J_i(h)}{T}\right) \cdot \nabla J_i(h)$
