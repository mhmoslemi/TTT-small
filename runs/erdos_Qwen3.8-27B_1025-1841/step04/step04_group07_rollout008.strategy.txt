We are going to propose a strategy that leverages the concept of "layered optimization" and "sparse representations" to minimize the overlap integral C5.

### Approach and Rationale
The Erdős minimum overlap problem requires finding a function h(x) that minimizes the overlap integral C5, which is the maximum, over all shifts k, of the integral of h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx. The key challenge is that the problem is non-convex and high-dimensional when represented on a dense grid.

Our approach will be to use a two-stage optimization process:

1. **Sparse Representation Stage**: We will represent h(x) in a sparser form, such as a sum of shifted impulse functions (or raised pulses) at strategic locations. This reduces the effective dimensionality of the problem. The idea is that an optimal h(x) might be composed of a few, well-placed "blocks" (intervals where h(x) is high).

2. **Refinement Stage**: Once a candidate solution is found in the sparse representation, we refine it by sampling the function at a fine grid and optimizing the continuous version of the sparse solution.

The central idea is that the sparse representation allows us to explore promising regions of the solution space more efficiently, while the refinement stage ensures that the solution meets the discretization requirements and constraints.

This approach addresses the bottlenecks in the initial algorithm (which relies heavily on dense representation and gradient-based methods) by shifting the optimization to a lower-dimensional space initially and then zooming in.
