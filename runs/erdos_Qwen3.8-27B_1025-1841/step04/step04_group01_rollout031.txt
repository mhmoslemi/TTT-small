But wait, the Hessian doesn't depend on h (it's constant for fixed active set). So we can compute it once and reuse it.

Actually, the Hessian of f_k(h) = ∑_i h[i] - ∑_i h[i]·h[i+k] is:
- The linear term ∑h[i] has zero Hessian
- The bilinear term ∑_i h[i]·h[i+k]: ∂²/∂h[j]∂h[l] = -δ(l-j-k)·I(valid) - δ(l-j+k)·I(valid)

So H_k is a band matrix with -1 on the k-th and (-k)-th diagonals (where valid).

For the log-sum-exp smoothed max, the Hessian is more complex (involves second derivatives of log-sum-exp), but for a "nearly hard max" (large T), it's approximately the Hessian of the maximizing f_k.

**Practical approach: Gauss-Newton on the active set**
1. Identify active set A (top K shifts)
2. Form H = ∑_{k∈A} w_k H_k (banded matrix)
3. Solve H · Δh = -g (Newton step)
4. Project Δh to maintain feasibility
5. Apply step

This is much faster than SLSQP because:
- H is banded (can be factored in O(n·b) where b is bandwidth)
- We don't need to recompute the Hessian each iteration
- Each iteration is O(n·b) instead of O(n²) for dense Hessian
