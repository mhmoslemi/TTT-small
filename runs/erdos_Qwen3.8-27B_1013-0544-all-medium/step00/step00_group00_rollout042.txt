**Better approach: Use scipy.optimize.minimize with a smooth objective**

The smooth max (log-sum-exp) gives a differentiable upper bound on the true max:
smooth_max(f, β) = (1/β) * log(Σ exp(β*f_k))

As β → ∞, this approaches max(f). For β = 50-100, it's a good approximation.

The gradient of smooth_max is:
∇ smooth_max = (1/β) * Σ_k (β*f_k / Σ exp(β*f)) * ∇f_k
= Σ_k p_k * ∇f_k where p_k = exp(β*f_k) / Σ exp(β*f)

This is a weighted average of the individual gradients, giving a smooth gradient that points in the "right" direction.

Then I can use L-BFGS-B or even just gradient descent with this smooth gradient.
