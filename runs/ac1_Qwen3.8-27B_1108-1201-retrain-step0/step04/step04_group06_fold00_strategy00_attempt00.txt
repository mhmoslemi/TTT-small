**1. Approach and Rationale**
- The core idea is to design a sequence where the coefficients are neither too concentrated (to minimize peak autocorrelation) nor too sparse (to keep the sum large enough to avoid diminishing the objective).
- Instead of fixing the length or using advanced spectral methods, I rely on simple but effective vector scaling and projection to explore less risky directions. This is based on the observation that the objective function is separable in scale and shape, and can be improved by first shaping the autocorrelation properties and then adjusting the scale.
- I use two complementary optimization strategies: 
  - Gradient descent in L2 space to drive the convolution towards zero (inspired by spectral water-filling).
  - coordinate descent to adjust contributing coordinates directly.
- The search is structured with phases to ensure we do not waste time and explore promising directions first.

**2. Mathematical Specification**
We seek sequences satisfying:
  (a) Non-negative coefficients
  (b) Each coefficient <= 1000
  (c) Sum >= 0.01 (passed through a convexity-preserving projection)
  (d) Minimize object \(2n \times \max \text{convolution} / (\text{sum}^2)\)

The search algorithm operates in iterations that combine:
  - An eigenvector-like search to emphasize the initial structure (useful for convexity).
  - Directional projection to increase diversity (avoids stagnation).

The objective is evaluated via:
  - \( n = \text{length of the sequence} \)
  - \( \text{sum} = \sum a_i \)
  - \( \text{max\_conv} = \max(\text{full\_conv}(a, a)) \)
