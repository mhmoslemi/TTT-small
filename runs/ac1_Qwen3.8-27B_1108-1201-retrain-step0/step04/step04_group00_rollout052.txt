They're different! The convolution is NOT the same as the correlation!

The relationship is: `conv(a, a)[k] = sum_i a[i]*a[k-i]`. This is like a "circular" mixing of the values, not a shift.

So my earlier analysis was WRONG. The peak of `np.convolve(a, a)` is NOT necessarily `sum(a^2)`.

Let me re-derive. `conv(a,a)[k] = sum_{i} a[i]*a[k-i]` where both indices must be in [0, n-1].

For k = n-1: `conv[n-1] = sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a[i]*a[n-1-i]` = inner product of a with its reverse.

This is NOT `sum a[i]^2` unless a is palindromic!

So for non-palindromic sequences, the "center" of the convolution is smaller than `sum a[i]^2`, and the peak might be at a different lag.

This is the key insight I was missing! The problem is about minimizing the peak of the full linear self-convolution, which is different from the autocorrelation.

For a palindromic (symmetric) sequence, `conv[n-1] = sum a[i]^2` and this is the peak (by the Cauchy-Schwarz argument applied to the split). But for non-symmetric sequences, the peak can be elsewhere and can be smaller.

So the strategy is: find a non-symmetric, non-negative sequence whose self-convolution has a small peak!
