Let me also think about the **dual problem**. The primal is:
min max_k (a*a)[k] s.t. sum(a) = 1, a >= 0

The dual (roughly) is:
max sum_j lambda_j - (1/(4*mu)) * ||...||^2
where lambda is a distribution over lags (the "dual variable" for the max constraint), and mu is the dual variable for the sum constraint.

At the optimum, the dual variable lambda is concentrated on the lags where (a*a)[k] = max. And the condition is that for all j with a[j] > 0:
2 * sum_k lambda[k] * a[k-j] = 2*mu

This means: for all j with a[j] > 0, the "weighted correlation" of a with itself (weighted by lambda) is constant.

This gives us an iterative algorithm:
1. Initialize a (e.g., uniform) and lambda (e.g., uniform over all lags)
2. Compute c = a*a
3. Update lambda: put mass on the argmax lags of c (or use softmax)
4. Update a: for j where 2*sum_k lambda[k]*a[k-j] > 2*mu, reduce a[j]; where < 2*mu, increase a[j]
5. Repeat

This is essentially the **primal-dual** or **mirror descent** approach.
