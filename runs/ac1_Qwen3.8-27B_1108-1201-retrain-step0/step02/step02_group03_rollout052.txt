The issue is that any smooth window has sum(a_i^2) > (sum a_i)^2/n by the Cauchy-Schwarz gap, and for the symmetric case, the peak of the convolution is at the center where it equals sum(a_i^2).

This means for symmetric sequences, we CAN'T beat 2! The optimal symmetric sequence is the constant one with score exactly 2.

So the sequences that achieve score < 2 must be ASYMMETRIC! This is a crucial insight.

For an asymmetric sequence, the peak of the convolution might not be at the center (k=n-1). It could be at other lags. And the value at k=n-1 is sum a_i * a_{n-1-i}, which for an asymmetric sequence could be less than sum a_i^2.

So the strategy is: make the sequence asymmetric such that:
1. The peak of the convolution is spread out
2. No single lag has a too-large value
