## 1. Approach and Rationale

The central idea is to explicitly design sequences that minimize peak autocorrelation (max convolution value) through mathematical construction, leveraging periodic amplification and symmetry breaking. The method starts with a single "entity" at the sequence center and iteratively expands it by duplicating and adjusting references to other existing entities, ensuring the convolution peaks remain limited to only specific lags contributing most significantly to the score. This differs from earlier "black-box" optimization approaches. The innovation comes from directly manipulating the autocorrelation structure through combination and scale adjustments, avoiding reliance purely on stochastic optimization.

We first hypothesize that the optimal solution exhibits "shift-sensitivity," meaning no two lags should be equally weighted. By ensuring antisymmetric or "differential" relationships between elements, and by propagating entity references, we can systematically reduce dominant autocorrelation peaks while maintaining non-negativity. This approach is particularly effective at reducing the score by targeting the most harmful lags without excessive mass contribution from any single entity.

## 2. Mathematical Specification

We consider sequences that are interpreted as "entities" (discrete components) influenced by reference points. Each entity's norm (magnitude) and "direction" (relative difference from neighbors) are crucial variables.

### Variables:
- Sequence `a` of length `n`
- "Entities" defined by indices `i` in the sequence
- "References" between entities `i` and `j` where `j = i ± 1` or other differential relations (but initially stay simple)

### Representation Details:
The sequence will be constructed as a piecewise adjusted exponential sequence, influenced by its endpoints and maintaining non-negative values.

### Objective Function (Simplified):
Minimize `score = max_conv / (sum(a)²)` via controls on entity expansions and exponential scaling.

### Constraints:
- Each `a[i] >= 0.01`
- No single entity exceeds 1000 -> handled by clipping (not critical, but enforced)
- The sequence must be "antisymmetric" in the sense that `a[i] - a[i+1]` alternates signs or is sufficiently differentiated to reduce convolution contributions

### Optimization Setup:
We will form an objective set `S` where:
```math
S = { (a[i] - a[j])² * (a[i] + a[j]) + |a[i] + a[j]|/max(1, |i-j|) + ... }
```

This is a non-convex optimization problem, and the Adam iteration will be performed on an exponential representation of `a` to capture large range differences effectively.

        for index in top_n:

## 4. Search Choices and Hyperparameter Variations

We specify several ways to enhance the search:

1. **Exponential Annealing**:
   - Decays the total energy quickly, preventing explosion in peaks
   - Maximizes the effect of early updates

2. **Periodic Expansion**:
   - Every `exponent_steps` update, we consider expanding the sequence length by doubling? No, this might not help, as `n` doubles increases the score roughly by factor `n` (since new convocations have larger `n`).

   We decide not to change `n` in the optimization, because theAnton optimization may become infeasible.

3. **Symmetry Breaking**:
   - The sequence should not be symmetric around its center, to avoid self-correlation
   - We achieve this by making adjustments to eventually achieve anAntisymmetric pattern.
