For the self-convolution, c(k) = sum a[j]a[k-j], the max can be at any lag.

The key difference: for self-convolution, the "opposing" indices are j and k-j, which sum to k. For autocorrelation, the indices are j and j+k, which differ by k.

So for self-convolution at lag k, we're pairing indices that SUM to k. This means:
- At lag 0: a[0]*a[0] (just the first element squared)
- At lag 1: a[0]*a[1] + a[1]*a[0] = 2a[0]a[1]
- At lag n-1 (center): a[0]*a[n-1] + a[1]*a[n-2] + ... + a[n-1]*a[0] = 2*sum_{j<n/2} a[j]a[n-1-j] + (middle term if n odd)

So the center involves pairing the first half with the second half (reversed). To minimize this, we want the first half to be "opposite" to the second half.

If a[0..n/2] is increasing and a[n/2..n-1] is decreasing (or vice versa), the center is minimized.

But then what about other lags? For lag k < n/2, we pair a[0..k] with a[k..0] (reversed), which is a small segment. For lag k > n/2 (but < n), we pair a[k-n+1..n-1] with a[n-1..k-n+1] (reversed), which is also a small segment from the end.

So if we make the sequence increase from 0 to n/2 and then decrease from n/2 to n-1 (a "tent" or "triangle" shape), then:
- The center pairs a[0] with a[n-1] (both small), a[1] with a[n-2] (both small), ..., a[n/2] with a[n/2] (the peak).
  Center ≈ sum of (small * small) + peak^2. The peak^2 term could be large.
- Lags near 0: involve only small values. Small.
- Lags near 2n-2: involve only small values. Small.
- Lags near n/2: pair the rising edge with the falling edge. Could be moderate.

The problem is the peak. If the peak is too high, the center will be dominated by peak^2.

Alternative: make the sequence increase monotonically (no decrease). Then:
- Center: pairs small with large. Minimized by rearrangement.
- Lags near 2n-2: only the largest values. c[2n-2] = a[n-1]^2.

So the trade-off is between the center (minimized by monotonicity) and the edges (maximized by large endpoint values).

The optimal solution likely balances these: a monotonically increasing sequence where the ratio a[n-1]/a[0] is not too large.
