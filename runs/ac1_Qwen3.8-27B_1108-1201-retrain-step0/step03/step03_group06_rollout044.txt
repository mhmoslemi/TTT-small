Let me think about what sequences achieve low peak autocorrelation:

1. **Linear chirp**: a[i] = exp(i*pi*i^2/n) - but this is complex, not non-negative.

2. **All-pass filters**: In the analog domain, all-pass filters have flat magnitude response, meaning their autocorrelation is a delta. But we're in the discrete, finite-length, non-negative setting.

3. **M-sequence (maximal length sequence)**: These are ±1 sequences with nearly flat autocorrelation. But they're not non-negative.

4. **Non-negative M-sequences**: If we take an M-sequence and add a constant to make it non-negative, we get aish. The autocorrelation will have the M-sequence's low sidelobes plus the autocorrelation of the constant (which is a triangle).

5. **Optimal window design**: In radar/sonar, the design of low-Peak-Sidelobe-Ratio (PSLR) windows is a well-studied problem. The " Griffin-Luo" or "adaptive window" methods iteratively adjust the window to minimize the peak sidelobe.

Actually, this is very close to our problem! The PSLR problem is: design a window w such that the peak of the sidelobes of |W(f)|^2 (equivalently, the peak of the autocorrelation of w) is minimized.

The key difference is that in the PSLR problem, the window can have negative values, while here we require non-negative values. This is a significant constraint.

With the non-negativity constraint, the problem is harder. The previous code achieves 1.51, which is better than the uniform (2.0) but not by a huge margin.
