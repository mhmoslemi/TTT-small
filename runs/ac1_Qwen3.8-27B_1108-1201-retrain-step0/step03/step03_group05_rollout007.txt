**New idea: What about a sequence that's periodic?**

If a has period p (repeating pattern of length p), then the convolution at lag k is approximately (n/p) times the convolution of the period with itself (for k not too close to the edges).

The peak of the period-convolution is at lag 0 (within one period), which equals sum of squares of the period. If the period has values {v_0, ..., v_{p-1}}, then the peak ≈ (n/p) * sum v_i^2.

sum_a ≈ (n/p) * sum v_i.

Objective ≈ 2n * (n/p) * sum(v^2) / ((n/p)^2 * (sum v)^2) = 2n * (n/p) * sum(v^2) * p^2 / (n^2 * (sum v)^2)
= 2p * sum(v^2) / (sum v)^2

So for a periodic sequence with period p and pattern v, the objective is approximately 2p * sum(v_i^2) / (sum v_i)^2.

By Cauchy-Schwarz, sum(v_i^2)/sum(v_i)^2 ≥ 1/p, with equality when all v_i are equal.

So the minimum is 2p * 1/p = 2, achieved when v is constant (which is just the constant sequence).

For non-constant periodic patterns, the objective is > 2. So periodic sequences can't beat 2 either!
