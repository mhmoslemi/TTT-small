**1. Approach and Rationale**

The central idea is to leverage a multi-stage optimization process to construct a sequence that minimizes the given objective function. This approach focuses on balancing exploration and exploitation by combining different optimization techniques at each phase. Unlike the original program, we adopt a variation called **Adaptive Geometric Flows (AGF)**, which iteratively adjusts the sequence using gradients derived from both the sum and the convolution metrics. This avoids the gradient explosion issues seen in FISTA and coordinate descent, allowing for smoother convergence to sequences with lower convolution peaks.

The rationale is twofold: 
- First, the sum constraint (normalized to S = √(2n)) introduces a scaling invariance. By decoupling the sequence updates from this constraint and incorporating gradients that account for both the magnitude and position, we can more aggressively drive the objective down.
- Second, the convolution peak (max(b)) is not solely determined by the sum. Geometric flows allow for localized updates, enabling the algorithm to focus on elements contributing most to the peak, thus achieving more precise optimization.

This strategy is particularly effective for sequences with moderate to large n, as it reuses the parent sequence initialization but applies new updates via conjugate gradient steps. Key limitations include the sensitivity to the initial sequence strength and the need for high-dimensional spaces (starting from 8192) to uncover strong global structures.

**2. Mathematical Specification**

We represent the sequence as a vector **a** of length n, initialized with the parent's length or an appropriate default. The objective O is defined as:
