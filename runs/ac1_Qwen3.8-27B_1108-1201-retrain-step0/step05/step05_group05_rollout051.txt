    K = len(active)

    A_eq = np.zeros((1, K + 1))
    A_eq[0, :K] = 1.0
    b_eq = np.array([1.0])
    
    bounds = [(0, None)] * K + [(None, None)]
    
    res = linprog(c_obj, A_ub=A_ub, b_ub=b_ub, A_eq=A_eq, b_eq=b_eq,

Actually, for the KKT subgradient, here's an efficient approach:

The subgradient of the max-type objective is any convex combination of the active subgradients. The "best" subgradient for descent is one that points most "inward" to the feasible region.

For our problem (minimize f(a) subject to a ≥ 0, Σa = S), the feasible directions from a are: d such that d_j ≤ 0 for a_j = 0, and Σ d_j = 0 (tangent to the simplex).

We want a subgradient g such that <g, d> < 0 for all feasible descent directions d. The "steepest descent" subgradient is the one that maximizes -<g, d> / ||d|| over all feasible d.

This is equivalent to: find g in the subdifferential that minimizes ||P_T(g)|| where P_T is projection onto the tangent cone. If ||P_T(g)|| = 0, we're at the optimum.

The tangent space of the simplex at a is: T_a = {d : Σ d_j = 0, d_j = 0 for a_j = 0}.

P_T(g): for the support of a (indices where a_j > 0), the projection of g onto the tangent space is: g_T = g_supp - mean(g_supp) (where g_supp = g restricted to the support).

So we want to find weights λ on the active lags such that ||(g_λ)_supp - mean((g_λ)_supp)|| is minimized, where g_λ = Σ λ_k * 2*a_{k-j} (j in support).

This is a QP in λ:
minimize ||(G_λ)_supp - c(λ)||² where c(λ) = mean of (G_λ)_supp
subject to: Σ λ = 1, λ ≥ 0
