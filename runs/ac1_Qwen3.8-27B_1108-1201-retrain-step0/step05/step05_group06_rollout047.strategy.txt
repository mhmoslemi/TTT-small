# Step-by-Step Optimization Strategy

## 1. Approach and Rationale
We aim to minimize the objective function \( f(a) = \frac{2n \cdot \max(b_{\text{sequence}})}{\text{sum}(a)^2} \), where \( b_{\text{sequence}} \) is the full self-convolution of the sequence \( a \). 
The central idea is to use **Fourier transforms** for efficient computation and optimization, while implementing a **multi-phase coordinate ascent** strategy with adaptive escaping mechanisms.

Why Fourier transforms? 
- They convert convolution operations into pointwise multiplications (O(n log n) complexity)
- Enable matrix-free optimization (avoiding dense Hessian storage)

Why coordinate ascent?
- We optimize one coefficient at a time, allowing local search
- Carefully select and update each coefficient

Key differences from previous strategies:
- More selective coordinate updates
- Adaptive escaping to avoid local minima
- Faster convergence via gradient projection

## 2. Mathematical Specification
### Cardinality Formulation
The problem depends only on:
- \( s = \sum_{i} a_i \) - total sum
- \( m = \max_j (a * a)_j \) - maximum convolution value

where \( (a * a)_j = \sum_{i+k=j} a_i a_k \) over suitable indices for full convolution.

### Optimization Problem
We wish to minimize \( f(a) = \frac{2n \cdot m}{s^2} \) subject to \( a_i \geq 0 \) for all \( i \) and \( s \geq 0.01 \).

Let \( l = \min_j (a * a)_j \), then \( m \) is the upper blue-noise counterpart.
