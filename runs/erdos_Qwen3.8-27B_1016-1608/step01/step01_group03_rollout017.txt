Actually, one more thought. Let me reconsider the problem from scratch.

C₅ = max_k ∫₀⁺∞ h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx

For this to be small, we need h to be "well-mixed" at all scales. The function h(x)(1-h(x+k)) should be small for all k.

If h(x) ≈ 1 and h(x+k) ≈ 0, the product is ≈ 1 (bad).
If h(x) ≈ 0 and h(x+k) ≈ 1, the product is ≈ 0 (good for this term but the other lag will be bad).
If h(x) ≈ h(x+k) ≈ 0.5, the product is ≈ 0.25.
If h(x) ≈ h(x+k) ≈ 0 or ≈ 1, the product is ≈ 0 (good).

So the ideal is h(x) ≈ h(x+k) for all k, which means h is constant (h=0.5, giving C₅=0.5). But we can do better by making h(x) and h(x+k) both high or both low more often.

Actually, for the product h(x)(1-h(x+k)) to be small, we want h(x) and h(x+k) to be correlated (both high or both low). This is the autocorrelation of h. We want high positive autocorrelation at all lags.

But h can't have high positive autocorrelation at ALL lags (that would mean it's nearly constant). There's a trade-off.

The optimal solution balances:
- High autocorrelation (to reduce the product)
- The constraint ∫h = 1 (which forces h to average to 0.5)
