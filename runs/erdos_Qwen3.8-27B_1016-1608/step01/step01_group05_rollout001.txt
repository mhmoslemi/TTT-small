Now let me think about the coordinate descent more carefully. The key insight is:

When we change h[j] by δ, how does corr change?
- corr_m = sum_i h[i] * (1-h[i+m]) for valid i (where m is the lag)
- h[j] appears in corr_m when j is in the valid range for that m
  - As the first factor: i=j, so m can be any lag where j+m is valid
  - As the second factor: i+m=j, so m = j-i, where i is valid

This is complex. Let me just recompute the full correlation after each change. For n=90, it's fast enough.

Actually, let me think about a smarter coordinate descent:
- Instead of trying all coordinates in order, focus on the coordinates that most affect the max correlations
- Identify which k values are at the max
- Find which h[j] values appear in those correlations
- Try changing those first

This "active set" approach should converge faster.

Let me also think about a different optimization strategy: **Alternating Minimization on k**.

1. Fix h, identify the max k*
2. Minimize f_{k*}(h) = sum_i h[i]*(1-h[i+k*]) subject to constraints
3. Repeat

This is like a subgradient method where at each step we minimize the "most violating" constraint. Since f_{k*} is a quadratic in h (it's sum of h[i] - h[i]*h[i+k*]), minimizing it is a QP... but not convex (the -h[i]*h[i+k*] term makes it non-convex).

Actually, f_{k*}(h) = sum_i h[i] - sum_i h[i]*h[i+k*]. The first term is linear, the second is a sum of products. This is a quadratic form but not necessarily convex or concave.

For a single k, the Hessian of f_k has 1s on the "shifted diagonal" (positions (i, i+k) and (i+k, i)) with coefficient -1, and the rest 0. The eigenvalues of this matrix are ±1, so it's indefinite. Not helpful for convex optimization.

OK let me just stick with the smooth max + SLSQP approach. It should work well.
