Hmm, actually the numpy docs say:
c[k] = sum_n a[n+k] * conj(v[n])

where k ranges from -(len(v)-1) to len(a)-1.

For our case: a = h, v = 1-h, both length n.
c[k] = sum_n h[n+k] * (1-h[n]) for valid n.

When k = 0: c[0] = sum_n h[n]*(1-h[n])
When k > 0: c[k] = sum_{n=0}^{n-1-k} h[n+k]*(1-h[n])
When k < 0: c[k] = sum_{n=-k}^{n-1} h[n+k]*(1-h[n])

In the "full" mode, the output has length 2n-1, indexed from 0 to 2n-2.
The mapping is: output[m] = c[m - (n-1)] where m goes from 0 to 2n-2.
So m = n-1 corresponds to k=0.
m = n-1+k corresponds to shift k > 0.
m = n-1+k corresponds to shift k < 0 (i.e., m < n-1).

For the overlap problem:
- overlap at shift k (k>0) = c[k] = sum_n h[n+k]*(1-h[n]) * dx
  = number of positions where h is 1 at position n+k and 0 at position n (times dx for binary)
- overlap at shift k (k<0) = c[k] = sum_n h[n+k]*(1-h[n]) * dx
  = number of positions where h is 1 at position n+k and 0 at position n

For binary h:
- overlap at shift 1: count of pairs (i, i+1) where h[i+1]=1 and h[i]=0
- overlap at shift -1: count of pairs (i, i-1) where h[i-1]=1 and h[i]=0

So overlap at shift +k and overlap at shift -k are different in general!
