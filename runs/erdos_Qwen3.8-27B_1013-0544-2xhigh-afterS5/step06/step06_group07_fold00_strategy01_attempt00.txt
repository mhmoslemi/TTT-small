The core challenge we face is minimizing the maximum overlap between a step function h(x) and its shifted version 1-h(x+k). Our approach leverages multidimensional optimization by decomposing the function representation and considering both continuous and combinatorial aspects for more efficient exploration of the solution space. Unlike previous methods that mixed smooth and binary representations with complex annealing schedules, we use a hierarchical strategy with three distinct phases:

1. Structure Optimization - Focuses on selecting "active" regions of the function to uncover hidden regularities
2. Component Optimization - Unrolls potential field components contributing to overlap
3. Filter Design - Frames shift invariance through wavelet-like filters to limit correlation peaks

The bottleneck these addresses is premature convergence to local optima.
