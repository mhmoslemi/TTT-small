1. Approach and Rationale
We propose a multi-resolution optimization algorithm that combines iterative projection techniques with adaptive grid refinement. Unlike previous approaches that directly optimize on fixed grids, our method leverages the solution anatomy (underlying structure) of step function approximations to:
   - Identify repeating patterns in optimal solutions
   - Construct multi-scale candidate bases
   - Use cross-scale transfer learning to improve accuracy
The key insight is that near-optimal solutions often exhibit sparse, periodic structures that can be truncated to lower resolutions. The central idea is to iteratively refine these structures through (a) spectral projection, (b) cross-frequency information transfer, and (c) basis vector selection from a learned dictionary.

This approach addresses the convergence bottlenecks of local optimizations by introducing:
   - Analytic divergence estimates to direct search
   - Optimization-invariant structure validation
   - Goal-oriented adaptive grid generation (n=300→150, 500, 1000)
The method can escape local optima by projecting gradients through the solution anatomy, effectively providing "intrinsic coordinates" for the optimization space.

   h(x) = Σᵢ θᵢ φᵢ(x)

   Constraint space: {h | ∫h dx=1, h∈[0,1]}

The optimization uses:
