1. Approach and Rational
We use a two-layer optimization approach combining global exploration with local refinement to tackle the non-convex Erdős minimum overlap problem. The core idea is to first identify promising regions in the discrete optimization space through comprehensive crossover and mutation operations, then refine each candidate solution via gradient-based optimization. The approach balances exploration (keeping solutions diverse) and exploitation (improving fitness) to escape local optima. The method favors solutions with structure—knot points that align well with the periodical nature of the problem while maintaining smoothness to avoid abrupt discontinuities that may increase overlap.

The critical bottleneck is the highly non-convex objective function with max and constraints, so we treat it as a mixed-integer problem where candidate points are optimized simultaneously. We build upon the known 0.3808 solution as an initial reference point but don't rely on it, using a seed-based population search to ensure robustness.

2. Mathematical Specification
We represent solutions by:
   - `h(x) ∈ [0,1] for x ∈ [0,2]`
   - Piecewise constant approximation with `n_points` intervals

The objective minimizes:
```math
C_5 = \max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx
```

with constraints:
```math
0 ≤ h(x) ≤ 1 &nd int_0^2 h(x)dx = 1
```

Discrete formulation:
   - Objective function: `max_k [dx * C(k)]` where `dx=2.0/n_points`
   - Constraints: `h[i] ∈ [0,1]` and `sum(h)*dx=1`

Derivative for piecewise constant:
```math
ΔC(k) = dx * [h[k:j] ⊙ (1-h[k-:k+1-j])].sum()
```
