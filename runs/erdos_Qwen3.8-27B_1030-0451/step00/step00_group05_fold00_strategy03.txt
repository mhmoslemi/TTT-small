1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The author is minimizing an exact finite-support, nonconvex minimax objective over the feasible set

0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1,
sum_i h[i] = n/2,
dx = 2/n,
C5(h) = max_k dx * np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")[k].

The supplied parent, if present, is likely a small-resolution local optimum for the published record near 0.38085. The remaining task is to continue local and semi-global search without letting feasibility, exact finite-support boundary terms, or the two lag orientations be compromised. The proposed implementable algorithm is:

A. Feasibility is enforced by a topology that does not clip and then renormalize independently. Most proposals are generated by exact equal-mass pairwise exchanges. Other arbitrary vectors are repaired by a box-plus-sum projection that changes every coordinate through a single offset followed by clipping, with a final exact-sum correction.

B. Candidates are generated from active-exchange moves derived from exact finite-support correlation gradients. The exact score of every proposed candidate is computed with np.correlate before any acceptance. Nonconvex surrogate models are only proposal generators; no surrogate can define the returned bound.

C. Optimization uses exact local exchange, Metropolis/Simulated-Exchange moves, direct backward/forward correlation transformations, and an optional smooth:SLSQP polish restricted to a top-weight smoothed local model. Results from all lengths and variants accumulate in a validated candidate pool. A fallback feasible vector is always available.

The practical improvement idea is: at a near-optimal plateau, the binding constraints are several nearly tied lags. By repeatedly generating many small exact feasible moves guided by the gradients of these active lags, and by allowing small uphill excursions, a local optimum can be escaped. The returned value is always the lowest validated exact correlation value found.

1.2 What makes this materially different

Compared with generic penalty descent or an abstract untrusted trust-region-model, this plan uses the feasible local topology directly. Since the equality constraint is one summation and the domain is a box, the natural feasible directions are pairwise transfers:

h[i] += δ,  h[j] -= δ.

This keeps the integral exactly fixed and bounds trivially feasible. Activity is still chosen with a smoothed top-max finite-difference gradient of zero plus-minus correlations, so the generated pairwise directions are multiple-constraint-aware. Unlike adaptive state-only filtering, every exact improvement is stored; every local uphill move is compressed to successful by new bounds after exact evaluation.

1.3 Limitations and factual status without overclaims

Nonconvexity is not solved in a global proof. No law.

Justified facts:
- The evaluator objective is exactly the max over the 2n-1 linear-correlation entries multiplied by dx.
- Projecting through an offset-sum monotone one-l and a careful final correction keeps box constraints and sum nearly or exactly within an enforced tolerance. Pairwise moves conserve sum exactly up to floating-point roundoff.
- The finite lag formula under the sign convention from np.correlate is derived explicitly and includes both positive and negative lags.
- The local model minimized by top weights or by SciPy smoothing is nonconvex, smooth only where selected components and weights are frozen. Even a false direction does not allow a wrong returned bound because candidates are scored exactly.
- At a KKT-like plateau, an active weights mean subtraction is compatible with the equality constraint.

Heuristic hypotheses:
- Many small moves plus annealing can produce a lower exact bound, especially due to duplicate decisions around record gap.
- Mirror, complement, symmetrized/approx of exactly feasible variants expose alternate local minima; only exact scores choose them.

No cross-resolution values can be carried over by a simple conversion of scores; after any length change, the exact evaluator must be run before selecting or comparing candidates.
