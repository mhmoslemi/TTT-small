    # C[c0+R] = sum_{i=0}^{n-1-R} h[i+R]*(1-h[i])
    # grad contribution for h[j]:
    #   j in [R, n-1]: p[c0+R]*dx*(1-h[j-R])
    #   j in [0, n-1-R]: -p[c0+R]*dx*h[j+R]
    pos_probs = p[c0+1:]  # R=1 to n-1
    for R in range(1, n):

        if w < 1e-12:
            continue
        m = n - R
        grad[:m] -= w * dx * h[R:R+m]
        grad[R:] += w * dx * (1.0 - h[:m])
    
    # Negative lags: k = c0-R, R=1..n-1
    # C[c0-R] = sum_{i=R}^{n-1} h[i-R]*(1-h[i])
    # grad contribution for h[j]:
    #   j in [0, n-1-R]: p[c0-R]*dx*(1-h[j+R])
    #   j in [R, n-1]: -p[c0-R]*dx*h[j-R]
    neg_probs = p[:c0][::-1]  # R=1 to n-1
    for R in range(1, n):
        w = neg_probs[R-1]
        if w < 1e-12:
            continue
        m = n - R
        grad[:m] += w * dx * (1.0 - h[R:R+m])
        grad[R:] -= w * dx * h[:m]
    
    return grad

- h[j] → h[j] - delta

The function f(delta) = max_k C[k](delta) is the max of one concave quadratic and many linear functions. Its minimum over the feasible interval can be found by:

1. If the concave quadratic is never the maximum (i.e., it's below all linear functions throughout the interval), then f is the max of linear functions = a piecewise linear convex function. Its minimum is at a kink point (intersection of two linears) or at a boundary.

2. If the concave quadratic IS the maximum in some region, then the minimum of f might be at the point where the quadratic transitions from being the max to not being the max, or at the vertex of the quadratic (but the vertex is a maximum of the quadratic, not minimum of f).

- Or at the minimum of the max of the linears (if the quad is never the max)
- Or at a boundary
