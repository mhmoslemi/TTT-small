1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

Use the supplied parent as the exact feasible anchor, then perform a resolution-aware, exact-objective hill/multiscale search over feasible zero-sum directions. For any candidate vector \(h\) and any finite-support direction \(d\) with \(\sum_i d_i=0\), the feasible segment is exactly the interval of \(\alpha\) for which all coordinates stay in \([0,1]\). Along that segment the evaluator is not approximated: the correlation values are polynomial functions of \(\alpha\), and the best feasible point on the line can be found to high precision. Candidates are always repaired by a joint water-filling projection that enforces simultaneously \(0\le h_i\le1\) and \(\sum_i h_i=n/2\), and every one is scored by the authoritative discrete evaluator.

The engine maintains a small pool of feasible candidates. New proposals are taken from one of the following complementary generators:
- random zero-sum directions in the whole feasible manifold;
- active-lag gradient-derived directions targeting currently largest correlations;
- mode-by-mode perturbations inherited from best candidates and added noise;
- crossover directions between two stored feasible candidates;
- active-lag crossing candidate generation obtained from pair intersections of the top quadratic lag polynomials along a chosen line.

This is materially different from parent-anchored fixed latent bases, active-set LP linearizations, and coordinate/pair line search. The line direction is arbitrary full-dimensional; the line optimization uses the true quadratic finite-support correlation along the entire feasible interval, not just adjacent pair swaps or a fixed latent subspace. It is also different from smoothed surrogate optimization because the total objective used for decisions is always the original max of all finite-support lag correlations, not a logistic or log-sum-exp proxy.

1.2 Why this addresses the bottleneck

At a near-optimal point, reduction of \(\max_\ell J_\ell(h)\) often requires a small motion with a detailed pattern across many variables. A small coordinate-only or pair-only move may be too local, while a fixed latent basis may not contain the relevant combination. Direct random and gradient-guided directions let the probe point travel through the full tangent geometry of the feasible top of the support correlation polytope. Since the line correlation objective is a quadratic sequence with exact finite-support boundary behavior, each direction can be optimized very aggressively without leaving feasible bounds or changing the integral. Resolution transfers allow the discrete vector to represent boundary features and boundary-cutoff effects that may give a lower compatible discretization score.

1.3 Justified facts versus hypotheses

Justified:
- For \(h(\alpha)=h+\alpha d\) with \(\sum_i d_i=0\), \(\sum_i h_i(\alpha)\) is constant.
- The box \([0,1]^n\) is automatically enforceable by the feasible interval of \(\alpha\).
- The full correlation sequence along a line is exactly a degree-2 polynomial sequence in \(\alpha\), derived by bilinearity of `np.correlate`.
- A monotone additive-and-clip projection can enforce the two constraints jointly.
- Every accepted candidate is tested by `max(np.correlate(h,1-h,\"full\"))*dx`, not by internal proxies.

Hypotheses without guarantees:
- Random, gradient, and crossover directions can find a descent or crossing improvement below the supplied parent.
- Transfers to higher resolutions can produce a lower discrete vector and provide a better basin.
- Pool mixing and repeated line Kabs can cross local finite-current-nor optimum.
- The resulting final vector may or may not reach the target \(0.3808\). If no improvement is found, it returns best feasible parent or another valid lower-score track.

1.4 Overall nonconvexity and claims

The finite problem is bilinear over a 10-sided box with simplex constraint, and the objective is max of bilinears. No convexity or global optimality is assumed. The method is projected exact vector line-channel (PEVLC) search plus resolution-aware pool mutation. It is local adaptation with multiple acceptation restarts. No statement is made that the line optimizer finds a literal global continuous minimum of the nonunimodal max of quadratics if interval brackets or intersections miss a rare target; all final decisions remain exact, so unadapted candidates may be ignored only because they did not score lower.

2. Mathematical specification

2.1 Discrete variables and constraints

Let \(n=\) `n_points`,

\(\mathrm{dx}=2.0/n,
\phi\text{target}=n/2.
\)

This maximizes a function of entries in equilibrium over all half intervals correct

\(0\le h_i\le1,
\quad
\sum_i h_i=\phi\text{target}.
\)

The mass integral is \(\sum_i h_i dx = (n/2)\cdot 2/n=1\), correctly deriving dx from current \(n\). The target works for any integer \(n\) in floating point, including odd values, because \(\phi\text{target}\) can be fractional and entries are continuous.

2.2 Exact evaluated objective

The authoritative score of a feasible candidate \(h\) of current \(n\) is

\[
C(h)=\max_{q=0}^{2n-2}\Bigl(\Phi_q(h)\Bigr),
\]
\[
\Phi_q(h)=\mathrm{dx}\;\bigl[\operatorname{np.correlate}(h,1-h,\mathrm{full})\bigr]_q.
\]

This is linear finite-support correlation, not circular. There are \(2n-1\) lags including both signs. This is the only objective used for selection and output.

For the custom exact lag mapping that also has the derivative helper below, define for lag integer \(k\in\{-(n-1),...,n-1\}\), with array position \(q=k+n-1\),

\[
s_{\max}=\max(0,k),
\quad
s_{\min}=\min(n,n+k),
\quad
I_q=\{i:s_{\max}\le i<s_{\min}\},
\quad
j_q=i-k.
\]

Then

\[
\Phi_q=\mathrm{dx}\sum_{i\in I_q} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i-k}\bigr).
\]

At program start, the derived segments are matched to the numpy raw result for several random vectors by direct numeric comparison. If this check fails, gradient segment propagation is disabled and only `np.correlate` is used for all scores and polynomial raw correlations.

2.3 Line representation

For any finite direction \(d\) satisfying

\[
\sum_i d_i=0,
\]

the parametrized vector

\[
h(\alpha)=h+\alpha d
\]

preserves the integral exactly in exact arithmetic. The only restriction is the box. For each coordinate, if \(d_i>0\),

\[
\alpha\ge (0-h_i)/d_i,
\quad
\alpha\le (1-h_i)/d_i,
\]

and if \(d_i<0\), the same two algebraic bounds reverse the roles of lower/upper. If \(|d_i|\) is numerically zero, no bound is imposed. The intersection is

\[
\alpha_{\min}=\max_i l_i,
\quad
\alpha_{\max}=\min_i u_i.
\]

The interval must contain \(0\). A missing original vector at \(0\) may result from numerical tolerance: if \(0\) is outside by a very small amount due to drift, use a clipped status and direct projection repair. If the interval is still empty, reject the line.

2.4 Exact polynomial line correlation

Write \(b=1-h\). For \(h(\alpha)=h+\alpha d\), also \(b(\alpha)=b-\alpha d\). Bilinearity of correlation gives exactly the same array order as the evaluator:

\[
\text{raw}(\alpha)
=
\mathrm{corr}(h,b)
+\alpha\left[\mathrm{corr}(d,b)-\mathrm{corr}(h,d)\right]
-\alpha^2\mathrm{corr}(d,d),
\]

where `corr` means NumPy `np.correlate(...,mode='full')` with the correct orientation. Let

\[
A_q=\mathrm{corr}(h,b)_q,
\quad
B_q=\mathrm{corr}(d,b)_q-\mathrm{corr}(h,d)_q,
\quad
D_q=-\mathrm{corr}(d,d)_q.
\]

Then

\[
A(\alpha)=\max_q\bigl(A_q+B_q\alpha+D_q\alpha^2\bigr).
\]

The final report multiplies raw values by \(\mathrm{dx}\). Since in one \(n\) problem \(\mathrm{dx}\) is positive and constant, optimizing the unscaled polynomial sequence max is equivalent to optimizing the final numerical score for that fixed dimension. All transient line decisions therefore may use raw values; all cross-resolution acceptance always multiplies by the current \(\mathrm{dx}\) before tie comparison.

Before using the polynomial speedup inside a run, do a numerical verification: choose a random feasible candidate, a random zero-sum \(d\), and a finite feasible \(\alpha\), then compare the raw difference of the updated actual `np.correlate` against the polynomial evaluated at that \(\alpha\). A tolerance of \(10^{-8}\) raw units is sufficient for the intended double numeric sizes; if it fails, disable the polynomial fast path and opt to propose by evaluating exact candidate versions only.

2.5 Correct gradient formula for active-lag directions

When the lag-segment orientation matched `np.correlate`, the gradient of raw \(\Phi_q\) with respect to \(h\) for lag \(k=q-(n-1)\) is supported only on the two index products that validly appear. For the valid index interval \([s_{\max},s_{\min})\) and \(j_q=i-k\):

- for every valid first endpoint \(i\), the derivative of h_i in the first factor is \(1-h_{j_q}\);
- for every valid second endpoint \(j_q\), the derivative in the second factor is \(-h_i\).

If a weight \(w_q\ge0\) is attached to lag value q000000000000, the weighted gradient is

\[
g_i=\sum_q w_q\frac{\partial\Phi_q}{\partial h_i}.
\]

The search needs a zero-sum direction. A steepest-descent-style direction for smooth top weights

\[
w_q\approx\frac{\exp(\beta(\Phi'_q-\Phi'_{\max}))}{\sum_{q'}\exp(\beta(\Phi'_{q'}-\Phi'_{\max}))}

whose top values are moderate but maximal, is

\[
d=\text{center}(-g)+\eta z,
\quad
\text{center}(x)=x-\bar{x},
\quad
\sum_i d_i=0.
\]

Here the “\(\Phi'\" for weights can use either raw values or scaled values; all lags current same resolution scales positive equal. A small noise \(z\) prevents degeneracy when the smooth gradient nearly estimates a zero-flow. If the gradient check failed, skip active gradient modes.
