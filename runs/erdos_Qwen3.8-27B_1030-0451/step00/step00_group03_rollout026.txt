But then for other shifts, h(x) and h(x+k) might both be 1, making 1-h(x+k) = 0, which is good. Or both 0, making h(x) = 0, also good. The bad case is when h(x)=1 and h(x+k)=0 or vice versa.

So the optimal strategy is to make h as "binary" as possible (close to 0 or 1) while arranging that for each shift k, the set where h=1 and the set where h=0 don't overlap too much after shifting.

This is related to the theory of Delone sets and low-discrepancy sequences! The idea is that if h is the indicator of a set S with measure 1 in [0,2], we want the set S and its translates S+k to have minimal "cross-overlap" (where one is in S and the other is not).

For a binary h (values 0 or 1), h(x)(1-h(x+k)) = 1 exactly when h(x)=1 and h(x+k)=0. So ∫h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx = measure of {x: h(x)=1, h(x+k)=0}.

We want to minimize the maximum over k of this measure.

This is the "minimum overlap" problem that Erdős studied. The constant C₅ is the infimum over all such h of the maximum overlap.

For the binary case with h being indicator of a set S with |S|=1 in [0,2], the measure of {x: x∈S, x+k∉S} is what we're looking at. This is related to the "aperiodic correlation" or "autocorrelation" of the set.

Now, for the 64-point discretization, the parent construction likely has a mix of 0s and 1s (or near-binary values) arranged to minimize the maximum cross-overlap.
