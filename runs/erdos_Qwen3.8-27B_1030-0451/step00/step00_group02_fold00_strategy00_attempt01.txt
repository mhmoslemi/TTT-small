1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea. The discrete objective is a maximum of many bilinear functions of the sample vector h:
    Phi_m(h) = dx * sum valid pairs h_i * (1 - h_j), dx = 2/n.
The product h_i(1-h_j) makes the problem strongly nonconvex, so no direct global convex solver, CVXPY relaxation solve, or simple maximum correlation identity can be treated as globally valid. The proposed solution is therefore a certified local-global hybrid: every accepted update is judged on the original evaluator, never on a surrogate. The search uses:

- the already supplied parent construction when available, as a strict fallback;
- several compactly generated feasible initializations, including thresholded versions of the parent, balanced mechanical/periodic patterns, and random balanced samples;
- exact objective-guided feasible pair moves and Frank-Wolfe style moves that preserve sum(h)=n/2 exactly by construction (or by a joint projection if a candidate is slightly perturbed);
- a sequence of smooth log-sum-exp local optimizations with exact acceptance/channeling back into local moves;
- small multidimensional-resolution probes, because higher resolution may expose a better discrete optimum, but only if exact evaluation is better and the resolution remains small (far below 2500 samples); a validated parent is always retained.

1.2 Why this may improve the supplied parent. The local optimum typically has several lag correlations nearly tied. Simple descent using one correlation's gradient would be unstable, so the update direction is taken from a smoothed weighted sum of large correlations. Exact evaluation is then used to accept only those moves that truly lower the maximum. Pair and Frank-Wolfe moves live inside the feasible polytope and therefore maintain sum exactly without clipping/rescaling hacks. Log-sum-exp SLSQP provides a smoother basin/entry point; if the smooth local solution has a smaller exact score after feasibility projection, it is promoted; if not it is retained as a perturbation generator. Random, mechanical, interval/binary-like and random balanced starts hedge against local plateaus. This does not claim global optimality and is not claimed to always reach the target; the best valid candidate found is returned before the deadline.

1.3 Bottlenecks addressed.
- The hard acceptance problem: the objective is an L-inf maximum over all full-lag correlations. A smooth surrogate alone is a bound, not the true optimizer. Acceptance always recomputes the full np.correlate objective.
- The feasibility problem: random clipping/rescaling generally destroys the area constraint, while scalar projection can destroy the bounding box. A joined projection or exact-pair/FW moves enforce both 0<=h<=1 and sum=n/2 within floating point.
- Boundary terms: the evaluator is linear finite-support correlation with zero outside support. The strategy keeps the evaluator's exact arrays and lags, including both directions, and does not replace the objective with an autocorrelation identity.
- Time: 1100s and one CPU. Most of the budget goes to an inexpensive local search on small dimensions (around 81 if present), while expensive operations are time-gated and conservative. Any new candidate is evaluated with the exact evaluator before being recorded.

1.4 Limits. The search is explicitly heuristic. Nonconvexity means it may not find the exact mathematical optimum; it can get stuck in local minima or side-step to worse regions. No convex solver used gives a true global certificate. The final bound is only an upper bound from a feasible construction. If no lower bound than the parent is found, the parent remains safe. This plan is intended to improve around the current best and try resolutions if needed; no guarantee of monotone parameter success.
