1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

Construct an improved valid discrete vector `h` by treating the problem as a finite, exact boxed-sum constrained max-optimization problem and attacking it with an active-cut sequential linear-programming (SLP) local descent scheme. At each iterate, we compute the exact correlation objective with the authoritative evaluator, choose only the currently large lags, linearize those selected correlation functions around the current vector, and solve a small linear model that says “push the maximum of these selected lag constraints down as much as the linear model permits, without leaving a small trust region.” Every proposal produced by this linear model is then evaluated by the original exact objective; it is accepted only if the exact objective improves. This keeps the nonconvex bilinear structure outside the solver: the LP solves a legitimate convex linear subproblem built from affine first-order models, while exact objective acceptance prevents false acceptance caused by linearization error.

This is materially different from a purely subgradient/smooth-max/proximal-gradient or epigraph nonlinear-programming polish: the local displacement is generated by a global, coordinated linearized-active-constraint LP over the whole hoisted simplex at that step, rather than small incremental gradient endpoints or smoothed scalar penalties. This is especially useful for the present task, where the supplied parent is already extremely close to target; improvement likely comes from a small structurally coordinated cut through the active constraints, not a one-variable stochastic jitter alone.

1.2 Why this should help the parent

The parent already satisfies the exact constraints and is within `5e-5` or less of the target `0.3808`. Maximizing several lags means opportunities are mostly dictated by the exact active set near the maximum. An active-cut LP can produce moves that reduce many of those active constraints simultaneously while respecting the sum and box constraints exactly. A local approximate relaxation is valid because the exact metric remains the gatekeeper for acceptance: a candidate failing the exact evaluator cannot be accepted. We avoid claiming global optimality for any local structural subproblem or overall method.

1.3 Secondary hybrid safeguards
