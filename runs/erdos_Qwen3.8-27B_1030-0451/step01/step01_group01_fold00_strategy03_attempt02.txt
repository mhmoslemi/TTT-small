1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The algorithm treats the sampled Erdős overlap problem as a deterministic exact minimization of a non-smooth maximum plus a stochastic global recounsel phase. For every candidate step vector `h`, it repeatedly chooses line segments in which exactly two entries `h_i` and `h_j` are changed and their sum is held fixed:
`h_i = t`, `h_j = s - t`, where `s = h_i + h_j` before the move. These segments lie entirely in the feasible set whenever `t` is restricted to `[max(0, s - 1), min(1, s)]`, preserve the required total mass exactly by construction, and make the exact correlation tensor along the segment a maximum of convex quadratic-plus-affine univariate functions. For such a segment, the original tuner evaluation `max_k np.correlate(h, 1-h)*dx` as a function of `t` is one-dimensional and easily optimized globally with a bounded line search. This is the primary difference from the parent program: instead of building smoothed log-sum-exp*S.aggregate LP/SLSQP code inside a differentiable or piecewise-linear crisp subspace, the method directly attempts to minimize the exact maximum correlation objective one feasible one-dimensional sum-preserving segment at a time. Local improvements are numerically certified by re-evaluating the whole vector under the immediate metric, and no different objective component is used to dressing up each accepted step.
