1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The supplied construction is already at a very high quality, so the remaining work is not a coarse global search but a precision descent inside a narrow, statistically degenerate min-max region. The previous approach used a single smooth maximum surrogate and a monotonically cooling smoothing temperature. That has two limitations:

1. It keeps attention on all correlations related by an exponential weight, including correlations that are far from the current active level. This can make the solver spend steps preserving nonsensitive constraints or choosing a direction that only finely balances the smoothed maximum.
2. It gives no device for proving that a lower target value is attainable. Convergence under the smooth surrogate can stop at a local plateau whose exact maximum is still above the required target.

The primary method chosen here is a sequence of explicit target threshold problems. For a candidate current maximum `M` and desired bound target `T`, optimize a smooth excess penalty

where the hinge is smooth so that the derivative is stable. This is materially different from entropy smoothing of the maximum because nonactive correlations receive essentially zero derivative, and active violations receive derivative proportional to their violation of the target. The objective directly asks: “Can the whole solution be pushed below T?” Exact evaluation of the original max objective decides acceptance. After each target solve, a second method performs exact active-gradient line search using the true maximum and its exact correlation gradient, with selected active constraints only. This disperses multiple target-optimal walls segmented over only active correlations and can exploit correlations near but below the maximum as hidden constraints.

This is not a convex or global optimizer. It is a strong combined direct programming/minimax descent. It can discover a construction below the current exact response, giving a tighter feasible construction. But it cannot claim global optimality. A candidate is accepted only if it remains feasible and improves the exact objective.
