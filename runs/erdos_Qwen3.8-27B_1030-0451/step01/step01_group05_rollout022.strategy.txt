1. Approach and rationale

The supplied parent is a feasible point near the known numerical optimum, so the main difficulty is not making small feasible perturbations but leaving the parent's local basin without losing exact feasibility. The parent itself is retained as a safe fallback. The proposed method is materially different from a direct smooth-gradient, SLSQP-annealing, or pair-line coordinate descent applied primarily to the full parent vector: it performs a stochastic global search in an adaptive basis/feasible-mapped search space, then uses the discrete evaluator itself to score and retain candidates. This is an “overshoot then polish” strategy: low-frequency modes are explored first, fine spacing comes in adaptive stages, and a terminal exact line-search polishes only solutions that already look competitive.

The central idea is to map unconstrained or box-constrained search variables into the feasible interval-vector set first, then evaluate the exact evaluator. Concretely:

1. Construct a comparable-length vector from a low-dimensional smooth or frequency-enhanced model.
2. Map it through a strict feasible mapping, namely a projection onto the intersection of [0,1]^n and the hyperplane sum(h)=n/2.
3. Score the mapped result by exactly
   np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode='full') * (2/n)
   and maximize over all 2n-1 lags.
4. Use differential-evolution-style stochastic global search over the mapped basis variables. The search goes through adaptive frequencies, starting from modes corresponding to smooth global patterns and progressing toward higher modes. The ultimate retained candidate is always chosen based on the exact discrete score, not on the smooth surrogate.
5. Finish with a precision exact two-point transfer line-search applied to the most competitive solutions. This final phase is not core to the global escape idea, but it provides feasibility-preserving local refinement and is guarded by an exact formula and sanity test. If the exact-formula line-linearity test fails, a correctness-preserving direct-score fallback for the local stage is used.

Why this could help:

- Adaptive frequency/basis modes can express global structural diversity that pure pair-coordinate moves and local smooth optimizer iterations may miss. They provide a restricted-manifold global search instead of trying to optimize all coordinates at once.
- The projected coordinate transformation makes each evaluated point feasible by construction: each proposal z normally results in a valid h satisfying 0<=h<=1 and approximately exact sum, followed by a joint feasibility repair procedure. No unacceptably many evaluations are wasted on infeasible grids, and the evaluator is always the authoritative objective.
- Differential evolution explores a population, avoids committing to one smooth-gradient path, and can handle nonsmooth objective maps because selection uses only returned scalar values.
- Retaining the parent unchanged at the top globally plus the exact line-search after the global stage gives a conservative fallback guarantee, while stochastic multiple-size/multiple-basis branches are essential to discover better basins.
