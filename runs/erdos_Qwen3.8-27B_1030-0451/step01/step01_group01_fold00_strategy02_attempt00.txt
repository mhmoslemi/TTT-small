1. Approach and rationale

1.1. Central idea

Use a stringent, exact one-dimensional line-search minimizer over many feasible, well-chosen directions, together with a diversity-preserving population and multi-resolution restarts. The core local operation is not a continuous `smooth-max` gradient descent, not an active-set LP, and not a fixed-epsilon coordinate move. Instead, for a proposed feasible direction `d`, solve the actual discrete minimization

    minimize over alpha  max_lag c_lag(h + alpha*d)
    subject to 0 <= h_i + alpha*d_i <= 1, sum_j d_j = 0,

using direct evaluations of the original evaluator, dense scalar sampling and local refinement. The objective is always the exact Euclidean/full-support correlation evaluated with `np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")*(2/n_points)`, never a relaxed or smoothed surrogate, and acceptance is only for candidates whose exact re-evaluated objective decreases.

This addresses a structural bottleneck of many minimax reductions: away from a global optimum, the upper envelope of many correlation terms is jagged and locally nonconvex. A smoothed objective hides the envelope, while a single linear trust-region approximation loses the actual bound of the current highest overlaps. Direct 1D line minimization preserves the entire upper envelope along the chosen direction and tests good scalar displacements exactly. Choosing many diverse directions, including FW-style transfers, active-lag gradients, population contrasts, Fourier/window probes, and simulated anneal perturbations, turns one-dimensional exact improvements into reliable movement in the full feasible hypercube.

The approach is heuristic and not guaranteed to be globally optimal. Its plausible advantage over the parent program is that the search directly penetrates the nonsmooth max landscape, can use highly localized feasible moves around a tight parent point, and avoids getting repeatedly trapped by the same smoothing parameter or LP linearization. Population basins and different discretizations provide finite prospects of escaping the current local optimum and reaching a C5 value below 0.38085.

1.2. Why it is substantially different

The parent program primarily alternated a log-sum-exp/SLSQP smooth surrogate, an LP trust-region active constraint model, projected subgradient, and pairwise coordinate moves with fixed epsilons. The proposed program:

- makes exact 1D minimization along a generated direction the fundamental descent move;
- groups feasible directions into adaptive pools from active envelopes, FW score rankings, population digit contrasts, randomized basis probes, and optional binary refinements;
- treats population novelty and multi-different parent resolutions as first-class search dimensions;
- performs anneal-type or abruptly perturbed basin-hopping restarts only as candidate generation, with every successor passed through exact line improvement;
- avoids treating the smooth objective as the merit value for acceptance.

1.3. Boundaries and limitations

- The exact solution of each scalar problem `max_lag quadratics(alpha)` by sampling/refinement is not exhaustively guaranteed if the scalar function has many local minima; the line can therefore be more promising than found. Full acceptance criterion remains exact, so a failed line search only means no recorded improvement.
- Multi-gridded continuous step approximations can exhibit boundary discretization artifacts. Candidates are always compared by the concrete evaluator after feasibility restoration and exact objective re-evaluation.
- High dimensions cannot be exhaustively searched. Objective-deteriorating random proposals are used for exploration but only leads through landmarks and basin structures under prior observation.
