1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The supplied construction is close to the target but not necessarily optimal at the current resolution, and the current continuous problem is nonconvex because objective correlations contain terms of the form `h_i (1 - h_j)`. A purely smooth log-sum-exp descent or single-pair transfer method tends to stagnate near local maxima of the same active correlations and often makes sacrifices not aligned with how all correlations move together.

The proposed algorithm treats the problem as a **linearized minimax optimization on the feasible polytope**. At the current feasible vector `h_k`, we:

1. Select the strongest exact correlations.
2. Linearize those correlations around `h_k`.
3. Solve a small linear program (LP) that minimizes a scalar surrogate for the maximum correlation, subject to:
   - exact mass preservation,
   - box constraints `0 <= h_i <= 1`,
   - an adaptive trust radius around `h_k`.
4. Take an exact line search along the LP direction.
5. Accept only by the actual task evaluator `C5(h) = max(np.correlate(h, 1-h, "full")) * dx`.
6. If the LP direction does not lower the true score, shrink the trust radius, perturb, or restart from another resolution/structural starting point.

This is materially different from earlier log-sum-exp polish because the update direction comes from **joint partial linearization of the actual active objective constraints**, not from a smooth aggregation of all lags. The method is explicitly heuristic: the LP is only linearizing a nonconvex quadratic-like objective, and no optimality claim is made.

1.2 Why it can improve the parent

- The LP considers gradients of all currently dangerous lags simultaneously. Activation often moves as a group; pairwise moves usually cannot.
- It naturally moves variables toward their bounds, because the LP side constraints encourage critical donor positions to decrease and overloaded receiver/compensating positions to adjust.
- The trust radius and exact line search stop harmful extrapolation. Every accepted candidate is validated by the exact evaluator.
- Multi-resolution rounding/resampling can reduce boundary discretization in the discrete objective, while the optimizer keeps the supplied parent as a fallback.
- Structured starts and transform variants allow the LP to find other basins rather than polishing only the supplied local basin.
- It is small-n compatible while also scalable to use larger resolutions, because the LP uses a capped active-constraint size.

1.3 Important limitations and assumptions

- The LP direction can increase the exact score because the real objective terms are quadratic and may have maxima that lose the linear sided direction. Exact rescore acceptance is mandatory.
- Only a truncated set of constraints can be used as length grows, so an inactive truncation with one small excursions can overseward the exact. Acceptance of truncations should be regressed during acceptance verification and only candidate-history new judges. original full-exact `corr` explained.
- No global optimum is claimed. The problem remains nonconvex.
- Resolution changes can make a candidate better or worse for boundary reasons; every candidate is judged with its own `dx = 2/n`.
