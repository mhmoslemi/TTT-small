Actually, let me think about this differently. We want h such that the autocorrelation R(d) = ∫ h(x)h(x+d) dx is as flat as possible (close to its minimum possible value at each d).

The minimum possible value of R(d) is 0 (if h and its shift are disjoint), but we can't achieve this for all d simultaneously.

The average of R(d) over all d is related to the L² norm: ∫ R(d) dd = ∫ h(x)² dx.

So if we minimize ∫ h(x)² dx (i.e., make h as close to 1/2 as possible), we minimize the average autocorrelation. But we care about the maximum, not the average.

This suggests that a good strategy is:
1. Start with h close to 1/2 (minimizes L² norm and average autocorrelation)
2. Add low-amplitude oscillations to "flatten" the autocorrelation
