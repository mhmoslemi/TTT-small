1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Primary idea

The central idea is to perform exact, one-dimensional local minimization along many carefully selected “mass transfer” directions in the simplex-constrained box. A mass transfer direction changes two coordinates in opposite directions, for example raising coordinate j while lowering coordinate i, or it changes several coordinates within a whole feasible line. Every proposed transfer is evaluated by the original discrete objective, `max(np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode='full')) * dx`, after forcing feasibility. The returned solution is therefore a certificate for the exact task objective, not for a surrogate.

This is substantively different from repeatedly smoothing the maximum of the correlations, fitting a TEXtP-norm penalty, projecting a single global subgradient, using logistic reparametrizations, or solving local linearizations of the active correlation constraints. Pair and low-dimensional transfer movements can be much more effective in a bilinear minimization over a constrained simplex, because the objective cares strongly about whether mass is moved from coordinates that appear as positive first factors to coordinates that appear as complement factors.
