2. Mathematical specification

2.1 Variables and exact metric

For vector `h` length \(n\), define

\[
dx = 2.0/n.
\]

Let

\[
\texttt{corr_raw}(h)=np.correlate(h,\,1-h,\,\text{mode="full"})
\]

and the unscaled correlation array be denoted \(r_t\) for \(t=0,1,\ldots,2n-2\). The exact task evaluates

\[
C(h) = dx\,\max_t r_t(h).
\]

If lag \(L=t-(n-1)\), the exact discrete relation is

\[
r_t(h)=\sum_{i:\,0\le i<n,\;0\le i-L<n} h_i(1-h_{i-L}).
\]

The normalized returned bound is exactly \(C(h)\). Enforced feasible constraints are

\[
0\le h_i\le1,\qquad \sum_i h_i=n/2.
\]

If \(n\) is odd, \(n/2\) is a half-integer. Use exact floating-comparable tolerances at output; don't separately rescale after clipping during projection.

2.2 Exact gradient for direction scoring only

Use the following row formula for gradient of raw value \(r_t\). For each output index \(t\), lag \(L=t-(n-1)\). For each valid pair \(i,j=i-L\), the raw term is \(h_i(1-h_j)\). Its partial derivatives:

- derivative with respect to \(h_i\): \(+1-h_j\)
- derivative with respect to \(h_j\): \(-h_i\)

When a term has \(i=j\), both relations apply once, giving derivative \(1-2h_i\), matching the derivative of \(h_i-h_i^2\). For the normalized objective \(c_t=dx\,r_t\), multiply all row derivatives by \(dx\). Direction likelihood via weighted maximum smooth uses raw derivative signs but physical step evaluations use exact `np.correlate`; the weights merely order or bias directions, they do not determine the final accepted decision via an approximate model.

Use softmax weighting over the usually most-correlated rows to produce weighted raw gradient \(\tilde g\):

\[
w_t = \frac{\exp(\beta(r_t-r_{\max}))}{\sum_s \exp(\beta(r_s-r_{\max}))},
\qquad
\tilde g_i=\sum_t w_t\frac{\partial r_t}{\partial h_i}.
\]

Typical \(\beta\) near \(30\) to \(300\). A smaller beta avoids unnecessary numerical overflow; bigger beta approximates derivative at the maximum. Any numerical safety concerns caused by zero weights or infinities produce an all-zero gradient and trigger random directions; no accepted move depends on a flawed gradient.

\sum_i u_i=0.
