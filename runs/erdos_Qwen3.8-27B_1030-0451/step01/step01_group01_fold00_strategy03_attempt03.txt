1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea: exact-evaluator portfolio search with active-target hinge solves

The supplied or generated vector is treated as a nonconvex feasible point. The proposed method does not optimize a log-sum-exp surrogate or rely only on linearized trust-region moves. Its core operation is a tight target-violation hinge minormization: at each iteration a target value τ that is slightly below the current best exact score is imposed, and a smooth penalization is minimized over all correlations that exceed τ. The exact evaluator is used for every acceptance test. In addition, candidates obtained by fast local moves are improved by exact one-dimensional minimization on quality, and several diverse candidate centers are kept in a pool. This makes the search more capable of removing multiple ugly correlations at once, rather than smoothing their maximum.

The ideas most likely to improve the parent are:

- Exact active-target control. Instead of moving because a soft maximum is high, we make the explicit requirement that all correlations be ≤ τ where τ = best_C5 − δ, with δ on the order of 1e−6 to 1e−3. A square-hinge loss includes constraints that are active and also hidden constraints that were just below the parent maximum. This can avoid local rigidity created by a log-smoothed objective that underweights near-equal lags.
- Exact best convex-q on candidates. A candidate direction may contain useful information even if its endpoint has a worse exact objective. Replacing heuristic decreasing step sizes by exact best points on interpolated lines makes the acceptance of curvature information exact. Since all trial points remain convex mixtures of two feasible step generators, the sum constraint remains exactly preserved except for final finite rounding, which is repaired.
- Multi-center and multiresolution portfolio. The work streams keep approximately no more than seven distinct candidate centers and periodically mix vectors discovered at different grid sizes. This attacks local optima by transfer/reoptimization rather than reusing the exact same parents.
- Resolution as search dimension. A low-dimensional discrete optimum may be improved by resampling its parent to a finer grid and then solving a local problem exactly in that larger vector space. Candidate acceptance always uses the exact correlation metric, never a transfer error proxy.

This is a heuristic search. The problem is explicitly nonconvex because h_i and h_j products appear in every correlation term. Only local improvements and exact upper bounds generated by safely feasible candidates are claimed. No one iteration proves global optimality.

1.2 Main loop in plain terms

At any round, obtain one or more valid starting points from the parent and from the candidate pool. Perform the following operations in some order:

1. Candidate synthesis from active correlations (optimized functions or direct descent).
2. Candidate refinement by minimizing a target-violation square-hinge loss with SLSQP, bounds, mass constraint, and trust box.
3. Chord minimization between the center and the candidate, and between useful pairs from the candidate pool.
4. Valid repair of the candidate by feasible projection, then evaluation only after projection.
5. Exact acceptance. The best part of any finite value sequence from this filtration becomes the new best if its actual threshold is smaller.
6. Formation of candidates.
7. Intermittent resolution transfers and random feasible starting points when the present center stagnates.
8. Return best before deadline.

The central implementation object is always exact feasible descriptors; all surrogate scoring is only proposal generation, and actual bounds are re-evaluated.
