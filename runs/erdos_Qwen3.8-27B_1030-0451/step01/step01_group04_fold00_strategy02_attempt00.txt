1. Approach and rationale

The current best construction can be improved by replacing the previous smoothed-envelope SLSQP trajectory with an active-set, exact-objective search. The bottleneck is not merely the optimizer method but the loss of active information: SLSQP on a log-sum-exp surrogate optimizes a smooth maximum and then projects/significantly bounds-violating candidates are accepted only if the exact objective drops. In practice this makes even tiny needed improvements fragile and can get stuck around one local convexified objective.

The proposed algorithm is new in the important parts:

- It maintains and minimizes the exact discrete objective
  \[
  F(h)=dx\max_r \operatorname{corr}_r(h,1-h),\qquad dx=2/N,
  \]
  over all full-output lags, not a smoothed objective.
- It chooses directions from the most active constraints using small linear programs in the primitive variables \(s\in[0,1]^N, \sum_i s_i=N/2\), then uses exact one-dimensional objective minimizations along the generated feasible directions. The LP directions are Frank-Wolfe-like linearizations of several active exact constraints. This is materially different from optimizing the full 350-dimensional SLSQP cost.
- It supplements LP directions with pairwise mass-transfer directions selected from an active-approximate gradient. These pair moves are optimal over exact long portions of the original objective and preserve the global mass constraint directly.
- It uses a correction to the correlation notation and line identity so that any full-direction candidate's objective curve can be evaluated cheaply from precomputed bilinear arrays, not by repeated full objective evaluations.

This approach is still nonconvex and local. It cannot be claimed to find the global optimum. However, every returned vector is exactly valid, and the accepted candidate always represents a genuine value of the authoritative evaluator. To get close to the target lower on C5, a budget is used with multiple resolution passes and random directional perturbations in the best basin. The parent solution is retained as a fallback; if no valid candidate has less than `parent_best`, it is returned.

Representation and exact objective

- dx = 2/N.
- The full correlation vector has length M = 2*N - 1, indexed by `full_idx = 0, ..., 2*N-2`.

  \[
  \mathrm{C5}(h) = dx * \max(\operatorname{np.correlate}(h, 1-h, mode=\text{"full"})).

- Feasibility requires:
  \[
