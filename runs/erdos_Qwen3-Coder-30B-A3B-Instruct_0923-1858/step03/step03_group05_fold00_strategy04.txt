# 1. Approach and rationale
The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks a step function h ∈ [0,1]^n that minimizes the maximum overlap integral over all shifts, while maintaining the integral of h equal to 1. This problem is nonconvex and challenging due to the discrete nature of the overlap calculations and the global maximum in the objective.

The proposed approach transforms the problem into a mixed-integer linear program (MILP) by:
1. Binarizing h into activation variables z[i] ∈ {0,1} indicating if a step is active.
2. Introducing a continuous variable β representing the height of active steps.
3. Linearizing the nonconvex overlap expressions using combinatorial identities.

This approach leverages the combinatorial structure of the problem, allowing us to handle the nonconvex overlap calculations through linear constraints. The binary representation enables systematic exploration of potential configurations while maintaining feasibility through precise constraints.

The key insight is that the overlap integral can be expressed combinatorially in terms of active steps, enabling us to avoid the nonconvex product terms in the original formulation. This transformation makes the problem amenable to efficient integer programming techniques while preserving the problem's essential structure.
