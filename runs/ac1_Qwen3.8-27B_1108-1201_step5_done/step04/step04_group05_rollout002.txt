Let me also think about whether there's a closed-form optimum.

The problem is: minimize max_k c[k] subject to a[i] >= 0, sum(a[i]) = 1.

This is a convex optimization problem (minimize a convex function over a convex set). The optimum is characterized by KKT conditions.

At the optimum, the "active" constraints are the k values where c[k] = max. The KKT conditions say that at the optimum, the subgradient of the objective (which is a convex combination of the gradients at the active k values) must be orthogonal to the feasible set (the simplex).

The gradient of c[k] w.r.t. a[i] is a[k-i] (for valid indices). So the subgradient of max_k c[k] at point a is a convex combination of the vectors (a[k*-i])_{i} for the active k* values.

At the optimum, this subgradient must be proportional to a constant vector (since the constraint is sum(a) = 1 and a >= 0, the gradient of the Lagrangian must be constant on the support of a).

So at the optimum: sum_{k* in active} w_{k*} * a[k*-i] = lambda (constant) for all i in the support.

This is a system of equations that determines the optimal a.

For the ramp, the active set is probably {n} (or {n, n+1} or similar), and the condition would be:
a[n-i] = lambda for all i in the support, i.e., a is constant. But the ramp is not constant, so the ramp is not optimal (unless only one k is active and the condition is trivially satisfied).
