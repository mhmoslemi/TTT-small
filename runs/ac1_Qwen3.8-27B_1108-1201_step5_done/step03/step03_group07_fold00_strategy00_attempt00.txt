# 2. Mathematical specification
**Problem structure:**  
We minimize \( \mathcal{L} = \frac{2n \max(\text{conv}(a,a))}{(\sum a)^2} \) for \( a \geq 0 \) and \( \sum a \geq 0.01 \). We use \(a\) normalized to sum \(1\) (via simplex projection) to remove scaling ambiguities, then evaluate the original sequence against evaluator's defenses.  

**Optimizer setup:**  
Define a lower-parametrization vector \( x = \log(a) \) for L-BFGS-B steps, where \(a\) is simplex-projected and clamped under `[0, 1000]`. Use the smooth_max function to approximate the max constraint:  
\[
\text{smooth\_max}(c, \beta) = \frac{M}{\beta} + \log(1 + e^{-\beta} \sum_{k} e^{\beta (c_k - M)}), \quad M = \max(c)
\]
This allows us to avoid direct max nonlinearity in optimization.  

**Adaptive β selection:**  
β governs the softness of the max penalty. We dynamically adjust β with \( \beta = \beta_{\text{start}} \times \beta_{\text{ratio}}^{-\text{iterations}} \) where \( \beta_{\text{ratio}} = 0.95 \), \( \beta_{\text{start}} = 80 \) (concentrating to small peaks initially). The softmax objective is parameterized by β:  
\[
\mathcal{L}(\beta) = 2n \cdot \text{smooth\_max}(\text{conv}(a,a), \beta)
\]

**Constraint handling:**  
Each optimization uses \( \sum_{i} a_i = 1 \) as simplex projection automatically ensures non-negativity (via analytic projection) and generates bounds-compliant candidates for evaluation. All sequences must be nonnegative and finite (\(\leq\)\`np.inf).

# 5. Core Analysis and Design Decisions

## A. β Selection Strategy
We introduce adaptive β adjustment using:  
\[
\beta_{\text{adjusted}} = \beta_{\text{start}} \times \beta_{\text{ratio}}^{\tau}
\]
allowing the optimization to move between aggressive (high β) and principled (low β) modes. This is implemented via the meet termination clause that terminates and returns the best candidate if β adjustment fails to give a solution under budget.

## B. Energy Budget Monitoring
The code uses `time.monotonic()` for strict budget adherence, recalculating the optimized TFR rate at each simulation phase to avoid time-outs.

## C. Dimension Management
Length decisions are made by a combination of exponential smoothing (\(\alpha\)) of recent solution evaluations and threshold-based decisions (favoring long lengths if tiny opportunities remain).

## D. Multi-Stage Optimization:
The algorithm uses a hybrid approach:  
1. **Frequency-domain init:** Key for good convolution properties without dense trials  
2. **L-BFGS-B optimization:** With adaptive β scales  
3. **PGD for faster early convergence:** When high β leads to issues  
4. **Coordinate descent refinement:** For full reconstruction of the sequence after successful optimization
