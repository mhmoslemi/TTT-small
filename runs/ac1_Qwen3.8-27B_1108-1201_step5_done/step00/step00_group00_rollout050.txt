Actually, I recall that for the problem of minimizing the peak of the autocorrelation of a non-negative sequence, the optimal solution for large n is related to "flat" spectra, i.e., sequences whose DFT has constant magnitude. This is related to "Zadoff-Chu" sequences or "CAZAC" (Constant Amplitude Zero Auto-Correlation) sequences.

But CAZAC sequences are complex and have constant amplitude. For non-negative real sequences, we can't use them directly.

However, there's a connection: if we can make the DFT of our sequence have constant magnitude, then the autocorrelation is a delta function (zero off the peak). But for non-negative sequences, the DFT at frequency 0 is S (the sum), and we can't make all other frequencies have the same magnitude as S without making the sequence have negative values (by the same argument that a non-negative sequence with constant-magnitude DFT must be an impulse).

So we need a different approach.
