The Erdős minimum–overlap constant \(C_{5}\) is a *max‑over‑shifts* functional of a step‑function \(h\) on \([0,2]\) with values in \([0,1]\) and average \(1/2\).  
Instead of heuristics that manipulate the full \(n\)‑dimensional vector of samples (the previous DE/SA pipeline), the new plan works in a **low‑dimensional frequency domain**, exploits the **exact Fourier expression of the autocorrelation**, and solves a **convex/low‑dim non‑convex optimisation** for the Fourier coefficients.  The obtained continuous function is finally discretised, projected onto the capped‑simplex, and polished with a cheap projected‑subgradient step.  The whole pipeline is deterministic apart from a few random seeds, runs well inside the 600 s budget, and is mathematically distinct from the earlier approach.

### 1. Mathematical Re‑formulation

1. **Fourier basis**  
   Let the fundamental angular frequency be \(\omega_{0}= \pi\) (because the interval length is 2).  
   Represent \(h\) as a *real* trigonometric polynomial of order \(M\):

   \[
   h(x)=\frac12+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Bigl[a_{m}\cos (m\omega_{0}x)+b_{m}\sin (m\omega_{0}x)\Bigr].
   \]

   The constant term \(\tfrac12\) guarantees the integral constraint automatically,
   since \(\int_{0}^{2}\cos(m\omega_{0}x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{2}\sin(m\omega_{0}x)\,dx=0\) for every integer \(m\).

2. **Box constraints as linear inequalities**  
   Sample the interval densely (e.g. \(N_{\text{sample}}=2000\) points \(\xi_{j}=j\Delta\), \(\Delta=2/N_{\text{sample}}\)).  
   For each sample enforce  

   \[
   0\le h(\xi_{j})\le1.
   \]

   Because \(h(\xi_{j})\) is affine in the coefficient vector
   \(\mathbf{c}=[a_{1},\dots,a_{M},b_{1},\dots,b_{M}]^{\!\top}\), each sample yields two linear inequalities
   of the form \(\mathbf{p}_{j}^{\!\top}\mathbf{c}\le 1/2\) and \(\mathbf{p}_{j}^{\!\top}\mathbf{c}\ge -1/2\) where
   \(\mathbf{p}_{j}\) contains the sampled sines and cosines.

3. **Exact autocorrelation via Parseval**  
   Define complex Fourier coefficients  

   \[
   \alpha_{m}= \tfrac12\bigl(a_{m}-\mathrm{i}\,b_{m}\bigr), \qquad m\ge1,
   \qquad \alpha_{0}= \tfrac12 .
   \]

   The (continuous) autocorrelation of \(h\) at shift \(\tau\) is  

   \[
   R(\tau)=\int_{0}^{2}h(x)h(x+\tau)\,dx
          =2\sum_{m=0}^{M}\!|\alpha_{m}|^{2}\cos(m\omega_{0}\tau).
   \]

   Since \(|\alpha_{0}|^{2}=1/4\) and \(|\alpha_{m}|^{2}= \tfrac14\bigl(a_{m}^{2}+b_{m}^{2}\bigr)\) for \(m\ge1\),

   \[
   R(\tau)=\frac12+\frac14\sum_{m=1}^{M}\!\bigl(a_{m}^{2}+b_{m}^{2}\bigr)\cos(m\omega_{0}\tau).
   \]

   The overlap functional becomes

   \[
   C_{5}(h)=\max_{\tau\in[0,2]}\;dx\;\Bigl[\underbrace{\int_{0}^{2}h(x)\,dx}_{=1}
                     -R(\tau)\Bigr]
          =dx\;\bigl[1-\min_{\tau}R(\tau)\bigr].
   \]

   Hence **minimising \(C_{5}\) is equivalent to maximising the minimum of a cosine series** whose coefficients are the non‑negative numbers  

   \[
   u_{m}=a_{m}^{2}+b_{m}^{2}\ge0,\qquad m=1,\dots,M .
   \]

   In terms of \(\mathbf{u}=[u_{1},\dots,u_{M}]^{\!\top}\),

   \[
   R(\tau)=\frac12+\frac14\sum_{m=1}^{M}u_{m}\cos(m\omega_{0}\tau).
   \]

4. **Linear‑in‑\(u\) formulation of the objective**  
   Discretise the shift set by the same grid used for the box constraints:
   \(\tau_{k}=k\,dx,\;k=0,\dots,n-1\) where we finally intend to output a vector of length \(n\)  
   (e.g. \(n=240\), \(dx=2/n\)).  
   Introduce an auxiliary scalar \(t\) that bounds the worst overlap:

   \[
   t\;\ge\;dx\Bigl[1-R(\tau_{k})\Bigr],\qquad k=0,\dots,n-1.
   \]

   Substituting the expression of \(R\),

   \[
   t\;\ge\;dx\Bigl[1-\tfrac12-\tfrac14\sum_{m=1}^{M}u_{m}\cos(m\omega_{0}\tau_{k})\Bigr]
     \;=\;dx\Bigl[\tfrac12-\tfrac14\sum_{m=1}^{M}u_{m}\cos(m\omega_{0}\tau_{k})\Bigr].
   \]

   All constraints are **linear in \((t,\mathbf{u})\)** because the cosines are known numbers.

5. **Coupling \(\mathbf{u}\) and the original coefficients**  
   The variables \(\mathbf{c}\) (the signed coefficients \(a_{m},b_{m}\)) must satisfy  

   \[
   u_{m}=a_{m}^{2}+b_{m}^{2}\;\;\;(m=1,\dots,M).
   \]

   This is a set of *quadratic* equalities, which can be relaxed using a **second‑order cone (SOC) representation**:

   \[
   \|(a_{m},b_{m})\|_{2}\le \sqrt{u_{m}}.
   \]

   Introducing auxiliary variables \(s_{m}\ge0\) with \(s_{m}^{2}=u_{m}\) leads to the SOC constraints  

   \[
   \|(a_{m},b_{m})\|_{2}\le s_{m},\qquad u_{m}=s_{m}^{2}.
   \]

   The product \(s_{m}^{2}\) can be modelled in a convex programme via the **rotated‑quadratic cone**  

   \[
   (2,s_{m}^{2},u_{m})\in\mathcal{Q}^{r}.
   \]

   In practice CVXPY supports the rotated‑quadratic cone directly.

6. **Final convex programme**  
   Decision variables:  

   * \(\mathbf{c}=[a_{1},\dots,a_{M},b_{1},\dots,b_{M}]\) (real, unrestricted),  
   * \(\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M}\) (the square‑roots of \(u\)),  
   * \(\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M}\),  
   * scalar \(t\).

   Constraints:  

   * **Box constraints** – the \(2N_{\text{sample}}\) linear inequalities from step 2.  
   * **SOC constraints** – \(\|(a_{m},b_{m})\|_{2}\le s_{m}\) for each \(m\).  
   * **Rotated‑cone constraints** – \(2\,s_{m}^{2}\ge u_{m}\) (equivalently \(u_{m}=s_{m}^{2}\)).  
   * **Shift constraints** – for each discrete lag \(k\),

     \[
     t \;\ge\;dx\Bigl[\tfrac12-\tfrac14\sum_{m=1}^{M}u_{m}\cos(m\omega_{0}\tau_{k})\Bigr].
     \]

   * **Objective** – minimise \(t\).

   This is a **convex conic programme** (linear objective, linear + SOC + rotated‑quadratic cone constraints).  It can be handed to any of CVXPY’s open‑source solvers (ECOS, SCS, or the CBC‑based QP solver).  The problem size is tiny: with \(M=8\) we have roughly \(2M\approx16\) variables for \(\mathbf{c}\), \(M\) variables for \(\mathbf{s}\), \(M\) for \(\mathbf{u}\), plus one for \(t\); the dominant number of constraints stems from the dense box sampling (≈ 2000 × 2 linear rows) and the \(n\) shift rows (≈ 240).  Solving such a model takes a few seconds.
