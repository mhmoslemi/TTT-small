**1. Reformulate the objective**

For a step function \(h\) on a uniform grid with spacing \(\Delta=2/n\) the overlap for an integer shift \(s\) is  

\[
O_s(h)=\int_0^2 h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+s\Delta)\bigr)\,dx
        =\Delta\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i\,h_{i+s}\Bigr)
        =\Delta\Bigl(t-\underbrace{\sum_{i}h_i h_{i+s}}_{\displaystyle\text{auto‑correlation }C_s}\Big),
\]

where \(t=\sum_i h_i = 1/\Delta=n/2\) (the integral constraint).  
Thus  

\[
C_5=\max_s O_s = \Delta\bigl(t-\min_s C_s\bigr).
\]

Minimising \(C_5\) is equivalent to **maximising the minimum auto‑correlation** \(\min_s C_s\).

**2. Exact gradient of a smooth approximation to the max**

The max is non‑differentiable. Replace it by a smooth log‑sum‑exp (soft‑max) with a temperature \(\alpha>0\):

\[
F_\alpha(h)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\!\Bigl(\sum_{s} \exp\!\bigl(\alpha\,O_s(h)\bigr)\Bigr).
\]

For any \(\alpha\), \(\max_s O_s \le F_\alpha \le \max_s O_s + \frac{\log L}{\alpha}\) (where \(L=2n-1\) is the number of shifts).  
When \(\alpha\) is large, \(F_\alpha\) is an accurate proxy for the max, while for moderate \(\alpha\) the gradient is well‑behaved.

The derivative of a single term \(O_s\) is elementary because the auto‑correlation is bilinear:

\[
\frac{\partial O_s}{\partial h_i}
   = -\Delta\bigl(h_{i+s}+h_{i-s}\bigr) \quad
   (\text{indices outside }[0,n-1]\text{ are ignored}).
\]

Hence the gradient of the soft‑max objective is a **weighted sum** of these simple patterns:

\[
\nabla F_\alpha(h) = \sum_{s} p_s \;\nabla O_s(h), \qquad
p_s = \frac{e^{\alpha O_s}}{\sum_{r} e^{\alpha O_r}}.
\]

All the weights \(p_s\) are non‑negative and sum to one.

**3. Efficient evaluation of \(O_s\) and of the gradient**

*Correlation*

1. Form the reversed vector \(h^{\text{rev}} = h[::-1]\).
2. Zero‑pad both vectors to length \(L = 2n-1\).
3. Compute \(C = \text{ifft}(\text{fft}(h^{\text{rev}},L)\cdot \text{fft}(h,L))\).  
   \(C\) is real‑valued; entry \(C_{s+n-1}\) equals \(\sum_i h_i h_{i+s}\).

Then \(O_s = \Delta\,(t - C_{s+n-1})\).
