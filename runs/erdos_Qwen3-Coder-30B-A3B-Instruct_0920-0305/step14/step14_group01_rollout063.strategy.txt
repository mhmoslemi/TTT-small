**1. Overall plan**  
We will search for a discrete step‑function `h` (length `N`, `0≤h[i]≤1`, sum `h·dx = 1`) that minimises  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{k}\int_0^2 h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx .
\]

The search will be performed on a moderately fine grid (e.g. `N≈200‑240`) using a **continuation of a smooth surrogate** for the max‑operator, combined with **projected gradient optimisation** and **multi‑restart exploration**.  The algorithm is deliberately different from the previous DE‑SA‑gradient hybrid: it works directly on the analytical gradient of a smooth approximation, uses a water‑filling projection, and leverages a deterministic continuation schedule.

---

**2. Discretisation and basic primitives**

* Choose an even `N` (default 200).  
* `dx = 2.0 / N`.  
* Target sum `S = N/2` because `dx·sum(h)=1`.  

**Correlation (`I`)**  
For a vector `h` define `b = 1‑h`.  
The full linear correlation `I = corr(h,b)` of length `L = 2·N‑1` is computed **via FFT** for speed:

```
h_rev = h[::-1]                 # reversed h
L     = 2*N - 1
I = real( ifft( fft(h_rev, L) * fft(b, L) ) )
```

The original bound is `C5 = dx * max(I)`.  

**Projection onto the feasible set**  
We need `h∈[0,1]^N` and `sum(h)=S`.  
The projection is the classic “water‑filling” problem:

* Find λ such that `∑ clip(x_i + λ, 0, 1) = S`.  
* Use a binary search on λ (bounds `[-1,1]`) with tolerance `1e‑12`.  
* Return `clip(x+λ,0,1)`.

This procedure is `O(N log N)` (binary search × `O(N)` clip) and is numerically robust.

**3. Smooth surrogate for the max‑operator**

The max over shifts is non‑smooth.  Introduce a **log‑sum‑exp (soft‑max)** with temperature ε:

\[
F_{\varepsilon}(h)=\varepsilon\;\log\!\Bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}
\exp\!\bigl(I_j / \varepsilon \bigr)\Bigr).
\]

*`F_ε`* is a smooth, convex‑in‑the‑exponential sense approximation of `max(I)`.  
The true objective is  

\[
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(h)=dx\;F_{\varepsilon}(h)
+ \lambda_{\text{sum}}\bigl(\sum h - S\bigr)^2,
\]

where the quadratic penalty enforces the sum constraint (λ\_sum≈10⁴).  Because the penalty is differentiable it can be ignored by the optimizer if we later **project** after each gradient step.

---

**4. Analytic gradient of the surrogate**

Define the soft‑max weights  

\[
w_j = \frac{\exp(I_j/\varepsilon)}{\sum_{k}\exp(I_k/\varepsilon)} .
\]

For a single shift `j` the correlation term is  

\[
I_j = \sum_{i} h_i\,b_{i+j},
\qquad b=1-h .
\]

The derivative w.r.t. a component `h_i` is  

\[
\frac{\partial I_j}{\partial h_i}=b_{i+j} - h_{i-j},
\]

with the convention that out‑of‑range indices give 0.  

Hence the gradient of the smooth max is the **weighted sum** of these per‑shift gradients:

\[
\nabla F_{\varepsilon}(h)_i = 
\sum_{j} w_j\bigl( b_{i+j} - h_{i-j}\bigr).
\]

All three vectors (`w`, `h`, `b`) are of length `L` or `N`.  
The sum can be performed **via two correlations**:

* `g1 = corr(w, b)` – gives the term `∑ w_j b_{i+j}`.  
* `g2 = corr(w_rev, h)` where `w_rev = w[::-1]` – gives `∑ w_j h_{i-j}`.  

Both correlations are again evaluated with FFT (same `L`).  The final gradient is  

\[
g = g1 - g2 .
\]

The penalty `λ_sum(∑h‑S)^2` contributes `2λ_sum(∑h‑S)·1_N` to the gradient (a constant vector), which is trivial to add.

All operations are `O(N log N)`.
