### 1.  High‑level idea  
Cast the minimisation of  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{k}\int_0^{2}h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx
\]

as a **convex SDP (semidefinite‑programming) relaxation**.  
Solve the SDP to obtain a fractional “certificate’’ \(h^{\star}\) and a covariance matrix that approximates the product terms \(h_i h_j\).  
From this SDP solution generate many **randomised roundings** that respect the box‑constraints and the total‑mass constraint, keep the best rounded vector, and polish it with a tiny deterministic local optimiser.  
The whole pipeline – SDP → rounding → local hill‑climb → optional gradient fine‑tuning – is mathematically distinct from pure gradient descent on the smoothed max and exploits a global convex bound on the non‑convex quadratic constraints.

### 2.  Discrete formulation

* Choose an **even** grid size \(n\) (e.g. 80 – 120).  
* Let \(dx = 2/n\) and denote the vector of samples by \(h\in\mathbb{R}^n\).  
* The exact discretised overlap for shift \(k\in\{-n+1,\dots ,n-1\}\) is  

\[
c_k(h)=dx\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+k}\bigr),
\qquad h_{j}=0\text{ if }j\notin[0,n-1].
\]

* Introduce the **product matrix** \(X = hh^{\!\top}\) (so \(X_{ij}=h_i h_j\)).  
  The term \(\sum_i h_i h_{i+k}\) that appears in \(c_k\) is simply the
  trace of \(X\) against the shift mask \(R_k\) where \((R_k)_{i,i+k}=1\) (zero elsewhere).

### 3.  Convex SDP model

Introduce variables

* \(h\in\mathbb{R}^n\) (continuous),  
* \(X\in\mathbb{S}^n\) (symmetric),  
* a scalar \(t\ge 0\) that will be an upper bound on the maximal overlap.

Constraints  

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| \(0\le h_i\le 1\) | box constraints |
| \(\sum_i h_i = n/2\) | normalisation (∫h = 1) |
| \(X \succeq hh^{\!\top}\) | lifted PSD condition (equivalently \(\begin{pmatrix}X & h\\ h^{\!\top}&1\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0\)) |
| For each shift \(k\):  \(\displaystyle dx\Bigl(\frac{n}{2}-\operatorname{Tr}(R_k X)\Bigr) \le t\) | linearisation of \(c_k\) using \(X\) |

Objective  

\[
\min\; t .
\]

All constraints are **linear** except the single PSD constraint, therefore the problem is a pure SDP.

*Why this works*:  
For any feasible \((h,X)\) we have \(X_{ij}=h_i h_j\) by the PSD condition, thus \(\operatorname{Tr}(R_k X)=\sum_i h_i h_{i+k}\) and the inequality is exactly the definition of \(c_k\le t\). Dropping the rank‑1 requirement \(X=hh^{\!\top}\) yields a convex relaxation; its optimum \(t^{\rm SDP}\) is a lower bound on the true minimal \(C_5\).
