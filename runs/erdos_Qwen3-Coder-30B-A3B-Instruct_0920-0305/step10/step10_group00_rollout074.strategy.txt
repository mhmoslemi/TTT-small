### 2. Smooth Surrogate for the Max‑Objective  

The direct objective `max_k C_k` is non‑differentiable.

\[

* As `α→∞`, `F_α → max_k C_k`.  
* For moderate `α` (10…100) the function is smooth, convex‑like in the neighbourhood of the maximum, and its gradient is a weighted average of the individual gradients.

We will use a **continuation** strategy: start with a small `α` (e.g. 10) to allow large steps, then gradually raise `α` (e.g. 20 → 40 → 80) after each optimisation stage.

### 3. Analytic Gradient of a Single Shift  

Define `b = 1‑h`.  
For a fixed integer lag `ℓ` ( `-n+1 ≤ ℓ ≤ n‑1` ) the correlation entry is  

\[
C_ℓ(h)=\sum_{i} h_i\,b_{i+ℓ},
\]

where out‑of‑range indices are ignored (zero‑padded).  

Derivative w.r.t. `h_j`:

\[
\frac{∂C_ℓ}{∂h_j}
=
\begin{cases}
b_{j+ℓ} & \text{if}\;0≤j+ℓ<n,\\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
-
\begin{cases}
h_{j-ℓ} & \text{if}\;0≤j-ℓ<n,\\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}.
\]

Hence the gradient for a single lag is a **difference of two shifted vectors**:
* add `b` shifted forward by `ℓ`,
* subtract `h` shifted backward by `ℓ`.

Both operations are realised efficiently by **convolutions** (FFT) or simple slicing because `ℓ` is an integer.
