**1. Mathematical Reformulation**  

For a discretised step function `h` on `n` uniformly spaced points of `[0,2]` (`dx = 2/n`) we have  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{k\in\{-n+1,\dots ,n-1\}} \; \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i\,(1-h_{i+k}) \;dx ,
\]

where the summand is taken only when the index `i+k` lies in `[0,n-1]`.  
Define the *auto‑correlation*  

\[
A_k(h)=\sum_{i} h_i\,h_{i+k},
\qquad
C_5(h)=1-\min_{k} A_k(h)\,dx .
\]

Thus minimising the worst overlap is equivalent to **maximising the smallest auto‑correlation** over all admissible shifts.

The constraints are  

* box: \(0\le h_i\le1\) for all `i`;
* affine: \(\sum_i h_i = n/2\) (because the integral of `h` must be `1`).

The optimisation problem is a **convex‑concave min‑max** problem with a non‑smooth objective (the maximum over `2n‑1` linear forms).  

**2. Sub‑gradient of the Objective**  

For a fixed shift `k` the correlation term  

\[
R_k(h)=\sum_i h_i\,(1-h_{i+k})
\]

is linear in `h`. Its gradient (with respect to `h_j`) is  

\[
\frac{\partial R_k}{\partial h_j}= 
\begin{cases}
1-h_{j+k} & \text{if } 0\le j\le n-1-k,\\[4pt]
-\,h_{j-k} & \text{if } k\le j\le n-1,\\[4pt]
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

If the current maximum is attained at a set **𝒦** of shifts (typically one or two), a sub‑gradient of `C_5` is the (convex) combination of the gradients of those active shifts:

\[
g = \frac{1}{|\mathcal K|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\nabla R_k(h).
\]

Because `R_k` is linear, `g` can be built **with only a few vector‑slice operations**:

*For a non‑negative lag `k`*  

```text
g[0:n‑k]   += 1‑h[k:n]          # contribution (1‑h_{i+k})
g[k:n]     -= h[0:n‑k]          # contribution (‑h_{i})
```

*For a negative lag `k = -d`*  

```text
g[d:n]     += 1‑h[0:n‑d]        # (1‑h_{i‑d}) with i = d…n‑1
g[0:n‑d]   -= h[d:n]            # (‑h_{i+d})
```

Thus a full sub‑gradient is obtained in **O(n)** time.

**3. Projected Sub‑gradient Descent (PSD)**  

We iteratively move `h` opposite to the sub‑gradient, then project onto the feasible set (box ∩ hyper‑plane).  

*Projection*:  
Given a vector `y`, the projection onto  

\[
\{x\mid 0\le x_i\le1,\; \sum_i x_i = n/2\}
\]

is the classic *water‑filling* problem. It is solved efficiently by a **bisection on the Lagrange multiplier λ**:

1. For a trial λ compute `x_i = clip(y_i + λ, 0, 1)`.
2. The sum `S(λ) = Σ x_i` is monotone in λ; find λ such that `S(λ) = n/2`.  
   A binary search with ~30 iterations yields machine‑precision accuracy.

This projection is cheap (O(n) per iteration) and guarantees that after each update the constraints are exactly satisfied.
