### 1. Reformulation as a smooth optimisation problem

The “max’’ is non‑differentiable, which makes gradient‑based methods noisy.  
Replace it by the **log‑sum‑exp** (LSE) smooth upper‑bound

\[
\Phi_\beta(h)=\frac1\beta\log\Bigl(\sum_{k}e^{\beta\,F_k(h)}\Bigr),
\qquad\beta>0 .
\]

* For any `β` we have  `max_k F_k ≤ Φ_β`.  
* As `β→∞`, `Φ_β → max_k F_k`.  
* `Φ_β` is differentiable; its gradient is a weighted sum of the individual
  gradients `∇F_k`, with weights

\[
w_k=\frac{e^{\beta F_k}}{\sum_j e^{\beta F_j}}\;( \sum_k w_k =1 ).
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2. Exact gradient of the LSE

For a fixed shift `k` (non‑negative, zero‑padded outside the interval)

\[
F_k(h)=\sum_i h_i\;(1-h_{i+k}) .
\]

Define `I_k = { i | 0≤i<n, 0≤i+k<n }`.  
Derivative w.r.t. `h_a` :

\[
\frac{\partial F_k}{\partial h_a}= 
\begin{cases}
(1-h_{a+k})      & a\in I_k\\[4pt]
-\,h_{a-k}       & a-k\in I_k\\[4pt]
0                & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\;=\;(1-h_{a+k})-h_{a-k},
\]
where out‑of‑range indices are treated as zero.

Hence the gradient of the smooth objective is

\[
\bigl(\nabla\Phi_\beta(h)\bigr)_a
= \sum_k w_k\bigl[(1-h_{a+k})-h_{a-k}\bigr]
= 1-\sum_k w_k\bigl(h_{a+k}+h_{a-k}\bigr) .
\]

The formula is valid for all interior points; at the two ends the sums simply
ignore the out‑of‑range terms (treated as zero).

All required quantities can be obtained with **two FFT‑based convolutions**:

* `F = corr(h, 1‑h)` (full mode) gives every `F_k`.  
* `g = 1 - (w * h) – (w_rev * h)` where `*` is a linear (zero‑padded) convolution
  and `w_rev[k]=w_{-k}`.

Both convolutions are `O(n log n)`; for `n=120` even the naïve `O(n²)` loop
is cheap, but the FFT version is used to stay well within the time budget.

### 3. Projection onto the feasible set

After a gradient step we must enforce simultaneously  

* Box constraints: `0 ≤ h_i ≤ 1`.  
* Linear equality: `∑h_i = S` where `S = n/2`.

The projection onto the intersection of a hyperplane and a box is performed by
the classic **“clipping‑plus‑λ”** method:

1. For a given `λ` compute `p_i = clip( h_i - λ , 0 , 1 )`.  
2. Find `λ` such that `∑p_i = S`.  
   The mapping `λ ↦ ∑p_i` is monotone, so a **binary search** (≈ 30–40 iterations)
   yields the exact `λ` to machine precision.

The same routine (`project_to_sum_box`) was used in the previous code; it is
