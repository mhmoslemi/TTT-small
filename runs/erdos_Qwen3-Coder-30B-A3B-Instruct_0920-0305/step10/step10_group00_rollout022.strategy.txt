**1. Problem recap and mathematical reformulation**  
- Discretise the interval \([0,2]\) into \(n\) equal sub‑intervals (\(dx=2/n\)).  
- Let \(h\in\mathbb R^{n}\) with \(0\le h_i\le1\) and \(\sum_i h_i = n/2\) (so \(\int_0^2h=1\)).  
- For an integer shift \(\ell\in\{-(n-1),\dots,n-1\}\) the overlap term is  
  \[
  C_\ell(h)=dx\sum_{i:\,0\le i,i+\ell<n} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+\ell}\bigr) .
  \]
- The quantity to minimise is the worst‑case overlap  
  \[
  C_5(h)=\max_{\ell}C_\ell(h).
  \]

Because the max makes the objective non‑smooth we replace it temporarily by a **smooth surrogate**
\[
\tilde C_\alpha(h)=\frac{dx}{\alpha}\log\Bigl(\sum_{\ell}e^{\alpha C_\ell(h)}\Bigr),
\]
where \(\alpha>0\) is a smoothing parameter.  As \(\alpha\to\infty\) the surrogate tends to the true max, but for moderate \(\alpha\) the function is differentiable and its gradient can be expressed analytically (see §4).
