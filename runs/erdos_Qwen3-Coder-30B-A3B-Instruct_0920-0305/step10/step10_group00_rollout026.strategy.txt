\]

### 1.  Smooth approximation of the max (log‑sum‑exp)

Replace the max by the smooth function  

\[
\Phi_\beta(h)=\frac{dx}{\beta}\,\log\!\Bigl(\sum_{L}e^{\beta C_L(h)}\Bigr),
\]

where \(\beta>0\) controls the sharpness.  As \(\beta\to\infty\),

\[
\frac{\partial\Phi_\beta}{\partial h}
   =dx\;\sum_{L} w_L\,\nabla C_L(h),\qquad
   w_L=\frac{e^{\beta C_L}}{\sum_{M}e^{\beta C_M}} .
\]

Thus the problem becomes a *smooth* (though still non‑convex) optimisation
that can be tackled with projected gradient methods.

### 2.  Computing the correlation and its gradient efficiently

*Correlation.*  
For a given \(h\) compute the full correlation vector \(C(h)\) with a single
FFT:

1. Form \(b = 1-h\).
2. Let \(h_{\rm rev}=h\) reversed.
3. Pad both vectors to length \(L=2n-1\) and compute  
   \(H=\operatorname{rfft}(h_{\rm rev},L),\;B=\operatorname{rfft}(b,L)\).
4. Obtain \(C=\operatorname{irfft}(H\!\cdot\!B,L)\) (real inverse FFT).  
   The entries of \(C\) are exactly the \(C_L(h)\).

*Gradient of a single correlation.*  
For a shift \(L\ge 0\),

\[
\bigl(\nabla C_L(h)\bigr)_i=
\begin{cases}
1-h_{i+L}, & 0\le i\le n-1-L,\\[2pt]
-\,h_{i-L},& L\le i\le n-1,\\[2pt]
0, &\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

For a negative shift the formula is symmetric.  The gradient for **all**
shifts can be assembled with two additional FFT‑based convolutions:

*   \(g^{(+)} =\) (b shifted left by \(L\))  →  convolution of \(b\) with a
    Kronecker delta at lag \(L\).
*   \(g^{(-)} =\) (‑h shifted right by \(L\)) → convolution of \(h\) with the same kernel.

Summing the weighted contributions \(w_L g^{(\pm)}\) over all \(L\) yields the full
gradient \(\nabla\Phi_\beta(h)\) in **O(n log n)** time.
