**1.  Problem reformulation**

For a discretisation with `n` points (`dx = 2/n`) the overlap for a lag ℓ is  

\[
r_\ell(h)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+\ell}\bigr) ,
\qquad h_{i+\ell}=0\; \text{when the index is out of range}.
\]

The Erdős bound is  

\[
C_5(h)=dx\;\max_{\ell} r_\ell(h) .
\]

Feasibility set  

\[
\mathcal{F}= \Bigl\{h\in\mathbb{R}^n \;\big|\; 0\le h_i\le 1,\;
\sum_{i}h_i = n/2\Bigr\}.
\]

The objective is non‑convex because each \(r_\ell\) contains a bilinear term
\(-\sum_i h_i h_{i+\ell}\).  Direct optimisation of the raw max is difficult, so we replace it by a **smooth surrogate** and use a **projected gradient descent** that works directly on the feasible set.

**2.  Smooth surrogate for the max**

Introduce a temperature β>0 and define  

\[
\tilde C_\beta(h)=\frac{dx}{\beta}\,
\log\Bigl(\sum_{\ell} \exp\bigl(\beta\,r_\ell(h)\bigr)\Bigr).
\]

- As β→∞, \(\tilde C_\beta\) converges to the true \(C_5\).  
- Its gradient is a convex combination of the individual lag‑gradients:

\[
\nabla\tilde C_\beta
 =dx\;\sum_\ell w_\ell\,\nabla r_\ell,
 \qquad 
 w_\ell=\frac{\exp(\beta r_\ell)}{\sum_j\exp(\beta r_j)} .
\]

The per‑lag gradient has a closed form (derived from the definition of \(r_\ell\)):

\[
\bigl[\nabla r_\ell\bigr]_i = 1 - h_{i+\ell} - h_{i-\ell},
\]
where out‑of‑range indices are taken as 0.  Hence  

\[
-\nabla r_\ell = \underbrace{h_{i+\ell}+h_{i-\ell}}_{\text{neighbour sum}}-1 .
\]

The function inside the bisection is monotone, so 40–50 iterations of binary search give machine‑precision accuracy.  The cost is O(n) per projection.
