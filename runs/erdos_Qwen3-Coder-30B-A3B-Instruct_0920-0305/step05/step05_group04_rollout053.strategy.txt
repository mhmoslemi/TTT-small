### 1.  Re‑formulation of the objective  

For a discrete step function `h∈[0,1]^n` (with `dx = 2/n`)  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{k\neq0}\int_0^2 h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)dx
      = 1-\min_{k\neq0}\underbrace{\int_0^2 h(x)h(x+k)dx}_{=:c_k}.
\]

Thus **minimising** `C₅` is equivalent to **maximising** the worst self‑correlation  

\[
\min_{k\neq0}c_k .
\]

The vector `c = (c_1,…,c_{n-1})` is the (non‑negative) part of the linear convolution  
`c = conv(h,h)` – the “auto‑correlation” of `h`.  `c_k` is a *quadratic* form in `h`,
so the problem is non‑convex.

### 2.  Smooth surrogate for the max‑overlap  

The max‑overlap can be replaced by a smooth **soft‑minimum** (log‑sum‑exp) which is differentiable:

\[
\phi_\beta(h)=\frac{1}{\beta}\log\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e^{-\beta\,c_k}\Bigr)
           \;\;\approx\;-\min_{k\neq0}c_k .
\]

The actual bound is then  

\[
\widehat C_5(h)=1+\phi_\beta(h) .
\]

For small `β` the surface is very smooth (easy to optimise); as `β→∞` the surrogate converges to the true max‑overlap.  We will **continue** `β` (increase it) during the optimisation – a classic homotopy / annealing technique.

### 3.  Analytic gradient of the surrogate  

The gradient of a single correlation term is easy:

\[
\frac{\partial c_k}{\partial h_i}= 
\begin{cases}
h_{i+k} & \text{if }i+k<n\\
0       & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
+
\begin{cases}
h_{i-k} & \text{if }i-k\ge0\\
0       & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}.
\]

Collect these contributions for all shifts weighted by the soft‑minimum weights  

\[
w_k=\frac{e^{-\beta c_k}}{\sum_{t}e^{-\beta c_t}} ,
\qquad
\nabla\phi_\beta(h)= -\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} w_k\,\nabla c_k .
\]

In vector‑matrix notation the gradient can be computed by **two convolutions**:

```
g = -(conv(h, w) + conv(h, reverse(w)))          # length‑n vector
```

where `conv` is a linear (zero‑padded) convolution.  Using FFT (`numpy.fft`) both the
correlations `c_k` and the gradient are obtained in **O(n log n)** time, very fast for
n up to a few hundred.

### 4.  Feasibility projection  

The feasible set is the *bounded simplex*  

\[
0\le h_i\le1,\qquad\sum_i h_i = n/2.
\]

After a gradient step we **project** the trial vector onto this set:

1. **Clip** to `[0,1]`.  
2. **Adjust the sum** by solving a 1‑dimensional root‑finding problem:  
   find a scalar `λ` such that  
   \[
   \sum_i\operatorname{clip}(h_i+λ,0,1)=n/2 .
   \]
   This is the standard “projection onto the simplex with upper bound 1” (see
   Duchi‑Shalev‑Shwartz‑Singer‑Chandra 2008).  A simple bisection on `λ`
   converges in ≤30 iterations because the function is monotone.

The projection is exact (up to machine precision) and cheap (linear in `n`).
