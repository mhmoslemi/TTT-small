\(h:[0,2]\rightarrow[0,1]\) with \(\int_{0}^{2}h(x)\,dx=1\) that minimizes  

\[
C_{5}(h)=\max_{k}\int_{0}^{2}h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx .
\]

Because \(\int h =1\) is constant,  

\[
C_{5}(h)=1-\min_{k}\int_{0}^{2}h(x)h(x+k)\,dx .
\]

Thus we have to **maximise the minimal autocorrelation** of \(h\) over all shifts.

Then the overlap for shift \(\ell\) is simply  

\[

### 3. Continuous refinement phase (projected sub‑gradient / mass‑transfer)

#### 3.1 Sub‑gradient of the max‑overlap objective
For a given shift \(\ell\),

\[
f_\ell(h)=\int h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+\ell)\bigr)\,dx = 1 - g_\ell(h).
\]

The gradient of the overlap term \(g_\ell\) w.r.t. \(h_i\) (discrete) is  

\[
\frac{\partial g_\ell}{\partial h_i}=dx\bigl( h_{i+\ell}+h_{i-\ell}\bigr) ,
\]
with the convention that out‑of‑range indices contribute 0.  
Hence the sub‑gradient of the *maximum* objective  

\[
F(h)=\max_\ell f_\ell(h)
\]

at the currently active worst shift \(\ell^*\) is  

\[
\partial_i F(h) =-dx\bigl( h_{i+\ell^*}+h_{i-\ell^*}\bigr).
\]

A descent direction is therefore  

\[
d_i =+dx\bigl( h_{i+\ell^*}+h_{i-\ell^*}\bigr) .
\]

#### 3.2 Projected update

\[
\tilde h = h + \alpha d .

#### 3.3 Mass‑transfer heuristic (equivalent viewpoint)
The same update can be interpreted as repeatedly moving a small amount \(\delta\) of “mass’’ from a low‑\(w_i\) index to a high‑\(w_i\) index, where  

\[
w_i = h_{i+\ell^*}+h_{i-\ell^*}.
\]

Implementation steps for each inner iteration:
1. Compute the worst shift \(\ell^*\) (the index of the maximal correlation).  
2. Compute the vector \(w\) for that \(\ell^*\).  
3. Select a source index \(p\) with **large** \(w_p\) and \(h_p<1\) (room to increase).  
4. Select a sink index \(q\) with **small** \(w_q\) and \(h_q>0\) (room to decrease).

6. Because the transfer keeps the total sum unchanged, no extra projection is needed; only clipping is required.
