### 1.  Reformulate as a binary (0/1) problem  

Empirically the optimal solution is at an extreme point of the feasible cube
(because the objective is a piecewise‑linear function of the products \(h_i h_{i+s}\)).
Hence we restrict the search to binary vectors  
\(h_i\in\{0,1\}\) with exactly \(n/2\) ones (the integral constraint becomes  
\(\sum_i h_i=n/2\)).  For a binary vector the overlap for shift s is

\[
M_s(h) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1-s} h_i\,(1-h_{i+s}) 
      = \frac{n}{2} - \sum_{i=0}^{n-1-s} y_{i,s},
\]

where \(y_{i,s}=h_i\cdot h_{i+s}\) is also binary.

Thus minimising \(\max_s M_s\) is equivalent to **maximising** the minimal
self‑overlap \(\sum_i y_{i,s}\) across all shifts.

### 2.  Exact ILP for modest n

```
y_{i,s} ≤ h_i
y_{i,s} ≤ h_{i+s}
y_{i,s} ≥ h_i + h_{i+s} – 1
```

\[

### 7.  Anticipated failure modes & mitigations  

* **ILP explosion** – for N > 80 the number of `y_{i,s}` variables grows

* **SA stagnation** – the swap move may become ineffective when T is already low.

* **Incremental update bugs** – incorrect update of the correlation vector could
