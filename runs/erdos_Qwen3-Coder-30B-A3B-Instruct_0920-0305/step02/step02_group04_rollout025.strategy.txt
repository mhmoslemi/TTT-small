The Erdős‑minimum‑overlap problem for *k = 5* can be written, after discretisation, as a **max‑min quadratic** optimisation problem

\[
\min_{h\in[0,1]^n,\;\sum h_i = n/2}
\;C_5(h)=\max_{\ell\in\{1,\dots ,n-1\}}
\sum_{i=0}^{n-\ell-1}h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+\ell}\bigr)\;\Delta x ,
\qquad 
\Delta x =\frac{2}{n}.
\]

Because  

\[
\sum_i h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+\ell}\bigr)=\Bigl(\frac n2\Bigr)-\sum_i h_i h_{i+\ell},
\]

the problem is **equivalent** to maximising the *minimum* auto‑overlap  

\[
\min_{\ell}\;S_\ell(h),\qquad 
S_\ell(h)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-\ell-1}h_i h_{i+\ell}.
\]

For a binary step‑function (the usual extremal case) the variables are
\(h_i\in\{0,1\}\) and each \(S_\ell\) is an integer.  Consequently the
optimal value of the *unscaled* objective  

\[
T = \max_\ell\;\sum_i h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+\ell}\bigr)
\]

is an integer and the continuous bound is simply  

\[
C_5 = T\;\Delta x = T\frac{2}{n}.
\]
