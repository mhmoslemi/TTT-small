**1. Re‑formulate the problem as a sequence of linear programs**

For a discretisation with an even number of points `n` (so that the integral constraint can be satisfied exactly) set  

* `dx = 2.0 / n`  
* decision variables `h = (h₀,…,h_{n‑1})` with `0 ≤ h_i ≤ 1` and  
  `∑_{i=0}^{n‑1} h_i = n/2` (this is equivalent to `∫₀² h = 1`).  

For a shift of `s` indices (`s = 0,…,n‑1`) the overlap integral is  

\[
L_s(h)=\int_0^2 h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+s\,dx)\bigr)\,dx
      = 1 - dx\;\underbrace{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1-s} h_i\,h_{i+s}}_{O_s(h)} .
\]

The goal is  

\[
\min_{h}\;\max_{s} L_s(h)
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\max_{h}\;\min_{s}\;dx\;O_s(h) .
\]

Because each `O_s` contains bilinear terms `h_i h_{i+s}`, the problem is a non‑convex QCQP.  
Instead of trying to minimise the non‑smooth maximum directly, **linearise the bilinear terms** and solve a linear program (LP) that is a first‑order outer approximation of the original constraints. Repeating this linearisation yields a **sequential linear programming (SLP)**/convex‑concave procedure that converges to a stationary point of the original problem.

**2. Linearisation of the bilinear part**

For a fixed shift `s` define  

\[
L_s(h)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i
      -\sum_{i=0}^{n-1-s} h_i\,h_{i+s}.
\]

At iteration `k` we have a current feasible point `h^{(k)}`.  
For any pair `(i,i+s)` the bilinear term is linearised by a first‑order Taylor expansion:

\[
h_i\,h_{i+s}
\;\approx\;
h_i^{(k)}\,h_{i+s}
\;+\;h_{i+s}^{(k)}\,h_i
\;-\;h_i^{(k)}\,h_{i+s}^{(k)} .
\]

Summing over `i` gives a **linear expression** for `L_s`:

\[
L_s(h) \;\approx\;
\underbrace{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}
\bigl[\,1
      -h_{i+s}^{(k)}-h_{i-s}^{(k)}\bigr]\,h_i}_{a_s^{\top}h}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\Bigl(-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1-s}
    h_i^{(k)}\,h_{i+s}^{(k)}\Bigr)}_{b_s}.
\]

(For indices outside the range the missing `h`‑terms are taken as `0`.)  
Thus for each shift we obtain a **pair (a_s, b_s)** such that the true inequality  

\[
L_s(h) \le t
\]

is approximated by the linear inequality  

\[
a_s^{\top}h + b_s \;\le\; t .
\]
