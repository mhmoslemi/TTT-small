**1. Re‑formulate the objective**

On a uniform grid of *n* points (Δx = 2⁄*n*) the integral becomes  

\[
C_5(\mathbf h)=\Delta x\; \max_{\ell\in\{-n+1,\dots,n-1\}} 
c_\ell(\mathbf h),\qquad  
c_\ell(\mathbf h)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+\ell}\bigr),
\]

with the conventions \(h_{i+\ell}=0\) if the index leaves [0, *n*–1].  
Because \(\sum_i h_i\) is fixed (= *n*/2),  

\[
c_\ell(\mathbf h)=\frac{n}{2}-\!\!\sum_i h_i\,h_{i+\ell}
        =\frac{n}{2}-A_\ell(\mathbf h),
\]

so minimising the maximum overlap is equivalent to **maximising the minimum autocorrelation** \(A_\ell\).

=-\Delta x\bigl(h_{i+\ell}+h_{i-\ell}\bigr)

Therefore  

\[

=-\Delta x\sum_{\ell} w_\ell\,
\bigl( \mathbf h \text{ shifted by }+\ell \;+\;

Both shifted sums are ordinary linear convolutions of the weight vector *w* with *h*.

* Multiply pointwise with the FFT of the *reversed* *w* to get the “–ℓ” term.  

The sum of the two central *n* entries is the gradient.
