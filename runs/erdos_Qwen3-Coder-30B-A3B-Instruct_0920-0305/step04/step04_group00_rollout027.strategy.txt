**1.  Problem insight**

For a step function \(h:[0,2]\to[0,1]\) with \(\int_0^2 h=1\) the quantity to be minimised is  

\[
C_5=\max_{k}\int_0^2 h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx
      =1-\min_{k}\int_0^2 h(x)\,h(x+k)\,dx .
\]

Thus a good \(h\) must keep the *self‑overlap* \(\int h(x)h(x+k)\) large for **all** admissible shifts.  
The integral is evaluated discretely as  

\[
\operatorname{corr}(k)=\sum_{i}h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+k}\bigr)dx,
\qquad C_5=\max_k\operatorname{corr}(k),
\]

where the sum runs over the overlapping indices only (zero padding outside \([0,2]\)).  
Large values of \(C_5\) are caused by the “edge” contributions: for a big shift most indices have no partner, so the term reduces to a plain sum of the edge values of \(h\). Consequently a **central, bell‑shaped** function with tiny values near the boundaries is expected to be optimal.

**2.  Reduce the search space – a low‑dimensional parametric family**

We restrict \(h\) to a symmetric, monotone‑decreasing shape about the centre \(x=1\).  
Let \(n\) be the number of discretisation points (choose \(n=200\); this yields a cheap \(\mathcal O(n^2)\) correlation and a smooth representation).  
Define a set of break‑points \(0=x_0<x_1<\dots<x_{K}=1\) (in the half‑interval) and associated heights  

\[
1=h_0\ge h_1\ge \dots \ge h_{K}\ge 0 .
\]

The full vector is obtained by linear interpolation on \([0,1]\) and mirroring to \([1,2]\).  
Because the area constraint is linear, after building the raw vector we simply **scale** it by a factor \(\lambda\) so that  

\[
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i\,dx = 1 .
\]

If any entry exceeds 1 after scaling we clip it to 1 and renormalise again (a few iterations converge rapidly).  
Thus the optimisation variables are the unordered heights \(\{u_j\}_{j=0}^{K}\in[0,1]\); after sorting them decreasingly we obtain the monotone sequence \(\{h_j\}\).  The dimension of the problem is \(K+1\) (typically \(K=4\) or \(5\)), far smaller than the full \(n\)-dimensional space.

**4.  Fine‑tuning the full‑resolution vector with a smooth surrogate**

The max‑operator in \(C_5\) is non‑differentiable, which hinders gradient‑based refinement.  
Introduce a smooth approximation

\[
L_\beta(h)=\frac{1}{\beta}\log\Bigl(\sum_{k}e^{\beta\,f_k(h)}\Bigr),
\qquad
f_k(h)=\sum_i h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+k}\bigr)dx .
\]

For \(\beta\approx 15\!-\!25\) the surrogate closely follows the true maximum while remaining differentiable.

*Gradient derivation* – for a fixed shift \(k\),

\[
\frac{\partial f_k}{\partial h_j}=dx\bigl(1 - h_{j+k} - h_{j-k}\bigr),
\]

where missing indices (outside \([0,n-1]\)) are treated as zero.  
The gradient of the surrogate is a convex combination of these per‑shift gradients:

\[
\nabla L_\beta = \sum_{k} w_k \,\nabla f_k,
\qquad
w_k = \frac{e^{\beta f_k}}{\sum_\ell e^{\beta f_\ell}} .
\]

*Projection onto the feasible set* – after a gradient step we must satisfy  

\[
0\le h_i\le 1,\qquad \sum_i h_i\,dx = 1 .
\]

This is the projection onto the intersection of a box and a simplex.  
The projection can be performed by the classic **water‑filling** algorithm:

1. Compute \(\tilde h_i = h_i - \lambda\) for a Lagrange multiplier \(\lambda\).  
2. Clip \(\tilde h_i\) to \([0,1]\).  
3. Adjust \(\lambda\) (binary search on a reasonable interval) until the clipped vector sums to the required total \(S=n/2\) (because \(dx=2/n\)).  

The procedure converges in a handful of iterations and is computationally negligible.
