### 1. Discretisation & Notation

where out‑of‑range indices contribute zero.

\[

\[
c_\ell(\mathbf h)=\frac{n}{2}-\sum_i h_i h_{i+\ell}

### 2. Fast Evaluation via FFT

Correlation can be obtained with a single FFT:

1. Reverse \(\mathbf h\): \(\mathbf h_{\text{rev}} = \mathbf h\) reversed.  
2. Form \(\mathbf b = \mathbf 1-\mathbf h\).  
3. Compute the convolution of \(\mathbf h_{\text{rev}}\) and \(\mathbf b\) of length \(L=2n-1\) using a real FFT.  
4. The resulting array `corr` satisfies `corr[ℓ+(n‑1)] = c_ℓ(\mathbf h)`; the maximum component gives the objective.  

Complexity is \(O(n\log n)\), allowing millions of objective calls within the 10‑minute budget.

\[

\]

* As \(\beta\to\infty\), \(\phi_\beta \uparrow C_5\).

**Gradient formula** (derived from the chain rule and the explicit gradient of each \(c_\ell\)):

\[
\frac{\partial c_\ell}{\partial h_i}= -\bigl(h_{i-\ell}+h_{i+\ell}\bigr)

\[

\sum_{\ell} w_\ell(\mathbf h)\;\nabla c_\ell(\mathbf h),\qquad

All required quantities (\(c_\ell\) and the weighted gradient) are obtained from the same FFT‑based convolution, preserving the \(O(n\log n)\) cost.
