**Goal** – Produce a step‑function `h` on [0,2] (discretised with ≤ 1 000 points) satisfying  

* 0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1,  
* Σ h · dx = 1 (i.e. Σ h = n/2),

that minimises  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_k\int_0^{2} h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx .
\]

The current best bound (≈ 0.381055) was obtained by many random restarts of SLSQP on the full‑dimension problem.  
The new plan replaces the “random‑restart‑local‑optimiser” pipeline with a **smooth‑max, projected gradient descent** that works directly on the minimax formulation, reduces the dimensionality by exploiting symmetry, and uses a low‑dimensional parametric “shape” as a warm‑start.  

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1. Discretisation & symmetry

* Choose an even number of points `n = 128` (any value 80 – 256 works; 128 gives a good trade‑off between resolution and speed).  
* Impose exact symmetry:  

  \[
  h[i]=h[n-1-i]\qquad(i=0,\dots,n/2-1).
  \]

  Only the first half `m = n/2` variables are independent.  
* The integral constraint becomes  

  \[
  \sum_{i=0}^{m-1} h[i] = \frac{n}{4}\; (= m/2).
  \]

  After each update we will **project** the full `h` onto the intersection of the box \([0,1]^n\) with this hyper‑plane.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2. Objective – a smooth surrogate for the max

For any shift `ℓ` (index lag, `ℓ = -(n-1)…(n-1)`) define  

\[
f_\ell(h)=\sum_{i} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+\ell}\bigr)\;dx
        = \underbrace{\sum_i h_i}_{=1/dx}=1\;-\;\sum_i h_i\,h_{i+\ell}\;dx .
\]

The term \(\sum_i h_i\,h_{i+\ell}\) is a **convolution** of `h` with itself.  
The original objective is  \(\max_\ell f_\ell(h)\).

Replace the nondifferentiable max by the **log‑sum‑exp** (smooth‑max)

\[
M_\beta(h)=\frac{1}{\beta}\log\Bigl(\sum_{\ell} e^{\beta f_\ell(h)}\Bigr),
\qquad\beta>0 .
\]

*When \(\beta\to\infty\), \(M_\beta\to\max f_\ell\).*

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3. Analytic gradient of the smooth surrogate

Let  

\[
w_\ell = \frac{e^{\beta f_\ell(h)}}{\sum_{j} e^{\beta f_j(h)}}\quad(\text{soft‑max weights}).
\]

The gradient of \(f_\ell\) w.r.t. a component `h_j` is (boundary‑aware)

\[
\frac{\partial f_\ell}{\partial h_j}=dx\Bigl[1-(h_{j+\ell}+h_{j-\ell})\Bigr] ,
\]

where out‑of‑range indices are omitted (treated as 0).  
Thus

\[
\nabla M_\beta(h)=dx\sum_{\ell} w_\ell\,
        \bigl[\,\mathbf{1}-(\mathbf{h}\_{\text{shifted left by }\ell}
                        +\mathbf{h}\_{\text{shifted right by }\ell})\bigr].
\]

All shifted copies can be obtained with **circular‑zero padding** and cheap `np.roll`/`np.pad` operations, so the full gradient is computed in \(O(n^2)\) but with vectorised NumPy it is negligible for \(n=128\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 4. Projection onto the feasible set

After a gradient step we must enforce both:

* **Box constraints** \(0\le h_i\le 1\).
* **Equality** \(\sum h_i = n/2\).

We use the Euclidean projection onto the **bounded simplex**:

1. Clip the vector to \([0,1]\).
2. If the sum equals the target (within tolerance) we are done.
3. Otherwise solve for a threshold \(\lambda\) such that  

   \[
   \sum_i \min\bigl\{1,\max\{0,\,h_i-\lambda\}\bigr\}=n/2 .
   \]

   This is a standard “water‑filling’’ problem solvable by sorting the clipped values once and scanning to find the correct λ in linear time.

Because of symmetry we apply the projection only to the `m` free variables, then mirror them to obtain the full `h`.

### 5. Optimization schedule

| Phase | β (smooth‑max) | Step size η | Iterations |
|------|----------------|------------|------------|
| Warm‑up | β = 5 | η = 0.2 · dx | 300 |
| Refinement 1 | β = 20 | η = 0.1 · dx | 300 |
| Refinement 2 | β = 80 | η = 0.05 · dx | 300 |
| Final tightening | β = 320 | η = 0.02 · dx | 200 |

Between phases we **re‑evaluate the true max** `C5 = maxℓ f_ℓ(h)` (no smoothing) and keep the best‑ever `h`.  
The step size is multiplied by `dx = 2/n` to keep updates of the same physical magnitude regardless of resolution.

**Annealing β** forces the optimiser first to improve the average of the overlaps (easy) and later concentrate on the worst shift (hard).  

A simple back‑tracking line search can be added: if an update raises the smooth objective `M_β`, halve η and retry (max 5 halvings).

* **Warm‑start** the first run with a smooth “Gaussian bump”:  

  \[
  h(x)=A\,\exp\!\bigl(-\alpha (x-1)^2\bigr),\qquad
  A\text{ chosen so that }\int_0^2 h=1 .
