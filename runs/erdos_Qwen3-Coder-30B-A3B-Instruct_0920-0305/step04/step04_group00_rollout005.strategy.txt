**1. Goal and mathematical formulation**  
We must produce a step function \(h:[0,2]\rightarrow[0,1]\) satisfying  

\[
\int_{0}^{2}h(x)\,dx=1                          
\]

and minimise  

\[
C_{5}(h)=\max_{k\in\mathbb R}\int_{0}^{2}h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx .
\]

In the discrete setting with \(n\) equally spaced samples  
\(h_i=h(x_i),\;x_i=i\;\Delta x,\;\Delta x=2/n\) the integral is approximated by  

\[
C_{5}(h)=\Delta x\; \max_{\ell\in\{-n+1,\dots ,n-1\}}
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+\ell}\bigr) ,
\]

where out‑of‑range indices give \(h_{j}=0\).

The bound improves when the mass of \(h\) is concentrated in the centre
so that for large shifts the overlapping term \(h_i\) is tiny.  
A small number of shape parameters can capture this effect far more
reliably than a high‑dimensional free vector.

**2. Low‑dimensional symmetric shape family**  

We restrict to symmetric, non‑increasing functions about the centre
\(x=1\).  The shape is completely described by a *plateau width* \(w\in[0,2]\)  
and a *curvature exponent* \(p\ge 1\).  Define the distance from the centre  

\[
r(x)=|x-1|,\qquad 0\le r\le 1 .
\]

For a given \(w\) we set a *cut‑off* radius  

\[
a = \frac{w}{2}\in[0,1],
\]

inside which \(h\) is flat, outside of which it decays as a power law to
zero at the boundary:

\[
h(x)=
\begin{cases}
H, & r\le a,\\[4pt]
H\Bigl(1-\dfrac{r-a}{1-a}\Bigr)^{p}, & a< r\le 1,\\[6pt]
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

The normalising constant \(H\) follows from the area constraint

\[
1=\int_{0}^{2}h(x)\,dx
   = 2\Bigl[ aH + \int_{a}^{1}H\Bigl(1-\frac{r-a}{1-a}\Bigr)^{p}\,dr\Bigr]
   = 2H\Bigl[ a + (1-a)\frac{1}{p+1}\Bigr].
\]

Hence  

\[
H=\frac{1}{2\bigl(a+(1-a)/(p+1)\bigr)}.
\]

The free parameters are therefore  

* \(a\in[0,1]\)  (controls the size of the central flat region),  
* \(p\in[1,\,\infty)\) (controls how sharply the function drops to
  zero; a large \(p\) yields a steep “tent”, \(p=1\) gives a linear
  ramp).

When \(a=0\) we obtain a pure power‑law tent (for \(p=1\) the classic
triangle), and when \(a=1\) the function is constant \(h\equiv 0.5\).

**3. Discretisation of the shape**

analytic formula at the points \(x_i=i\Delta x\) (\(i=0,\dots,n-1\)).  
Because the formula already respects the bounds \([0,1]\) and the area
constraint exactly, no additional clipping or scaling is required.

**6. Local refinement of the full vector**  

The DE optimum provides a *high‑quality seed* for a full‑dimensional
search that can adjust the values beyond the strict parametric form:

1. **Projection onto the feasible set**  
   After a gradient step we must enforce  
   \[
   0\le h_i\le1,\qquad \sum_i h_i = n/2 .
   \]  
   This is a Euclidean projection onto a simplex with box constraints.
   It can be done analytically by a water‑filling (binary search for a
   Lagrange multiplier \(\lambda\)):  
   \[
   \tilde h_i = \operatorname{clip}\bigl(h_i-\lambda,\,0,\,1\bigr),\qquad
   \text{choose }\lambda \text{ such that }\sum_i \tilde h_i=n/2 .
   \]

2. **Smooth approximation of the max** – replace the nondifferentiable
   max with a log‑sum‑exp:
   \[
   \Phi_{\alpha}(h)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\!\Bigl(\sum_{\ell}
   \exp\bigl(\alpha\,f_{\ell}(h)\bigr)\Bigr),\quad
   f_{\ell}(h)=\Delta x\sum_i h_i(1-h_{i+\ell}) .
   \]
   With \(\alpha\approx 15\!-\!20\) the value is virtually the true max,
   yet \(\Phi_{\alpha}\) is smooth and its gradient is a convex combination
   of the gradients of the individual shifts:
   \[
   \nabla\Phi_{\alpha}= \frac{\sum_{\ell}
      \exp(\alpha f_{\ell})\nabla f_{\ell}}{\sum_{\ell}\exp(\alpha f_{\ell})}.
   \]

   The gradient \(\nabla f_{\ell}\) for shift \(\ell\) is
   \[
   (\nabla f_{\ell})_j = \Delta x\Bigl[1-h_{j+\ell}
                        -\mathbf{1}_{j-\ell\ge0}\,h_{j-\ell}\Bigr],
   \]
   where out‑of‑range indices give zero.

3. **Projected gradient descent (PGD)** – initialise with the DE‑derived vector,
   then iterate
   * compute \(\Phi_{\alpha}(h)\) and its gradient,
   * take a step: \(h' = h - \eta \nabla\Phi_{\alpha}(h)\) with a diminishing step size
     (\(\eta_k = \eta_0/(1+k)\)),
   * project \(h'\) onto the feasible set (step 1).

   Stop when the improvement in \(\Phi_{\alpha}\) falls below a tiny tolerance
   or when the allocated time slice expires.

Because \(\Phi_{\alpha}\) closely tracks the true objective, PGD drives the
worst‑case shift down while keeping the constraints satisfied.

```text
        if time_exhausted: break
        if improvement_small: break

    final_C = max_corr(h) * dx
    return (h.tolist(), final_C, n)
```

All helper functions (`build_h_from_ab`, `max_corr`, `smooth_max_and_grad`,
`project_onto_simplex_box`) are pure NumPy code and run in linear or
quadratic time.

search (DE) on the shape parameters** followed by a **smooth‑max projected

for many random restarts with SLSQP, avoids the problematic non‑convex
bilinear constraints, and concentrates the optimisation effort on the
