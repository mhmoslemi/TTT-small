**1. Problem reformulation**

- Discretise the interval \([0,2]\) with \(n\) equidistant points (\(n\) will be chosen ≈ 150‑200 – still far below the 1 000 point “speed” limit).  
- Let the step‑function values be the vector \(h\in[0,1]^n\).  
- The integration step is \(dx=2/n\); the mass constraint is \(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i=n/2\).

For a shift index \(s\in\{-(n-1),\dots ,n-1\}\) define  

\[
c_s(h)=\sum_{i}h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+s}\bigr) \qquad(\text{out‑of‑range indices give 0}).
\]

The quantity to minimise is  

\[
C_5(h)=dx\;\max_s c_s(h).
\]

Because \(\sum_i h_i\) is constant,  

\[
c_s(h)=\frac n2-\sum_i h_i h_{i+s},
\]
so minimising \(\max_s c_s\) is equivalent to **maximising the minimal autocorrelation**
\(\displaystyle a_s(h)=\sum_i h_i h_{i+s}\).

**2. Smooth surrogate for the max**

The max is non‑smooth, which makes gradient‑based optimisation difficult.  
Replace it by the *log‑sum‑exp* (soft‑max) surrogate

\[
F_\varepsilon(h)=\varepsilon\,
\log\Bigl(\sum_{s} \exp\bigl(c_s(h)/\varepsilon\bigr)\Bigr),
\]

with a temperature \(\varepsilon>0\).
When \(\varepsilon\to0\) we recover \(\max_s c_s\).  
\(F_\varepsilon\) is smooth and its gradient can be written analytically.

**3. Analytic gradient**

Let  

\[
w_s=\frac{\exp(c_s/\varepsilon)}{\sum_{t}\exp(c_t/\varepsilon)}\quad(\text{softmax weights}),
\qquad
W=\sum_s w_s=1.
\]

Derivative of one correlation term (using the definition of \(c_s\)) is

\[
\frac{\partial c_s}{\partial h_i}= (1-h_{i+s})-\;h_{i-s},
\]

with the convention that any out‑of‑range index contributes 0.
Summing with the weights gives the gradient of the surrogate:

\[
\frac{\partial F_\varepsilon}{\partial h_i}= 
\sum_{s} w_s\bigl[(1-h_{i+s})-h_{i-s}\bigr].
\tag{1}
\]

Expression (1) can be evaluated **in \(O(n\log n)\)** using two convolutions:

* Compute the weight vector \(w\) (length \(L=2n-1\)).  
* Let \(w^{\rm rev}=w_{\!L-1},\dots ,w_0\).  
* Convolution \(p=\operatorname{conv}(w,h)\) and \(q=\operatorname{conv}(w^{\rm rev},h)\) (full mode).  
* With an offset \(o=n-1\),

\[
\bigl[p_{o},\dots ,p_{o+n-1}\bigr]=\sum_s w_s\,h_{i-s},\qquad
\bigl[q_{o},\dots ,q_{o+n-1}\bigr]=\sum_s w_s\,h_{i+s}.
\]

Therefore the gradient vector is

\[
g = \mathbf{1} - (p_{o:o+n}+q_{o:o+n}) .
\tag{2}
\]

All operations are vectorised; the two convolutions are performed with FFT for speed.

**4. Projection onto the feasible set**

After a gradient step we must enforce:

* box constraints \(0\le h_i\le1\),
* the linear equality \(\sum_i h_i=n/2\).

This is a classic *box‑plus‑affine* projection and can be performed by the *water‑filling* (binary‑search on a Lagrange multiplier) method:

1. Shift the vector by a scalar \(\lambda\): \(\tilde h_i=\operatorname{clip}(h_i+\lambda,0,1)\).
2. Find \(\lambda\) such that \(\sum_i\tilde h_i=n/2\).  
   Because the sum is monotone in \(\lambda\), a simple bisection with tolerance \(10^{-12}\) converges in ≈ 30 iterations.
