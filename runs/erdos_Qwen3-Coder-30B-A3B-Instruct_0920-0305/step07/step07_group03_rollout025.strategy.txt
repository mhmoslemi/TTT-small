**1. Problem re‑formulation**  
For a discretised step function `h∈[0,1]^n` with `∑h = n/2` and step size `dx = 2/n` the overlap for an integer shift `ℓ` is  
\[
C_ℓ(h)=dx\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+ℓ}\bigr),
\]  
where `h_j = 0` when `j` is outside `[0,n‑1]`.  
The objective is  
\[
C_5(h)=\max_{ℓ} C_ℓ(h).
\]  
Because the sum‐over‐shifts is a pointwise maximum of linear (in the products `h_i h_{i+ℓ}`) functions, a natural sub‑gradient is the gradient of the *active* shift(s) that attain the maximum.

**2. Fast evaluation**  
Implement a routine that, given `h`, computes the full aperiodic correlation `corr = np.fft.ifft(fft(h_rev)*fft(1‑h))` (real part) and returns `C5 = dx * max(corr)`. This runs in `O(n log n)` and is cheap for `n≈120‑200`.

**3. Projection onto the feasible set**  
Define a projection `Π(x)` that solves  
\[
\min_{y\in[0,1]^n}\|y-x\|_2 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum y_i = n/2 .
\]  
A standard “water‑filling’’ (binary‑search on a Lagrange multiplier) yields the exact projection and will be used after every update of `h`.

**4. Global stochastic search on the combinatorial core**  
*Purpose*: locate a promising arrangement of the `n/2` unit masses (a binary pattern) that already yields a low `C5`.  

- **Initialisation**: generate a binary vector `h⁰` by drawing a random subset of `n/2` indices to be `1`.  
- **Neighbourhood**:  
  - *Swap*: pick a `1`‑position `i` and a `0`‑position `j` uniformly at random and exchange them.  
  - *Block swap*: choose two disjoint contiguous blocks of equal length (length 1‑3) and swap their contents; this moves several masses at once and can escape plateaus where a single swap does not change `C5`.  
  - *Three‑cycle*: rotate values among three positions while preserving the total sum.  
- **Acceptance rule**: simulated annealing. Let `Δ = C5(new)‑C5(old)`. Accept the move with probability `1` if `Δ≤0`, otherwise with probability `exp(‑Δ/T)`.  
- **Temperature schedule**: start with `T₀ = 0.1` (in the same units as `C5`) and geometrically cool to `T_f = 10⁻⁴` over a pre‑allocated number of iterations (e.g. 30 000).

- **Result of phase 1**: the best binary vector `h_bin` (still `0/1`) and its `C5_bin`.

  \begin{cases}
