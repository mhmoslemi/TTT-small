### 1.  Why a binary MILP is natural

For a binary vector (\(h_i\in\{0,1\}\)) the product \(h_i h_{i+\ell}\) is itself binary and can be represented exactly by a new variable  

\[
y_{i,\ell}=h_i\land h_{i+\ell}.
\]

The constraints  

\[
\begin{aligned}
y_{i,\ell} &\le h_i,\\
y_{i,\ell} &\le h_{i+\ell},\\
y_{i,\ell} &\ge h_i+h_{i+\ell}-1,\\
y_{i,\ell}\in\{0,1\}
\end{aligned}
\]

force \(y_{i,\ell}=h_i h_{i+\ell}\) whenever the \(h\)’s are binary.
The overlap for lag \(\ell\) is then a **linear** expression  

\[
f_\ell=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1-\ell} y_{i,\ell}.
\]

Thus the original max‑over‑lag problem becomes a pure **max‑min linear** problem:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{maximise}   & \lambda\\[2mm]
\text{subject to} & \displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N-1-\ell} y_{i,\ell}\ \ge\ \lambda,
                    \qquad \forall\;\ell=0,\dots ,N-1,\\[2mm]
                  & \displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} h_i\ =\ N/2,\\[2mm]
                  & y_{i,\ell}\text{ linked to }h_i\text{ by the three McCormick inequalities above},\\[2mm]
                  & h_i\in\{0,1\},\ y_{i,\ell}\in\{0,1\},\ \lambda\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\end{array}
\]

Because \(C_5=1-dx\lambda\) (with \(dx=2/N\)), maximising \(\lambda\) is exactly equivalent to minimising \(C_5\).
