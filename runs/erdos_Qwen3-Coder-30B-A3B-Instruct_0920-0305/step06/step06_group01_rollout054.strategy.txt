**1.  Problem reformulation**

For a discretised step‑function `h` on `[0,2]` (length `n`, spacing `dx=2/n`) we have  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{k}\int_0^2 h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx
      =1-\min_{k}\underbrace{\int_0^2 h(x)h(x+k)\,dx}_{S_k(h)} .
\]

Thus minimising `C5` is equivalent to **maximising the minimum auto‑correlation**
\(\min_k S_k(h)\).  The auto‑correlation at lag `k` (including the zero padding used by `np.correlate`) is

\[
S_k(h)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i\,h_{i+k},\qquad k=-(n-1),\dots ,n-1 .
\]

**2.  Smooth surrogate for the non‑smooth “minimum”**

The minimum can be approximated by the soft‑minimum (log‑sum‑exp)  

\[
G_{\alpha}(h)= -\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\!\Bigl(\sum_{k}e^{-\alpha S_k(h)}\Bigr),
\]

with a temperature parameter `α>0`.  
- For small `α` the function is very smooth and its gradient points towards
  regions where *all* correlations increase.  
- As `α` grows the surrogate concentrates on the *worst* lag, turning the optimisation
  into a true max‑min problem.

Because  

\[
C_5(h)=1-\min_k S_k(h) \approx 1+G_{\alpha}(h),
\]

maximising `Gα` directly reduces the bound on `C5`.

**3.  Exact gradient of the surrogate**

Let  

\[
w_k=\frac{e^{-\alpha S_k(h)}}{\sum_{\ell}e^{-\alpha S_{\ell}(h)}}\quad(\,w_k\ge0,\;\sum_k w_k=1\,).
\]

The derivative of `Gα` w.r.t. a component `h_j` is

\[
\frac{\partial G_{\alpha}}{\partial h_j}

\]

with the convention `h_i=0` for indices outside `[0,n-1]`.  
Consequently the full gradient vector is

\[
g = \bigl(g_0,\dots,g_{n-1}\bigr),\qquad
g_j =\sum_{k} w_k\bigl(h_{j+k}+h_{j-k}\bigr).

**4.  Fast computation of the gradient**

The weight array `w` is symmetric (`S_k=S_{-k}` ⇒ `w_k=w_{-k}`), therefore we can
form a symmetric kernel  

\[
W_{n-1+k}=W_{n-1-k}=w_k,\qquad k=0,\dots,n-1 .
\]

A single **convolution** yields all needed sums:

```

g    = conv[n-1 : n-1 + n]                       # centre of conv
g   += w0 * h                                     # double contribution of lag 0
```

where `w0 = w_{0}` (the centre element of `W`).  
Convolution is `O(n log n)` when performed with FFT, cheap for `n≈200‑300`.

hyperplane and the box is obtained by a **binary search on a scalar λ**:

```
proj(y) = clip(y + λ, 0, 1) ,
choose λ s.t. Σ proj(y) = n/2 .
```

The monotonicity of the sum in λ guarantees convergence in ≈40 iterations,
which is negligible compared with the overall optimisation time.
