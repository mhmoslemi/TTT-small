### 1. Mathematical framing  

- We work with a discretisation of the interval \([0,2]\) into **n** equally spaced points (dx = 2/n).  
- The decision vector **h** ∈ [0,1]^n must satisfy the linear equality  

  \[
  \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i = \frac n2 \qquad(\text{so } \int_0^2 h = 1).
  \]

- For an integer shift \(s\in\{-n+1,\dots ,n-1\}\) the overlap is  

  \[
  O_s(h)=dx\;\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+s}\bigr),
  \]

  where indices outside \([0,n-1]\) are treated as zero (exactly the definition used by
  `np.correlate` with `mode='full'`).  
- The quality to be minimised is  

  \[
  C_5(h)=\max_{s} O_s(h) .
  \]

  Equivalently, maximise the minimal autocorrelation
  \(\displaystyle R_s(h)=dx\sum_i h_i h_{i+s}\) because  
  \(O_s(h)=1-R_s(h)\).

### 2. Core computational primitives  

| Primitive | Description | Implementation hints |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Projection onto the feasible set** | Clamp to \([0,1]\) and then enforce the sum exactly \(\frac n2\). | Use the water‑filling / binary‑search method `project_to_sum_box`. |
| **Fast evaluation of all overlaps** | Compute the full correlation of **h** with its complement \(1-h\) by FFT and multiply by dx. | `O_s = dx * np.max(np.fft.ifft(...))` – O(n log n). |
| **Gradient of a single worst shift** | For a fixed shift \(s\) the sub‑gradient of \(O_s\) w.r.t. \(h\) is \(\displaystyle g_i = -(h_{i+s}+h_{i-s})\) (indices outside the interval are ignored). | Build the gradient with two slice operations; the constant “1’’ from the derivative of the linear term is irrelevant after projection. |

### 3. Two‑phase optimisation framework  

The algorithm consists of a **global stochastic search** that explores the highly non‑convex landscape, followed by a **deterministic local refinement** that squeezes the best candidate.

#### 3.1 Global stochastic search – Simulated Annealing on a feasible manifold
