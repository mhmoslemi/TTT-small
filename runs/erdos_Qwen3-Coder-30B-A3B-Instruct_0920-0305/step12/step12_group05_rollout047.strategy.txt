### 1.  Mathematical Reformulation

The objective is  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{k\in\mathbb R}\;\int_{0}^{2} h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx
        =1-\min_{k}\;A(k),\qquad 
A(k)=\int_{0}^{2}h(x)h(x+k)\,dx .
\]

Hence we have to **maximise the minimal autocorrelation** of \(h\) over all shifts while keeping  

* \(0\le h(x)\le1\)  (box constraints)  
* \(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}h(x)\,dx=1\)  (hyper‑plane constraint).

After discretisation with \(n\) points and step size \(\Delta =2/n\),

* \(h\in[0,1]^n\) ,  \(\mathbf 1^{\!\top}h = n/2\)  (because \(\sum h\,\Delta=1\)).
* \(A(k)\) becomes the discrete correlation
  \[
  A_\ell=\Delta\;\bigl(h\star h\bigr)_\ell=
  \Delta\sum_{i}h_i\,h_{i+\ell},
  \qquad -n+1\le\ell\le n-1 .
  \]

The true objective is  

\[
f(h)=1-\min_{\ell}A_\ell = \max_{\ell}\bigl(\Delta\,h\odot(1-h_{\!shift})\bigr)_\ell .
\]

The max makes the problem non‑smooth.  
We replace the max by a **smooth soft‑max** (log‑sum‑exp) with a temperature parameter \(\beta>0\):

\[
\tilde f_{\beta}(h)=\frac{\Delta}{\beta}\,
\log\!\Bigl(\sum_{\ell}\exp\bigl(\beta\;c_\ell(h)\bigr)\Bigr),
\qquad
c_\ell(h)=\sum_i h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+\ell}\bigr) .
\]

When \(\beta\to\infty\) the soft‑max converges to the exact max, while for modest \(\beta\) the objective is differentiable and its gradient can be expressed analytically.  This offers a **continuation (homotopy) strategy**:
start with a small \(\beta\) (smooth landscape), optimise, then increase \(\beta\) and repeat.

### 2.  Projection onto the Feasible Set

We need the orthogonal projection of a vector \(x\in\mathbb R^{n}\) onto  

\[
\mathcal{F}=\{h\mid 0\le h_i\le1,\; \mathbf 1^{\!\top}h=n/2\}.

A fast *water‑filling* algorithm (O(n log n)) does this:

1. Shift all components by a common scalar \(\lambda\): \(y_i=x_i+\lambda\).
2. Clip: \(\tilde y_i=\operatorname{clip}(y_i,0,1)\).
3. Choose \(\lambda\) such that \(\sum_i\tilde y_i=n/2\).  
   This is a monotone 1‑D root‑finding problem; a few bisection steps give machine‑precision accuracy.

The projector will be used after every gradient step and after any random perturbation.

### 4.  Gradient of the Smoothed Objective

Let  

\[
c = \operatorname{corr}(h,\,1-h) \in\mathbb R^{2n-1},
\qquad
p_\ell = \frac{\exp\bigl(\beta c_\ell\bigr)}{\sum_j\exp\bigl(\beta c_j\bigr)} .
\]

\(p\) are the soft‑max weights (they already sum to one).  
The derivative of the soft‑max objective w.r.t. \(h\) is

\[
\nabla_h \tilde f_{\beta}
 =\Delta\sum_{\ell}p_\ell\;\nabla_h c_\ell .
\]

For a fixed lag \(\ell\) the exact derivative is  

\[
\frac{\partial c_\ell}{\partial h_i}= 
\begin{cases}
1-h_{i+\ell} &\text{if }0\le i+\ell<n,\\[4pt]
0 &\text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
 - 
\begin{cases}
h_{i-\ell} &\text{if }0\le i-\ell<n,\\[4pt]
0 &\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Collecting all lags leads to a *convolution* expression:

\[
g \;:=\;\nabla_h \tilde f_{\beta}

   \bigl( p_{\text{rev}} * (1-h) \;-\; p_{\text{rev}} * h \bigr)_{[n-1:2n-1]} .
\]

Here \(p_{\text{rev}}\) is the reversal of the weight vector,
`*` denotes the linear (non‑circular) convolution, and the slice

Both convolutions are evaluated **via FFT** (zero‑padded length \(L=3n-2\)), giving an \(O(L\log L)\) operation.

