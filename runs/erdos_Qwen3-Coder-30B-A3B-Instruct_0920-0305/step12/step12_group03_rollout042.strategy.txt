The steps for incremental update: Suppose we swap positions i (0->1) and j (1->0). Let Δ_i = +1 (since we add a 1 at i) and Δ_j = -1 (remove a 1 at j). For each shift k, the product contributions are:

- New contribution for pair (i, i+k): previously, before swap, contributed h_i * h_{i+k} (maybe zero). After swapping, h_i new = 1, thus new product = 1 * h_{i+k}. So change = (1 - old_h_i) * h_{i+k} - old_h_i * h_{i+k} = (new_h_i - old_h_i) * h_{i+k} = Δ_i * h_{i+k}.

Similarly pair (i-k, i) where i-k >=0: contribution update = Δ_i * h_{i-k} (since product symmetric). Also for j, Δ_j = -1, contribution change = Δ_j * h_{j+k} + Δ_j * h_{j-k}.

Then C_k = total_f - S_k (where total_f constant). So delta_C_k = -delta_S_k.

### 2.  Simulated‑annealing on binary swaps with O(n) updates

A *swap* exchanges a `0` at index `i` with a `1` at index `j`.  
The total number of ones stays `n/2`, no projection is required.

**Incremental update of the correlation.**  
For a binary pattern, changing `h_i` by `Δ = ±1` modifies the autocorrelation
as follows (using the shift‑convention of `np.correlate`):

```
for each shift k with i+k in [0, n‑1]:
      ΔC_raw[ k ]   -= Δ * (1 – h[i+k])          # i appears as the first factor
for each shift k with i‑k in [0, n‑1]:
      ΔC_raw[ -k ]  -= Δ * h[i‑k]                # i appears as the second factor
```

Because `Δ = +1` when a zero becomes a one and `Δ = –1` when a one becomes a
zero, the two updates for the two swapped indices can be summed to obtain the
full delta vector `ΔC_raw` of length `2n‑1`.  Computing it requires at most
`2·(n‑1)` array accesses → **O(n)** operations.

* always accepted if `new_max < cur_max` (strict improvement);
* otherwise accepted with probability `exp( -(new_max‑cur_max) / T )`,
