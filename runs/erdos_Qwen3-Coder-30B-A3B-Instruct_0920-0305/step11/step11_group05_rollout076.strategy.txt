**Goal** – Produce a step‑function `h` on `[0,2]` (discretised into `n` equal pieces) satisfying  

* `0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1` for all `i`  
* `∑ h[i]·dx = 1`  (i.e. `∑ h[i] = n/2`)  

such that the **Erdős overlap constant**  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{k}\;\int_{0}^{2} h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx
\]

is as small as possible.  

---

### 1. Reformulation of the objective  

With the discretisation (`dx = 2/n`) the integral becomes  

\[
C_5(h)=dx\;\max_{s\in\{-n+1,\dots ,n-1\}}
\Bigl(S-\!\!\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i\,h_{i+s}\Bigr),\qquad
S=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i=\frac n2 .
\]

Because `dx·S = 1`, minimising `C_5` is **equivalent** to  

\[
\boxed{\max_{h}\;\;\underbrace{\min_{s}\;\sum_i h_i\,h_{i+s}}_{\displaystyle t(h)} }
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
0\le h_i\le1,\;\;\sum_i h_i=S .
\]

Thus we must **maximise the minimal autocorrelation** `t(h)`.  

---

### 2. Majorisation‑Minimisation via alternating linearisation  

The term `∑_i h_i h_{i+s}` is bilinear.  Introduce two copies of the vector,
`x` (the *optimising* copy) and `y` (the *frozen* copy).  For a *fixed* `y`,
each shift‐constraint becomes **linear** in `x`:

\[
\sum_i x_i\,y_{i+s}\;\ge\;t\qquad\forall s.
\]

If we could make `y = x`, the constraints would be exactly the original quadratic
ones.  Therefore we perform the following *outer* iteration:

1. **Fix** `y = h^{(k)}` (the current estimate).  
2. **Solve** the linear programme (LP)  

   \[
   \begin{aligned}
   \max_{x,t}\;&t\\
   \text{s.t. }&\sum_i x_i\,y_{i+s}\;\ge\;t\;\;(\forall s),\\
               &\sum_i x_i = S,\\
               &0\le x_i\le 1 .
   \end{aligned}
   \]

   The LP yields a new feasible vector `x^{(k+1)}` and a higher lower bound
   `t^{(k+1)}` on the minimal autocorrelation.  

3. **Update** `h^{(k+1)} ← x^{(k+1)}` and repeat until `t` stops improving.

Because each LP maximises `t` under a *linear* under‑approximation of the
bilinear constraints, `t` is **monotonically non‑decreasing**, and the true
objective `C_5 = 1 - dx·t` is monotically non‑increasing.  This is a genuine
majorisation‑minimisation scheme, fundamentally different from the
gradient‑based / simulated‑annealing loop used previously.

1. **Pre‑compute** the full correlation array of the current `h` using an FFT

2. **Iteration** – repeat while time remains:
