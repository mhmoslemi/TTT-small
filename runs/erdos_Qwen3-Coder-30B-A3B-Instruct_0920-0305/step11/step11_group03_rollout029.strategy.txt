### 1.  Core mathematical reformulation  

For a (discretised) step function  

\[
h=(h_0,\dots ,h_{n-1})\in[0,1]^n ,\qquad 
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i = \frac n2\;(=:M) ,
\]

define the **autocorrelation vector**

\[
r_k \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{n-1-k} h_i\,h_{i+k}\qquad (k=0,\dots ,n-1).
\]

With the step width  
\(\displaystyle \Delta x = \frac{2}{n}\)   
the overlap integral appearing in the definition of \(C_5\) is

\[
g_k(h)=\int_0^2 h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k\Delta x)\bigr)\,dx
      = 1 - \Delta x\,r_k .
\]

Hence  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_k g_k(h)=1-\Delta x\;\min_{k} r_k .
\]

*The optimisation problem* is therefore **equivalent** to  

\[
\boxed{\;\max_{h\in[0,1]^n,\;\sum h_i=M}\ \min_{k} r_k\;}
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\min C_5 = 1-\Delta x\;\max\min_k r_k .
\]

Thus we only have to **push the smallest entry of the autocorrelation vector up**.

### 2.  From the vector \(r\) to a convex description  

The autocorrelation vector \(r\) can be represented as the first row of a symmetric Toeplitz matrix  

\[
T = \operatorname{Toeplitz}(r_0,r_1,\dots ,r_{n-1})\in\mathbb{S}^n .
\]

For any real sequence \(h\) we have  

\[
T = hh^{\!\top} .
\]

Consequently  

*   \(T\succeq 0\) (positive semidefinite) – this is a **necessary** condition for a vector to be an autocorrelation of *some* real sequence.  
*   The **mass constraint** \(\bigl(\sum_i h_i\bigr)^2 = M^2\) can be written **linearly** in \(r\) (by expanding the double sum)

\[
\boxed{\;\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (n-k)\,r_k = M^2\;} .
\tag{mass}
\]

*   Because \(0\le h_i\le 1\) we also have the obvious bounds  

\[
0\le r_k \le \min\{M,\;n-k\}\qquad(k\ge0).
\tag{box}
\]

These constraints involve only the autocorrelation vector; they are completely **convex** (PSD + linear + box).

The only missing ingredient is the **rank‑one** condition \(T=hh^{\!\top}\).  
Removing it yields a *relaxation*: any feasible \(r\) of the relaxed problem is a **lower bound** for the true optimum, because any admissible \(h\) produces such an \(r\).  

Thus we obtain the following **semidefinite programme (SDP)**:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{maximize}\;&\ t \\[2mm]
\text{subject to}&\; r_k \;\ge\; t \qquad(k=0,\dots ,n-1)\\
                 &\; T=\operatorname{Toeplitz}(r_0,\dots ,r_{n-1})\;\succeq\;0\\
                 &\; \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (n-k)\,r_k = M^2\\
                 &\; 0\le r_k\le \min\{M,n-k\}\quad(k\ge0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{SDP}
\]

If the optimal value is \(t^\star\) then any feasible \(h\) must satisfy  

\[
\min_k r_k \le t^\star .
\]

Consequently  

\[
C_5^{\text{best}}\;\ge\;1-\Delta x\;t^\star .
\]

Conversely, a feasible \(r\) with a large \(t\) can be **rounded** to an actual step function, giving a concrete candidate \(h\) that almost achieves that bound.

### 5.  Rounding the relaxed solution to a concrete step function  

The SDP gives us an optimal autocorrelation vector `r̂` and a lower bound `t̂`.  
We now need a concrete `h` whose autocorrelation is *close* to `r̂`.  
The standard way is **spectral factorisation**:

1. **Discrete Fourier transform** of `r̂`.  
   Because `r̂` is the first row of a Toeplitz PSD matrix, its DFT `S = FFT(r̂)` is a **non‑negative real vector** (the power spectrum).  
2. Take the **square root** element‑wise: `A = sqrt(S)`.  
   `A` corresponds to the magnitudes of the Fourier coefficients of a *candidate* real signal `h̃`.  
3. Choose a **phase vector** `φ`.  
   * The simplest viable choice is `φ = 0` (all phases zero). This yields a *symmetric* real signal, automatically non‑negative at all points because the inverse FFT of a non‑negative even spectrum is a **positive definite** kernel.  
   * If the resulting signal violates the box constraints (some entries > 1 or < 0), we will later project it (see § 6).  
4. Compute the inverse FFT:  
   `h̃ = ifft( A * exp(1j·φ) ).real`.  
   By construction `h̃` satisfies the autocorrelation within numerical tolerance: `Toeplitz(h̃) ≈ T̂`.  

Because the phase is a free degree of freedom, we may also **randomise the phase** a few times and keep the best `h̃` (measured by the exact overlap objective). This is cheap (a handful of FFTs) and often yields a signal with smaller peaks, helping the forthcoming projection.

**Projection algorithm (exact, O(n log n))**

1. **Clip** `h̃` to the box `[0,1]` obtaining `ĥ`.  
2. Compute the current sum `s = ∑ ĥ_i`.

4. Otherwise find a scalar `λ` such that

5. Return the final projected vector `h_proj`.

The output `h_proj` is guaranteed to satisfy all box and mass constraints, and it remains **very close** to the spectral reconstruction, so its autocorrelation stays high.

Even if the rounding is not perfectly tight, the subsequent gradient polishing typically moves the solution **inside the feasible region** and often **breaks the previous record** (e.g. brings `C₅` from ≈ 0.380975 to ≤ 0.3805).

### 11.  Anticipated failure modes & mitigations  

| Possible issue | Why it can happen | Mitigation |
|----------------|-------------------|------------|
| SDP does not converge within allocated time (large `n`). | Interior‑point methods scale poorly with PSD size. | Start with `n=80`; only enlarge to `n=100` if the first solve finishes early. |
| Obtained spectrum leads to a signal with many entries > 1. | The PSD constraint does not enforce the pointwise bound `h_i≤1`. | The projection step always brings the signal back into `[0,1]`; the loss of autocorrelation is modest because projection is a small perturbation (the water‑filling move is a simple shift). |
| Rounded `h` after projection yields a *worse* `C₅` than the SDP bound (gap). | Rounding may destroy the delicate balance of autocorrelations. | Use **multiple random phase vectors** (≥ 4) and keep the best; also perform the short projected‑gradient polishing that can recover lost autocorrelation. |
| Numerical round‑off makes the Toeplitz matrix slightly non‑PSD, causing the SDP solver to report infeasibility. | Finite‑precision DFT of `r̂`. | Add a tiny PSD regularisation (`T + 1e‑9·I ⪰ 0`) inside the SDP; this does not affect the optimum significantly. |
| Time runs out before phase 5 finishes. | Gradient polishing may be too long. | The refinement loop monitors elapsed time and truncates after the remaining budget falls below a 10‑second safety margin. |

### 13.  Summary of the plan  

1. **Formulate** the problem as a convex SDP in the autocorrelation variables.
