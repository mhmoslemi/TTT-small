**Overview**  
The Erdős‑5 problem is a constrained min‑max optimisation.  With a uniform discretisation the objective is  

\[
F(h)=\max_{k\in\{-n+1,\dots ,n-1\}} c_k(h),\qquad  
c_k(h)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+k}\bigr) ,
\]

where out‑of‑range indices are interpreted as zero, the step size is \(dx=2/n\) and the constraints are  

* box: \(0\le h_i\le 1\),  
* linear equality: \(\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i = n/2\)  (so that \(\int_0^2 h =1\)).  

The constant to be reported is \(C_5 = dx\;\max_k c_k(h)\).  

The new strategy uses **analytic gradients**, a **smooth surrogate for the max**, **accelerated projected gradient descent**, a **continuation on the smoothing parameter**, a **pure sub‑gradient polishing phase**, and **multiple intelligently chosen starts**.  All components are inexpensive (FFT‑based correlation, vectorised slice operations) and respect the strict runtime limit.
