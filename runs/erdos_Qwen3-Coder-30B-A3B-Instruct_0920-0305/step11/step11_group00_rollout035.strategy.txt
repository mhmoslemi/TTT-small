### 1. Convex SDP formulation (relaxation of the exact problem)

Introduce a matrix variable  

\[
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},\qquad X\succeq0,
\]

and a scalar variable \(\tau\ge0\) that will represent the (continuous) upper bound on \(C_5\).

* **Link between \(X\) and \(h\)**  
  \[
  \operatorname{diag}(X)=h .
  \]  
  This forces every diagonal entry to be the corresponding component of the step function.

* **Box and mass constraints**  
  \[
  0\le h_i\le1,\qquad \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i=\frac{n}{2}.
  \]

* **Overlap constraints**  
  For a shift \(k\in\{-n+1,\dots ,n-1\}\) the discrete overlap is  

\[
\operatorname{ov}_k(h)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1-k}h_i\,h_{i+k}
   =\sum_{i,j}X_{ij}\,M^{(k)}_{ij},
\]

where \(M^{(k)}\) is the \((0,1)\)-mask that has a 1 on the *k‑th* diagonal
(\(M^{(k)}_{ij}=1\) iff \(j=i+k\) and both indices lie in \(\{0,\dots ,n-1\}\)).  

The objective function satisfies  
\(c_k(h)=\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}-\operatorname{ov}_k(h)\).  
Hence requiring \(C_5\le\tau\) is equivalent to

\[
\operatorname{ov}_k(h)\;\ge\;\frac{n}{2}-\frac{\tau}{dx}
   \;=\;\frac{n}{2}\bigl(1-\tau\bigr),\qquad\forall k .
\]

All overlap constraints are linear in the entries of \(X\).

* **SDP problem**  

\[
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\;h,X,\tau}&\;\;\tau \\
\text{s.t. }&
\;X\succeq0,\;\;
\operatorname{diag}(X)=h,\\
&0\le h\le 1,\;
\sum_i h_i =\frac{n}{2},\\
&\; \operatorname{trace}\!\bigl(M^{(k)}X\bigr)
   \;\ge\;\frac{n}{2}\bigl(1-\tau\bigr),
   \qquad k=-n+1,\dots ,n-1,\\
&\tau\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

All constraints are affine in \((h,X,\tau)\); together with the PSD cone this is a **convex semidefinite programme**.

### 3. Extracting a feasible step function from the relaxed solution

2. **Leading‑eigenvector rounding** –

### 4. Deterministic polishing – projected gradient descent on the true max

The seed already satisfies all linear constraints, so we now directly minimise the **true** max‑overlap objective.

#### 4.1. Analytic sub‑gradient of the max‑overlap

For a fixed shift \(k\) the correlation term is  

\[
c_k(h)=\sum_i h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+k}\bigr) .
\]

Its gradient (with respect to the vector \(h\)) is  

\[
\bigl[\nabla c_k(h)\bigr]_j = 
\bigl(1-h_{j+k}\bigr)-h_{j-k},
\]

where out‑of‑range indices are interpreted as zero.  
If several shifts attain the current maximum, the sub‑gradient of the max is the **average** of the individual gradients.

```
    else:
        step ← step*0.5                  # back‑track
```

*The projection* is performed by solving for a scalar \(\lambda\) such that  

\[
\sum_i\operatorname{clip}\bigl(h_i+\lambda,0,1\bigr)=\frac{n}{2},
\]

which is a monotone one‑dimensional problem solved by a 30‑iteration binary search.

#### 4.3. Escape from plateaus

Every 250 iterations (or whenever the objective has not improved for 200 steps) perform a tiny **swap‑move**:

* pick two distinct indices \(i,j\) uniformly at random,
* transfer a small amount \(\delta\in[-0.02,0.02]\) from \(i\) to \(j\) (clipping to keep both in \([0,1]\)),
* re‑project,
* accept the move only if the true \(C_5\) decreases.

This simple stochastic perturbation helps the deterministic walk escape shallow local minima without sacrificing the overall runtime budget.

### 5. Multi‑start orchestration

To increase the chance of reaching the global‑ish optimum within the 600 s limit:

1. **Two discretisation choices** – first run the whole pipeline with \(n=120\); if time permits, repeat with \(n=140\) (finer grid).
2. **Three distinct seeds** –  
   * the SDP‑derived seed (best of the three rounding methods),  
   * the classic “triangular” U‑shape (linear drop from 1 at the ends to 0 at the centre, exactly satisfying the mass constraint),  
   * a purely random feasible vector (uniform samples projected onto the feasible set).  
   Each seed is polished independently; the best final bound across all runs is reported.

The outer loop checks the remaining wall‑clock time after each seed; if the budget is nearing exhaustion (e.g. less than 5 s left) the algorithm aborts further starts and returns the best solution found so far.

### 6. Numerical choices & safeguards

| Issue | Remedy |
|-------|--------|
| **Overflow in exponentials (if a later smooth surrogate is tried)** | Subtract the maximum before `np.exp`. |
| **Projection binary‑search fails to converge** | Initialise search interval with \(\lambda_{low}=-\max(x)-1\) and \(\lambda_{high}=1-\min(x)+1\); 30 iterations give machine‑epsilon accuracy. |
| **SDP solver returns a matrix with tiny negative eigenvalues** | Force PSD by eigen‑decomposition and replace negative eigenvalues by zero before rounding. |
| **Time‑budget overflow** | Every outer loop checks `time.time() - t_start`; all inner loops break early if the remaining time falls below a safety margin (≈5 % of the total budget). |
| **Rounding produces a vector violating the box** | The projection step restores feasibility before any evaluation. |
| **Poor quality of the SDP solution (tau_opt ≈ 0.38 but final C5 still > 0.381)** | Apply the deterministic polishing stage, which reliably pushes the true max lower than the SDP lower bound by exploiting the exact gradient information. |

### 8. Implementation outline (no code, just structure)

1. **Utility functions**  
   * `proj_sum_box(v, target)` – λ‑shift binary search.  
   * `corr_full(h)` – FFT‑based correlation (returns raw integer‑shift vector).  
   * `grad_shift(h, k)` – vectorised implementation of \((1-h_{j+k})-h_{j-k}\).  
   * `max_overlap(h)` – `dx * np.max(corr_full(h))`.

2. **SDP builder** (`build_sdp(n)`) – pre‑computes the constant masks `M_k`, creates CVXPY variables, sets constraints, returns a `Problem` object.

3. **Solver wrapper** (`solve_sdp(problem)`) – solves with `SCS`/`ECOS`, extracts `h_sdp, X_sdp, tau_opt`.

4. **Rounding module** (`round_from_sdp(h_sdp, X_sdp)`) – implements the three rounding strategies, projects each candidate, evaluates `C_5`, returns the best candidate.

5. **Polisher** (`polish(h_start, n, dx, budget_remaining)`) – runs the sub‑gradient Nesterov loop with swap‑moves, stops when the local time budget expires, returns polished `h` and its bound.

6. **Main driver (`run`)** – orchestrates the discretisation choices, seeds, time bookkeeping, and finally returns `(h.tolist(), float(best_C5), n)`.

### 9. Failure‑mode checklist

| Failure | Detection | Mitigation |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| SDP infeasible for all τ (e.g. due to too small *n*) | Solver status `infeasible` | Reduce *n* (fallback to 120) or relax the overlap RHS slightly (increase τ upper bound). |
| Solver stalls (excessive iterations) | `prob.status` not `optimal` after max_iters | Increase `max_iters`, switch to the alternative solver (`ECOS`). |
| Rank‑1 extraction yields many negatives | Post‑projection C5 > previous best | Increase number of Gaussian draws, or fall back to the diagonal seed. |
| Polishing does not improve after many iterations | No decrease in C5 for 500 consecutive steps | Restart polishing from a fresh random feasible vector; or switch to the smooth‑surrogate APG for a few iterations before returning to sub‑gradient mode. |
| Overall runtime > budget | `time.time()` check in outer loop | Immediately break and return the best solution obtained so far. |
