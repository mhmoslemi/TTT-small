**1. Reformulate the objective**

The quantity to minimise is  

\[
C_{5}(h)=\max_{k}\int_{0}^{2}h(x)\,[1-h(x+k)]\,dx .
\]

For a shift \(k\ge0\) the integrand vanishes outside the interval
\([0,2-k]\); therefore  

\[
C_{5}(k)=\int_{0}^{2}h(x)\,dx-
        \int_{0}^{2-k}h(x)h(x+k)\,dx 
      =1-\underbrace{A(k)}_{\displaystyle 
        A(k)=\int_{0}^{2-k}h(x)h(x+k)\,dx}.
\]

Thus  

\[
C_{5}(h)=1-\min_{k}A(k) .
\]

The problem is **equivalent** to maximising the *minimum* of the auto‑correlation
\(A(k)\) over all admissible shifts.  This max‑min structure is the key to a
more powerful algorithm.

**2. Discretise once and for all**

Choose a power‑of‑two grid size \(n\) (e.g. \(n=128\)).

\Delta x =\frac{2}{n},\qquad 
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\dots ,h_{n-1})\in[0,1]^{n},

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_{i}= \frac{n}{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 
\int_{0}^{2}h(x)dx=1 .

Zero‑padding outside \([0,2]\) implements the non‑periodic shift.

**3. Fast evaluation of the auto‑correlation**

For any \(\mathbf{h}\) define  

\[
\mathbf{a}= \mathbf{h}, \qquad \mathbf{b}= \mathbf{h},
\]

and compute the *full* discrete correlation  

\[
c_{k}= \sum_{i} a_{i}\,b_{i+k},
\qquad k=-(n-1),\dots ,(n-1).
\]

Because \(A(k)=c_{k}\,\Delta x\) for \(k\ge0\) (and symmetrically for
\(k<0\)), we obtain all values of the objective by a single FFT:

* reverse one of the vectors,
* zero‑pad both to length \(L=2n-1\),
* multiply the FFTs, and
* inverse‑FFT the product.

The resulting real array `c` has length \(L\); the minimum auto‑correlation is
\(\min_{k}c_{k}\).  Consequently  

\[
C_{5}(\mathbf{h}) = 1 - \Delta x\;\min_{k}c_{k}.
\]

All evaluations are \(O(n\log n)\) and cheap for the chosen \(n\).

\[
\Phi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{h}) \le \min_{k}c_{k}\le
\Phi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{h})+\frac{\log L}{\alpha},

F_{\alpha}(\mathbf{h}) = 1 - \Delta x\,\Phi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{h}).

**5. Gradient of the smooth surrogate**

Let  

\[

          {\sum_{j}\exp\!\bigl(-\alpha c_{j}\bigr)} .
\]

Then  

\[
\nabla_{\!h_i}\Phi_{\alpha}
    = \sum_{k} p_{k}\,\frac{\partial c_{k}}{\partial h_i}.

The derivative of a single correlation entry is elementary:

\[
