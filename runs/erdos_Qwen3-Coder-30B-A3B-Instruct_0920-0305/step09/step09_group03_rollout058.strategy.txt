`h = (h₀,…,h_{n‑1}) ∈ [0,1]^n` and enforce the integral constraint  

\[
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i = \frac{n}{2}\; .

Define the discrete overlap vector  

\[
f_k(h)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+k}\bigr),
\qquad k=-(n-1),\dots,n-1
\]

(where indices outside `0…n‑1` are taken as zero).  
The quantity to be minimised is  

\[
C_5(h)=dx\;\max_k f_k(h) .

Because `dx` is a constant factor, we minimise  

\[
F(h)=\max_k f_k(h).
\]

`f_k` is linear in `h` except for the bilinear term `h_i h_{i+k}`; the maximum makes the problem non‑convex.

**Smooth‑max surrogate**

Replace the hard maximum by the Log‑Sum‑Exp (soft‑max)

\[
T_{\alpha}(h)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\!\Bigl(\sum_{k} e^{\alpha f_k(h)}\Bigr),
\]

with a temperature parameter `α>0`.  
- For `α→0`, `Tα` is the average of the `f_k`.  
- For `α→∞`, `Tα → max_k f_k`.  

Optimising `Tα` for a sequence of increasing `α` (continuation) drives the solution toward a true minimiser of the original max while keeping the landscape smooth early on.

**Gradient of the surrogate**

The derivative of a single `f_k` w.r.t. `h_j` is  

\[
\frac{∂f_k}{∂h_j}=1-\bigl(h_{j+k}+h_{j-k}\bigr),
\]

(with zero padding for out‑of‑range indices).  
With the soft‑max weights  

\[
w_k = \frac{e^{\alpha f_k}}{\sum_{\ell} e^{\alpha f_{\ell}}}

the gradient of `Tα` is  

\[
∇T_{\alpha}(h)_j = 1-\sum_k w_k\bigl(h_{j+k}+h_{j-k}\bigr)
