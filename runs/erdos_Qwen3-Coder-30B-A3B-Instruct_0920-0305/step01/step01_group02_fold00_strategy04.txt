### 2. Exact projection onto the capped simplex

For any real vector \(\mathbf{y}\) we need the Euclidean projection onto  

\[
\mathcal S\:=\Bigl\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^N\;\big|\;\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}x_i =\tfrac{N}{2}\Bigr\}.
\]

The projection is the solution of  

\[
\min_{\mathbf{x}}\;\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|_2^2 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad 
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal S.
\]

Because the feasible set is a hyperplane intersected with a box, the solution can be expressed as  

\[
x_i = \operatorname{clip}\bigl(y_i-\lambda,\;0,\;1\bigr), \qquad i=0,\dots ,N-1,
\]

where \(\lambda\) is a scalar chosen so that \(\sum_i x_i = N/2\).  
The function  

\[
\phi(\lambda)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\operatorname{clip}\bigl(y_i-\lambda,\;0,\;1\bigr)-\frac{N}{2}
\]

is continuous, strictly decreasing, and satisfies \(\phi(-\infty)=N-\frac{N}{2}>0\) and \(\phi(+\infty)=-\frac{N}{2}<0\).  
Hence a simple **bisection** on \(\lambda\) converges to the unique root.  
A safe initial interval is \([\min_i y_i-1,\;\max_i y_i]\); 50 bisection steps give machine‑precision accuracy.

This projection is used after each gradient step, guaranteeing feasibility without the “scale‑and‑clip’’ shortcut that can violate bounds.

### 3. Sub‑gradient of the max‑overlap objective

Define the *overlap vector* (full correlation)  

\[
\mathbf{o} = \Delta x\; \mathbf{c} \quad\text{with}\quad
c_k = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} h_i \bigl(1-h_{i+k}\bigr),\;k\in\{-N+1,\dots,N-1\},
\]

where out‑of‑range indices are treated as zero.

*The objective* is \(f(\mathbf{h}) = \max_k o_k\).

Let \(k^\star\) be an index attaining the maximum (if several attain it, pick any).  
Because each \(c_k\) is affine in \(\mathbf{h}\) plus a bilinear term, its partial derivatives are easy:

For a *positive* shift \(k\ge 0\),

\[
\frac{\partial c_k}{\partial h_j}= 
\begin{cases}
  1 - h_{j+k}, & 0\le j\le N-1-k,\\[4pt]
  -h_{j-k},    & k\le j\le N-1,\\[4pt]
  0,           & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

For a *negative* shift \(k<0\) (write \(k=-\ell\) with \(\ell>0\)) the formula swaps the roles of the two indices:

\[
\frac{\partial c_k}{\partial h_j}= 
\begin{cases}
  -h_{j+\ell}, & 0\le j\le N-1-\ell,\\[4pt]
  1 - h_{j-\ell}, & \ell\le j\le N-1,\\[4pt]
  0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Multiplying by \(\Delta x\) yields \(\partial o_{k^\star}/\partial h_j\).  
Because \(f(\mathbf{h}) = o_{k^\star}\) at the current point, any sub‑gradient of \(f\) is exactly the gradient of the active shift \(k^\star\).  
Thus a **sub‑gradient step** is

\[
\mathbf{h}^{\;new}= \Pi_{\mathcal S}\!\bigl(\mathbf{h} - \eta\,\nabla o_{k^\star}\bigr),
\]
