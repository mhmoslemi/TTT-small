\[

* box: `0 ≤ h_i ≤ 1` for every grid point `i`;
* mass: `∑_i h_i·dx = 1`   (equivalently `∑_i h_i = n/2` for a grid of `n` points).

### 1. Re‑formulation

Because `∑_i h_i` is constant,  
\[
C_{5}=1-\min_{s}R(s),\qquad  
R(s)=\sum_{i}h_i\,h_{i+s}\,dx
\]
where `s` indexes the discrete shift (zero‑padding is used automatically by `np.correlate`).  
Thus minimising `C5` ⇔ **maximising** the worst auto‑correlation `min_s R(s)`.  The raw objective is a *max* over a set of linear forms in the *quadratic* terms `h_i h_{i+s}` – a non‑smooth, non‑convex function.

---

### 2. Smooth approximation of the `min`  

Introduce a temperature parameter `λ>0` and replace the exact minimum by a **log‑sum‑exp soft‑minimum**:
\[
\Phi_{\lambda}(h)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\!\Bigl(\sum_{s}e^{-\lambda R(s)}\Bigr) .
\]
For any `λ`, `Φ_λ` is a smooth, convex function of the vector of correlations `R(s)`.  The original loss is
\[
L(h)=1-\min_{s}R(s)=1-\bigl(-\lambda\Phi_{\lambda}(h)\bigr),
\]
hence minimising `L` is equivalent to **minimising** `Φ_λ` (the `1` is a constant).  As `λ→∞`, `Φ_λ` converges to the exact minimum, so we can start with a small `λ` (smooth landscape) and raise it gradually (annealing) to focus on the worst shift.

---

### 3. Analytic gradient of the surrogate

Let  
\[
w_s=\frac{e^{-\lambda R(s)}}{\sum_{t}e^{-\lambda R(t)}}\quad (w_s\ge 0,\;\sum_sw_s=1).
\]
Then
\[
\frac{\partial\Phi_{\lambda}}{\partial h_j}
   =-\sum_{s} w_s\;\Bigl(h_{j+s}+h_{j-s}\Bigr) .
\]
All out‑of‑range indices are treated as zero (zero padding).  Hence the **descent direction for `Φ_λ`** is
\[
g_j=\sum_{s} w_s\bigl(h_{j+s}+h_{j-s}\bigr) .
\]

The vector `g` can be computed in \(O(n^2)\) by simple slice operations, or in \(O(n\log n)\) by a single convolution:
\[
g = \bigl(w\;+\;w^{\mathrm{rev}}\bigr) * h ,
\]
where `*` is the (full) convolution and `w^{rev}_s = w_{-s}`.  With `n≈200` the direct slice implementation is fast enough and easier to verify.

---

### 4. Projection onto the feasible set

After a gradient step `h ← h + α g` we must restore feasibility:

* **Box** – each component must stay in `[0,1]`.
* **Mass** – `∑ h_i = n/2`.

The Euclidean projection onto the intersection of a hyperplane and a box is a **water‑filling** operation: there exists a scalar `τ` such that
\[
h_i^{\text{proj}} = \min\bigl(1,\;\max\bigl(0,\;h_i^{\text{raw}}+\tau\bigr)\bigr),
\]
and the chosen `τ` makes the sum equal to `n/2`.  `τ` can be found by a monotone binary search (≈ 30 iterations) because the sum of clamped affine functions is monotone in `τ`.  This projection is exact, cheap, and respects both constraints simultaneously.

### 5. Annealed projected gradient algorithm

1. Compute the full correlation `R = np.correlate(h, h, "full")`.
2. Form the soft‑weights `w` via a numerically stable log‑sum‑exp (subtract the maximal `R` before exponentiation).
3. Build the gradient direction `g` (using the slice‑loop described above).
4. Perform a back‑tracking line search on `α`:

5. Update `h ← h'`.
