**1.  High‑level picture**  
The quantity to minimise is  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{k\in[-2,2]}\int_0^2 h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx .
\]

Because \(\int_0^2 h(x)\,dx = 1\), we can rewrite  

\[
C_5(h)=1-\min_{k}\int_0^2 h(x)h(x+k)\,dx .
\]

Thus the problem is a *max‑min* problem: maximise the smallest auto‑correlation of \(h\).  
The discrete version on a uniform grid of \(n\) points has variables  

\[
h_i\in[0,1],\qquad i=0,\dots,n-1,
\]

with the linear equality  

\[
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i = S:=\frac{n}{2}\quad\left(dx=\frac{2}{n}\right).
\]

The direct objective \(\max_s C_s\) is non‑differentiable, which made the earlier SLSQP‑restart approach get stuck.

**2.  Smooth surrogate for the max**

For each integer shift \(s\in\{-n+1,\dots,n-1\}\) define the (unscaled) correlation  

\[
c_s(h)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+s}\bigr),
\]

where out‑of‑range indices are treated as zero (the same convention used by `np.correlate`).  
The true objective is \(\max_s c_s\) (scaled later by \(dx\)).

Introduce the *log‑sum‑exp* (soft‑max) surrogate  

\[
F_{\alpha}(h)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\Bigl(\sum_{s}e^{\alpha\,c_s(h)}\Bigr),\qquad \alpha>0.
\tag{S1}
\]

* For \(\alpha\to0\) the surrogate reduces to the average of the \(c_s\) and is very smooth.  
* As \(\alpha\) grows the surrogate approaches the true max.  
* The surrogate is differentiable for any finite \(\alpha\), which gives us a usable gradient.

**3.  Analytic gradient of the surrogate**

Let  

\[
w_s=\frac{e^{\alpha c_s}}{\sum_{t}e^{\alpha c_t}}\quad\bigl(\sum_s w_s=1\bigr)
\tag{S2}
\]

be the softmax weights.  Differentiating (S1) gives  

\[
\nabla_h F_{\alpha}= \sum_s w_s\,\nabla_h c_s .
\tag{S3}
\]

From the definition of \(c_s\),

\[
c_s=\underbrace{\sum_i h_i}_{=S}\;-\;\sum_i h_i h_{i+s},
\]

so for any component \(i\)

\[
\frac{\partial c_s}{\partial h_i}=1-\bigl(h_{i+s}+h_{i-s}\bigr),
\tag{S4}
\]

with the convention \(h_j=0\) when \(j\notin[0,n-1]\).  Substituting (S4) into (S3) yields the compact gradient formula  

\[
\bigl(\nabla_h F_{\alpha}\bigr)_i 
= 1-\sum_{s} w_s\bigl(h_{i+s}+h_{i-s}\bigr).
\tag{S5}
\]

Thus the gradient is a *weighted sum of forward and backward shifted copies of* \(h\), plus a constant vector of ones.

**4.  Exact projection onto the feasible set (capped simplex)**

All iterates must satisfy  

\[
0\le h_i\le1,\qquad \sum_i h_i=S .
\]

The Euclidean projection of an arbitrary vector \(y\) onto this set is the solution of  

\[
\min_{x}\|x-y\|_2^2\quad\text{s.t.}\quad 0\le x_i\le1,\; \sum_i x_i=S .
\tag{P1}
\]

The solution is obtained by a single scalar threshold \(\lambda\):

\[
x_i = \operatorname{clip}(y_i-\lambda,\,0,\,1),\qquad
\text{choose }\lambda\text{ so that }\sum_i x_i=S .
\tag{P2}
\]

Because the left‑hand side of (P2) is a monotone decreasing function of \(\lambda\), a simple bisection (≈ 50 iterations) finds \(\lambda\) to machine precision.  This projection is **exact**, cheap (\(O(n\log\varepsilon^{-1})\)), and respects both the box constraints and the equality in one shot.
