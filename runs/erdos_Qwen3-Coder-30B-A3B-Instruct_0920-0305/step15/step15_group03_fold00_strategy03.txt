Instead of searching directly in the huge space of pointwise values (DE, sub‑gradient, MILP), we treat the quadratic terms that create the overlap as variables of a *semidefinite program* (SDP).  
The SDP gives a provable lower bound on the optimal value for a chosen discretisation, and a feasible vector is obtained afterwards by a deterministic rounding‑and‑polishing stage. In the exact problem we would have `T = h hᵀ` (rank‑1).  In the relaxation we **drop the rank‑1 requirement** and keep only the *moment* constraint

`M ⪰ 0` guarantees the existence of a (possibly high‑rank) factorisation consistent with the linear relations between `h` and `T`.

### 3. Linear representation of the overlap constraints  

For a shift `k ∈ {0,…,n‑1}` the discrete overlap (before multiplying by `dx`) is  

```
F_k = Σ_i h_i·(1 – h_{i+k}) = Σ_i h_i – Σ_i h_i·h_{i+k}
                           = n/2 – Σ_{i=0}^{n‑1‑k} T_{i,i+k} .
```

Because the first term `Σ_i h_i` equals `n/2` by the integral constraint, each `F_k` is an *affine* function of the entries of `T`.  

The required objective is `C₅ = max_k F_k`.  Introduce the scalar variable `t` and enforce  

```
t ≥ F_k   for every shift k .
```

Written in terms of `T` this becomes a family of linear inequalities  

```
t ≥ n/2 – Σ_{i=0}^{n‑1‑k} T_{i,i+k} ,   k = 0,…,n‑1 .
```

### 4. Full SDP model  

Minimise `t` subject to  

1. **Box constraints**  
   `0 ≤ h_i ≤ 1`  (elementwise)

2. **Sum constraint**  
   `Σ_i h_i = n/2`.

3. **Moment PSD constraint**  
   `[[1, hᵀ]; [h, T]] ⪰ 0`.

4. **Overlap upper‑bound constraints**  
   For every `k`  
   `t ≥ n/2 – Σ_{i=0}^{n‑1‑k} T_{i,i+k}`.

All constraints are linear or convex, so the model is a *semidefinite program*.
