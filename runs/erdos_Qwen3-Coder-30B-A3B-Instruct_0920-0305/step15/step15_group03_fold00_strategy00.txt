### 1.  Reformulation & useful formulas  

The quantity to be minimised is  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{k\in\{0,\dots ,n-1\}} 
F_k(h),\qquad 
F_k(h)=\int_0^2 h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx .
\]

With a uniform grid of `n` points (`dx=2/n`)  

\[
F_k(h)=dx\;\sum_{i=0}^{n-1-k} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+k}\bigr) .
\]

Because \(\int_0^2 h=1\) is forced,  

\[
F_k(h)=1-dx\;\sum_{i=0}^{n-1-k} h_i h_{i+k}.
\]

Hence minimising the **maximum** of the \(F_k\)’s is the same as **maximising the minimum** of the autocorrelations
\(\displaystyle A_k(h)=dx\!\sum_{i}h_i h_{i+k}\).

For a fixed shift \(k\) the gradient of \(F_k\) (including the factor `dx`) is the vector  

\[
\bigl(\nabla F_k\bigr)_j=
\begin{cases}
(1-h_{j+k})-h_{j-k}, & 0\le j-k\;\text{and}\;j+k<n\\[4pt]
1-h_{j+k},            & j-k<0\;\text{and}\;j+k<n\\[4pt]
-\,h_{j-k},           & 0\le j-k\;\text{and}\;j+k\ge n\\[4pt]
0,                    & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\!\times dx .
\]

In a compact implementation the two terms can be added by simple slice operations:

```
grad[:n-k] += a[k:]          # a = 1‑h   → (1‑h_{j+k})
grad[k:]   -= b[:n-k]        # b = h     → -h_{j‑k}
```

### 2.  Handling the “max” – a smooth surrogate  

The max operator is non‑differentiable.  Use the **log‑sum‑exp** smooth
approximation

\[
\widetilde C_5(h;\alpha)=\frac1\alpha\log\Bigl(\sum_{k}e^{\alpha F_k(h)}\Bigr)
      =\max_kF_k(h)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\!\Bigl(\sum_k e^{\alpha(F_k-\max F)}\Bigr)
\]

For any \(\alpha>0\) the gradient is a **convex combination** of the
individual gradients:

\[
\nabla\widetilde C_5
    =\sum_{k} w_k(\alpha)\,\nabla F_k, \qquad
w_k(\alpha)=\frac{e^{\alpha F_k}}{\sum_j e^{\alpha F_j}} .
\]

Thus a single weighted sum of the slice‑gradients gives the descent direction.
Start with a modest \(\alpha\) (smooth landscape) and increase it
gradually so that the algorithm finally focuses on the true maximum.

### 3.  Projection onto the feasible set  

Feasibility =  

* box constraints: \(0\le h_i\le 1\)  
* linear equality: \(\sum_i h_i = n/2\).

The Euclidean projection onto the intersection of a box and a hyperplane
has a closed form:

\[
x_i=\operatorname{clip}\bigl(v_i-\lambda,0,1\bigr),\qquad
\sum_i x_i = n/2 .
\]

The scalar \(\lambda\) is monotone decreasing in the left‑hand side,
so a **bisection** (≈ 60 iterations) finds it to machine precision.
The routine `project(v, target)` therefore:
1. Initialise `lo=-max(v)-1`, `hi=max(v)+1` (ensures the sum is respectively > target and < target).  
2. Repeat: `lam=(lo+hi)/2`; `x=clip(v‑lam,0,1)`; adjust `lo/hi` depending on the sign of `x.sum()-target`.  
3. Return the final `x`.
