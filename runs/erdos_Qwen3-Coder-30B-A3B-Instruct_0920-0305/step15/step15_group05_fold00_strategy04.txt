**1. Problem Re‑statement and Discretisation**  
- We work with a uniform grid of `n` points on `[0,2]`, spacing `Δ = 2/n`.  
- The decision vector is `h = (h₀,…,h_{n‑1})ᵀ` with `0 ≤ h_i ≤ 1` and `∑ h_i = n/2` (so that the integral of `h` equals 1).  
- For an integer shift `k∈{-(n‑1),…,n‑1}` the overlap term is  

\[
F_k(h)=\Delta\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+k}\bigr)
\qquad (h_{i+k}=0\; \text{if }i+k\notin[0,n-1]).
\]

The quantity we must minimise is  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_k F_k(h) 
      = \Delta\Bigl(\frac{n}{2} - \min_k O_k(h)\Bigr),
\qquad  
O_k(h)=\sum_{i}h_i h_{i+k}
\]

i.e. we want to maximise the **minimal autocorrelation** `min_k O_k(h)`.

**2. Smooth Min‑Approximation (log‑sum‑exp)**  
The function `min_k O_k` is non‑smooth and causes optimisation difficulties.  
Replace it by a differentiable surrogate:

\[
\operatorname{SmoothMin}_\beta (O) 
    = -\frac{1}{\beta}\log\!\Bigl(\sum_{k} e^{-\beta O_k}\Bigr),
\]

where `β>0`. As `β→∞` the surrogate converges to the true minimum, while for small `β` it is a gentle average that yields a smooth landscape.

Define the *smooth objective*  

\[
\Phi_\beta (h)=\frac{n}{2}-\operatorname{SmoothMin}_\beta\!\bigl(O_k(h)\bigr) .
\]

Since `C₅ = Δ·max_k F_k = Δ·Φ_{∞}(h)`, minimising `Φ_β` for a sequence of increasing `β` drives the solution towards the true optimum.

**3. Gradient of the Smooth Objective**

1. Zero‑pad `h` to length `L = 2n‑1` (or the next power of two).

3. Obtain `O = IFFT( H·conj(H) ).real` – the linear autocorrelation (entries correspond to shifts `-(n‑1)…(n‑1)`).  

For a given `β` the **weight** of each shift is  

\[
w_k = \frac{e^{-\beta O_k}}{\sum_j e^{-\beta O_j}} .

The gradient of `Φ_β` w.r.t. `h` follows from the chain rule. For a fixed shift `k` the derivative of `O_k` is (see the derivation in the analysis):

\[
\frac{\partial O_k}{\partial h_i}=
\begin{cases}
h_{i+k} &\text{if } i+k<n,\\

h_{i-k} &\text{if } i-k\ge0,\\

**4. Projection onto the Feasible Set (Box + Sum)**  
After a gradient step `h←h−α∇Φ_β` the iterate may violate the bounds `[0,1]` or the exact sum constraint.

1. Clip the tentative vector to `[0,1]`.  
2. If the sum equals the target `S=n/2` we are done.  
3. Otherwise find a scalar `λ` such that  

   \[
   \sum_i \operatorname{clip}(x_i+λ,0,1)=S,
