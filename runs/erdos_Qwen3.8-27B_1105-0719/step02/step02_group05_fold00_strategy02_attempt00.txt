1. Approach and rationale

Central idea

The supplied objective is a constrained discrete minimax problem:

    minimize  C(h) = max_lag  dx * np.correlate(h, 1 - h, mode="full")[lag]

subject to, for each sample count n, 0 <= h_i <= 1 and sum_i h_i = n/2, where dx = 2/n. The evaluator uses all finite-support correlation lags, including both forward and backward shifts and the zero shift at index n-1. The objective is generally nonconvex because terms h_i(1-h_j) create products, and a local solver may only provide a locally improved upper bound, not a certificate of the global discrete minimum.

The proposed solver replaces smooth maximum approximations and direct epigraph SLSQP polishing with a nonlinear trust-region minimax method whose master model is a linear program. At each current point the method:

- memikirkan exact correlations at the current point with the actual evaluator;
- creates a linear model of currently important correlation constraints;
- solves a bounded LP for an epigraph variable t and a candidate including a regularized movement penalty;
- evaluates the LP proposal by the exact objective;
- backtracks along the LP direction if the true objective does not improve;
- updates stored active constraints from hidden violations observed in exact evaluations.

The primary search is performed in even symmetric coordinates. Let n=2m. For any q in [0,1]^m,

    h(q) = concatenate(q, 1 - q[::-1])

exactly contains entries in [0,1] and

    sum(h) = sum(q) + sum(1 - q) = m = n/2.

Thus, once one is optimizing q in bounds, the integral constraint holds by construction and no feasibility projection is required after the main q-step. The full-space method can still use a general feasibility-repair helper, but the q-mode receives primary priority because it gives cheap, exact feasibility, half the dimension, and an exact rank structure.

Why this could improve the parent

The earlier program is built around logsumexp regularization, smooth descents, parent polishing, and nonlinear epigraph SLSQP reductions. These approaches frequently move in the same local basin and can become numerically sensitive because they approximate the active maximum with gradients while products h_i h_j are quadratic. The new method uses explicit polyhedral cuts and bounded trust regions. Its accepted steps are based only on exact evaluations. This produces a different optimization path. Especially plausible benefit comes from multiple sizes and multiple root sources. Limitations

- The problem is nonconvex. Our method can improve quality, but cannot prove global optimality.
- The LP may linearize contradictory quadratic correlation constraints, and model-based reductions may not be valid in reality. A combined trust region is essential and given.
- Symmetry h=[q, 1-q_mirror] need not contain non-symmetric minima. A full-space LP pass is therefore included as an independent search.
- Scaling resolution changes objective discretization. Transfers and exact comparisons may find better or lower output constraints. Results are handled exactly; don't treat smaller resolutions as free.

    C(h) = max(corr_scaled).
