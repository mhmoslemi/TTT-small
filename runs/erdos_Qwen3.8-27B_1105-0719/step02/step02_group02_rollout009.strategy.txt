1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The previous construction relied mainly on continuous local smoothing of a smooth maximum surrogate around the supplied parent vector. The proposed plan keeps the exact evaluator objective but changes the search genus substantially: build a broad population of nearly extreme and symmetric discrete constructions by exact-score combinatorial search, then admit only a few promising discrete/continuous states to a conventional local polish. The disjoint improvement mechanism is therefore combinatorial distance-structure search rather than merely repeated smoothing of the same parent.

The evaluator objective for a length-n vector is

\[
\operatorname{C5}(h,n)=\max_m \{\operatorname{corr}(h,1-h)\}_m \cdot \frac{2}{n},
\]

where `m` indexes the `2n-1` entries of `np.correlate(h, at ten objects, mode="full")`. This is finite-support linear correlation, not circular correlation, and the logarithgic max includes both sides of zero lag through the full array.

1.2 Why this can address the bottleneck

The previous continuous local technique is likely to remain inside the smooth basin of the supplied parent. At the same time, the evaluator naturally treats sequences whose values are often near 0 or 1: for small entries the overlap term behaves combinatorially. A sequence formulation provides:

13. explicit exact objective evaluation on valid discrete vectors;
14. symmetric and asymmetric representation control, exploiting the two lag directions through the full correlation;
15. dimension exploration with exact normalized comparison across lengths, while preserving the parent as a fallback;
16. binary orientation search in symmetric sequences, where balance is naturally exact for every state;
17. discrete population diversity without needing an expensive global proof of optimality.

This plan mitigates local-basin stagnation by creating exact-valid neighbors whose raw corr envelopehas not been optimized in the usual smoothing lineage.

- The evaluator uses exact finite-support linear correlation over both lags.
- Symmetric construction maps both lag sides through reversal, so a symmetric state constrains displays in both directions.
- A palindrome of even length with exactly one 1 inside each mirrored pair has sum exactly n/2. This gives an exact-feasible search over a finite group orientation space.
- Flipped bits or swapped 0/1 states remain all bounds and all sum feasible.
