The upper envelope can be computed in O(K log K) using a convex hull trick (since we're looking at the upper envelope of lines evaluated at x, and we want to find where the minimum of the envelope occurs).

But actually, there's a simpler approach for 1D: the minimum of the max of lines occurs where two lines intersect (or at a boundary). I can find it by:
1. Compute f(0) and f(1)
2. If f(0) <= f(1), the minimum is in [0, some point]
3. Use a "megiddo-style" or simply binary search on the subgradient

For a max of lines, the subgradient at x is the set of slopes of the active (maximizing) lines at x. The minimum is where 0 is in the subgradient, i.e., there exist active lines with both positive and negative slopes (or a single line with slope 0).

I can binary search: 
- At x=0, if all active lines have positive slope, the minimum is at x=0
- At x=1, if all active lines have negative slope, the minimum is at x=1
- Otherwise, the minimum is in between, where the "critical pair" of lines cross

Finding the critical pair: at the minimum, two lines a_i + b_i*x and a_j + b_j*x are equal and all other lines are below. This gives x* = (a_j - a_i) / (b_i - b_j).
