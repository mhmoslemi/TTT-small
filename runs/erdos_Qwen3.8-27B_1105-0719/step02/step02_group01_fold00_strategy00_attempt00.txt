1. Approach and rationale

The objective is the finite-support cross-correlation

$$
F(h)=\max_d \; \frac{2}{N} \sum_{i\in I_d} h_i(1-h_{i+d}),
\qquad d=-(N-1),\ldots,N-1,
$$

where the valid index set $I_d$ is chosen so that both $i$ and $i+d$ lie in $\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$. This is exactly represented by

$$
F(h)=\max_k \left(\operatorname{np.correlate}(h,1-h,\text{mode="full"})\right)_k \frac{2}{N}.
$$

The problem is nonconvex because each term is bilinear in $h$. The supplied parent has already been improved by generic smoothing and local polishing, so the main bottleneck is not the arithmetic of evaluating the objective but the quality and accessibility of the local basins reached by full-dimensional smoothing. When only full-dimensional nonsmooth or softmax minimization is used, two groups of shifts alternate, and the local solution often stagnates near a flat arrangement of active shifts with slow exact convergence.

The proposed improvement is therefore not just another single local optimizer. It combines three linked mechanisms:

1. **Exact antipalindromic reduction.**  
   Map a feasible $h$ into the symmetry subspace
   $$
   h_i + h_{N-1-i}=1,
   $$
   and parametrize only the first side monotonically/pairwise. This gives roughly half the number of optimization coefficients, removes the equality constraint exactly, and produces much smoother, more controllable gradients for the cross-correlation objective. The full objective remains the original evaluator; the reduction is only a nonconvex parametrization, not a proof of global optimality.

2. **Low-dimensional logarithmic/steady-state global search.**

Use two exposure schedules: ordinary log-sum-exp and an active-top variant restricted to the currently strongest shifts. 2. Mathematical specification

Let total length $N$, grid spacing $dx=2/N$, target sum $T=N/2$. A feasible vector satisfies
$$
0\le h_i\le1,\qquad \sum_i h_i=T.
$$

Exact correlation objective for offset $d$:
$$
C_d(h)=dx\sum_{j=\max(0,-d)}^{\min(N-1,N-1-d)}h_j(1-h_{j+d}),
$$
and
$$
F(h)=\max_d C_d(h),\qquad d=-(N-1),\ldots,N-1.
$$
The full correlation array is symmetric in its indexed set to $d$; the official using NumPy uses this same finite vector.

Gradient helper for all offsets or subset. For offset $d$, contribution gradient with respect to h:
$$
\frac{\partial C_d}{\partial h_p}=dx\Big(
\mathbf{1}_{0\le p\le N-1, 0\le p+d\le N-1}\,(1-h_{p+d})
-\mathbf{1}_{0\le p-d\le N-1}h_{p-d}
\Big).
$$
For any weighted objective
$$
L=\sum_d w_d C_d(h),
$$
its gradient is $\sum_d w_d \nabla C_d(h)$.

Surrogate max values using log-sum-exp with exact $C_d dx$ coordinates:
$$
s_\beta(h)=\frac{1}{\beta}\log\sum_d \exp(\beta C_d(h)).
$$
Its gradient:
$$
\nabla s_\beta=\sum_d \frac{\exp(\beta(C_d(h)-c_m))}{\sum_j \exp(\beta(C_j-c_m))}
\nabla C_d,
$$

## 1. Approach and rationale

The current parent construction is already quite close to the present record, so the full global problem is unlikely to be solved by one more unconstrained local smoothing pass in the full array space. The practical bottleneck is that the true objective is the maximum over `2N-1` bilinear finite-support correlations. Full-dimensional smooth-max (softmax) methods can move the surrogate objective while the exact maximum changes only slowly or changes to another branch; active descent without a well-chosen parametrization can also stagnate on competing correlation branches.

This plan uses the original evaluator unchanged and accepts a solution only when it strictly improves the exact artificial fractional correlation objective. It introduces three mathematically sound changes that are both local and exploratory:

1. **Antipalindromic exact-feasibility reduction.**  
   Map a feasible `h` into the symmetry
