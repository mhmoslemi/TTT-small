## 1. Approach and rationale

### 1.1 Central idea

The central algorithmic change is **not** to smooth the maximum and then ask a generic nonlinear program to minimize that smooth surrogate. The live evaluator is an exact max over many finite-support bilinear correlations, and the current construction has a very tight margin: one must improve by only a few parts in 10⁵ or less. Smooth maxima can stall and cannot necessarily find the sharp active constraint changes needed at that scale.

The proposed method uses a **direct, exact, non-smooth feasible-direction search** based on *mass-preserving pair transfers*:

- A feasible direction is specified explicitly by saying: move a small amount of mass from one grid point `a` to another grid point `b`.
- Every move preserves `sum(h) = n/2` exactly by construction (up to float noise corrected once), because one grid point decreases by the same amount the other increases.
- Each proposed pair-transfer direction is tested by exact evaluations of the real evaluator. No projected clipping machinery is needed along the line search, except at initialization/parent validation.
- The pair directions are generated not only as single-component gradient moves, but by solving a small **active LP surrogate** whose feasible directions include simultaneous useful changes beyond one pair. However, even where a true direction is found from smoothing or neighboring local structure, nothing is accepted until exact `np.correlate(h, 1-h)` makes a strict improvement.

This differs substantively from the parent program because the parent program mainly pursued SLSQP with increasing smooth-max temperature, with occasional smoothing polish and LP modeling after ` отличное`. A major bottleneck in that parent pipeline is that the smooth objective becomes extremely ill-conditioned when trying to lower the true max while staying feasible, and the numerical optimizer can terminate at a small smooth improvement that does not render any visible improvement under the exact objective. Here, the core search explicitly optimizes under exact nonsmooth acceptance, and its geometric neighborhood is the simplex-transfer geometry inside the feasible set `h ∈ [0,1]^n, sum h = n/2`. Measures the proposed descent using exact objective scoring directly at each step. This also improves numerical reliability: feasibility is preserved along search lines by design, by using algebraic feasibility-preserving directions rather than repeated clipping + rescaling.
