    off = k - (n - 1)

        g[j] += (1.0 - h[i])

The key challenge is that the objective is max of many bilinear functions. The SLP linearizes at the current point, which is good locally but might miss global structure.

One important observation: at a local minimum of the max function, there should be a set of active constraints that "balance" - the gradient of the max (which is a convex combination of the gradients of active constraints) should be orthogonal to the feasible set (i.e., proportional to the sum constraint gradient, which is all-ones vector).

In other words, at the optimum, there exist weights w_k ≥ 0 (summing to 1) over the active k such that:
sum_k w_k * grad_k = λ * 1 (for some λ)

This is the KKT condition. The SLP should converge to such a point.

The dual problem (minimizing the max) can be formulated as:
min T
s.t. for all k: r_k(h) ≤ T
     sum h = n/2
     0 ≤ h ≤ 1

The dual of the (linearized) problem gives us the KKT multipliers, which tell us the weights w_k.

In the LP we solve, the dual variables for the inequality constraints give us these weights. We can use them to verify convergence.
