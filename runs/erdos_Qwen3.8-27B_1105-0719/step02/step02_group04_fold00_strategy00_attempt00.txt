1. Approach and rationale  
============

1.1 Central idea.  
Represent the feasible search space by a closed, always-conservative symmetric representation and minimize a sequence of exact-objective replacements that range from smooth global averages to near-hard maxima. The representation guarantees the mass and box constraints without post-hoc projection, so every trial is feasible and can be accepted purely on its exact score. This removes a major weakness in earlier methods: repairing sum after clipping or projection can reduce one overlap while increasing another hidden one, causing nonmonotone and premature acceptance behavior. In the new representation, an accept step is mathematically canonical: build a symmetric vector, run a smooth surrogate, exactly evaluate with the authoritative evaluator, and update the current best only if the exact value improves.

1.2 Why this may improve the supplied construction.  
The parent is already approximately optimal under earlier active-constraint smoothing. Improving a near-optimal point of this nonconvex bilinear objective requires resistance to two failure modes:

- hidden violations: constraints that are just below the current maximum become the new maximum during the step;
- basin rigidity: the current point is a local optimum of one surrogate but far from a better local optimum of the exact maximum.

To counter hidden violations, replace purely active-only smoothed maximum objectives with a centered log-sum-exp surrogate over all lags. A log-sum-exp objective automatically gives positive gradient weight to constraints that are close below the current maximum, not only those already in the top candidate set. It lets loose peaks that may become active later be reduced before they block relaxation of the current maximum.

To counter basin rigidity, maintain several recently useful patterns and explicitly inject controlled low-amplitude inversions through surrogate trajectory, interpolation, crossover, and noise. Do not claim a global optimum; instead exploit the fact that the target improvement is tiny but can be obtained if several hidden potential maxima are traded off better.

1.3 Primary representation.  
Use even lengths only and optimize a half-length vector `q` defining the symmetric antisymmetric form

h_i = q_i, for i=0,...,m-1,
h_i = 1 - q_{n-1-i}, for i=m,...,n-1,

where n = 2m. If q_i ∈ [0,1], then every h_i is automatically between 0 and 1. The sum is

sum(h) = sum(q) + (m - sum(q)) = m = n/2.

The constraints are satisfied exactly without bisection, projection, or post-directed clipping. This is the most important correctness advantage over general full-variable projections.

1.4 Limitations.  
Symmetric representation may exclude some better asymmetric optima or continuous intermediate values. The stated plan therefore keeps the supplied parent as a fallback and re-evaluates every returned vector exactly. The transformed symmetric subsets of global solutions may not always dominate the parent unless both feasible. No method can certify global optimality of the nonconvex problem unless a complete branch-and-bound or exact search is used, and the current mechanism is explicitly a local stochastic/hybrid search. The algorithm must make no global-optimality claim; it only reports the best exact value found and its dimension. The objective remains nonconvex and maximization properties may vary by representation; the algorithm does not use global guarantees.
