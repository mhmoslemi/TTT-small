1. Approach and rationale

The objective is a finite, nonsmooth, nonconvex discrete minimax problem. The authoritative objective is evaluated by the supplied evaluator as

    exact_score(h) = max_k [np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")_k] * (2.0 / n),

with h ∈ [0,1]^n and sum_i h_i = n/2. This is not a convex problem: the quadratic terms -h_i h_{i+L} make the individual correlation terms indefinite or nonconvex. Therefore no direct convex CVXPY formulation is claimed, and no surrogate value will be accepted as the returned bound.

The proposed algorithm centers on several properties that are often underused in smoothed epigraph or soft-max approaches:

- Feasibility can be preserved without re-projection by changing two coordinates while keeping their sum fixed. Every genuine dual coordinate move has a closed interval for one variable and no destruction of the equality constraint except tiny floating-point drift.
- For a fixed pair of coordinates, every full-lag correlation output is exactly a quadratic polynomial in the moved coordinate. This permits an exact or near-exact global one-dimensional minimax search over the feasible pair interval using coefficient fits and quadratic-root interval logic, rather than assuming convexity or relying only on dense golden-section sampling.
- When pairwise descent stalls, a low-dimensional feasible linear direction can be requested from the current active top-lag constraints using a small linear program. The direction proposal is by itself only a linear guess; it is accepted only if exact evaluation along a finite-support feasible line improves the true evaluator.
- The parent construction is retained as a validated feasible fallback. The search starts from it but does not claim that any one method is globally optimal.

The central bottleneck attacked is that local result improvements often require deliberate correlated changes across two coordinates: preserving sum while shifting two values can change very different lags with widely varying boundary windows. Surrogate smoothing can prefer tiny eigen-biased movements and smooth out missed local minima. The dual-coordinate exact/minimax polynomial move with a logical penalty/direction backup directly navigates the nonsmooth active Lagrangian objective usefully, with exact evaluation only determining acceptance.

Justified facts:

- The changed vector sums exactly when p and q are altered by preserving p + q.
- For a fixed vector h, fixed p, and q with s=p+q, changing a variable a=p and b=s-q, each product h_i(1-h_j) is either no term, a linear term in a, or a quadratic term from product of two interpreted values. Hence each individual lag value f_k(a) is exactly quadratic in a.
- The range of a is [max(0, s-1), min(1, s)], no present clipping nor projection is necessary for feasibility within an accepted single pair move.
- The final reported value is the exact evaluator of a re-evaluated feasible output vector. Any surrogate or linearized internal value can decrease or increase through numerical under/over; it is only a local decision guide.

Limitations and heuristics:

- No method guarantees global optimality. Pairwise global approximate, active LP, transfer, SLSQP, and structural restarts are all local or heuristic methods.
- One pair subproblem may be nonconvex because zero-lag self terms make some component functions concave in the fixed-pair-sum variable. The root-interval pair optimizer handles max-of-quadratics without claiming a convex minimizer. If root-extraction numerics fail, a dense robust fallback is used.
- During line LP critical constraint may include linear terms not fully representative of stepwise geometry due static computed derivative. Exact line search prevents acceptance of harmful proposed directions.
- Transferring resolutions can introduce new endpoints and surrogate qualities, not monotonically better. Transfer candidates require calculations on new dimension before comparison.

Materially different from previous ideas: the primary engine is not smoothing/soft-max/heuristic local estimates with soft acceptance, but an exact/scalar pair hyper-cluster method built from quadratic roots and quadratic constraints, taking large step moving pairs until line stack returned not reliance smoothing-based scoring; pair solving directly near objective constraints and feasible endpoints.
