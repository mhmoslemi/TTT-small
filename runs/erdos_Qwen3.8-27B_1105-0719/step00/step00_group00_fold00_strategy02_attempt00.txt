1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

Use the supplied discrete evaluator exactly as the only acceptance test, and generate coordinated zero-sum moves by solving a small linear program based on the directional derivatives of the currently most restrictive correlation constraints. For each candidate vector `h`, compute the full finite-support correlation profile with `np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")`. Select the lags near the current maximum. For those lags, compute the directional derivative of the correlation profile with respect to a vector direction `d` whose coordinates satisfy `sum(d)=0`. Solve a linear program:

- transfer mass only from decreasing coordinates to increasing coordinates,
- preserve the discrete mass exactly,
- respect the bounds from all coordinates that are currently at or numerically close to the lower/upper limits,
- require all selected active derivative constraints to decrease by at least a common positive amount `m`,
- normalize the direction so that its `L1` norm is one.

Then use the resulting direction `d` as a fixed line-search direction. Along the line

`h(t) = h + t d`

with `sum(h(t))` preserved by construction, every lag value of the correlation array is an exact quadratic function of the step `t`. Because correlation is bilinear in its two arguments, the entire full lag profile is explicitly:

`R(h + t d) = R(h) + t R(d, 1-h) - t R(h, d) - t^2 R(d, d)`

so the finite-dimensional objective can be searched very accurately by evaluating only this exact quadratic family. Only steps whose exact final score improves are accepted.

This is materially different from either a pure pair-transfer search or a smooth log-sum-exp surrogate. A pair-transfer line search considers a very small rank-one coordinate subspace and can miss moves requiring several coordinates to move simultaneously. A smooth surrogate optimizes an approximating scalar value and treats the exact maximum only after the fact. In contrast, the proposed primary method optimizes the first-order decrease of the actual near-maximum correlation vector directly, uses only linear programming in the active-gradient invariant, and accepts or rejects every step using the original evaluator.

1.2 Why this could improve on the parent

The supplied parent of `n=45` is likely a local optimum for simple pair transfers. In a constrained local optimum, the only directions that reduce the maximum must move several coordinates together among the very few near-optimal constraints. A linear program over the vector of active derivative constraints represents a much larger family of coordinated moves. For the fixed line obtained from the LP, the exact quadratic form of each correlation term enables an accurate one-dimensional maximum profile search. Therefore, the algorithm can test globally consistent small changes that involve many grid samples simultaneously and prevent early stopping after a one-dimensional move misses scheduling constraints.

The method is deliberately conservative: LP, linearization and step selection are used only as a search mechanism; all accepted solutions are fully valid minimizers of the discrete objective found through exact evaluation. It cannot certify global optimality even for the discrete problem due to the bilinear nonconvex nature. Also, the first-order LP active representation may not find a true local descent direction if the descent relies only on second-order terms or on moving away from currently inactive lags, but curvature-aware step selection and multi-resolution transition help explore such cases. The plan does not claim convexity or a global optimum.

1.3 Primary architectural decisions

- Retain the exact evaluator as the objective and final score.
- Search via active-set directional LPs rather than only pair directions.
- Fix the zero-sum condition via an LP equality constraint; no clipping and rescaling as a joint feasibility mechanism.
- Understand `h` by considering only cell centers. Transfers between resolutions define cells as spanning the constraints and restoring exact feasibility with a joint capacity-preserving mechanism.
- Use local line-search polish, store good candidates of different lengths, transfer and polish between lengths, and stop with current validated selection before resources expire.

1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

Use the supplied discrete evaluator exactly as the only acceptance test, and generate coordinated zero-sum moves by solving a small linear program based on the directional derivatives of the currently most restrictive correlation constraints. For those lags, compute the directional derivative of the correlation profile with respect to a vector direction `d` whose coordinates satisfy `sum(d)=0`. Solve a linear program:

- the direction preserves the discrete mass exactly;
- all selected near-maximum lags have a decreasing first-order derivative by a common scalar improvement margin `m`;
- the direction is normalized so that its `L1` norm is one;
- coordinates that are already at the lower or upper bound have only one-sided signs in the direction.
