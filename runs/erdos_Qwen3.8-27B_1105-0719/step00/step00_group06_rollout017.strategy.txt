1. Approach and rationale

The supplied parent is a length-70 discretization with exact integral. The target improvement is small, so the most reliable strategy is to use the parent as an anchor and perform aggressive constrained nonconvex local refinement, together with complementary coarse-resolution searches. The core difficulty is that the objective is a finite max of many bilinear overlap terms on the simplex-equivalent set {0 ≤ h_i ≤ 1, Σ h_i = n/2}. A direct conic/LP/QP formulation is not globally valid because the overlap products are nonconvex; therefore, a convex relaxation cannot be the main construction. The accepted candidates must be checked against the exact finite objective.

The proposed method combines four coherent waves:

1. Exact constrained local refinement from the parent and controlled perturbations. A log-sum-exp epigraph smoothing makes the max objective differentiable. SLSQP with tight box bounds and an exact linear equality uses the actual analytic gradient (with finite-difference fallback). This is the primary mechanism for chipping away from the parent record.
2. Exact post-processing: projection to box+sum, then binarization with exact half-sum when the length is even, followed by local swap moves on the binary vector. This often lowers the zero-lag cost and improves crossing-complement overlap.
3. Random exact descent and pairwise interpolation on valid vectors. Since the problem is nonconvex, these cheap steps create guided neighborhood candidates not necessarily inferred from one local gradient basin. They always satisfy the constraints because the updates and interpolation keep each vector valid, and every candidate is tested by the original objective.
4. Multi-resolution search. Even though the supplied parent has 70 samples, smaller feasible lengths are explicitly searched and accepted only if their exact discrete objective is better. The plan is deliberately local/nonconvex and makes no global optimality claim.
