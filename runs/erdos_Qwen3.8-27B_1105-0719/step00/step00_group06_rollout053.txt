Let me also think about the binarization + swap approach more carefully.

For a binary vector b of length n with n/2 ones:
- C5 = max_lag (count of positions where b and 1-b differ at that lag offset) * (2/n)
- At lag 0: always 0 (since b*(1-b) = 0)
- At lag k: count of i where b[i+k] = 1 and b[i] = 0 (for k > 0)

This is related to the autocorrelation of the binary sequence. We want to minimize the maximum cross-correlation between the binary sequence and its complement at all lags.

For the swap moves: try swapping a 1 at position i with a 0 at position j. This changes the profile only at lags involving i or j. We can compute the change efficiently.

The Erdős minimum overlap problem asks for the minimum over all measurable sets E ⊂ [0,2] with |E| = 1 of the maximum over all t of |E ∩ (E+t)| where the intersection is with the complement... wait, let me re-read.

The problem is: C₅ = max_k ∫ h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx

This is the maximum overlap of h with the complement of h shifted by k. In terms of sets, if h is the indicator of a set E, then h(x)(1-h(x+k)) is the indicator of x ∈ E and x+k ∉ E. So it's measuring how much of E "sticks out" under translation by k.

For h to be a good function, we want E to have the property that for every shift k, E ∩ (E+k)^c is small. Equivalently, E should have small boundary in all directions.

This is related to the concept of "thin" sets or sets with small difference set. The constant C₅ is the minimum possible maximum "missing overlap."

The minimum possible value is related to the structure of binary sequences with low autocorrelation. This is a well-studied problem in coding theory.
