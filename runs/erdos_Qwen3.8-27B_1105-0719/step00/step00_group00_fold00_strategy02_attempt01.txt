1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea and why it addresses the parent bottleneck  
The evaluation is an exact, low-dimensional nonconvex quadratic maximization problem:
sets `h_i in [0,1]`, preserves `sum(h_i) = n/2`, and minimizes `dx * max_s corr_s(h)`. The supplied parent is a discrete point that very likely cannot be slightly improved by a single coordinate-pair transfer, but it may be improvable by simultaneous adjustments across several currently active lags. The proposed method therefore treats the best parent-like local solution as the center of an exact-active-set SQP/LP descent loop rather than as a fixed block to be perturbed only pairwise.

At a current feasible `h`, each full correlation term is a sum over pairs `(i,j)` on one finite-support diagonal:
`corr_s = sum_{(i,j) in P_s} h_i (1 - h_j)`.
The differences between lag values are caused by different finite boundary supports for `s = 0,...,2n-2`. This boundary structure makes `corr_s` a maximum over many bilinear terms. A rank-one pair move changes only all constraints whose diagonals contain the two coordinates, whereas the main mechanism constructs a vector step whose first-order linearization of every full lag is simultaneously minimized in an LP. This lets the optimizer move in coordinated directions that equalize or cut down several active lags, which is a more powerful local mechanism than repeated rank-one search.

The finite problem remains nonconvex, so the LP is only a locally valid descent model over a trust region. Therefore every core step is judged by the exact evaluator. A quadratic-aware pair polishing loop is added as a complementary fallback because it tests a coordinate pair over its full exact feasible interval rather than solely its linearization.

1.2 Distinction from previous ideas  
This is not primarily a smooth `log-sum-exp` optimizer, nor a naive repeated projection between clipping and rescaling, and it is not exclusively a rank-one line-search family. The main core is a linearized active-lag `LP/SQP` model in the original discrete `h_i` variables, preserving the sum analytically through an equality constraint, followed by an exact one-dimensional line test. Pairwise exact line search is only a secondary finishing/diagnostic mechanism. The method therefore attacks the local KKT failure modes of simple directions and is less confident than they are that the parent is locally fully optimized.

The plan cannot certify a global optimum and may stop at a local feasible optimum. 2.3 Local linearized active-lag constraints  
Write `r_s(h)` exactly as the finite diagonal sum. Let `G_s(h)` be its gradient. Direct pair expansion avoids a separate derivative implementation bug:
For every pair `(i,j)` in `P_s`, contribution is `h_i * (1 - h_j)`. Thus at `h`, initialize row term constants `r_s = 0` and gradient row `G_s` of length `n` zeros. For each pair,

  `r_s += h_i * (1 - h_j)`  
  `G_s[i] += (1 - h_j)`  
  `G_s[j] -= h_i`

After processing all pairs, for a candidate variable step `d`, the first-order estimate of the lag is

  `L_s(h + d) = r_s + step_s @ d`.

Scale objective entries to the objective units already used by final metric:

  `b_s = dx * r_s`  
  `A_s = dx * G_s`.

Then a valid linearized proposal satisfies

  `b_s + A_s dot d <= t` for every `s`.

Since all constraints are linear in `d` and `t`, for a chosen trust set, the problem

  `minimize t` over `(t,d)`
subject to `b_s + A_s dot d <= t` for all `s`,
`sum_i d_i = 0`,
exact bounds on components, and bounds on `t`
