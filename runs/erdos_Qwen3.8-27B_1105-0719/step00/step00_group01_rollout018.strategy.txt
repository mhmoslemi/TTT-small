1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea: exact feasible-line descent in the original max problem

The proposed method is an exact, local, direction-by-direction refinement method for the finite-dimensional nonsmooth program obtained from the supplied discretization. The objective is always evaluated by the official metric

    C(h) = max( dx * np.correlate(h, 1 - h, mode="full") ),

with dx = 2 / len(h), and every accepted update is re-checked with that same metric. The method does not approximate the maximum by a soft-max, does not drop boundary lags, does not transform the problem into a circular convolution, and does not claim that a surrogate maîtrize or a local optimum is a global optimum.

The central primitive is a feasible one-dimensional line through a current point. Given a feasible h and a direction d satisfying sum(d)=0, one considers

    h(t) = h + t d,     t in a box-safe interval,

so sum(h(t)) remains exactly N/2 up to floating-point cleanup, and each component can be kept in [0,1] by restricting t to the exact feasible interval. For such a line, one evaluates the exact objective C(h(t)) at a deterministic set of samples and refines around the best sample. If the refined point has a smaller exact objective, it replaces the current working point and is stored globally.

The method remains close to the supplied parent but uses a large, repeatedly re-targeted search over many feasible directions: coordinate-exchange directions, local clustered directions, random feasible directions, and "activation" directions generated from the current worst lags. The local passes are combined with a controlled downward convergence pass and a small controlled drift/annealing pass. If time remains, a short inexact nonlinear POLY Präsentation is attempted over the whole vector or a proven reduced subspace, but its output is accepted only after full exact evaluation and feasibility repair.

1.2 Why it can improve the supplied parent

The parent is supplied at a length near a known local construction and may be already near a local optimum. A single global search is too expensive and often unnecessary; a one-micro-improvement, precisely moving mass to reduce one or several near-critical lags, can exceed target. Exact one-dimensional moves are particularly appropriate because the objective along h+t d is easy to evaluate and its nonsmooth shape behavior is automatically handled by exact sampling/refinement. A line through two coordinates transfers probability mass between h positions without breaking sum-h = N/2. If both coordinate preferences become useful, local multi-coordinate moves are generated using random/cluster-based directions.

The pass avoids the main dangers: approximate penalties ignore lags they underestimate, epigraph interfaces can drift because of non-smooth constraint intersections, and naive clipping/rescaling destroys exact sum-h = N/2.

1.3 Differences from earlier strategies

The previous plans centered on either a soft-max control global descent, active linear-programming guesses, or a direct epigraph nonlinear program in full dimensions. The present plan is materially different in both primary mechanism and acceptance:

- Primary objective is not fixed? The primary is exact nonsmooth feasibility, via exact evaluation of the objective along feasible lines: one-dimensional slice minimization for the original max.
- The step geometry is coordinate-preserving lines: box-bounded intervals of placement generated from h+t d. This keeps feasibility native, avoiding dependence on generic projection for every step.
- The method is a structured line-search coordinate process and a controlled locally converging drift process rather than a fully nonconvhe epigraph budget non NLP. Smooth surrogates are optional diagnostics, not decision source.
- Multidirectional directions are generated directly in the tangent of the sum constraint. The goal is to attack exact active lags, but every candidate is judged exactly by max(np.correlate(...)).

1.4 Limitations and what is not claimed

This is explicit local refinement: it does not prove that C5 global optimum is reached, nor even that the search space reaches the true finite-dimensional optimum at any resolution. It is also heuristic one-dimensional search, where line objective not strictly unimodal. Refinement must therefore be robust: global storage of all strictly valid exact better candidates, archive preserving subsets, controlled random acceptance, and final validation protect against inaccurate progression.
