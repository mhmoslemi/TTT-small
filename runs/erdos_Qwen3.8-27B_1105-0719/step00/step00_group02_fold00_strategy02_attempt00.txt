1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The discrete problem is to minimize

    F(h) = max_l F_l(h),     F_l(h) = dx * [np.correlate(h, 1-h, "full")_l],

over the feasible polytope

    K_n = {h in [0,1]^n : sum(h) = n/2},   dx = 2/n.

The feasible set is convex, but the objective is nonconvex because each lag value is a sum of quadratic terms h_i(1-h_j). The proposed algorithm is not a direct CVX, branch-and-bound, or exhaustive solver. It is a safeguarded polyhedral jump search:

1. At the current feasible point h, inspect the active or near-active lag values defined by the authoritative evaluator.
2. Form a weighted first-order model of those important lags.
3. Solve the exactly supported linear subproblem of finding the best extreme point of K_n for that linear model.
4. Move from the current feasible h to that extreme point in a straight line, h(gamma) = (1-gamma)h + gamma h_ext. This convex-combination move is always exactly feasible because both endpoints are in K_n and K_n is convex.
5. Accept the step using only the original exact evaluator. Smoothed or linearized surrogate scores are used only for proposing directions; accepted candidates and returned bounds always come from np.correlate by definition.
6. Combine this global/jump-like polyhedral mechanism with smaller exact feasible pair-transfer moves and restarts. Pair moves preserve sum and box feasibility by construction, and are used to reduce small active-lag excesses after the FW-like steps stagnate.
7. Run on multiple resolutions when useful. Every candidate is regenerated, projected, validated, and scored at its actual runtime dimension. The maintained parent or best verified auxiliary candidate is always a valid fallback.

This differs from the earlier pair-minimax interval-bisection and smooth-p-norm/projected-gradient plans in a substantive way: the main new structural mechanism is the use of exact extreme points of K_n produced by linear optimization over the entire box-sum polytope, followed by exact line-search acceptance. Previous strategies remain helpful as useful descriptors of the problem, but they are not implemented in the same role. Extreme-point linear optimization is convex and exactly supported; the remaining nonconvex difficulty is addressed only by exact evaluation, restarts, and local feasible pair moves. No global optimum is claimed.

The active solution is already close to a tight local basin. A pair-transfer move has only two degrees of freedom and a single path. The line-search dimension gamma continuously interpolates between staying still and making the complete vertex move. Exact line search on the real objective can discover a nonmonotonic but globally lowering intermediate point, which the exact objective evaluates directly.
