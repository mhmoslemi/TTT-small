1. Approach and rationale  
===========

1.1 Central idea  
The discrete problem is a bound-constrained, fixed-mass, nonconvex minimax problem: minimize the maximum over all admissible lags of the sampled cross-correlation of h and 1−h. The parent is already close to the current record, so the decisive task is local and stochastic polishing rather than inventing a brand-new global construction. The proposed algorithm keeps the parent as a verified fallback and searches around, partially reconstructing, and lifting it in ways that are likely to reduce top-lag overlaps while preserving feasibility exactly.

The plan combines four complementary mechanisms:

- Feasibility-preserving projected moves: any proposed vector is first projected onto the box-and-sum constraint set by the Euclidean projection, then given a tiny exact repair. This avoids compounding clipping and rescaling errors.
- A smooth upper surrogate of the max: use log-sum-exp smoothing over the correlation lags and projected gradient-style line search on that surrogate. The surrogate changes the objective, but every accepted candidate for the final solution is re-scored with the original evaluator and kept only under a conservative acceptance rule. Smoothed descent reduces very high isolated peaks efficiently.
- Exact pair-transfer moves: pairwise transfers of mass from one sample to another preserve ∑h= n/2 automatically and maintain [0,1] by volume clamping. These moves give an exact objective-descent layer without requiring surrogate gradient correctness.
- Stochastic restart/annealing basis hitting: a small number of parent-perturbed, random, and elite restarts escape local traps. Because the objective is nonconvex, the optimizer accepts only exact-objective coupons to report progress and returns the best feasible candidate seen, never a smoothed or relaxed value.

1.2 Why this could improve the supplied parent  
The supplied parent appears to be a strong near-optimal construction. Improvements of order 5×10^{-5} are usually local boundary-effects failures: some large lags produce peaks that cannot be lowered simultaneously by naive local changes. Smoothing turns the hardest few lags into a distributed penalty, allowing collective small moves to reduce peak margins. Exact pair transfers polish residual nonsmooth active-lag conflicts. Random restarts test nearby basins and transforms that the smooth projection may miss.

The plan uses the same or nearby resolutions. The reported bound always comes from the real evaluator, so improvements are only credited if they survive exact scoring and constraint repair. Multi-resolution variants can be tried, but the parent’s format remains the primary safe candidate.

1.3 Limitations and honest claims  
No global optimality is claimed. There is no convex reformulation of the full product-max objective that supports CVX methods directly. Smooth log-sum-exp changes the objective but must ultimately be judged by exact discretized max correlation. The search is heuristic: it can fail to improve from a disguised local optimum, hence the explicit parent, peer candidate, and best-snapshot mechanisms are required.  
Heuristic hypotheses include: lowering LSEs of correlation maxima improves peak values, nearby restarts can avoid stalls, smaller variables into exact pair moves can reach target. Concrete facts: pair transfers preserve ∑h exactly; log-sum-exp is a soft max exact enough for numeric gradients; Euclidean projection with a joint repair resolves the joint bound-sum constraint.
