5. Project.
6. Repeat.

For the binary case, the problem is equivalent to: find a binary string b of length n with exactly n/2 ones such that for all k = 1, ..., n-1:
- #{i : b[i]=1, b[i+k]=0} ≤ M (for i+k < n)
- #{i : b[i]=0, b[i+k]=1} ≤ M (for i+k < n)

where M is to be minimized (and C₅ = M * 2/n).

This is equivalent to: for all k, the number of "rising edges" and "falling edges" at distance k is at most M.

A "falling edge" at distance k means b[i]=1, b[i+k]=0.
A "rising edge" at distance k means b[i]=0, b[i+k]=1.

Total falling + rising at distance k = number of disagreements at distance k.

For a "balanced" string, we'd expect about (n-k)/2 disagreements at each distance k (since half the pairs agree and half disagree). We want this to be at most 2M (split evenly between rising and falling).

For n=48, k=1: we want ≤ 2M disagreements out of 47. If M=9, that's ≤ 18 disagreements, meaning ≥ 29 agreements out of 47 adjacent pairs. This means the string has at most 18 transitions, i.e., at most 19 blocks.

For k=1, the minimum possible number of transitions in a string with 24 ones and 24 zeros is 2 (one block of 24 ones, one block of 24 zeros). So 18 transitions is not very restrictive for k=1.

The more restrictive constraints are for small k. Let me think about k=2:
We want #{i: b[i]=1, b[i+2]=0} ≤ M and #{i: b[i]=0, b[i+2]=1} ≤ M.
Total disagreements at distance 2 ≤ 2M.
There are n-2 = 46 pairs at distance 2.

For a "random-looking" string, the expected disagreements at distance 2 is 46/2 = 23. We want ≤ 18 (for M=9). That's a bit restrictive.
