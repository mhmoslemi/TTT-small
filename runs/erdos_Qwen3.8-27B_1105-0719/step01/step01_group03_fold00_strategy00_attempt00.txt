1. Approach and rationale

The target is a local, multistart, symmetry-aware minimax optimization of

C5(h) = max_{k=-(n-1),...,n-1} Φ_k(h),  Φ_k(h) = (2/n) times the finite-support linear overlap.

The previous method mostly used exact epigraph-style SLSQP and random perturbations. It found a good parent but became stuck because:

- smoothing was very narrow or absent; random perturbations were inefficient for the many nearly active lags;
- no targeted attempt was made to reduce the central quadratic term Φ_0 through rounding/blending;
- once one local optimum was found, there was little opportunity to combine different basins of attraction;
- variables were essentially asymmetric unless randomized, whereas the problem is invariant under reversal, hence a symmetric subspace can be searched much more cheaply;
- candidate acceptance across multiple resolutions was weakly coupled to final best storage.

The proposed method keeps the exact evaluator as the final objective and never claims a global optimum. Its main idea is a candidate-pool optimizer with several different engines and extension operations:

A. A stable log-sum-exp smoothing sleeve does projected first-order descent in the constraint set {0 ≤ h ≤ 1, sum(h) = n/2}. Starting with large smoothing parameters explores many lags simultaneously, then progressively focusing produces a point very close to the original max objective.

B. A trust-region active-set exact line search uses only the currently active cluster of lags. This engine starts after smoothing, and performs much better near a near-entrance equality point: the descent gradient is the weighted gradient of the near-maxing constraints, combined with-box-and-sum projection.

C. Rounding and crossover create candidate families, unlike previous perturbations around a single parent. Rounding to approximately 0/1 suppresses the central value Φ_0 and can move the candidate into a different basin. Crossover combines top parents; individual history and the optimizer’s acceptance controls.

D. A symmetric search space is used when possible. If n is even, render half variables length n/2 and fill the full vector by reflection. This reduces dimension by half approximately, and contract radii decrease. Symmetric repaired candidates also improve performance. We still compute exact objective over the full length.

E. The final output is always repaired metric-free, feasible, finite, then immediately reproved with `np.correlate(h, 1 - h, mode="full")*(2.0/n)`.

Large-smoothing and randomized spline transfer loss or maximization plan decreasing over approachable steps. We caution heuristics rather than dispatching random-free batching convergence globally. One cannot declare a globally optimal bound.
