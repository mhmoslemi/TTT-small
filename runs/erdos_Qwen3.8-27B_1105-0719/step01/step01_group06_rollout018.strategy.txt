1. Approach and rationale
=========================

1.1 Central idea
----------------

The solution proposed here is a newly organized, exact-objective “line exchanger” method that combines three mechanisms not used together in the earlier strategies:

1. **Exact one-dimensional affine exchange lines.**  
   The vector is moved only along feasible mass-preserving affine directions of the form  
   `h(τ) = h + τ d`, where `sum(d)=0`. Along each affine line, every overlap-lag profile value is exactly a quadratic polynomial in `τ`, because the overlap profile is bilinear in `h` and `1-h`. Three profile evaluations fit the whole profile exactly for that line. One-dimensional candidates are chosen from the fitted quadratics, and then the best two or three `τ` values are recomputed with the original evaluator and accepted only if the true evaluated objective improves the incumbent.

2. **Feasible exchange directions and block movements.**  
   The search does not use a general log-sum-exp smoothing surrogate, a full linearized Chebyshev LP, or an integer/MILP model as the primary mechanism. Instead, proposals are generated from principled mass-preserving subtrees and blocks: pairwise exchanges between coordinates that “want to go up” and coordinates that “want to go down,” random block transfers, low-frequency random directions, and selected vectors from coarse binary/parent-derived bases. These moves modify the same object favored by the evaluator while keeping `sum(h)=n/2` exactly up to floating-point tolerance and keeping values in `[0,1]`.

3. **A parent-plus-diversified seed basin, with binary-derived starters.**  
   The supplied parent vector, if available, is always validated, repaired, and retained as the safe fallback. Around it and around a few explicitly meaningful alternate vectors, the exact line exchanger runs multiple alternating chains. The alternations include simple coarse-resolution deterministic or parent-thresholded binary constructions and continuous smoothing/rescaling of them. Binary starting points are feasible vectors in the original discrete sense: all entries are exactly 0 or 1, and their sum is exactly `n/2` provided `n` is even. Vector transformations such as reversal and complement reversal produce further legal vectors.

The central hypothesis is that the previously found 0.381096 point is a shallow local minimum in a narrow exact-lengkapi behavior where active lag sets shift and traditional smooth methods fail to make tiny gains. A line-exchange method has two practical advantages: it respects the exact piecewise-quadratic structure without a nonconvex general nonlinear solver, and it can initiate from genuinely different binary basins that are invisible to a continuation algorithm starting from the same parent. No global optimality claim is made.
