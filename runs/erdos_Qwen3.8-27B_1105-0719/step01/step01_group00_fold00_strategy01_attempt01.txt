1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The previous construction was polished mainly by smoothing the maximum of the correlation values with a log-sum-exp or similar surrogate, followed by constrained local pushes and a few low-dimensional retries. That approach can become unreliable near the target precision: the smooth surrogate changes the direction of descent as `beta` changes, it can assign too much weight to near-maximum constraints, and the final result can remain slightly above the exact minimax point because the evaluation never enforces that the exact maximum constraint itself is minimized to the desired tolerance.

The plan below changes the optimization mechanism in three substantive ways:

1. **Exact target-threshold descent, not global smoothing only.**  
   At a current candidate, instead of minimizing only a smoothed maximum, the algorithm fixes an explicit target level `T` just below the current exact score and minimizes a directed penalty over the lags that violate that target. The penalty weights violations above `T`, while constraints below `T` do not contribute. This forces the optimizer to attack the actual top lags rather than a broad surrogate distribution. As the true maximum is lowered, the target is lowered again in a monotone ladder.

2. **Norm-p tharigest penalty family.**  
   The violation penalty uses an `ell_p`-type loss over only the violated lags. As `p` is increased, the loss concentrates more heavily on the largest violations. This gives a separate homotopy from smoothing that is not equivalent to log-sum-exp: it is targeted against violations above a chosen threshold and can be much better at reducing the top plateau while leaving lower lags unaffected.

3. **Finite exact polishing on the active top plateau.**  
   After the target-threshold phase, a small active-set minimax epigraph subproblem is solved only on the currently dangerous lags and their neighboring lags. This is local and nonconvex, so it is not claimed globally exact. However, it removes the main limitation of the old smoothing when all dominant constraints are nearly equal: the exact constraints among those dominant lags are optimized directly rather than through a high-`beta` surrogate.

4. **Independent seeded constructions and dimension transfers.**  
   To prevent intrinsic trapping around the parent, the design also generates compact Fourier/spline constructions and nearby dimensions algorithmically from formulas and the current best point. Dimension transfers are optional and only accepted by exact re-evaluation. Whenever a smaller dimension gives a score close to the best larger-dimensional solution, the algorithm stores the smaller one because the evaluation favors smaller vectors.

Hardware constraint: one CPU and about 1100 seconds are available. The method is deterministic from the seed except for a few controlled pseudo-random restarts. All accepted candidates are validated with the exact objective formula every time.

1.2 What bottleneck is addressed?

The highest limit is not the cost of one exact evaluation; it is the stiffness of the local minimax surface. At the final precision, many lags lie close to the maximum. A smooth surrogate with fixed or increased beta tends to specify a weighted average of derivatives, but the exact minimax solution often needs a different weighted flow that equalizes the formerly smallest violations higher or reduces a plateau. The target-threshold family creates lower targets, penalizes only lags crossing the target, and transfers the optimizing incentive directly to active and newly dangerous lags. The small epigraph afterward then gets a final correction using the exact selected constraints.

1.3 What is justified?

- The evaluator and objective definition are exact.
- Feasibility repair is explicitly designed, and acceptance requires direct feasibility violation checks.
- The objective is judged always by direct evaluation, never by the surrogate objective.
- The method is local; no global optimality is claimed.
- The idea of focusing on target violations, norm-p violations, and a finite exact active polish is independent of the parent and not merely a parameter change.

1.4 Important limitations

- The problem is nonconvex because it contains products `h_i h_j`.
- Multiple local optima are possible. The code should seek many starts but cannot guarantee global optimality.
- The penalty step may move the maximum to a previously safe lag. Exact evaluation and acceptance handle this; a candidate is accepted only if its **exact** score improves.
- If the target level is reduced too aggressively, the penalty problem may be difficult or impossible locally. The algorithm avoids that with an adaptive target scaling and fallback to previous best.
- The active epigraph polish is a local SLSQP-style nonlinear programming solve; it is useful but nonconvex.
