1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The supplied parent algorithm is essentially a smoothed-maximizer pipeline: it replaces the exact max-over-lags objective with a log-sum-exp surrogate, runs SLSQP repeatedly, and tries a bounded amount of local/random polishing. That is a fair continuous heuristic, but it has two structural weaknesses for this problem:

1. It spends most of its budget on one scalar smoothing parameter path. Near convergence, the exact problem has a few active correlations that dominate the objective; a scalar smoothing term either averages many irrelevant terms or becomes numerically stiff.
2. It lacks a direct way to exploit the active set at any current iterate. The exact objective is nonconvex, but in a neighborhood of a fixed h the correlations are smooth functions of h. The minimization of their maximum can be modeled very accurately by a convex minimax linear/trust-region subproblem, evaluated exactly only at the new iterate.

The new program should therefore be centered on a direct exact-objective active-lag trust-region successive linear program:

- Start from the supplied parent vector if available, keeping it as a certified fallback.
- At each current feasible vector h, compute the exact array of correlation values for all lags and the exact/main-coordinate linearization of each correlation around h.
- Solve a convex quadratic trusted minimax subproblem for a small continuous step preserving sum h and bounds.
- Evaluate the exact task metric on the resulting candidate and accept only if the exact score improves.
- Shrink the trust radius on failure and expand/reduce step regularization intelligently on success.

To avoid being trapped in the parent's basin, the advisory mechanism should surround the main trust-region engine with a compact global explorer using feasible mass-transfer moves. Rather than running a continuous smoothed surrogate for global diversification, the explorer uses stochastic annealing of the exact objective itself, with moves chosen especially from active-lag pairs that currently create large term h_i(1-h_j). This aligns exploration with the actual structure of the constraints and objective. Periodically, the explorer seeds new trust-region polish from interacting.

The primary consistent improvement expected is not a mathematical claim of global optimality. This is a nonconvex mixed-variable situation. The improvement source is:

- Direct handling of active max constraints improves local descent below smoothed paths, if the parent is not already an exact active-constrained local optimum.
- Stochastic higher-condition active moves can find a better basin from which the same direct local engine gives a very good repair.
- Resolution transformations use exact finite-grid objective values; a different finite grid can be easier to arrange.

The design also targets speed by returning a validated candidate trajectory and stopping before any deadline.
