1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central new idea

The supplied parent construction is one local point of a nonconvex box-constrained sequence problem. The previous style of approach centered on soft-max objectives, SLSQP smoothing, and simple binary exchange. The present plan replaces that main mechanism with a different local optimization engine:

- use exact piecewise-resolution transfers between nearby sample sizes, especially frequently trying a resolution that is finer than the parent;
- perform a strict one-move coordinate/line minimization in two-dimensional coordinate pairs that preserve the global sum constraint exactly;
- explain every line subproblem as the minimization of an exact max-of-quadratics function, and solve that one-dimensional subproblem by fitting the profile exactly from three known points and searching candidate minima;
- supplement the local line search with binary simulated annealing and multi-size transfer so that the search is not over-constrained to one coarse grid or to one local basin.

The important nontrivial observation is the following: if the optimization is parameterized by one pair of entries whose sum is fixed, all correlation overlaps are exact quadratic functions of the motion parameter. Hence the exact one-dimensional subproblem obtained by holding all other entries fixed is either globally easy to characterize approximately or can be searched by a small candidate set: endpoints, quadratic vertices, and intersections among locally active quadratic functions. This gives a more direct local search than soft-max SLSQP; it uses all lag constraints exactly and preserves feasibility at every step.

1.2 Why this can improve the target

The current objective gap is only a few 1e-4. Therefore improvements can come from three purely numerical mechanisms:

1. Resolution-dependent artifacts: a 48-sample local optimum can become better after interpolation/refinement on a 64- or 96-sample grid because the exact rectangular-cell objective changes with dx and bit alignment.
2. All-direction active lag structure: lowering the maximum profile may require simultaneous mixing of nearby lags. A coordinate pair that preserves the integral can move a small amount of mass in a way that several offsets absorb better than isolated scalar moves.
3. Local non-optimality due to bubble constraints: previous methods accept only smooth gradient/SLSQP or discrete swap moves. Exact quadratic line optimization on pairs is stronger locally; binary annealing provides leaps to new local structures that are recovering prior maxima poorly.

No claim is made that this method always finds the global optimum. It is a bounded-time local/activated stochastic minimization strategy. Every accepted candidate is checked by the authoritative evaluator, and the nearest verified solution is returned.

1.3 Limitations and hypotheses

Important distinctions:

- Fact: the lower move using pair coordinates preserves sum exactly if the two values are constrained to have their current sum constant and both remain in [0,1].
- Fact: for a fixed pair and constant remaining entries, each directed correlation output is indeed a polynomial of degree at most two in the chosen coordinate.
- Hypothesis: active quadratic intersections are the main sources of local minima of the max-of-quadratic line subproblem. A coarse grid plus vertices plus active intersections is robust in practice here.
- Hypothesis: symmetric/anti-symmetric constructions can reduce dimension and sometimes escape coarse grid artifacts better because the sum constraint is automatic. This is not assumed to be globally necessary, so anti-symmetric search is used as a useful variant rather than the only method.
- Limitation: a 1100-second single-core run cannot prove a global bound. It is trying to find a feasible h that improves the previous constant. If the observed gap arises from nonlocal phenomena, one must rely on restarts, resolution changes, and annealing.
