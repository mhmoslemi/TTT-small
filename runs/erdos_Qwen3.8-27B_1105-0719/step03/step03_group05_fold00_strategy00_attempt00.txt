1. Approach and rationale

Central idea. The previous parent reaches about 0.380917 by epigraphic smooth-descent/SLSQP on a fixed medium-resolution step vector. Two bottlenecks remain: (a) a step resolution of about 70–160 samples may discretize the continuous optimum with error around the target gap (1e-4), and (b) active-set/LSE/SLSQP methods are constrained-gradient methods that can stall on a flat surface formed by many near-maximal overlap lags. A different search primitive is exact pairwise line optimization: choose two coordinates `i` and `j`, move them in opposite directions to preserve the integral exactly, and evaluate the true maximum overlap along feasible scalar steps by repeated direct correlation. This uses the *true evaluator* rather than a first-order model or constrained epigraph and can cross flat plateaus where a singular gradient step makes negligible progress. Combine this primitive with (i) multi-resolution interpolation and smoothing starting only from the supplied `initial_h_values`, (ii) anti-symmetric symmetry-reduced epigraph search, and (iii) shortened active-set logarithmic-smoothing runs. Slower methods expose candidates; the exact best candidate at all times is retained and independently repaired/validated. This is a time-adaptive local/global hybrid; it has no proof of global optimality, but escapes common local traps while respecting all evaluator constraints exactly.

Why it can improve the supplied parent. Target improvement requested here is only about 1.2e-4 from the prior value. At n=70, a single discrete correlation count changes C by 0.0286, but fractional h allows much lower quantization. At larger even resolutions, the discrete C may move close to the continuous limit or benefit from optimum-dependent quantization. Multi-resolution pair-line search can find a slightly better minimum on another grid and benefit from continuous-domain interaction through interpolation plus smoothing. The exact pair move ensures that moves can accept small beneficial asymmetry improvements not captured by a single weighted gradient. Anti-symmetry-enforced optimization can be smaller and solve maximum surface more efficiently. All accepted values are filtered by exact objective and by direct feasibility evaluator-like sum check. Known limitations: no global optimality; interpolation has discretization errors; the pair objective may be nonconvex along a pair line; bounded-time search may fail to find the target if the continuous/global surface is insufficiently explored. These are hypotheses and practical heuristics, not proof.
