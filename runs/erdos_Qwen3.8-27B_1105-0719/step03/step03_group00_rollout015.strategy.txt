1. Approach and rationale
1.1 Central idea
The supplied parent is a high-quality construction produced by linearized/active-set methods. The remaining gap to a better number is small, so the main bottleneck is not finding “feasible-but-bad” points but escaping a coordinatewise/tangent-shaped local valley of the true finite-length correlation objective. I therefore use a candidate path whose directions are generated from an exact full-lag smooth-sum surrogate and whose accepted points are then polished by exhaustive two-coordinate exact moves on the evaluator itself.

The surrogate is not a convex relaxation. It is `L_q(corr(h))`, where
`corr(h) = np.correlate(h, 1.0 - h, mode="full")`
and the final objective is always
`C5(h) = (2.0/n) * max(corr(h))`.
The surrogate smoothly emphasizes all correlation lags through powers `corr(k)^q`, not just a hard active-set exact Lagrangian. This is different from the previous construction, which primarily used hard sets of top correlations and linearized LP directions. Here, low and medium correlations influence the search and can “drag” the current maximum downward in configurations that the parent’s top-lag tangent model misses.

After each smooth phase, I apply a genuinely second-order-like exact search move that cannot be captured by full-linearized coordinates: change two coordinates `(p,q)` by the exact sum-preserving substitution
`(h[p], h[q]) -> (h[p] + t, h[q] - t)`
and minimize the original evaluator over `t`. This captures the quadratic terms `h_i(1-h_j)` directly. It is a low-dimensional global coordinate move, judged only by the true evaluator.

The whole workflow is anchored by the parent. If the search fails to improve, it returns the parent. If it improves on a parent-grid construction, it returns that construction. Rescaling / resolution transfer is only a bootstrap: every returned best candidate is re-evaluated with the direct evaluator, and only strict exact decreases are accepted.

1.2 Why this addresses the previous method’s bottleneck
A hard active-set LP first-order model tends to stall when many correlations are tied or near-tied, because it only faults the current top correlations and ignores the surrounding correlation “shelf.” Although the true objective is a maximum, the point becomes flat when a non-top correlation can rise as the current maximum falls. `L_q(corr)` decomposes this flat transition: by retaining near-top correlations in the continuous weights, the smoother surrogate can choose a direction that climbs over a local ridge after leaving, where lower smooth costs were reduced enough to finally lower the maximum.
