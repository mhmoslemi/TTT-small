1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The discrete Erdős overlap problem is the finite-dimensional nonconvex optimization

    minimize_h  score(h) = max_k profile_k(h)
    subject to  0 <= h_i <= 1, sum_i h_i = n/2,

where, for length n and dx = 2/n,

    profile_k(h) = dx * (np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full"))[k].

The current parent construction is already close to a local optimum, so the bottleneck is not another crude linearized active-set descent step, but the combination of three difficult features of the objective:

1. The maximum profile is nondifferentiable whenever two or more lags tie.
2. The feasible set, the box, and the exact-sum equality intersect in a high-dimensional polytope; arbitrary Euclidean updates break mass conservation.
3. The parent basin may contain several active constraints, and purely linear cached LP directions are too local and too easily blocked by the nondifferentiable kink.

The proposed solution is materially different from a purely linearized LP-active descent procedure. It is an annealed smooth-max search on the actual feasible manifold, followed by exact epigraph polishing. The central smoother is the log-mean-exp of the complete correlation profile:

    L_tau(h) = p_max(h) + tau * log(
                   sum_k exp((profile_k(h) - p_max(h)) / tau)
               ),

with tau > 0 decreasing toward zero. Every candidate is then judged by the original exact objective score(h). L_tau is a temperature-controlled aggregate surrogate, but it is not part of the final returned certificate. Final returned constants are always recomputed directly with the supplied evaluator formula.

The actual algorithm consists of:

- Exact projection back onto the feasible polytope {0 <= h <= 1, sum h = n/2} by a waterfill threshold.
- Annealed projected descent/Frank-Wolfe-style line search using the exact gradients of all correlation profile coordinates.
- A randomized annealed jitter mechanism to overcome stuck smooth-optima.
- Multiple structured and random starts at the parent dimension and at nearby dimension transfers.
- Selection among many safe candidates by recomputation with the original objective.
- Final exact epigraph SLSQP polishing, using full or top-active correlation constraints, followed by exact feasibility repair and full evaluator verification.

This is not claimed to prove global optimality. It is a heuristic global/local solver based on transformed plateau reduction, exact projection, and exact objective re-evaluation.

1.2 Why this could improve a near-parent search

A local linearized active-profile LP works well when the active region is a simple polyhedral surface. Near the record, the landscape often contains several distributed plateau lags, coordinated gradients, and hidden kinks. A log-mean-exp temperature keeps the optimizer sensitive to high active lags and also holds information from globally high inactive lags. As tau decreases, the search moves down the plateau structure without losing information permanently when a currently inactive lag becomes critical. Annealing plus jitter gives access to neighboring basins of the nonlinear bilinear problem, while safe waterfill projection guarantees projected candidates remain feasible. The final epigraph step repairs smooth-exact gap by minimizing the actual max-via-extension only at the current best region.
