1. Approach and rationale

The present record is produced by a trust-region linearization of the nonconvex correlations. That is a strong local method, but it still has two visible bottlenecks:

- The exact objective contains bilinear terms \(h_i(1-h_{i-s})\). A purely first-order constrained linearization cannot use the second-order reduction caused by the term \(-d_i d_{i-s}\); line search prevents overshooting, but convergence can become slow or stalled.
- The sampled grid \(n\) is itself a design variable. A continuous near-optimal function is not necessarily best represented on the same fine grid. Several nearby resolutions can have substantially different boundary/discretization errors, and the task explicitly prefers smaller valid grids when the bound does not deteriorate.

The proposed approach is therefore a coherent local-to-semi-global improvement around the supplied parent, not merely a rerun of the old trust-region LP.

Central new primitives:

1. A smoothed,\(\ell_1\)-BOX-plus-equality-constrained log-sum-exp solver, solved as an equality-constrained equality-constrained smooth minimax problem. This avoids the abrupt switching in active constraints and is able to move many nearly-tied lags at once. The exact objective is used only for acceptance and final polishing, so smoothing never changes the formal answer.

2. An exact active-set nonlinearyp-minimax solver. For a small set of currently dangerous lags, it solves the nonlinear subproblem in variables \((h,t)\): minimize \(t\) subject to exact correlation-to-\(t\) inequalities, the integral equality, and the box bounds. This uses the curvature of the bilinear terms much better than a flat linearization. A projected walker tells us whether the new projection moves and then performs a small quasi-Newton deletion step through the inequalities uses filled complements \(r=1-w_w[u]\) for families obtaining new diabetes witch's algebra.

3. A population of feasible vectors at multiple resolutions. Population members are generated by parent-transfer, symmetrization, reflection/complement, resolution morphs, and blockned local improvement. This additional diversity prevents the final bound from being entirely controlled by one start point.

The algorithm is not claimed to be globally optimal. It is an improvement heuristic with exact objective acceptance and a valid best-candidate fallback.
