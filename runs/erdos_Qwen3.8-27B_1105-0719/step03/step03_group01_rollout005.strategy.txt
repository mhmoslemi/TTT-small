1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The previous line of attack used a combination of local active-constraint linearizations, quantized sorting, and simulated-annealing-like randomization. The method proposed here is materially different: it treats the finite-support evaluator exactly as a smooth constrained epigraph problem

minimize t
subject to  t >= dx * C_k(h) for every lag k,
            0 <= h_i <= 1 for every sample,
            sum_i h_i = n/2,

where the constraints are not linearized in the optimization subproblem. The variables being optimized are the epigraph variable t and the sample values h. The objective is simply t, while each finite-support correlation value contributes one nonlinear inequality constraint. This formulation includes the bilinear h_i * (1 - h_j) curvature directly, rather than replacing it by a forced top-active linear approximation or by a discrete stochastic move set.

A feasible point for this epigraph problem with t equal to the maximum correlation is locally equivalent to evaluating the original C_5 bound. The method is still heuristic and nonconvex, but the optimization model is the exact mathematical problem at a state space that a nonlinear constraint solver can interpret. Accepted candidates are always verified with the original evaluator and repaired prior to return; solver output is never trusted unmodified.

1.2 Why this can improve the parent

The bottleneck for reaching a benefit of order 10^{-5} in C_5 is usually not a global random search but the simultaneous handling of several local maxima: active lags whose correlation values are nearly equal and whose gradients actively conflict. A local minimax improvement needs

- the exact constraints for the relevant lags,
- exact gradients of the quadratic correlation expressions,
- the equality sum constraint,
- exact treatment of box-bound sides, and
- verification against the evaluator.
