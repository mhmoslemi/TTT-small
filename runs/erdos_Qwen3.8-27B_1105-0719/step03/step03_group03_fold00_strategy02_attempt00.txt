1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The supplied parent is a legal length `n0` vector with a nearly local-ish score, but earlier approaches concentrate on small active-set linearizations and a few local gradient/convex-relaxation proposals from the same basin. The critical bottleneck is that the true discrete objective is the non-convex minimax of all linear-correlation lags. A direction that lowers a small chosen set of lags sometimes raises a hidden nearby lag; simultaneous shifts of several coordinates are often required, and a low-resolution cell quantization has already imposed a strong discretization floor.

The proposed method changes the search substrate from “linearized small active-set proposals” to “exact one-dimensional minimax over complete feasible affine rays”, combined with a multiscale pool of mechanically controlled feasible starts. For a vector `v` whose entries sum to zero, the candidate family

  `h(α) = h + α v` exactly preserves the integral constraint. Since `np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode='full')` is composed of terms `h_i(1-h_j)`, along this ray every full-lag profile is exactly a quadratic polynomial in `α`. Therefore, given three exact full-profile evaluations at `α=-1,0,1`, the complete objective along the ray is known exactly (up to floating precision) and can be minimized over the feasible `α` interval without solving a separate nonlinear optimization. Every proposed minimizer is then retested by the authoritative evaluator.

This differs substantively from previous plans because the core candidate generator is an exact polynomial line solver over arbitrary feasible simultaneous-transfer directions, not a small active-set linear subproblem or fixed pairwise mass transfer only. It naturally moves many coordinates at once, can pass through complexes of many near-max lags, and is judged by the authoritative finite-support linear-correlation profile. Around this solver, the algorithm:

- starts from the provided parent and from a broad set of exact feasible resampled and randomly perturbed vectors, including some deterministic binned/exchange-sampled discrete seeds;
- performs repeated exact-direction ray searches where direction choices are generated from simple physically motivated sums and random subspace directions;
- escapes plateaus with controlled random feasible jumps and rebased ray descents;
- transfers the best vector to other lawful resolutions by exact repetition / interpolation / averaging, reduces some final lengths by exact block averaging, and accepts translations only if they respect feasibility and improve (or later recover) the exact objective;
- reserves final time for feasibility repair and re-evaluation.

The method is heuristic in global behavior and does not guarantee a certificate that it reaches `0.3808`. It is justified as an engineering route to lower an already near-feasible bound: many reported small bound decrease-by-`~1e-4` tasks come from finding simultaneous feasible transfers and giving resolution choices freedom, not from a slack LP projection alone. To remain safe, every returned candidate is validated directly with the task metric. The final result always falls back to the accepted best parent or another feasible constructed vector within the time budget.

1.2 Bottleneck addressed by this approach

- Nondifferentiability from multiple simultaneous maxima: previous calculated weights over a few active lags can ignore a “future” constraint. A ray search uses the full `2n-1` complete profile, so candidate search never ignores any side lag.
- Simultaneous moves: any vector `v` with zero sum can shift a whole batch of high-mass/no mass simultaneously while keeping the constrained sum exactly, avoiding the problem that pairwise unit moves hit local barriers.
- Finite discretization error: the evaluator is resolution dependent. Exact repeat or inter-sample created candidates and block averaging at lower length test whether multiplying the discretization improves the valid step-function representation. The compute structure here is designed to handle dimension transfers cheaply.
- Feasibility quality: all feasibility adjustments are exact; clipping and sum redistribution happen together, and the final output always answers the scorer they publish.

The method is limited by the compact feasible set being a non-convex bilinear minimax finite problem; a local optimum with respect to the sampled directions is not proven globally optimal. Direction selection and MH-like perturbations outside the basin are deliberately local-explosion mechanisms.
