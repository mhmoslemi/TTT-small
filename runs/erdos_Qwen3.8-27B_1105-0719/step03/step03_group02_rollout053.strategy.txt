1. Approach and rationale
1.1 Central idea
The task is a nonconvex box-simplex minimax problem: minimize the maximum full-support lag correlation of h and 1-h. The previously supplied program works mainly by smoothing the maximum with a log-sum-exp objective, local SLSQP steps in h or logit/h-sigmoid coordinates, linearized active-set LP steps, and pair-exchange polishing. That is a sound local method, but it can stagnate because the objective itself is evaluated through a smoothed surrogate and the final “direction” is only linearized; nonactive lag constraints can become the new active constraints before the direction is accepted.

The proposed method takes the original non-smooth objective as the primary operator inside each trial move. For every generated upward- and downward-feasible direction d satisfying sum(d)=0 and box constraints, the actual function
F(alpha)=max_k dx * correlate((h + alpha d), (1 - h - alpha d), full)[k]
is minimized along alpha. Thus each trial move sees all lag constraints exactly along the path, not a smoothed surrogate or only the linearization of selected active lags. The algorithm has three components:

    (a) A feasible-edge, projected-gradient, and active-LP direction generator.
    (b) Joint exact line minimization for every direction over its full box-feasible alpha interval.
    (c) Multi-start, multi-resolution, and symmetry/reflection seed transformations, followed by strict acceptance from the original evaluator.

The central direct operator is different from merely optimizing a log-sum-exp surrogate: candidate validity is judged by the exact evaluator after projection, and every move direction is scored by the exact maximum-correlation profile along that edge before being used as a new local iterate. This addresses the local-stagnation bottleneck where a smoothing surrogate gives a direction that is attractive under the surrogate but changes the true maximum in an uncontrolled way.

The method is intentionally heuristic and non-global. It will not claim a global optimum. Every retained candidate must be a strictly better exact score. A validated parent or fallback construction is always retained for final return.

1.2 Materials and expected gain
