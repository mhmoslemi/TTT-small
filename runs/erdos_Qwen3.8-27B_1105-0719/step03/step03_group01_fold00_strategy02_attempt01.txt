1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The task is to minimize the exact discrete minimax quantity
`C(h) = max_s corr_s(h, 1-h) * dx`, where `corr` is the finite-support linear correlation used by `np.correlate(h, 1-h, "full")`. The supplied parent is already in a narrow local basin. The previous style of attack (active-set linear LP plus general smoothing/perturbation) stops when the linearized active objective has no descent direction inside the current pair of constraint-bound cells, even though an exact non-linear feasible move may reduce the true maximum.

The proposed program is deliberately different in its primary data structure and transformations:

- It searches on exact-sum integer level grids `a_i / M`, for many grid sizes `M`, using constrained Metropolis moves that transfer one level unit from one sample to another. These moves preserve `sum(h) = n/2` and `0 <= h_i <= 1` exactly without continuous repair, projection, or clipping after every proposal. This is a genuinely different feasible space than the continuous LP family from the supplied parent.

- It promotes good integer-level candidates by a strong, exact, non-linear line descent: for a pair of indices `(dec, inc)`, it tests the family `h[dec] -= t`, `h[inc] += t` over a feasible interval of `t` and evaluates the exact evaluator directly at edge, quarter, endpoint and midpoint transfers. This can jump over nonconvex parts of the transfer curve that a first-order LP sees as no linear improvement, because the true objective on that curve is a possibly non-unimodal quadratic.

- The line descent is directed toward active correlation lags. At every polish pass, it computes the full exact raw correlation vector, identifies top lag indices, extracts the largest individual pair terms responsible for those lags, and makes the exact move that directly reduces the high product term (decrease the first offset weight; increase the second-offset complement variable).

- Global exploration at zero feasibility cost uses cyclic shifts and reversals of existing valid arrays. These operators preserve exact mass and box bounds exactly, but they change the linear interval endpoints of the correlation shift windows. They supply a different boundary arrangement and are a cheap way to leave a local arrangement without any return-risk repair.

The central hypothesis, not a proof: the current local minimum is boundary-sensitive and somewhat quantization-sensitive; the exact-sum level-grid neighborhood plus targeted quadratic pair descent can lower the true max overlap by `~1e-4` without using the continuous LP active-set formulation used in earlier routines.

1.2 Why this may improve the parent

The supplied discrete construction has `n=126`, and the normalization integral corresponds to exactly 63 “units”; lattice states are therefore exact discretizations of the measure constraint. Level-grid moves examine arrangements distorted by assuming continuous sample values can rearrange infinitesimally. In integers, a move of unit size `1/M` is a finite literal pixel move. Targeting active-pair terms uses the true optimizer structure: for a shift lag currently at its c5 max, the largest terms are of the form `h[j]*(1-h[i])` with `j = i + shift`. 1.4 Limitations
