1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

Construct and optimize structured analytic arrays instead of treating the problem only as an unconstrained local search in the original `n`-dimensional box.

The supplied parent at `n = 70` is very close to the target `0.3808`, and the parent value `0.380917` is close to the continuous upper bound obtained by the squared sine function
\[
h(x)=\sin^2(\pi x/2)=\frac{1-\cos(\pi x)}{2},\qquad x\in[0,2].
\]
This shape has integral one and gives key discrete shift profiles in the same range. The new plan exploits three properties not emphasized by the previous implementation:

1. Exact discrete mean preservation using midpoint samples and integer Fourier modes. For midpoint samples
   \[
   x_i=\frac{2(i+1/2)}{n},\qquad i=0,\ldots,n-1,
   \]
   the discrete sum of `cos(pi*q*x_i)` and `sin(pi*q*x_i)` is zero whenever `q` is a positive integer smaller than `n`, up to floating roundoff. Therefore
   \[
   h_i=\frac{1-c(\cos(\pi q x_i)+s(\sin(\pi q x_i)))}{2}
   \]
   gives `sum(h) = n/2` automatically, without adaptive clipping.
2. Global exploration over compact families (single and low-dimensional trigonometric families, phase/frequency/amplitude sweeps, and algebraic seedings) rather than only local active-set descent from the parent.
3. Exact acceptance only: every internal lower-dimensional map may be a nonconvex relaxation or projection, but returned candidates are accepted strictly by the task evaluator
   \[
   S(h)=\max_m \left[\texttt{np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")}\times \frac{2}{n}\right].
   \]

The new primary search mechanism is a deterministic parameter-space minimax optimization over feasible mapped Fourier arrays, followed by full-resolution exact minimax epigraph polishing. The previous program mainly used local linear/subproblem machinery in original coordinates. Here, a low-dimensional analytic manifold supplies many diverse basins that are known to express the sine-square bound, and all remaining error is reduced directly with exact profiles.

1.2 Why this addresses the bottleneck

The supplied parent is only about 17 parts in `10,000` above the goal. A small nonlinear perturbation of a known-shaped harmonic can be sufficient, but the search must explore phase, wavenumber, and low-harmonic coefficients rather than only unknown coordinate descent. The root-of-unity/sine construction gives feasible candidates without repair, so the internal objective does not have to fight infeasible-to-feasible projections. This increases exploration quality while retaining exact evaluation discipline.

Limitations and honest assumptions:

- The nonconvex problem is not solved globally. The structure gives diverse feasible constructions and exact candidate acceptance. It does not certify global optimality.
- The exact discrete score of a sampled sine-square may be above the continuous value due to finite resolution or aliasing. The actual geometric objective is always used to select final candidates.
- The low-dimensional map may not express the optimal high-frequency discrete pattern. Full high-dimensional exact polishing provides a fallback for the residual mismatch and for moderate improvement beyond low-dimensional models.
- Starting from many `n` is mathematically allowed because any valid length `n` is a step function construction. If two valid length constructions exist, keep the lower exact score, with ties broken by shorter length.

2. Mathematical specification

2.1 Exact representation and evaluator

Each candidate has length `n` (handle `n>=16`; otherwise construct fallback length 40). Define
\[
dx=\frac{2}{n},\qquad x_i=\frac{2(i+1/2)}{n}.
\]
The raw constraints are:
\[
0\le h_i\le 1,\qquad \sum_i h_i=\frac{n}{2}.
\]
The authoritative optimization objective is exactly:
\[
S(h)=\max_j \left[\left(\texttt{np.correlate}(h,1-h,\texttt{"full"})_j\right)dx\right].
\]
This matches the evaluator. Never replace it by circular convolution, autocorrelation of `2h-1`, or a theorem without accounting for all boundary lags used by `np.correlate`.

For selected local models, define the profile
\[
P(h)=\texttt{np.correlate}(h,1-h,\texttt{"full"})dx,
\qquad m=2n-1.
\]
The task is
\[
\min_{h} \max_j P_j(h).
\]

2.2 Feasibility repair without destroying box constraints

Use one joint feasibility projection for arbitrary vectors. It clips directly and then adapts entries in order of spare feasibility capacity.

Algorithm `make_feasible(v)`:

1. Convert to one-dimensional float64, replace nonfinite entries with 0.5, clip to `[0,1]`, and form `delta = (n/2)-sum(v)`.
2. If `delta > 0`, compute slack `s_i=1-v_i`, choose indices sorted by descending slack, add to each entry the minimum of remaining `delta` and slack. Stop when remaining is near machine epsilon.
3. If `delta < 0`, use remaining `-delta`, choose indices sorted by ascending `v_i`, subtract the minimum of remaining and `v_i`.
4. If any residual remains, choose `k=argmax(1-v_k)` if residual is positive or `k=argmax(v_k)` if negative, set `v_k=clip(v_k+residual, 0,1)`. Clip the whole array again.
5. Return a copy. Use this before and after every non-mapped candidate. For basis candidates (`2.3`) use it only as a final numerical safety projection; if it changes the candidate, it automatically becomes a feasible candidate.

2.3 Low-dimensional exact-feasible Fourier maps

Build two types of mean-zero columns for every positive integer frequency `q<n`:
\[
c_{q,i}=\cos(\pi q x_i),\qquad s_{q,i}=\sin(\pi q x_i).
\]
The corresponding sums vanish in exact arithmetic for `q` not a nonzero multiple of `n`; for odd vectors in control, enforce exact mean by directly centering raw rows:
\[
r_i\leftarrow r_i-\operatorname{mean}(r).
\]
After centering, `sum(r)=0` under the usual numerical roundoff. A scalar residual caused by floating arithmetic is handled by final projection.

Parameterized construction:

1. Let `beta_i` be raw coefficients.
   For each estimated `beta_i`, form `r_i = sum(config_i)`.

3. If `max(abs(r))>1`, rescale all values to hold `max <=1`.

5. If tiny numerical tolerance triggers `r>=1e-15`, use `make_feasible(h)` at the very last time. Avoid clipping-then-rescaling separately.
