## 1. Approach and rationale

The central idea is to use multi-resolution optimization combined with particle swarm optimization (PSO) to escape local minima that gradient-based methods often fall into. Unlike gradient descent which focuses on local improvements, PSO explores the entire search space by maintaining a population of candidate particles that move towards the best solutions found so far. The key improvements over the current approach are:  
- **Multi-resolution strategy**: Candidates are optimized at coarser grids (2^k for k=6..10) before being refined at higher resolutions. This allows the PSO to operate in lower-dimensional spaces where optimization is easier, then transferring the best solution to higher resolutions.  
- **Adaptive PSO parameters**: We dynamically adjust the inertia weight and acceleration constants to balance exploration and exploitation, gradually shifting from wide exploration to focused search as better solutions are found.  
- **Feasibility repair**: A specialized projection method ensures all candidates satisfy both the sum constraint (∑h = n/2) and box constraints (0≤h[i]≤1) through a combination of scaling and clipping.  

This approach addresses the limitations of gradient-based methods by leveraging population-based search, which is more likely to find the global minimum. The algorithm's robustness comes from its ability to escape local optima through particle interactions and diversity maintenance.
