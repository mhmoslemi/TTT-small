## Approach and rationale

The core idea is to use a locally optimal solution obtained by Gurobi to generate a parametrized family of functions and then tune the parameters to find the best solution. This approach leverages Gurobi for integer programming to find a high-quality starting point, then refines it by optimizing continuous parameters.

The main rationale is that the Erdős minimum overlap problem is combinatorial in nature, and a mixed-integer programming formulation can provide a strong upper bound, which subsequently guides the search for continuous solutions. By parametrizing the solution and optimizing the parameters continuously, we can find a better solution than pure integer programming.

Limitations:
- Gurobi requires commercial license (though this might be available in academic settings)
- The parametrization must be designed carefully to not miss the optimal structure
- Combinatorial components (like avoiding certain overlaps) are approximated by continuous relaxation
- Performance may depend on the specific parametrization chosen

Important considerations:
- The formulation replaces periodic patterns by explicit constraints
- Continuous parameters control the distribution rather explicitly specifying each bit
- The transition from discrete solution to continuous relies on function space embeddings
- We maintain validity of the optimization at all times

## Mathematical specification

We represent h(x) as a step function with fixed height 1, defined by the positions of n $n$ transitions within [0,2]. Let $0 < x_1 < x_2 > ... > x_n < 2$ be the transition points. Then:

$$
h(x)=\begin{cases} 
0 & x \le x_1 \\
1 & otherwise 
\end{cases}
$$

However, to allow continuous values we modify to:
$$ h(x) = \sigma(g(x) - t) $$
where $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function, $g(x)$ is a piecewise linear function, and $t$ is a threshold parameter.

The optimization formulation:

Variables:
- n transition points $x_1, x_2, ... , x_n \in [0,2]$ with ordering constraint
- Threshold $t$
- Scale parameter $s$
- Base parameter $b$

Constraints:
1. The function $h(x)$ must have an integral of exactly 1
2. The function is increasing ($1$)
3. The transitions $x_1,...,x_n$ are strictly increasing

Objective:
Minimize $\max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx$
