**1. Approach and Rationale**

We propose a population-based optimization method combined with local search to escape local optima. The core idea is to use a population of continuous functions, each evolved to minimize the overlap integral $C_5$. We build upon the provided initial $h(x)$ by re-sizing it to higher resolutions and applying randomized local optimizations.

Key improvements:
- **Resolution Scaling**: Upscale the parent function to higher grid points for finer detail.
- **Population Diversification**: Start with multiple initialized candidates including Thue-Morse, Legendre sequence, and truly random.
- **Adaptive Local Search**: Use projected gradient descent with momentum adapted to individual candidates.

This approach probabilistically explores different behaviors that might outperform the fixed-gradient descent in the parent code, and uses population diversity to avoid premature convergence.

**2. Mathematical Specification**

*Variables:* $h \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{points}}$ where $0 \leq h[i] \leq 1$ $\forall$i and $\sum h[i] \cdot dx = 1$ (with $dx = 2.0 / n_{points}$).

*Objective:* Minimize $C_5 = \max_k dx \cdot |\text{full\_correlation}(k)|$ where `full_correlate` is the discrete convolution of `h` and `1-h`.

Constraints:
1. $\sum_{i} h[i] \cdot dx = 1$
2. $0 \leq h[i] \leq 1$ $\forall$i

*Representation:* Step function on a grid, up-sampled from the provided $12 \rightarrow 64$ parent.
