Let me think about what the optimal sequence looks like. We want to minimize max_k conv[k] where conv = a*a (full).

If we think of a as a probability distribution on {0, ..., n-1}, then conv[k]/S² is the "probability" that two independent draws from this distribution sum to k. We want to minimize the maximum of this "sum distribution".

The sum distribution of two independent discrete uniform random variables on {0, ..., n-1} is triangular, with max = n/(n-1)² ≈ 1/n. So f ≈ 2n * 1/n = 2.

To minimize the max of the sum distribution, we want a distribution that makes the sum as uniform as possible. This is related to "perfect hash" or "sum-free" structures.

For n points, the sum takes values in {0, ..., 2n-2}. We want P(X+Y = k) to be as uniform as possible, i.e., close to 1/(2n-1) for all k.

For the discrete uniform distribution, P(X+Y=k) = (k+1)/n² for k < n and (2n-1-k)/n² for k ≥ n. The max is n/n² = 1/n, and the target uniform value is 1/(2n-1). The ratio is (2n-1)/n ≈ 2.

To achieve the uniform sum distribution, we'd need a distribution that's very non-uniform. For example, if we use a distribution that's concentrated at specific points, the sum distribution could be more uniform.

Actually, for the continuous case, if X has a uniform distribution on [0,1], then X+Y has a triangular distribution on [0,2]. To make X+Y uniform on [0,2], we'd need X to have a distribution with a specific density.

In the discrete case, this is related to "aperiodic" or "costas" constructions, but for nonnegative sequences it's different.
