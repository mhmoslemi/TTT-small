For any nonnegative sequence a:

The score is 2n·max(conv)/sum².

For the constant sequence a_j = c: max(conv) = nc², sum = nc. Score = 2n·nc²/(nc)² = 2.

I proved that for symmetric sequences, score ≥ 2 (by Cauchy-Schwarz on conv[n-1]).

Let me think about this more generally. The problem is:
minimize 2n·max_k conv_k(a,a) / (Σa)²  subject to a ≥ 0.

Equivalently (by scale invariance): minimize max_k conv_k(a,a) / (Σa)² with Σa = 1 (normalization).

This is: minimize max_k conv_k(a,a) subject to a ≥ 0, Σa = 1.

The max of the convolution is the "energy" of the signal in a certain sense. We want to minimize the peak energy.

This is related to the problem of finding sequences with minimal peak-to-average power ratio (PAPR) in the time domain, which is dual to the frequency domain PAPR problem.

For the linear convolution (not circular), the "average" is sum(conv)/(2n-1) = (Σa)²/(2n-1).

We want max(conv) to be as close to the average as possible. The minimum possible max(conv) is at least the average = (Σa)²/(2n-1).

If we could achieve max(conv) = (Σa)²/(2n-1) (perfectly flat convolution), the score would be 2n/(2n-1) ≈ 1.
