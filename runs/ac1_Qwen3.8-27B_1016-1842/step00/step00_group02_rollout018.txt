I think the answer involves a sequence that's roughly:
- Small values at one end
- A "bump" or plateau at the other end
- The convolution peak is at an off-center position where the overlap involves the bump with the tail

For the specific case of the "state of the art" approach with LP, the idea is to iteratively find the best "dual" direction. But for our purposes, a simpler gradient-based approach should work.

Let me think about the gradient approach more carefully:

The objective is F(a) = 2*n*max_k c[k] / S².

Let k* = argmax c[k]. Then:
∂F/∂a[j] = 2n/S³ * (S * ∂c[k*]/∂a[j] - 2*c[k*])

And ∂c[k*]/∂a[j] = a[k*-j] (if 0≤k*-j≤n-1) + a[k*-j-n+1] (if 0≤k*-j-n+1≤n-1)

This is O(n) to compute given k*.

Algorithm:
1. Start with a (e.g., the parent, or a better initialization)
2. Loop:
   a. Compute c = fft_convolve(a, a) [O(n log n)]
   b. Find k* = argmax c
   c. Compute gradient g[j] = 2n/S³ * (S * (a[k*-j]+a[k*-j-(n-1)]) - 2*c[k*]) [O(n)]
      (with proper bounds checking)
   d. Take step: a_new = a - lr * g, clip to [0, 1000]
   e. If F(a_new) < F(a), accept; else reduce lr
   f. Repeat until deadline

The step size lr needs to be adapted. Since the problem is non-convex (max of linear functions), we might need to use subgradient methods.

Actually, there's a subtlety: the "max" makes the function non-smooth. When multiple k values are near the maximum, the subgradient is the average of the gradients at those k values. But for simplicity, we can just use the argmax.

Another approach: since the score is scale-invariant, we can fix S = 1 (or any constant) and just minimize max_k c[k]. This simplifies the gradient:
∂(max c[k])/∂a[j] = a[k*-j] + a[k*-j-(n-1)] (with bounds)

And we just do gradient descent on max c[k] with the constraint sum(a) = S and a ≥ 0.

But the nonnegativity constraint makes this tricky. Projection (clipping to 0) after each step is a standard approach but may not be optimal.
