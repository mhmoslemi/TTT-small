1. **Gradient-based search**: Compute the gradient of the score with respect to each coefficient. The gradient involves the convolution, which can be done in O(n log n) via FFT. But we can only use 1 CPU and the constraint says no dense matrices. FFT is fine (O(n log n)).

2. **Subgradient method**: Since max_b = max_k (f*f)(k), the subgradient w.r.t. f is 2*(f * g) where g is a delta at the argmax. We want to minimize 2n*max_b/sum_a². The subgradient direction would push us to reduce the autocorrelation at the peak lag.

3. **Active-set / LP approach**: The strategy mentions an LP approach. At each step, we identify which autocorrelation lags are at the maximum, then find the direction that reduces those specific lags while maintaining nonnegativity.

4. **Spectral approach**: Since the autocorrelation is the inverse FFT of |F|², we can work in the frequency domain. We want |F|² to be as flat as possible (to make the autocorrelation flat). This is related to "flat spectrum" sequence design.
