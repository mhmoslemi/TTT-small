This suggests that for larger n, a monotone (or unimodal) decreasing sequence might give good results. The idea is that the autocorrelation peak is at the center (lag n-1), and by making the sequence decrease, we reduce the central peak while the edge peaks (C[0] = a[0]^2, C[2n-2] = a[n-1]^2) stay small.

Actually, for a decreasing sequence a[0] > a[1] > ... > a[n-1], the autocorrelation:
- C[0] = a[0]^2 (largest single element squared)
- C[n-1] = sum a[j]*a[n-1-j] (central, sums products of "large*small")
- C[2n-2] = a[n-1]^2 (smallest)

The challenge is to balance C[0] and C[n-1]. If a[0] is too large, C[0] dominates. If the sequence is too flat, C[n-1] is large.

For the n=4 example, [0.35, 0.3, 0.2, 0.15]: C[0]=0.1225, C[2]=0.23, C[3]=0.225. The maximum is C[2]=0.23, not C[0]. So the "internal" lags matter more.

For a decreasing sequence, a[j]*a[n-1-j] has largest products in the middle (j ≈ n/2). So C[n-1] is dominated by the central terms.
