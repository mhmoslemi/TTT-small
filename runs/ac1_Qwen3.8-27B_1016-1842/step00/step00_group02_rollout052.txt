The problem: for high powers, the last element dominates, and its self-convolution a[n-1]^2 = (n^p)^2 = n^{2p} gives a huge peak at lag 2n-2.

So the max is NOT at the center for high powers! It's at the edge (lag 2n-2) where the largest element appears.

For a[i] = (i+1)^p:
(a*a)[2n-2] = a[n-1]^2 = n^{2p} (approximately).
(a*a)[n] ≈ n^{2p+1} * B(p+1,p+1) (the center).

The ratio: center/edge ≈ n^{2p+1} * (p!)^2/(2p+1)! / n^{2p} = n * (p!)^2/(2p+1)!.

For p=3: n * 36/5040 = n/140. For n=6962: ≈ 49.7. So center >> edge.
For p=4: n * 576/362880 = n/630. For n=6962: ≈ 11.1. Center >> edge.

But for small n (n=4, p=3): n/140 = 4/140 < 1. So edge > center for small n.

For n=6962, p=4: center/edge ≈ 6962/630 ≈ 11. So center is 11x the edge. The max IS at the center for large n.
