### Approach and Rationale

We approach the problem of minimizing the AC1 upper bound by focusing on sequences that are sparse in the convolution domain. Our central idea is that sequences with minimal autocorrelation energy will exhibit a lower AC1 bound. We specifically target sequences that can be represented as a combination of few intervals of constant height. 

The key insight is that maximizing the number of zero-valued intervals while ensuring the convolution maxima are minimized leads to improved performance. We use a parameterized form of a piecewise constant sequence (discrete triangular distribution) which inherently concentrates energy away from the center. This approach not only reduces the maximum convolution value but also ensures the sequence remains feasible due to its sparsity properties.

The method has several limitations. First, we use a fixed interval count per block (typically 10 or 20), which might not capture more complex optimal distributions. Second, the exact optimization is non-convex, so we rely on iterative refinement rather than analytical guarantees. Third, we use simpler interval selection strategies rather than adaptive ones, though this maintains clarity over computational cost tradeoffs.

### Mathematical Specification

Given a sequence length n (often 6733 if a parent sequence exists, else 256):
- We represent the sequence as m non-overlapping intervals, each with constant height.
- Let 0 ≤ c₀ < c₁ < ... ≤ c_{m} ≤ n be the interval boundaries (inclusive indices).
- Define segment heights h₀, h₁, ..., h_{m-1} ∈ [0, 1000] and accumulated sums Sᵢ = ∑_{j=0}^{i-1} hᵢ₊₁ * (c_{j+1} - c_j).
- The total sum is S = h₀*c₀ + S₁ + ... + (h_{m-1})*(n - c_{m-1}).

The AC1 bound is computed via:
L = 2*n*max( |full convolution of segments| ) / S²

Our optimization surface is governed by:
- Feasibility: hᵢ ≥ 0 for all i; boundaries 0 ≤ cᵢ ≤ n.
- Objective: minimize L by iteratively relocating boundaries and rescaling heights.
