1. Approach and rationale
The central idea is to minimize the AC1 upper bound by exploring sequences that exhibit a flat auto     correlation spectrum over a wide range of lags. This is achieved through a two-phase optimization: first, an analytic initialization based on Fourier-domain properties to generate promising seeds, and second, a local refinement using stochastic gradients and momentum to polish the solution. The rationale stems from the observation that sequences with controlled frequency content (analytic functions) often yield smaller AC1 bounds compared to random or heuristic methods, as their complex Fourier transforms help reduce peak autocorrelation values. However, analytic families focus on local minima, so local search is crucial to escape these and find the global minimum. The algorithm materializes by combining an explicit frequency-based model with implicit optimization via directional derivatives, addressing the non-convexity of the problem head-on.

  2. Mathematical specification
  - Representation: The sequence a is represented as a real vector of length n, with elements in [0, 1000] and sum at least 0.01.
  - Objective: Minimize B(a) = 2n * max_k (convolve(a, a)[k]) / (sum(a))^2, where the convolution is performed with mode="full" as per the evaluator.
  - Mathematical Equivalents:
    - Preconditioned form: Let s = sum(a), then minimize the normalized bound B_normalized = max_k ( |convolve(a, a)[k]| ) / s.
    - Fourier-relation: The AC1 bound is related to the spectral properties; specifically, the bound is minimized when the sequence has a concentrated frequency spectrum that limits excessive autocorrelation peaks.
  - Constraints:
    - Non-negativity: a_i ≥ 0 for all i.
    - Bound clipping: 0 ≤ a_i ≤ 1000 for all i.
