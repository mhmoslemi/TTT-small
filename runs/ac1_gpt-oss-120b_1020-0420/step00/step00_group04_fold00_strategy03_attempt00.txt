Approach and Rationale

The core idea behind this strategy is to employ simulated annealing (SA), a probabilistic optimization technique inspired by physical annealing processes, to minimize the AC1 upper bound. Unlike local search methods (e.g., block-perturbation or gradient descent), which often get trapped in local minima, SA introduces controlled randomness and occasional acceptance of worse solutions to explore broader regions of the search space. This balance is crucial for finding a global minimum in non-convex problems like this one. The high-dimensional, coupled nature of the convolution operation (requiring full evaluation) is addressed by using sparse, linear-memory perturbations based on block operations, as seen in the previous implementation but adapted for a temperature-scheduled exploration. By starting from a high exploration state and gradually contracting, SA can repeatedly scrutinize the problem's complex topography.

The main limitation of this approach is its dependence on the evaluator for every candidate assessment, which can be computationally expensive due to the full convolution (O(n^2) operation via `evaluate_sequence`). However, given that NumPy functions are vectorized (though convolution is O(n)), this is mitigated by the block-perturbation technique, which modifies only small portions (width ≈ n^0.9) of the sequence at a time, keeping auxiliary storage linear in n. SA is not guaranteed to find the global minimum, but its theoretical properties and practical performance on such problems (e.g., optimization of autocorrelation bounds) offer a plausible path to improvement beyond the initial 2.000 upper bound.

Mathematical Specification

The state variable is the sequence \( x \in \mathbb{R}^n \) with \( n = 3809 \), non-negative and bounded by 1000, ensuring positivity and no burstiness via post-processing clamping. The objective function to minimize is:

\[ \text{score}(x) = \frac{2n \cdot \max_{k=0,\ldots,2n-1} |\text{full convolution}|}{(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i)^2} \]

derived directly from `evaluate_sequence`, with a finite sum enforced. The search space is defined by box constraints \( 0 \leq x_i \leq 1000 \) for all i. The perturbation step relies on a matrix-free, sparse operation that generates a neighbor by randomly selecting a contiguous segment (start and width) and applying a log-normal multiplicative scaling or a reflection (with probability 0.35). This maintains feasibility without dense storage. The optimization direction is stochastic, accepting impairment with probabilities based on a Boltzmann distribution guided by temperature, reflecting the need for convergence in a non-convex landscape.
