### 1. Approach and Rationale

This approach generates candidates by strategically placing high values (close to 1000) at specific positions within the fixed sequence length to minimize the maximum autocorrelation peak while ensuring the total sum of the sequence remains sufficiently large (≥0.01). The key bottleneck in previous approaches is the lack of structural optimization for autocorrelation peaks. Our method prompts a structured, optimization-engineered candidate rather than relying on local perturbations. By maximizing the denominator (sum²) relative to the maximum convolution (which grows superlinearly), we lower the AC1 score. This is achieved by forcing non-overlapping high-values, preventing multiplicative auto-correlation penalties.

### 2. Mathematical Specification

**Variables & Representations:**
- Input: Parent sequence length `n = 4806`.
- Output: Coefficients ` a_i ∈ [0, 1000]` for `i ∈ [0, n-1]`.
- Objective: Minimize `score = 2n * max(convolve(a, mode="full")) / (sum(a))²`.

**Constraints:**
- Non-negativity: `∀i, a_i ≥ 0`.
- Finiteness: No NaNs or Infs.
- Sum: `sum(a) ≥ 0.01`.
- Max value: `∀i, a_i ≤ 1000`.

**Derivations:**
- The score increases with the maximum autocorrelation (numerator) and decreases with the total sum (denominator). Thus, we aim to maximize the sum by packing high values just below 1000 (999.9) while minimizing peak autocorrelation.
- Influence matrix: The full convolution matrix is defined by lag-dependent overlap. To reduce impact, high-value clusters are isolated by zeros.

**Algorithm:** We implement a genetic-inspired optimizer (`GeneticPeakFinder`) that mutates random bits (though unused for performance) to simulate sparse placement of high values. The optimizer uses:
- Crossover + Mutation (matrix-free).
- Fitness evaluation via `evaluate_sequence`.

### 3. Implementation Procedure

**Steps:**
1. **Initialization:** Use `height_sequence_1` (if valid) or uniform low-values. (See fallback).
2. **Optimization:** Execute `GeneticPeakFinder.optimize_chain`, which:
   - Starts from random but feasible points.
   - Uses a controlled crossover/mutation schedule.
   - Tracks best fitness (`score`) for chains of `CHAINS=5` candidates.
   - Keeps a running history of top candidates (`best_score`, `best_chain`).
3. **Termination:** After `NUM_ITERATIONS=20` chain iterations, return best candidate.
4. **Validation:** Uses `evaluate_sequence` for exact fitness.

- `sample_ancestor(n, index)`: Generates pseudorandom parent candidate.
