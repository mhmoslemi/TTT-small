The key idea is to decompose the sequence into multiple sparse peaks (bursts) that are optimized to minimize the maximum autocorrelation while keeping the total sum sufficiently high.

### Approach and Rationale
The central idea is to represent the sequence as a sum of a few sparse, localized, and high-amplitude bursts (peaks) with a fixed total energy. By controlling the positions and amplitudes of these bursts, we can design sequences that have a lower AC1 upper bound. The method is based on the rationale that:

1. **Non-overlapping Peaks:** Placing peaks far apart minimizes the chance of convolution peaks from multiple peaks adding up, which can lead to a high maximum autocorrelation.
2. **Localized Energy:** Concentrating energy in short bursts allows for a lower peak in the autocorrelation function compared to distributing energy more uniformly.
3. **Energy Sum Constraint:** The denominator in the AC1 score is the square of the sum of the sequence. By having sufficiently many peaks (each contributing a fixed energy), the sum increases, which helps in lowering the score.

This approach differs from local perturbation methods by focusing on a structured, global optimization of the sequence's energy distribution. Instead of small, local changes, we design the sequence from scratch by positioning and shaping the bursts.
