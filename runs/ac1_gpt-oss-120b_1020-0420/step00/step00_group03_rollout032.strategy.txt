## 1. Approach and Rationale

The central idea is to leverage gradient information from the AC1 bound to drive an optimization process towards a sequence with lower autocorrelation peaks, while ensuring the constraints and using a spectral decomposition for efficiency. We propose a frequency-domain optimization that transforms the sequence into its spectral coefficients, allowing us to manipulate the autocorrelation properties directly. This approach differs from previous block perturbations by focusing on global frequency tuning, enabling faster convergence to regions with reduced AC1 values. The gradient-based method uses projected subgradient steps to adjust the sequence, similar to curve-fitting norms, and incorporates a fallback to the parent structure if optimization stalls. This method addresses the bottleneck of high autocorrelation peaks that limit the AC1 minimization in uniform or random sequences.

**Important Limitations and Constraints:**
- The optimization cannot assume a specific form for the optimal sequence and relies on the prov-descent in the objective via gradient steps.
- Gradient computation is approximate due to numerical finite differences, limited to a small epsilon size, to monitor the CVXPY or solver tolerance.
- The algorithm explicitly handles non-negativity and clipping constraints to maintain candidate validity.
- For runtime memory constraints with n~2500, we limit the FFT-based operations to compute gradients in h for high covariance-based caching.

## 2. Mathematical Specification

The objective to minimize is the AC1 bound: \(F(\mathbf{a}) = 2n \cdot \max_{k=0}^{2n-2} (\text{convolve}_{\text{full}}(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{a}))[k] / (\text{sum}(\mathbf{a}))^2\), evaluated by the given function.

To handle scale and optimization, we fix the sum \(\sum \mathbf{a} = S\) to a target value \(S_{\text{target}} = 1000\) (the maximum coefficient; adjusted for large n if needed, but we use scaling). The optimization variable is the perturbation vector \(\mathbf{d}\) such that \(\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{a}_{\text{ref}} + \mathbf{d}\), where \(\mathbf{a}_{\text{ref}}\) is the parent sequence's scaled version or during initial optimization, we set \(\mathbf{a} = S_{\text{target}} \cdot \mathbf{x}\) with \(\mathbf{x} \in L^\infty(0,1)\). Alternatively, use mean value optimization; however, n large makes full gradient infeasible. Thus, we use a projected gradient approach with numerical gradients for efficiency.

Define the decision variables via FFT decomposition for n=2535 (odd). FFT of \(\mathbf{a}\) gives complex coefficients, but for real signals, \(X_k = X_{N-k}\) for \(k=1\) to \(N-1\). We optimize the real part for low frequencies and the DC component. The gradient of \(F\) is approximated by finite differences.

For the optimization problem, we minimize \(F(\mathbf{a})\) subject to \(\mathbf{a} \geq 0\), \(\mathbf{a} \leq 1000\) (after scaling to sum 1000), and \(\sum \mathbf{a} \geq 0.01\).

The objective is reduced by manipulating the spectrum to lower autocorrelation peaks. The gradient descent is defined as:
- At iteration step, compute \(\mathbf{a}_{t+1} = \text{Proj}_{[0,1000]} (\mathbf{a}_t - \gamma_t \nabla F(\mathbf{a}_t))\), with \(\gamma_t\) the step size, and \(\text{Proj}\) the projection onto the box constraint.

For numerical gradient, use central-difference approximation:
