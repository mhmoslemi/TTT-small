1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The supplied parent construction is close to the desired threshold, so the most practical route is a strong feasible local/globalized refinement of the discrete optimization problem. The primary method will be:

- Treat the 0-1 overlap values exactly as quadratic functions of the discrete vector.
- Minimize over the epigraph formulation: variables are the sample vector `h` plus one scalar `t`; constraints require every discrete overlap value to be at most `t`; the objective is to minimize `t` only.
- Supply analytic Jacobians for all constraints instead of using a log-sum-exp or squared-max smoothing surrogate.
- Maintain the true, all-lag, finite-support objective with `np.correlate` after every proposal.
- Use exact feasible repair after every transformed proposal before any acceptance decision.
- Run a small portfolio of feasible starts: the parent, several small feasible perturbations, snapped thresholded versions, and a few coarser domain transfers. Stop with the smallest/exact-score best candidate that is feasible under the original metric.

This is materially different from a smoother that minimizes a penalty surrogate or an unconstrained approximate maximum. It seeks the actual maximum constraint boundary by treating the epigraph constraints as the object of the nonlinear local search. Since each overlap row is a differentiable quadratic, the epigraph subproblem has correct first-order stationarity information.

1.2 Why this can improve the current bound

The exact objective over the discrete grid is

`C5(h) = max(Cy(h))`, where `Cy(h)[r]` is the discretized overlap at one of the `2n-1` full-support lags multiplied by `dx=2/n`.

A direct local method can move the active lags downward while respecting all inactive lags and maintaining `sum(h)=n/2`. 1.3 What is known versus speculative

Justified facts:

- The objective is exactly the maximum of the vectors produced by noncircular finite-support correlation; no cyclic wrap-around is permitted.
- For a fixed DIAG representation, every overlap row is `dx` times a bilinear polynomial in the variables, and its gradient can be derived exactly.
- The feasible set defined by `0 <= h_i <= 1` and `sum(h)=n/2` admits an exact joint repair: a monotone scalar shift followed by clipping and then a one-variable sum correction.

- A global optimum.
- Existence of a convex reformulation.
- That the target value is guaranteed under every parameter tuple.
