1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The current construction is already close to the published record, so a naïve custom projector or mode-only smoothing may not move the score below the target. I propose a materially different certified upper-bound-ramp method: a global quadratic majorant epigraph problem, solved repeatedly as a dense but small convex quadratic program.

For every lag, the overlap score is a quadratic polynomial in the vector h. At the current feasible point y, its exact Taylor expansion on Euclidean h-space is finite, so we can construct a rigorous global quadratic upper approximation whose expansion around y coincides with the first-order term. Since the objective is the maximum over lags, instead of linearizing the active lag maximum or imposing the original bilinear system as a nonconvex epigraph, we replace each lag function by its global quadratic upper support. The resulting epigraph is convex. Because every feasible x in this epigraph satisfies original score <= majorant <= t, any exact-solver solution can have its exact C5 bounded using the original evaluator. This creates monotone, certified descent from the parent when full lag sets and the curvature bound are verified.

This differs from the earlier proposals in being centered on a global upper quadratic majorant, not linear tangent/epigraph truncation nor a first-order-only local LP/linearized descent. It also reuses the premise that the original scoring functions are quadratic, and includes a matrix-supported convex QP formulation.

1.2 Why it may reach below 0.3808

Existing parent h_start is near optimum: the exact active set is difficult to escape with pure projection. Yet full quadratic upper support keeps all lags constrained while giving positive local safe movement freedom. Solving the convex upper envelope may exploit safe offsets within the parent feasible set that exact linear truncation would not prescribe. Active parent differences are tiny, so local stochastic exchange, multi-resolution transfers, and active constraint heuristics form a support engine. They do not claim optimality and only accept candidates with exact evaluation.

1.3 Scope of rigor versus heuristics

The following facts are mathematical: for each score term, constructing a global quadratic upper bound with coefficient >= 2.05x dx yields F(x) <= M(x). Given a candidate such that M_m(x) <= t for all lags, exact C5(x) <= t provided numerical tolerances, gradient orientation, and mass constraints are respected. Therefore a feasible full-lag majorant problem can produce an exact score smaller or equal to the current certified level monotonically when solved without failures and after final evaluation acceptance.

The following are heuristics: stopping updates, active-subset solves without a full certificate, threshold transforms, random moves, dimension forcing, early simulation/SA.
