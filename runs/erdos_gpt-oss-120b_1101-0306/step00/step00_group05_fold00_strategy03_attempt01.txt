1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea and why it addresses the current bottleneck

The exact introduced scoring formula is the maximum of finite-support cross-correlations of a sampled vector `h` with its complement `g = 1 - h`:
```
score(h) = max_lag raw_corr_lag(h) * dx,   dx = 2 / n,   raw_corr_lag(h) = Σ h_i (1-h_j)
```
where each lag includes only indices for which both sampled positions exist. Boundary/at-pieces overlap terms are therefore intrinsic and cannot be replaced by circular correlations, complement sums, or `1 - min autocorrelation`. Every proposal in this plan is judged by the supplied exact evaluator on the concrete feasible `h` actually produced.

The objective is nonconvex: each fixed lag contains terms `h_i(1-h_j) = h_i - h_i h_j`. Hence no local method can be trusted merely because it decreases a smooth or relaxed surrogate. The plan nevertheless exploits the exact lags by treating them three different ways:

1. First-order descent by low-displacement “descent directions” proposed by making the largest value of lag-wise linear models as small as possible. The first-order nonconvex model is solved as a linear program (an LP) in a displacement vector `d`. The resulting step size is then selected from the exact objective, making mistakes only harmless proposal failures.
2. Two fixed-substitution LP proposals: solve a linear LP that chooses a new function while temporarily holding either the old complement fixed or the old function fixed. These produce global together-change of many coordinates, which is not expressible by a pair exchange. They are line-scanned through the exact original `h` and complement.
3. Local exhaustive exact correction using two-step feasible exchange transfers. The LPs are used for proposals while sharp by arrangement into one or two coordinates. The exact scoring is applied at all proposal endpoints and line positions.

The important domain-specific observation is that, once the model is held fixed or replaced by a first-order linear approximation, the minimization of a maximum over all sampled lags is an LP. 1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Problem structure and why the primary method should help

The exact objective to minimize is the maximum, over all sampled shifts, of the finite-support cross-correlation between a sampled vector h and its complement 1-h, multiplied by dx = 2/n. The maximum and the shift-boundary correlations cannot be reduced safely to a circular convolution, to an autocorrelation, or to a lower-level expression involving only total mass. Therefore the returned record must be the exact value computed on one specific feasible vector by the supplied scoring function.

The bottleneck for a slight improvement, for example reducing max_c from about 0.38085 to 0.38080, is in finding a simultaneous direction that lowers several active correlation lags while preserving the box and integral constraints. Pairwise coordinate transfers search only one more direction per proposal and can be too narrow. Projections of smoothed full-dimensional nonlinear surrogates can produce feasible-looking vectors that are unrelated to the finite maximum being minimized.

This plan uses the exact shifted correlations as the optimization geometry. For a current feasible h, it proposes a new feasible vector in three ways:
- A one-step-displacement LP solves a linear-model approximation to every lag, with variables representing simultaneous small fixed-direction还不到 all coordinates. The displacement is then tested as a line passing through exact h against the original output via golden numerical grid line search to achievable exact improvements, not just decreasing a surrogate. Since tests and intended objective agreement determine the step using differences inside active regimes. Often improves propagation through restrictive systems.
