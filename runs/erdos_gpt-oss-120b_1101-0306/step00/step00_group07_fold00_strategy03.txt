1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

Construct a family of feasible candidate step vectors by solving small linear programs that are projections of the original nonconvex problem, then convert every LP proposal into an exact candidate on a feasible one-dimensional chord. The exact candidate acceptance rule is always the task evaluator, so the returned number is a certified upper bound for the feasible vector that is returned.

The method is organized around three ingredients:

1. Direct finite-support overlap terms. Define the exact objective as a maximum of shifted bilinear terms using a lag formula that independently enumerates all full evaluator lags in a fixed order. This gives mathematically unambiguous term indices and coefficients.
2. Fixed-complement linear programs. For a feasible vector h, replace the nonlinear product h(x)(1-h(x+k)) by a term in which the complementary vector is frozen at either 1-h_old or h_old. Every frozen term is linear in the scalar objective variable and in the decision vector. Each LP minimizes the capped target plus an explicit adjustment penalty, subject to the integral constraint and a trust radius. Thus each LP proposal is a simultaneous adjustment of many coordinates, not a pair exchange only.
3. Exact chord correction. Since the feasible set is convex, any feasible current vector and any feasible LP proposal define a feasible segment. The max-over-lag value along that segment is an exact quadratic polynomial envelope at every scalar point. Refine and test candidate points on that exact segment, always recomputing the evaluator only on returned accepted candidates. This converts a rough LP direction into a candidate that may nevertheless fail after an LP/chord cancellation; only exact improvements are stored.

The broader search also uses multi-resolution transfers of the parent and a small pool of diversified feasible ancestors: complement, reversal, averaging, cyclic rotations, resized versions, and a few bounded nan审定 points. This prevents a single parent basin from dominating budget.

1.2 Why this could improve the supplied parent

The supplied parent is likely close to a local constrained optimum. Conventional local searches may be blocked because a pair exchange decreases some active shifted overlaps but immediately worsens others. Classically this is visible from non-smooth maxima. The fixed-complement LP is a convexized approximation that reduces all active or near-active shifted terms within a trust region at once, assigning mass to coordinates as determined by the LP. Its decision vector encodes potentially dozens of fractional transfers that coordinate descent cannot discover by one-spool. The chord exact correction then travels from the current point toward that coordinated direction and chooses a scalar level using the original exact objective along the segment. This combination is specifically designed for blocked non-smooth overlap maximization: coordinate moves alone move in a direction against more adactic objectives, while simultaneous moves by chord linearization can shift across a local corner.

The plan additionally inspects reduced resolution and alternate feasible bases. If the simplest bound occurs by an alternate integral at smaller or different resolution, it will be tested. Different grid sizes and boundary discretizations can escape a parent whose active shifts include only unlucky finite lag placements. Since the given n may not encode the true optimum, branch to nearby smaller/equal resolution may show a slightly lower bound.

- Every accepted adjustment proves only the evaluator value of the output candidate; no optimality claim and no descent proof for original objective is made.
