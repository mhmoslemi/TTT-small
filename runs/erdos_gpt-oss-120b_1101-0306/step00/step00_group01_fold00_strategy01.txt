1. Approach and rationale

The central idea is to solve the exact finite minimax form of the discrete Erdős overlap objective directly, rather than replacing it by an autocorrelation-only surrogate. With samples h in R^n, dx = 2/n, and the required normalization sum(h)=n/2, the evaluator is

C5(h) = max_{k=0,...,2n-2} dx * sum_{valid pairs in lag k} h_i (1 - h_j).

The method uses an epigraph local solver

minimize t
subject to C_k(h) <= t for every lag k,
0 <= h_i <= 1,
sum(h) = n/2,

with exact overlap formulas and analytic Jacobians. This is nonconvex because of terms h_i h_j, so no CVXPY relaxation is claimed globally optimal. The plan is a robust hybrid local multi-start and multi-resolution procedure. It combines:
- an exact feasibility-preserving projection onto the box-plus-sum set;
- an epigraph SLSQP solver designed for the exact finite-support correlation constraints;
- a smoothed-max SLSQP objective as an alternative smoother map near nonsmooth kinks;
- exact pair-exchange local polishing that preserves the sum constraint by construction;
- resolution transfers from the supplied parent and synthetic family-based starts;
- final acceptance only under the authoritative evaluator objective recomputed from the returned h vector.

This addresses the key bottleneck: the objective is nonsmooth and nonconvex, and the correlation boundary terms are not the zero-lag autocorrelation or a simple 1 - autocorrelation at nonzero lags. The plan keeps the original max-over-all-overlaps semantics throughout, and only accepts candidates by exact re-evaluation. No parent entries are copied into literals; the parent is used algorithmically as the initial h array and is transformed through projection, interpolation, averaging, and random perturbation.

Limitations:
- This is a heuristic global/local search, not a proof of global optimality and not a convex certificate.
- SLSQP is a local method; the `[5; 6]` variety of starts, resolutions, smoothing, and pair moves is meant to escape bad parents without claiming meeting a lower bound.
- The exact discrete metric depends on n, dx, and all lags; improvements at one n are compared only using this evaluator.
