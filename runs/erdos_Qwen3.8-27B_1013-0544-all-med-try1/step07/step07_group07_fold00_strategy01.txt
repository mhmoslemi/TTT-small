1. Approach and rationale

The central idea is to represent the step function h(x) as a binary sequence with fixed integral 1, and use a multi-start global optimization algorithm to minimize the maximum overlap under shift. This approach diffErs from the previous which used a monomorphic gradient-based optimization that could get stuck in local optima. The bottleneck addressed is the difficulty of finding the global optimum with traditional local search methods. By using a combination of initial perturbations and simulated annealing, we aim to explore a larger portion of the search space and escape local minima. The key properties of this approach are:
- Representing h as a binary sequence with fixed number of samples and sum constraint
- Using a hybrid optimization strategy combining random intersection with deterministic steps
- Selecting candidate solutions based on projected autocorrelation values
- Ensuring feasible points through repair operators

2. Mathematical specification

The optimization minimizes the maximum overlap over all shifts, defined as:
```
max_k ∫ h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx
```
Represented discretely:
```
C_5 = (1/dx) * max_k ∑_{i} h[i] * (1-h[i+k])
```

subject to:
- h[i] ∈ {0,1} for all i
- ∑ h[i] = N/2  (integral constraint)
- dx = 2/N_points

- Flipping bits (0/1 values) while adjusting sum via moves
- Ensuring sum remains N/2 by swapping equal numbers of 0s and 1s
