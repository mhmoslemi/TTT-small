Hmm, the "focused" approach doesn't actually focus on the worst lag in a meaningful way, because any move affects all lags. Let me remove the "focus" and just use the general random approach.

Actually, let me think about this differently. The key observation is:

c_k = sum_i h[i] * (1 - h[i+shift]) where shift = k - (n-1)

For the worst lag, the "shift" is fixed. The pairs (i, i+shift) that contribute to c_k are the ones where h[i]=1 and h[i+shift]=0 (or fractional). To reduce c_k, I want to:
1. Decrease h[i] where h[i+shift] is already 0 (reduces the "1-0" pairs)
2. Increase h[i+shift] where h[i] is already 1 (also reduces "1-0" pairs)

But I need to be careful not to increase other c_k values.

This suggests a more targeted strategy: identify the specific pairs (i, i+shift) that contribute most to the worst lag, and make moves that specifically address those pairs.
