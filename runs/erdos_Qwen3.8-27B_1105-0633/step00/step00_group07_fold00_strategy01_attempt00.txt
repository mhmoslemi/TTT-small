<strategy>
1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea and why it targets the supplied problem

The task is a finite-dimensional nonsmooth minimax optimization over the feasible polytope
P = {h ∈ [0,1]^n : sum(h) = n/2}. The exact objective is
C(h) = dx * max_t r_t(h), where dx = 2/n and r_t are entries of
np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full"). Each r_t is quadratic/bilinear in h, so the global problem is nonconvex. A direct convex solver is therefore not justified.

The proposed method attacks the bottleneck of the current parent rather than claiming a global optimum. It uses:

- A smooth log-sum-exp surrogate F_β(h) to expose the largest few overlap lags without using a hard maximum; β controls how sharply inactive lags are suppressed.
- Frank-Wolfe (conditional gradient) steps directed toward vertices of P. These vertices are simple mass/dimension literals: floor(n/2) entries equal 1, ceil(n/2) entries equal 0, and one middle entry 1/2 only if n is odd. Moving toward these extreme vertices promotes the high variance required to reduce diagonal overlap while preserving the mass constraint exactly because convex combinations of feasible points remain feasible.
- Exact evaluation and acceptance after every candidate: the surrogate is never treated as the truth. This avoids accepting a falsely improved solution because of smoothing.

- A persistent pool of exact evaluated feasible candidates, with the parent preserved on the same inner machine log preservation.

This differs materially from generic projected gradient or LP active-set refinement: the search direction is generated by exact linearization followed by resolution over the entire feasible box-sum polytope, and the sequence is projected only through convex combination. The Frank-Wolfe framework also automatically exposes equality-preserving endpoint transfer patterns without needing a separate simple simplex projection.

1.2 Facts versus heuristics

Justified facts:

- The exact precision of P ensures any convex combination h + γ(v - h) with γ ∈ [0,1] remains feasible if h,v ∈ P.
- Maximization of min linear-form objective over P is explicitly solved by sorting the gradient coefficient reasonably. The vertex assignment follows from order statistics.
- The surrogate F_β coincides in a logarithic-exponential fashion against exact Gauss-term delays, and its derivative can be computed through Bayes divided weights.

Hypotheses and heuristics:

- Higher β approximates max better; too high β suppresses useful inactive constraints, so point changes can degrade exact objective. Therefore every exact candidate needs restarts/versions.
- Frank-Wolfe for a nonconvex smooth function has no global optimality guarantee and may terminate in a nonvertex stationary point. Intermixed random perturbations, transfers and neighboring-resolution spinner partly addresses that.
