### Approach and Rationale

We use a combination of block-wise perturbations and simulated annealing to explore the search space efficiently. The key idea is to start from a given initial sequence (or a uniform-like fallback) and iteratively perturb it while preserving constraints. The AC1 upper bound is minimized through a gradient-free search due to the non-smooth nature of the objective function (involved convolution maximum). Block-wise perturbations (both local and global) allow exploration while clip and repair operations maintain feasibility.

This approach avoids the cost of local gradient computation and dense matrix operations. It balances exploration (global block changes) and exploitation (local fine-tuning) by perturbing segments of the sequence with either Gaussian noise or Cauchy extremes. Simulated annealing helps escape local minima by probabilistically accepting worse moves, gradually tightening constraints with temperature decay.

### Mathematical Specification

- **Objective**: Minimize `AC1 = (2 * n * max(convolve(a)) / (sum(a))^2`
- **Variables**: Non-negative coefficients `a_i ∈ [0, 1000]` with `sum(a) >= 0.01`
- **Convolution**: Linear mode (non-circular), producing `2*n - 1` terms
- **Perturbations**:
  - **Local**: Add Gaussian-multiplicative noise (90% of moves)
  - **Global**: Multiply Cauchy-multiplicative noise to a contiguous block (10% of moves)
- **Constraint Handling**: Post-perturbation clipping followed by uniform repair if sum is below 0.01
