For the Erdős minimum overlap problem, the optimal construction is related to the "tetris" or "tiling" approach. The idea is to create a function that, when shifted by any amount, has minimal overlap with its complement.

A specific construction: Let h be periodic with period p, and within each period, h = 1 on a fraction 1/2 of the interval. The key is to arrange the 1-regions so that for any shift k, the overlap ∫h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx is minimized.

For a two-level (0/1) function with ∫h = 1 (so h=1 on a set of measure 1 out of [0,2]):
∫h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx = measure of {x: h(x)=1, h(x+k)=0}

This is the "disagreement" between h and its shift. We want to minimize the maximum disagreement over all shifts k.

For a 0/1 function with measure 1/2 (i.e., h=1 on half the domain), the minimum possible maximum disagreement is related to the "autocorrelation" of the indicator function.
