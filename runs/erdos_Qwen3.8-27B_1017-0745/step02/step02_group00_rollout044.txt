Let me also think about the correlation direction. np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode='full') computes:
result[k] = sum_i h[i] * (1-h)[k-i] for valid i

Actually, NumPy's correlate is:
c_k = sum_n a_n * conj(v_{n-k})

For real vectors, this is:
c_k = sum_n a_n * v_{n-k}

where a = h and v = 1-h.

So c_k = sum_n h[n] * (1-h)[n-k]

For the "full" mode, k ranges from -(n-1) to n-1.

The problem asks for:
∫ h(x) * (1-h(x+k)) dx ≈ sum_i h[i] * (1-h[i+k]) * dx

This is c_{-k} in the NumPy convention (since we need h[n] * (1-h)[n-(-k)] = h[n] * (1-h)[n+k]).

But since we take the max over all k, the direction doesn't matter - the set of all 2n-1 values is the same, just in reverse order. So max(np.correlate(h, 1-h, 'full')) = max of all the lag values.
