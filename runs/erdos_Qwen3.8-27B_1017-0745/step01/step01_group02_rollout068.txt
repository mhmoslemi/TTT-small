So q_s for s≠0 is NOT concave in general! It's indefinite quadratic.

Let me recompute. q_s = dx * sum_{i in I_s} h[i] - dx * sum_{i in I_s} h[i]h[i+s]

The first part is linear. The second part: -dx * sum h[i]h[i+s].

The Hessian of sum h[i]h[i+s] has entries (1 at positions (i, i+s) and (i+s, i) for each i in I_s). This is the adjacency matrix of a graph where each node i is connected to i+s. For fixed s, this graph has maximum degree 2 (each i connects to i+s and i-s if they exist). The eigenvalues of such an adjacency matrix are in [-2, 2].

So the Hessian of -dx * sum h[i]h[i+s] has eigenvalues in [-2dx, 2dx] (negative of eigenvalues of the adjacency matrix scaled by dx).

Therefore, the Hessian of q_s has eigenvalues in [-2dx, 2dx]. It's indefinite for s≠0.

So q_s is NOT concave for s≠0. My earlier analysis was wrong.

This means the linear tangent is NOT an upper bound. I need the quadratic majorant.

For the quadratic majorant: q_s(y) <= q_s(x) + ∇q_s(x)·(y-x) + (L/2)||y-x||² where L is an upper bound on the largest eigenvalue of -H_q_s (i.e., L >= ||H_q_s||_spec in the positive direction... actually I need the bound on the positive part of the Hessian).

Actually, the correct Taylor inequality for a twice-differentiable function with Hessian bounded above (in Loewner order) by L*I is:
f(y) <= f(x) + ∇f(x)·(y-x) + (L/2)||y-x||²

where L >= λ_max(H_f). For our case, λ_max(H_q_s) <= 2dx for all s.

So L = 2dx = 4/n works.

The MM subproblem is:
minimize (L/2)||y-x||² + t
s.t. t >= q_s(x) + ∇q_s(x)·(y-x) ∀s
y in K

        best_h = h.copy()

def _dedup(starts):

                break
        if not dup:
            unique.append(s)
