Approach and rationale\n\nUse full-covariance CMA-ES (covariance matrix adaptation evolution strategy) to search feasible h vectors, scoring every decoded candidate with the evaluator’s exact finite-support correlation. This is substantively different from pairwise mass transfers, fixed-frequency-subspace differential evolution, projected gradients, SLSQP epigraph refinement, and curvature-majorant descent: it samples correlated whole-vector changes and adapts their covariance from ranked candidates. For the supplied n=45 construction, the search uses all n−1 independent mass-preserving directions, rather than a fixed low-dimensional frequency basis. The covariance and evolution paths can learn coupled directions that are not apparent from one-coordinate or pairwise proposals.\n\nThe justified facts are that the feasible vectors form a capped simplex, the evaluator uses all 2n−1 lags of a finite linear correlation, and the objective is a generally nonconvex, nonsmooth maximum of quadratic functions. CMA-ES does not require gradients or a convex solver. The hypothesis is that covariance adaptation and multiple parent-derived restarts may find a lower local value while exact scoring and a retained feasible parent protect the returned result. This is a heuristic, not a guarantee of improvement or global optimality. Projection makes the latent search map nonlinear; for n>64 the bounded-dimension basis also restricts each individual restart.\n\n2.
