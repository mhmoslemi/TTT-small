Correlation has finite support: no wraparound terms are included. At nonzero offsets only the indices in the overlap contribute; do not replace the score by a full-mass or circular-correlation expression.\n\nFor convenient indexing define, for each d in {-(n-1), ..., n-1}, I_d = {i : 0 <= i < n and 0 <= i+d < n}, and q_d(h) = dx * sum over i in I_d of h[i]*(1-h[i+d]). NumPy's full correlation at offset k is q_(-k) before scaling conventions are separated, so the q_d are exactly the same set of scaled correlation values in reverse order. Thus max_d q_d equals the evaluator's C5 and includes both lag directions and all boundary terms.\n\nFor temperature tau > 0, optimize the smooth upper approximation F_tau(h) = tau*log(sum_d exp(q_d(h)/tau)). Its weights are p_d = exp((q_d-m)/tau) / sum_e exp((q_e-m)/tau). The exact derivative is grad F_tau = sum_d p_d*grad q_d, with\n\npartial q_d / partial h[j] = dx * (1[j in I_d]*(1-h[j+d]) - 1[j-d in I_d]*h[j-d]).\n\nA term whose indicated index is outside its valid overlap is omitted. This formula also handles d=0, where both terms contribute. Although F_tau is smooth, it remains generally nonconvex because q_d contains products of entries of h. The search is a local heuristic; only the original exact score determines the returned result.\n\nProjection onto C is the Euclidean projection.
