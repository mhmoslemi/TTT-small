Approach and rationale\n\nUse surrogate-free differential evolution (DE) to search feasible candidates in a compact, zero-mean DCT subspace, scoring every candidate with the evaluator’s exact finite-support correlation objective. This is materially different from the supplied program’s smoothed projected-gradient and SLSQP approach, and from the previous strategy’s continuous two-entry exchanges, scalar line searches, and basin-hopping acceptance: the search here mutates and recombines whole coefficient vectors in a population, with no gradients, pair moves, line searches, or annealing.\n\nThe justified facts are that the evaluator uses all 2n−1 lags of a finite linear correlation, the constraints define a capped simplex, and the objective is a maximum of quadratic expressions and is generally nonconvex and nonsmooth. DE can compare such candidates without derivatives. The hypothesis is that coupled changes across low, middle, and selected high DCT frequencies, combined with population diversity and a separate full-dimensional noisy anchor, may expose useful basins that local methods or coordinate-pair moves miss. This is a heuristic, not a guarantee of improvement or global optimality. The DCT search is restricted, projection makes its coefficient-to-vector map nonlinear, and finite evaluation budgets may miss better constructions.
