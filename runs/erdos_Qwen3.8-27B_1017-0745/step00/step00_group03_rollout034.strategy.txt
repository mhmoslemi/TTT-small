Mathematical specification\nLet N be the runtime number of samples, h ∈ R^N, Δx = 2/N, and m = N/2. The feasible set is 0 ≤ h_i ≤ 1 and Σ_i h_i = m. For integer lag k ∈ {−(N−1), …, N−1}, define I_k = {i : 0 ≤ i < N and 0 ≤ i+k < N} and\nq_k(h) = Σ_{i∈I_k} h_{i+k}(1−h_i).\nThis is the finite-support, non-circular correlation convention: q_k is entry k+N−1 of np.correlate(h, 1−h, mode='full'). The exact objective is F(h) = Δx max_k q_k(h). Every lag, including both directions and truncated boundary overlaps, must be included. Do not replace it with a circular correlation or an autocorrelation expression that drops boundary terms. The products make F generally nonconvex and nonsmooth.\n\nFor a direction d with Σ_i d_i = 0, write h(α)=h+αd. The feasible α interval is the intersection of each coordinate’s box interval. For every i with d_i ≠ 0, form r0=−h_i/d_i and r1=(1−h_i)/d_i; then α_lo=max_i min(r0,r1) and α_hi=min_i max(r0,r1). Coordinates with d_i=0 impose no restriction. The current feasible point gives α=0 in the interval, up to floating-point tolerance.\n\nFor each lag, expand q_k(h+αd)=A_k+B_kα+C_kα². With i ranging over I_k,\nA_k = Σ h_{i+k}(1−h_i),\nB_k = Σ [d_{i+k}(1−h_i) − h_{i+k}d_i],\nC_k = −Σ d_{i+k}d_i.\nFor k=0 these give A_0=Σh_i(1−h_i), B_0=Σd_i(1−2h_i), and C_0=−Σd_i². Minimizing max_k q_k(h+αd) minimizes the exact score along the line because Δx is a positive constant.\n\nOn a closed α interval, the maximum of finitely many quadratic polynomials can attain its minimum at an interval endpoint, at a stationary point of an active polynomial, or where two polynomials intersect. Therefore, in exact arithmetic, the complete candidate set is α_lo, α_hi, every in-interval value −B_k/(2C_k) with C_k ≠ 0, and every in-interval real root of (C_k−C_l)α²+(B_k−B_l)α+(A_k−A_l)=0 for k<l. Identical polynomials need no isolated intersection: their stationary points and the interval endpoints are already included. This is a one-dimensional global minimization result only; it is not a global solution of the N-dimensional problem.\n\nFor joint feasibility restoration, project a finite vector z onto the capped simplex by minimizing ½||h−z||² subject to the box and sum constraints. The KKT form is h_i=clip(z_i−θ,0,1), where θ solves Σ_i clip(z_i−θ,0,1)=m. This sum is monotone nonincreasing in θ, so bisection followed by a bounded residual correction provides a joint box-and-sum repair. Clipping and then separately rescaling is not valid.\n\n3.
