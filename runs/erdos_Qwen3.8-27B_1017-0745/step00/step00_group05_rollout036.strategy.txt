{"plan":"1. Approach and rationale\n\nUse a bounded, derivative-free population search based on adaptive differential evolution (DE). Each population member is a feasible sequence, and its fitness is the exact finite-support correlation maximum. DE forms new sequences from differences between population members, crosses them with existing members, and restores feasibility by projection onto the box-constrained mass simplex. Successful mutations adapt the scale and crossover parameters. A bounded restart rule injects diversity after stagnation.\n\nThis is materially different from smoothing the maximum for a nonlinear optimizer, linearizing lag constraints in an LP, or minimizing along a selected set of one-dimensional directions: the search uses no gradients, surrogate objective, solver model, or line search. Its advantage is that selection compares candidates directly under the nonsmooth objective while a population explores multiple basins. The exact correlation formula and constraints are facts; the expected benefit of population diversity, adaptive parameters, and restarts is a heuristic. This local stochastic method has no global-optimality guarantee and may not reach C₅ ≤ 0.3808. It optimizes the supplied discretization only.\n\n2. Mathematical specification\n\nFor n samples, define dx = 2/n and target mass m = n/2. The feasible set is K = {h in R^n: 0 ≤ h_i ≤ 1 for every i and sum_i h_i = m}. This equality is precisely the discrete integral constraint because dx times the sum must equal 1.\n\nFor each integer lag t from −(n−1) through n−1, define I_t = {i: 0 ≤ i < n and 0 ≤ i+t < n} and q_t(h) = sum over i in I_t of h_(i+t)(1−h_i). The exact score is C(h) = dx times max_t q_t(h), equivalently max(np.correlate(h, 1−h, mode='full') * dx). The full-correlation array contains all 2n−1 signed lags, with finite-support boundaries changing by lag. Use that NumPy expression as the authoritative score. Do not use circular correlation or replace the objective by an autocorrelation identity that omits boundary terms. The products make the problem nonconvex, and taking the maximum makes it nonsmooth.\n\nFor any proposal v, restore feasibility by its Euclidean projection onto K:\nΠ_K(v) = argmin over y in K of one-half times ||y−v||².\nThe KKT conditions give y_i = clip(v_i−λ, 0, 1), with λ chosen so that sum_i y_i = m. The clipped sum is continuous and nonincreasing in λ, so bisection finds the required mass. This is a joint box-and-mass projection, not sequential clipping and rescaling.\n\nDE keeps population members x_i in K. Its default current-to-pbest mutation is v_i = x_i + F_i(x_p−x_i) + F_i(x_r1−x_r2), where x_p is selected from the best fraction of the current population, x_r1 is another population member, and x_r2 is selected from the population or a bounded archive of replaced members. A minority of trials use v_i = x_r1 + F_i(x_r2−x_r3) for additional exploration. Both formulas preserve the sum before crossover when their inputs are feasible; binomial crossover generally does not, so every crossed vector must be projected before scoring. No mutation model or adaptive parameter is used as a substitute for the exact objective.\n\n3. Implementation procedure\n\n3.1. Program structure and runtime inputs.
