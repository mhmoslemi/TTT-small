- **Why this is different**: Prior strategies focus on a single solution and its gradient updates. My approach introduces **stochastic exploration** through a population of candidates, allowing for better global optimization. Additionally, **adaptive weighting of gradients** based on lag contributions ensures that the most impactful lags are prioritized.
- **Bottleneck addressed**: Local optima and suboptimal convergence by focusing only on top lags. By maintaining a population and using stochastic sampling, we can escape these and explore more diverse solutions.
- **Limitations**: This method is more computationally intensive due to population-based evaluation. However, it's designed to balance exploration and efficiency by using a controlled population size and adaptive learning rates.

### 2. Mathematical Specification

**Representation**:
- `h` is represented as a vector of length `n_points` with entries in `[0, 1]`.
- The integral constraint is enforced via: `Σh[i] = n_points / 2`.

**Objective**:

- The integral is computed over the interval `[0, 2]`, with `dx = 2.0 / n_points`.

**Constraints**:
- 0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1 for all i
- Σh[i] = n_points / 2

**Gradient Calculation**:
For a population of candidate solutions, gradients are computed for each solution:
- For each lag `k`, the gradient for position `i` is derived from the derivative of the overlap integral with respect to `h[i]`:
  ```
  ∇h[i] = -Σ (1 - h(x + k)) * indicator(x + k ∈ [0, 2])
  ```
  where the indicator ensures the lag is within the domain.

**Adaptive Weighting**:
- Gradient contributions are weighted by the **current maximum overlap** for the given lag. This ensures that lags with higher overlap values are prioritized during updates.

**Population Dynamics**:
- A fixed population size `P` is maintained.
- Each candidate is updated using the gradient, with a learning rate adjusted based on the population's diversity and improvement trends.
