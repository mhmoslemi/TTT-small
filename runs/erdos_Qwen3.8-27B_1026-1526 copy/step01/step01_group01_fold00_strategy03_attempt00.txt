1. Approach and rationale  
The Erdős minimum overlap problem requires minimizing the maximum overlap of a step function \( h(x) \) with its shifted versions. Traditional methods such as gradient descent and SLSQP often get stuck in local optima. This approach uses Fourier-Bessel series expansions to represent \( h(x) \) as a combination of orthogonal functions. By projecting the problem into a sparse coefficient space, we can reduce the dimensionality while retaining the ability to represent complex shapes (e.g., multiple peaks). The optimization targets a small number of coefficients, balancing between the function's representational capacity and computational feasibility.

2. Mathematical specification  
Define \( h(x) \) on \([0, 2]\) using Bessel functions of the first kind \( J_\nu \):
$$
h(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} c_i J_0(\beta_{i} x)
$$
where \( \beta_i \) are the eigenvalues from Bessel's equation, determined by boundary conditions at \( x=0 \) and \( x=2 \). The coefficients \( c_i \) are real numbers satisfying \( \sum_{i=1}^{M} c_i^2 \leq C \) (to bound the function's amplitude), and the integral constraint:
$$
\int_0^2 h(x) \, dx = 1.
$$
The overlap integral \( C_5 \) is redefined using the Fourier-Bessel representation's orthogonality to derive a computationally cheaper objective function:
$$
C_5 = \max_k \frac{2}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \sum_{j=1}^{M} c_i c_j J_0(\beta_i k) J_0(\beta_j k) 
$$
subject to \( c_i \in \mathbb{R} \), \( \sum_{i=1}^{M} \approx n/2 \), and \( h(x) \in [0, 1] \). The discrete representation uses \( M = 4 \) Bessel modes (enough for multi-peak structures) and \( N = 47, 94, 190 \) grid points for validation.

3. Implementation procedure
