**1. Approach and Rationale**  
We are minimizing the ratio \( \frac{2n \cdot M}{\sum a_i^2} \), where \( M \) is the maximum of the *linear* (not circular) full convolution of the sequence. The challenge is that standard optimization methods may get stuck in local optima, especially for large sequences. Our strategy involves:  
- Hybrid Search: Starting with high-frequency oscillations (Bartels-Seidel) and morphing them into long-range correlations (gradient-based optimization).  
- Progressive Tightening: Using evolutionary algorithms on small subarrays to sieve for promising patterns, then embedding them into larger contexts.  
- Terminal Safety: A fallback that combines global and local optima with aggressive sum balancing to avoid overdue local optima.  

**2. Mathematical Specification**  
Let \( a \) be a sequence of length \( n \). The full linear convolution \( c = \text{full\_convolve}(a, a) \) has length \( 2n-1 \), so \( M = \max(c) \). The score is \( \frac{2n \cdot M}{( \sum a_i )^2} \).  

Our method transforms the problem:  
- The score is homogeneous in \( a \), so we optimize \( a \) directly.  
- We represent sequences as normalized smoothing filters to handle non-negativity.  
- For each solution candidate, we validate it by recomputing the score with the original function.
