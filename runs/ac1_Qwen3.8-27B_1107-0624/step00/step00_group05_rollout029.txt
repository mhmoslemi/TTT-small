For a binary sequence with flat autocorrelation (excluding the peak at lag 0), the autocorrelation at non-zero lags is approximately constant. But in our case, we're looking at the convolution, not the autocorrelation.

For a binary sequence, the convolution at lag k is the number of pairs (i, j) with i+j=k, a[i]=1, a[j]=1. This is different from the autocorrelation (which counts pairs with j-i=k).

For a symmetric binary sequence (a[i] = a[n-1-i]), the convolution equals the autocorrelation (shifted). So we want a symmetric binary sequence with low peak autocorrelation.

The best known binary sequences with low peak autocorrelation are:
- Barker sequences (length up to 13)
- m-sequences (peak sidelobe = (N-1)/N ≈ 1 for the normalized case)
- For longer sequences, the best known have peak sidelobe around $\sqrt{N}$.

But for our problem, we don't need binary. We can use any non-negative values.
