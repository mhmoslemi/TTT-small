## Approach and Rationale

This algorithm aims to minimize the objective function `2*n*max(convolve(a,a))/sum(a)**2` for an input sequence `a`. We observe that the problem is equivalent to finding a sequence that minimizes the maximum value of its full convolution under the normalization constraint `sum(a) >= 0.01`. 

Key observations:
1. The uniform sequence yields an objective of `2*n²` which is high (2.0 in the example).
2. The optimal sequence should concentrate energy to reduce the self-bias in the convolution.
3. The problem is non-convex and requires a search over sequences with variable length (up to 1800 items).
4. We need a balance between aggressiveness and feasibility: too short a sequence might not have enough "attacksurface", but too long might be computationally expensive.

The proposed approach is a multi-length, adaptive local search combined with a priority-based strategy to explore sequences that are likely to yield improvements. We generate multiple initial sequences using different lengths and distributions, then iteratively improve them using local fluctuations (perturbations) and directionality. The algorithm starts with a sequence of length 256 (the parent fallback) and explores neighboring lengths with varying aggressiveness in updates.

## Mathematical Specification

Let `n` be the length of the sequence, `n` should be between 2 and 1800 inclusive. The evaluator function scales the sequence to `L = √(2n)` in its initial step, but we must maintain the sequence generation with `sum(a) >= 0.01`.

The core of our search is the gradient information from the convolution. We consider the sequence as `a = (x_0, x_1, ..., x_{n-1})` with `x_i >= 0`.

Define:
- `c_k = sum_{i+j=k} x_i * x_j` for `k ∈ [0, 2n-2]` (full convolution).
- `L_∞ = max_k |c_k|`, but since all coefficients are non-negative, `c_k` is always non-negative.
- The objective is `J(a) = 2*n*L_∞/(sum_x)**2`.

We wish to minimize `J(a)`.

We parameterize the sequence by its distribution and use the gradient of `J` to guide our updates.

The domain is: `x_i ≥ 0` and `sum_x ≥ 0.01`. The objective is scale-invariant under `sum_x` normalization, so we focus on the shape.
