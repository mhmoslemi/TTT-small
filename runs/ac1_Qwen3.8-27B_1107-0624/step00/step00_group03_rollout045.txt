For a probability distribution p on Z_n (circular), the circular autocorrelation is R[m] = sum_i p[i] * p[i+m] (circular). And R[0] = sum p[i]^2 (the "energy"). By the definition, R[0] is always the maximum of the circular autocorrelation for non-negative p (by Cauchy-Schwarz: R[m] = sum p[i]p[i+m] <= sqrt(sum p[i]^2 * sum p[i+m]^2) = R[0]).

Wait, that's for circular! For circular convolution, R[0] = sum p[i]^2 is the maximum. And by the Białynicki-Birula and Mycielski inequality (or just Cauchy-Schwarz), R[m] <= R[0] for all m.

So for circular convolution of non-negative sequences, the maximum is always at lag 0 and equals sum p[i]^2.

For linear convolution, it's different because the "wrap-around" terms are missing. The maximum of the linear convolution can be at a different position than the "center."

But wait, for the linear case, let me reconsider. The linear convolution at position n-1 is:
(p*p)[n-1] = sum_{i=0}^{n-1} p[i] * p[n-1-i]

sum p[i] * p[n-1-i] <= sqrt(sum p[i]^2 * sum p[n-1-i]^2) = sum p[i]^2.
