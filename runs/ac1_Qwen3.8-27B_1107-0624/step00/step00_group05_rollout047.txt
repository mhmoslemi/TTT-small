**Improved subgradient approach:**

The idea is:
1. Compute the peaks P (set of lag positions where c[k] is near max)
2. For each peak position k, the "energy" c[k] = sum_i a[i]*a[k-i]
3. We want to reduce c[k] while maintaining sum(a) = 1
4. The most effective way to reduce c[k] is to decrease the products a[i]*a[k-i]
5. For each element a[j], its "peak contribution" is g[j] = sum_{k in P} a[k-j] (the amount it contributes to the peaks)
6. We should decrease a[j] in proportion to g[j] and increase a[j] in proportion to (1-g[j])

But the key issue is the renormalization. If we decrease a[j] for high g[j] and renormalize, the mass gets redistributed proportionally, which might not help.

delta[j] = step * g[j]  (amount to remove from position j)
total_removed = sum(delta)

redistribution[j] = total_removed * (1-g[j]) / sum(1-g)
a_new[j] = a[j] - delta[j] + redistribution[j]

This preserves the sum and moves mass from high-peak-contribution to low-peak-contribution positions.
