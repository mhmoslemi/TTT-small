1. Approach and rationale
This solution combines a re-sampling strategy with an abrupt structural shift to explore areas beyond standard incremental optimization. Fundamentally, although the problem requires minimizing the evaluation function, we interpret it broadly to also satisfy a particular linear algebra constraint. Specifically, any returned sequence `a` must satisfy `max(connected_components(a)) <= 1.5`, defining "connected components" as consecutive, non-zero blocks of the sequence. This constraint effectively restricts our search to sequences consisting of at most one contiguous, non-zero block. The rationale is two-fold: (1) the original objective remains unchanged in gradient, (2) this block constraint often prevents excessive signal aliasing making the minima more achievable, and (3) sequences violating it tend to produce a very large convolution norm. This method produces sequences with the lowest possible block structure complexity, making it easier to find optimal tapering near the lower end.

2. Mathematical specification
Given the provided `height_sequence_1`, let `n` be its length. Define the objective as `O = 2*n*M/S` where:
   `S = sum(a)`
   `M = max(convolve_full(a, a))`
   `convolve_full` is the full convolution (non-circular, `mode='full'`)
Define a "connected component" of `a` as a maximal contiguous subsequence where no zero appears. Then our new constraint is:
   `min(|blocks|)` for all `a` we consider is such that `max(black_block_sum) = sum(a)`, and we require `len(blocks) <= 1` for the optimum to be found. Indeed, any sequence with multiple non-zero blocks will have its connected components' sums as `S_i`, and the addition of their artifacts (edge ringing) tends to increase the convolution norm.
