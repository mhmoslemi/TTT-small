For the LP:
- Normalize f to have sum = sqrt(2n) (or any constant, doesn't matter since the LP is homogeneous)
- M = max(f*f)
- Solve: max sum(g) s.t. (f*g)[k] <= M for all k, g >= 0
- The solution g will have sum(g) > sum(f) = sqrt(2n) (otherwise f itself would be optimal)
- Rescale g to have sum = sqrt(2n)
- Update f <- (1-t)*f + t*g

The key insight is that the LP is finding a "better" sequence g that has a larger sum while maintaining the same autocorrelation bound. After rescaling, g has the same sum as f but (g*g) might have a lower max.

Wait, but the LP only constrains f*g <= M, not g*g <= M. So g*g might have a larger max than M. The improvement comes from the fact that the convex combination (1-t)*f + t*g has autocorrelation:
((1-t)*f + t*g) * ((1-t)*f + t*g) = (1-t)^2 * (f*f) + 2t(1-t) * (f*g) + t^2 * (g*g)

We know (1-t)^2 * (f*f) <= (1-t)^2 * M and 2t(1-t) * (f*g) <= 2t(1-t) * M.
So the first two terms contribute at most [(1-t)^2 + 2t(1-t)] * M = (1-t)(1+t) * M = (1-t^2) * M.

The t^2 * (g*g) term could be problematic if max(g*g) >> M. But for small t, t^2 * max(g*g) is small.

So for sufficiently small t, the new max will be less than M. The line search finds the best t.
