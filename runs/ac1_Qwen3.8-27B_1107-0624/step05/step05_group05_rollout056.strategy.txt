#### Approach and Rationale

1. **Geometric Programming (GP)**: The step function sequence is transformed into a geometric program, where each coefficient represents a geometric mean. This leverages GP's capability to minimize cones, aligning with the objective's logarithmic nature.
2. **Geometric Differentiation**: Unlike typical differentials, geometric differentiation involves replacing *fsum(a<inf>a<inf>)* with a logarithmic framework. This turn makes the maximization task numerically guided by geometric interpretation.
3. **Tailoring Convolution**: The convolution is adapted to align with GP rules, where the magnitudes are cues for the descent direction. This ensures the algorithm doesn't differentiate or circular absolute points.
4. **Fallback Mechanism**: A concrete fallback sequence—length 256 with uniform values—is included. This sequence is logged via an authoritative score and verified upon reaching the termination condition.

#### Mathematical Specification

Let the sequence \(a\) of length \(n\) have non-negative elements. The score to minimize is:

\[
score(a) = \frac{2 \cdot n \cdot \max(\text{convolve\_full}(a, a))}{(\sum a)^2}
\]

We approach this in two forms:

1. **Normalization**: Convert \(a\) to a sequence where \(\sum a = \sqrt{2 \cdot n}\) (like the original LP approach) for concreteness.
2. **Geometric Program**: Formulate the ascent task as a LP auxiliary to the sequence. The update will be:

\[
proposal = (1-\epsilon) \cdot a + \epsilon \cdot (convolve\_full]+=M)
\]

Here, \(convolve\_full++\) is a selectively boosted sequence. This is done via a LP that:

- Sets bounds: \(a_i \geq 0\) for all \(i\).
- Boosts the interval until the convolution's intercept is aligned.
