## 1. Approach and Rationale

### Primary Approach
We will use a **two-phase optimization** procedure to minimize the given evaluation function. The first phase will involve a **coarse-grid optimization** over a range of sequence lengths, while the second phase will perform a **fine-tuning** within the best-performing length. This is adapted from the previous algorithm but introduces variations to generalize and potentially find better candidates.

### Rationale
- **Sequence Length Optimization**: The evaluation function's value depends significantly on both the sequence length `n` and the sequence coefficients. By optimizing across multiple lengths and refinancing the best length, we avoid getting stuck at a local optimum due to fixed-length optimization in the previous program.
- **Base Initialization**: Without a parent sequence, we initialize using a **Tapered Sinc Profile** (as opposed to a uniform sequence) which has more structure and might yield a better starting point.
- **Local Optimizer**: We use **Adam** optimization with a logarithmic step-space (scaling coefficients by a factor between `1e-4` and `1e4`) to perform efficient local search. The previous FISTA phase is integrated but with significantly different lengths for coarse optimization.
- **Gradient Handling**: The previous program used a convolution-based gradient (due to the max operation) which is now defined explicitly and verified.

### Bottleneck Addressed
The main limitation we address is the **previous program's fixed focus on n=4096**. If the best solution lies at another length (or n=4096 is a local minimum), we fail to find it. Our approach explores a broader range of sequence lengths (2 to 4000) and uses interpolation and re-normalization to adapt known strong candidates.

### Limitations
- The range of sequence lengths [2, 4000] might be computationally expensive. However, note the time budget is generous (1800s) and the previous program already addressed large n (4096) successfully.
- We assume `evaluate_sequence` performance scales reasonably with sequence length (O(n^2)).

## 2. Mathematical Specification

### Variables
- **`sequence`**: A vector of non-negative real numbers, normalized to have sum ≥0.01 (but typically 1 to simplify computation).
- **`n`**: The length of the sequence.

### Objective to Minimize
- **`evaluation(sequence)`**: returns `float(2 * n * max_b / (sum_a ** 2))`, where `max_b` is the maximum autocorrelation value (from full convolution) and `sum_a` is the sum of `sequence`.

### Constraints
- All elements in `sequence` must be non-negative.
- After evaluation, any negative numbers (due to intermediate steps) are set to zero and the sequence is renormalized.

### Optimization Approach
- **Adam Minimization**: We transform the coefficients to log space and use Adam's step-size selection (to avoid boundary problems) on the normalized problem. The gradient is:
  `grad[a_i] = 2 * n * (conv[a][i] - max_conv) / (sum_a^2)`, where `conv[a]` is the full linear convolution result.
