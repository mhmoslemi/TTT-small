To minimize this, maximize sigma (the standard deviation of p). The maximum standard deviation for a distribution on {0,...,n-1} is achieved by a two-point distribution at 0 and n-1, giving sigma = (n-1)/2.

But a two-point distribution gives `conv(p,p)` with only 3 non-zero elements (at 0, n-1, and 2n-2), so max_p_conv = 1/4 (at the middle), and score = 2n/4 = n/2. Terrible!

The issue is that the normal approximation only works when the distribution is "smooth" enough. For a two-point distribution, the convolution is concentrated.

So there's a trade-off: we want the distribution to be spread out (high variance) but also smooth (so the convolution is flat).

The optimal shape is something like a "top-hat" with smooth edges, or a raised cosine. These have moderate variance and produce relatively flat convolutions.
