And $(a*a)[k] = \sum_j a[j] a[k-j]$ is a quadratic form in $a$. The max of quadratic forms is convex in $a$. So we're minimizing a convex function over the probability simplex. This is a convex optimization problem!

The proximal gradient method, FFrank-Wolfe, or any other convex optimization method would work. But L-BFGS on the smoothed objective should converge fastest.

Actually, I realize there's an even simpler way to compute the gradient using the structure of the problem. Let me define:

$c = \text{conv}(a, a)$ (length $2n-1$)
$w = \text{softmax}(c / \beta)$ (length $2n-1$)

Then $g_j = \frac{\partial}{\partial a_j} [2n \cdot \text{soft\_max}(c, \beta)] = 2n \cdot \sum_k w_k \frac{\partial c_k}{\partial a_j}$

And $\frac{\partial c_k}{\partial a_j} = a[k-j] + a[j-k]$ (with $a[m]=0$ outside $[0,n-1]$).

Now, note that $\sum_k w_k a[k-j]$ is the cross-correlation of $w$ and $a$ evaluated at lag $j$. And $\sum_k w_k a[j-k]$ is the same thing (by symmetry, since $a$ is the same sequence).

Wait, are they the same? Let's check:
$\sum_k w_k a[k-j]$ vs $\sum_k w_k a[j-k]$

These are NOT the same in general (unless $w$ is symmetric). The first is the cross-correlation at lag $j$, and the second is the cross-correlation at lag $-j$ (or equivalently, the convolution at index $j$).

But wait, $c_k = \sum_j a[j] a[k-j]$ is symmetric in the sense that $c_k = c_{2(n-1)-k}$ (the convolution of a sequence with itself is symmetric). However, $w$ is the softmax of $c$, so $w$ is also symmetric: $w_k = w_{2(n-1)-k}$.
