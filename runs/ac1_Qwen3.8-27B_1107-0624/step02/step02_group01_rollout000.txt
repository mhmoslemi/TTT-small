Actually, let me reconsider. The previous program achieved 1.507 with n=512. Let me think about what kind of sequence would give such a low score.

The score is 2n*max(conv)/sum^2. For this to be < 2, we need max(conv) < sum^2/n.

But sum^2/n is the minimum value of sum(a_j^2) (achieved when all a_j = sum/n). And we showed that max(conv) can be less than sum(a_j^2).

Actually, I realize the key: for a non-symmetric sequence, the peak of the convolution (at the middle) can be significantly less than sum(a_j^2). This is because the middle value of the convolution is sum a[j]*a[n-1-j], which is the inner product of a with its reverse. For a sequence that is "anti-correlated" with its reverse (i.e., high values at one end and low at the other), this inner product can be small.

Example: a = [1, 1, 1, ..., 1, 0, 0, ..., 0] (first half ones, second half zeros).
conv middle = sum_{j=0}^{n/2-1} 1*0 + sum_{j=n/2}^{n-1} 0*1 = 0!
