We want to minimize `max(np.convolve(a, a))` subject to `a >= 0`, `sum(a) = 1`.

The minimum possible value of `max(np.convolve(a, a))` is at least `1/(2n-1)` (by the pigeonhole principle: the sum of all elements of the convolution is 1, and there are 2n-1 elements, so the max is at least 1/(2n-1)).

The theoretical lower bound is `2n * (1/(2n-1)) ≈ 1` for large n.

The previous result of ~1.5 corresponds to `max(conv) ≈ 1.5/(2n) = 0.75/n`, which is between `1/(2n)` and `1/n`.

So we're in the regime where the convolution is somewhat flatter than the triangular shape but not perfectly flat. The task is to make it even flatter.

Now, for the U-shaped initialization: if `a_i` is large at the edges and small in the middle, the convolution will have:
- Large values near the edges (from products of large edge values)
- Smaller values in the middle (from products of small middle values)

This is the opposite of the uniform case (where the convolution peaks in the middle). So the U-shape "inverts" the convolution peak from the center to the edges. But the edge values of the convolution are `a[0]^2` and `a[n-1]^2`, which could be large.

The optimal balance is: make the edges large enough to reduce the center, but not so large that the edge convolution values exceed the center.

This is a trade-off that the optimization will find.
