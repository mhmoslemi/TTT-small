**Mathematical insight**: Consider the problem in terms of the distribution function. If `a` is a probability distribution on `{0, ..., n-1}`, then `conv(a,a)` is the distribution of the sum of two independent random variables with that distribution. We want the sum distribution to be as flat as possible.

By the Central Limit Theorem, if `a` is close to uniform, the sum distribution is close to a discrete uniform (on the range where it's valid). But the edges will be lower. The maximum will be at the center.

To make the sum distribution more uniform (including at the center), we need `a` to be non-uniform in a specific way. Specifically, we want to REDUCE the center of the sum distribution. This means making `a` "anti-concentrated" - having more mass at the edges and less in the middle.

Wait, actually the center of `conv(a,a)` (at lag `n-1`) is `sum_i a[i]*a[n-1-i]`. To minimize this, we want `a[i]` to be small when `a[n-1-i]` is large. This is an "anti-symmetric" pattern: `a[i]` large at one end, small at the other.

But if `a` is anti-symmetric (like `a[i] = 1 - a[n-1-i]`), then `conv(a,a)[n-1] = sum_i a[i]*(1-a[i]) = sum a[i] - sum a[i]^2 = 1 - sum a[i]^2`. And `sum a[i]^2` is minimized when `a` is uniform, giving `1 - 1/n`. So the center would be `1 - (1-1/n) = 1/n`. But other lags might be large.

Actually, for a perfectly anti-symmetric `a` (where `a[i] + a[n-1-i] = c` for all `i`), we have:
`conv(a,a)[n-1] = sum_i a[i]*(c - a[i]) = c - sum a[i]^2`.

With `c = 2/n` (so that `sum a[i] = n*c/2 = 1`), we get `conv(a,a)[n-1] = 2/n - sum a[i]^2`.

To minimize this, we want to maximize `sum a[i]^2`, which means making `a` as "bipolar" as possible: half the elements at `c` and half at 0. But then `conv(a,a)` at other lags might be large.

This suggests that the optimal solution has some structure related to anti-symmetry or at least a "tilt" (more mass at one end).
