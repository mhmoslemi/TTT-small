The key insight is that `max_k conv(a,a)[k]` can be written as:
`max_k conv(a,a)[k] = max_{w: w≥0, sum(w)=1} sum_k w[k] * conv(a,a)[k]`

`min_a max_w sum_k w[k] * conv(a,a)[k]`
subject to `a ≥ 0, sum(a) = 1`

And for fixed `w`:
`min_a sum_k w[k] * conv(a,a)[k] = min_a a^T C(w) a`
where `C(w) = sum_k w[k] * C_k` and `C_k` is the matrix for the k-th convolution coefficient.

`a^T C(w) a = sum_k w[k] * (sum_j a[j]*a[k-j]) = sum_{j,l} a[j]*a[l] * (sum_k w[k] * indicator(j+l=k))`

This is a **quadratic program**! And `Q` is a Hankel matrix (constant along anti-diagonals).

For the outer problem, we maximize over `w` on the simplex.

1. Initialize `w` (e.g., uniform or concentrated at the current max)
2. Solve the QP: `min a^T Q a` s.t. `a ≥ 0, sum(a) = 1`
3. Update `w` to be concentrated at the new max of `conv(a,a)`
4. Repeat
