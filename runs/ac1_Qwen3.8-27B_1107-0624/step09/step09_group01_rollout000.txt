Actually, I think the key insight is different. Let me think about this more carefully.

For a symmetric sequence (palindrome), the convolution is also symmetric, and the peak is at the center lag = n-1.

For an asymmetric sequence, the convolution is not symmetric, and the peak can be anywhere.

The question is: can we make the peak of the convolution smaller by making the sequence asymmetric?

The answer is YES, as shown by the [1,1,2] example.

The intuition: for a symmetric (rising then falling) sequence, the convolution peak at the center captures the "full weight" of the sequence. For an asymmetric (monotonic) sequence, the convolution spreads the weight differently.

Actually, I realize the key insight: for a monotone sequence like [a_1, a_2, ..., a_n] with 0 <= a_1 <= a_2 <= ... <= a_n, the convolution at lag k is:
C[k] = sum_{j=max(0,k-n+1)}^{min(n-1,k)} a[j+1] * a[k-j+1]

For lag k = n-1: C[n-1] = sum_{j=0}^{n-1} a[j+1] * a[n-j]
This is the "anti-diagonal" sum.

For lag k = 0: C[0] = a[1]^2
For lag k = n-1: C[n-1] = a[1]*a[n] + a[2]*a[n-1] + ... + a[n]*a[1]

If the sequence is monotone increasing, then a[1]*a[n] + a[2]*a[n-1] + ... is maximized when the sequence is "spread out" (by the rearrangement inequality, the sum of products is maximized when sequences are similarly sorted, minimized when oppositely sorted).

Actually, the "anti-diagonal" sum a[1]*a[n] + a[2]*a[n-1] + ... + a[n]*a[1] is the dot product of a with its reverse. By the rearrangement inequality, this is minimized when one is sorted and the other is reverse-sorted, which is exactly the case here (a dotted with reverse(a)).

So for a monotone sequence, C[n-1] = a · reverse(a) is the MINIMUM possible anti-diagonal sum (by rearrangement inequality). This means the peak of the convolution is minimized when the sequence is monotone!

But we also need to check that no other lag has a larger value.

For a monotone increasing sequence, C[k] for k < n-1 involves products of nearby elements (which are all small), while C[k] for k near n-1 involves products of elements that are far apart in the sequence (large * small). The peak should be near the center.
