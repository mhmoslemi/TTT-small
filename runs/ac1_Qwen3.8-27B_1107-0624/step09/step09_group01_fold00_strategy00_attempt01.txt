1. Approach and rationale
The core idea behind this algorithm is to iteratively refine a sequence of nonnegative coefficients to minimize the evaluation objective \( \frac{2n \cdot \max(\text{convolve}(a,a))}{(\sum a)^2} \). This is defined by a nonnegative step function, where the step function is characterized by its coefficients in the output list. The evaluation function `evaluate_sequence` calculates the normalized max-norm after self-convolution, which is \( \max(\text{convolve}(a,a)) \), the sum \( \sum a \), and the length \( n \) of the sequence.

To achieve a minimal value for the objective, we will:
- Start with a given initial sequence (if provided) or create a fallback tapered sequence.
- Pinpoint and modify coefficients causing high convolution values.
- Use a combination of gradient-based and stochastic search methods guided by the `evaluate_sequence` function.

The key limitation is the potential to fall into local minima. The algorithm balances computational speed with sufficient exploration via restarts and stochastic perturbations. By focusing on the exact constraints and the provided evaluation function, we ensure valid sequences are maintained.

2. Mathematical specification
The evaluation objective is computed as:
   - \( n = \text{len}(a) \)
   - \( \text{sum}_a = \sum a \)
   - \( \text{convolve}(a,a) = a * a \) (full convolution)

    n = (0.9 * length).astype(int)
    taper = np.sin(np.linspace(0, np.pi, n, endpoint=False)) ** 0.7
    return np.sqrt(taper) / sum(taper)  # ps+ final output
