The key insight: for the score to be < 2, we need the sequence to be "anti-symmetric" in some sense - the front part should be "large" where the back part is "small" and vice versa, so that the cross-products at the center are minimized.

But wait, all elements are non-negative! So `a[j] * a[n-1-j] >= 0` always. The minimum of `sum_j a[j] * a[n-1-j]` given `sum a[j] = 1` is achieved when the "front" and "back" don't overlap, but they always do (since it's the same sequence).

Actually, `sum_j a[j] * a[n-1-j]` is the dot product of `a` with its reversal. By the rearrangement inequality, this is minimized when one sequence is increasing and the other is decreasing (i.e., when `a` is monotone).

So for a monotonically increasing (or decreasing) sequence, the center value `b[n-1]` is minimized!
