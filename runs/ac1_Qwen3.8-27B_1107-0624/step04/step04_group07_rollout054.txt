The score is `2n * max_k conv[k] / (sum a)^2`. We want to minimize this.

One approach: think of it as a min-max problem. We want to minimize the maximum of the convolution, given a fixed sum.

For the LP approach: given a fixed "template" a, find b that maximizes sum(b) subject to conv(a,b) <= M. This is a valid direction of improvement (it's like a block coordinate descent).

For a different approach: what if we directly optimize using gradient methods?

The objective is `f(a) = max_k (a^T Q_k a)` where Q_k are the matrices representing the k-th convolution coefficient. This is the max of quadratic forms.

The subgradient of f at a point where conv[k*] = M (the max) is `Q_{k*} a` (the gradient of the active constraint). If multiple k achieve the max, the subgradient is in the convex hull of their gradients.

So a subgradient descent approach: at each step, compute the convolution, find the max element(s), and take a gradient step in the direction of the subgradient (projected onto the constraint set sum(a) = 1, a >= 0).

This is essentially what the LP approach does implicitly, but we could do it more directly.
