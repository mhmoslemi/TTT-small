## Approach and Rationale
The previous approaches optimized sequences using complex numerical methods but struggled to achieve the target lower bound. The core idea is that the optimal sequence for minimizing the convolution maximum over the sum squared \( (2n \cdot \max(\text{full convolve}(a, a)) / (\sum(a))^2 \) is one that **flattens the prominence of non-zero segments**. This means distributing the sequence's "activity" as uniformly as possible across its length, minimizing the contribution of any single segment to the convolution peak.

We'll implement this by:
1. Ensuring the entire sequence is treated uniformly
2. Avoiding any operation that might refocus mass (e.g., interpolation)
3. Using rotations and symmetrization to explore the extended solution space

This method effectively prevents any part of the sequence from unnecessarily amplifying the convolution result.

## Mathematical Specification
We represent the sequence as:
\[
a = \text{const\_scaling} \times \text{ones}
\]
where \(\text{const\_scaling}\) is chosen such that \(\sum(a) \geq 0.01\). This avoids the previous bias toward local extrema and guarantees a homogeneous impact from all elements.

The objective function is:
\[
\text{score}(a) = \frac{2 \cdot n \cdot \max(\text{full convolve}(a, a))}{(\sum(a))^2}
\]

For this representation, the convolution is dominated uniformly: each term in the convolution is bounded by \(\frac{\text{const\_scaling}^2}{n^2}\) (from \(\text{full convolve}(a, a)[i+j] = \frac{\text{const\_scaling}^2}{n}\) initially). The maximum convolution value is then \(O(\frac{1}{n})\) with a minimal scale.

### Rotation and Symmetry Handling
