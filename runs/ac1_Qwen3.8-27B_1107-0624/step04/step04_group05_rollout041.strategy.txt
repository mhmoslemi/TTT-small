We start with a fallback sequence if available, then generate sequences with 4 spikes at specific positions, and allocate part of the time budget to optimize these positions via numerical hill climbing or gradient-based methods.

Our primary idea is to use a reduced-parameter space—sequences with four distinct peaks—making the problem more tractable. The score is minimized by finding peaks that are both high in magnitude (to increase the maximum convolution value) but also spaced sufficiently apart (to limit the overall impact on the score). We'll first use the current best sequence from the previous program's output (or the fallback) as a base, then generate new sequences by placing four large coefficients at different positions and optimizing their arrangement to minimize the score.

## Mathematical Specification

Let \( n \) be the sequence length (1024 for base, or interpolated as needed). The goal is to find a sequence \( a = [a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_{n-1}] \) of non-negative floats such that:

- \( \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i \geq 0.01 \)
- \( \|f * f\|_\infty \), where \( f(a) \) is the sequence, is minimized for a fixed \( n \).

But note: the score is \( \frac{2 \cdot n \cdot \max(convolve(a))]}{(sum(a))}^2 \). We are to minimize this by parameterizing \( a \) with only four nonzero elements. Let these elements be positioned at indices \( k_1, k_2, k_3, k_4 \), and assign them with large coefficients \( c_1, c_2, c_3, c_4 \). The sequence is built by:

\[ a_i = \begin{cases} c_j & \text{ if } i = k_j \text{ for one of j in } 1..4 \\ 0 & \text{ otherwise }
\end{cases} \]

This reduces the problem to four real-valued variables (\( c_1, c_2, c_3, c_4 \)) and four integer variables (\( \{k_1,k_2,k_3,k_4\} \)) for positions. We further simplify by fixing the positions initially and then optimizing the magnitudes, and vice versa.

The objective function (score) for a candidate sequence is:

\[ \text{score}(a) = \frac{2 \cdot n \cdot \max_{k} convolve(a)[k]}{(\sum_{i} a_i)^2} \quad \text{subject to} \quad \sum_{i} a_i \geq 0.01. \]

But since we are clamping each coefficient to \( [0, 1000] \), we ensure: 
- positions are in the range [0, n-1]
- \( c_j \) are positive

We formulate the optimization in the space of four coefficients and four positions. The gradient of the score with respect to the coefficients and positions needs to be approximated.
