So the minimum is strictly greater than 1/(2n-1). The linear profile achieves 4/3, which corresponds to max C[k] = (4/3)/(2n) * (sum a)^2 = 2/(3n) (when sum=1). The average is 1/(2n-1) ≈ 1/(2n). So the linear profile's peak is about 4/3 times the average, i.e., the peak-to-average ratio is 4/3.

Can we do better? What's the theoretical minimum peak-to-average ratio for non-negative sequences?

This is actually a well-studied problem in combinatorics and signal processing. For non-negative sequences, the minimum peak sidelobe level (for the aperiodic autocorrelation) is not known in closed form, but there are bounds.

However, for our specific problem (minimizing the peak of the full convolution, not the autocorrelation), the answer might be different.
