Now, for the flatness objective, we can also use:
f(a) = sum_k C[k]^2 / (sum_k C[k])^2 * (2n-1)

Since sum_k C[k] = (sum a)^2, and we're working with normalized a (sum = 1), this is just:
f(a) = sum_k C[k]^2 * (2n-1)

Minimizing this is equivalent to minimizing sum_k C[k]^2, which is what we had.

The nice thing about this objective is that it's smooth (C^∞) and has efficient gradients. We can use L-BFGS or other second-order methods very effectively.

However, minimizing the L2 "flatness" doesn't directly minimize the max. A sequence can have low L2 flatness but still have a high peak (one large spike and the rest near zero). Conversely, a sequence with a moderate peak everywhere might have higher L2 but lower max.

So the strategy should be:
1. First minimize L2 flatness to get a "flat" convolution
2. Then minimize the max (via log-sum-exp or active set) starting from the flat solution

This two-step approach should work well because:
- Step 1 gets us close to the "flat" region of the space
- Step 2 fine-tunes to reduce the actual peak

Let me also think about another objective: the "peak-to-mean ratio" (PMR):
PMR = max(C) / mean(C) = max(C) * (2n-1) / (sum C) = max(C) * (2n-1) / (sum a)^2

This is proportional to our score (up to the factor 2n/(2n-1)).
