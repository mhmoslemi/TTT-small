Specifically, if X is "bimodal" (concentrated at two points), then X+Y is concentrated at three points (sum of the two, twice the first, twice the second), which is more peaked. Bad.

If X is "exponential" (geometric distribution on {0,...,n-1}), then X+Y has a "bathtub" shape (decreasing then increasing), which has lower peak than triangular.

Let me compute the score for a geometric distribution on {0,...,n-1}:
p[i] = (1-r) * r^i for i = 0,...,n-1, where r < 1.

For large n (ignoring the truncation at n-1):
conv[k] = (1-r)² * r^k * sum_{i=0}^{k} 1 = (1-r)² * r^k * (k+1) (for k < n)

Wait, that's not right. conv[k] = sum_i p[i] p[k-i].
p[i] p[k-i] = (1-r)² r^i r^{k-i} = (1-r)² r^k.
So conv[k] = (1-r)² r^k * (number of valid i's).

For the infinite geometric (p[i] = (1-r)r^i on {0,1,2,...}):
conv[k] = (1-r)² r^k * (k+1) for k >= 0.

The maximum of r^k(k+1) is at k = 1/(-ln r) - 1.
Let m = 1/(-ln r). Then max ≈ r^{m-1} * m = e^{-1} * m = 1/(e * (-ln r)).

For the truncated version (n terms), if m < n, the max is at k ≈ m-1 with value (1-r)² * e^{-1} * m.

sum(p) = (1-r)(1-r^n)/(1-r) = 1-r^n ≈ 1 for small r or large n.

Actually for the truncated geometric on {0,...,n-1}:

Score = 2n * max(conv) / (sum p)² ≈ 2n * (1-r)² * e^{-1} / (-ln r) / (1-r^n)²

For r close to 1: let r = 1-ε. Then -ln r ≈ ε, (1-r)² = ε², r^n ≈ e^{-εn}.
