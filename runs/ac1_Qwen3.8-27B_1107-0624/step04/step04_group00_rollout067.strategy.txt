### 1. Approach and rationale
The objective is to minimize the value of \( f(a) = \frac{2 \cdot n \cdot \max(\text{full\_convolve}(a, a))}{(\sum(a))^2} \). The challenge is that the sequence has to be a step function with non-negative coefficients, and we have to find the best sequence without getting stuck in local minima.

Our chosen approach is an evolutionary strategy with adaptive mutation and local search. We will search across a fixed sequence length (4096) by maintaining a population of sequences. The key rationale is that an evolutionary approach with crossover and mutation can efficiently explore the large search space and escape local optima.

We use uniform crossover to combine the best sequences from the population, and mutation is performed by randomly replacing some coefficients and then applying a local nudge in the direction that minimizes the function. We track the best candidate over time.

This approach is more global in nature (unlike gradient-based) and can potentially find sequences that are not easily discovered by local search.

### 2. Mathematical specification
We represent a sequence \( a \) as a vector of fixed length \( n = 4096 \), where \( a_i \in [0, 1000] \) and \( \sum a_i \geq 0.01 \).

The objective function is:

\[ f(a) = \frac{2 \cdot n \cdot \max_{k}( \text{full\_convolve}[a][a]_{k} )}{(\sum a_i)^2} \]

where \( \text{full\_convolve}[a] \) is the full convolution of \( a \) with itself.

We will use a population-based approach, and in each generation, we report the mean fitness of the population and update the best candidate if we find a sequence with a lower objective.
