That's TERRIBLE! I made an error earlier. The maximum is NOT at the center for non-symmetric sequences.

This is a crucial insight! For non-symmetric (non-palindromic) sequences, the maximum of the convolution can be well away from the center, and it can be much larger than the center value.

So the strategy should be to make the sequence SYMMETRIC (palindromic) so that the maximum is at the center, and then optimize the center value.

For a symmetric sequence a[i] = a[n-1-i], the convolution is also symmetric, and the maximum is at the center c[n-1] = sum_j a[j]*a[n-1-j] = sum_j a[j]^2 (since a[n-1-j] = a[j] for symmetric a).

Wait, that's the autocorrelation at lag 0! So for symmetric sequences:
c[n-1] = sum_j a[j]^2

And by Cauchy-Schwarz, sum_j a[j]^2 >= (sum a)^2 / n.

So for symmetric sequences: score >= 2n * (sum a)^2 / (n * (sum a)^2) = 2.

The uniform sequence achieves exactly 2 for symmetric sequences. So for symmetric sequences, 2 is the minimum!

But we've seen that non-symmetric sequences can achieve less than 2 (like [1,1,2,2,3,3] for n=6 giving 1.587). The key is that for non-symmetric sequences, the maximum of the convolution might NOT be at the center, and it might be smaller than sum_j a[j]^2.

Wait, but I just showed that [1,1,1,2,3,4] has max = 25 at c[8], which is much larger. So non-symmetric sequences can be very bad if the maximum shifts away from the center.

The trick is: we want a sequence where:
1. The convolution maximum is as small as possible
2. The maximum doesn't shift to a position where it becomes large
