## Approach and Rationale
The central idea is to modify the existing step function minimization algorithm by introducing multi-scale optimization across different resolutions. While previous strategies attempted direct optimization within fixed-length sequences, the key insight is that minimizing the evaluation function requires candidates that are both structurally sound and offer diverse resolution backgrounds. This approach combines several benefits:
1. Fast convergence on promising candidates through targeted subroutines
2. Extensive structural diversity via multi-length initializations
3. Gradient-based polishing to refine solutions found at various scales
4. Explicit fallback mechanism to exit local search early when beneficial

The evaluation function's sensitivity to sequence length means we must systematically explore how candidate structures change with varying n. Direct mode optimization typically produces sequences with clustered peaks at optimal points, but signs of self-similarity in these structures suggest further domain decomposition could lower the objective.

Multi-length optimization works by ensuring that very low-resolution sequences (like 64-length) are first identified through analytic auto-convolution, then refined through finer-grained optimizations. For each such "core" sequence, the algorithm repeatedly up-samples it to create candidates at higher resolutions while maintaining the cluster-to-trough efficiency observed at coarser scales. This systematically explores the phyllotaxis (leaf arrangement) of optimal structures.

The crucial nonconvex component is that sequences achieving low objective at one resolution may be improved by higher-resolution versions. This strategy distinguishes `lp_frank_wolfe`-style local optimization from `coordinate_descent` global searches via resolution switching, which was considered an "attack surface" extension.

## Mathematical Specification
We maintain three key components:

### 1. Resolution Dimension:
Let L be an integer resolution parameter. The complete sequence has length n = 64L.
We generate candidate basis sequences:
- At L=1: basic 64-length sequences with potentially extreme features
- At L=k: sequences expanded by k-point interpolation from L=1, with objective aspen-defined score function.

### 2. The Primary Search Variable:
We define two classes of fundamental sequences:
- **Feature-Conserving (FC) Sequences**: These preserve local structure under full mode convolution, optimized using a recursive pattern refinement.
- **Orthogonal Symmetry-Enhanced (OSE) Sequences**: These enforce palindromic or mirror symmetry around critical dimensions detected via Fourier analysis, increasing the autoconvolution's coherence cancellation effect.

For a basis sequence (from L=1), the objective for a candidate at resolution L is:
    μ(a, L) = (2 * 64L * max_convolve(a ⊗ L)) / (sum(a)⊗L)^2
where ⊗L denotes resolution extension by factor L.

### 3. Stochastic Optimization Connection:
We map observed convergence bottlenecks via the following procedure:
    - Launch OSE initialization assuming Fourier modes yield candidates with at least 30% improvement potential
    - For each OSE candidate, apply multi-phase coordinate descent pruning at factors of two above/near the autoconvolution peak
    - Then sequence-filter via analytic local descriptors penalizing peaks at partial indices

All parameters are explicitly derivable: the evaluation function uses the original convolution (mode='full'). The scaling is done using FFT-aided source expansion, but only after confirming the objective score falls below 90% of the previous iteration's minimum.
