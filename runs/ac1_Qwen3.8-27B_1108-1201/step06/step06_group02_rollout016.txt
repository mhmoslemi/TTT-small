Let me think about the mirror descent / multiplicative weights approach more carefully.

We want to minimize f(a) = max_k b_k subject to sum(a) = 1, a >= 0.

The subgradient of f at a is: for the set S = {k : b_k = max}, the subgradient is the convex hull of {2 * H(e_k) * a : k in S}, where H(e_k) is the row of the Hankel matrix corresponding to peak k.

For the "top-k" approximation, use:
sg[i] = 2 * sum_{k in top_k} w_k * a[k-i] (where w_k are weights, e.g., softmax over top peaks)

Mirror descent update (KL projection):
a_new[i] = a[i] * exp(-eta * sg[i]) / sum_j a[j] * exp(-eta * sg[j])

This is the "exponential tilting" update. It naturally keeps a_i > 0 and adjusts the relative weights.

The step size eta can be adaptive: if the objective increases, halve eta.

Let me also think about a **direct approach to the peak**.

The peak is at position k*. We have:
b_{k*} = sum_i a[i] * a[k*-i]

This is a "dot product" between a and the reversed/shifted a. To minimize this:
- If k* is near the center (k* ≈ n-1), then a[i]*a[n-1-i] should be small for all i. This means a should be "anti-symmetric" (high where a[n-1-i] is low).
- If k* is near the edge (k* ≈ 0 or k* ≈ 2n-2), then a[0]^2 or a[n-1]^2 should be small.

The optimal k* is probably near the center (since that's where the uniform sequence has its peak).

For the center peak (k* = n-1):
b_{n-1} = sum_i a[i]*a[n-1-i]

To minimize this while keeping sum(a) = 1:
- Make a[i] large for small i and small for large i (or vice versa)
- This is like "anti-correlating" a with its reversal

The minimum of sum a[i]*a[n-1-i] subject to sum a = 1, a >= 0 is 0 (achieved when a[i] * a[n-1-i] = 0 for all i, i.e., a is supported on at most one of {i, n-1-i} for each pair).

But we can't just minimize the center peak - we need to minimize the MAX over all k. So we need a balanced approach.
