Approach and rationale
The core idea is to optimize the AC1 upper bound by projecting the parent array onto the simplex of unit sum and constraints while minimizing the convolution maximum. This leverages the observation that the AC1 objective is invariant to scaling, allowing the search to fix the sum of coefficients to a constant (here, 1) to simplify optimization. The algorithm uses FFT-based convolutions for efficient gradient approximation and multiplicative updates to maintain non-negativity. It sparsely samples high-convolution lags to compute gradients, reducing computational complexity. By maintaining a running minimum and periodic evaluation, it balances exploration and exploitation to converge to a low AC1 value.

Mathematical specification
- **Representation**: Non-negative real coefficients \(a_0, \dots, a_{n-1}\) summing to 1, \(0 \leq a_i \leq 1000\).
- **Objective**: Minimize \(2n \cdot \max_k(\text{convolve}(a, a)_k)\), where convolution is in `full` mode.
- **Constraints**: \(0 \leq a_i \leq 1000\), \(\sum a_i = 1\).

- **Update Rule**: Deference multiplicative scaling \(a_i \leftarrow a_i \cdot \exp(-\epsilon \cdot \text{gradient})\).
- **Implementation**: Use FFT convolution for efficiency, clip values after updates, and renormalize to maintain sum=1.
