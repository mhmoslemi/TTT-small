## Approach and rationale

The central idea is to minimize the AC1 upper bound via an iterative optimization process that leverages gradient information from the convolution peak while ensuring global exploration through stochastic perturbations. The scale-invariance of the objective (O) is key: it depends solely on the distribution of the sequence, not its absolute level, allowing implicit normalization. This differs from the base strategy by focusing on gradient-based updates with line search and early termination, rather than relying heavily on directional modifications and local gradients. The method starts from the parent sequence, computes accurate gradients using FFT-based convolution surrogates, and uses directional simplex reflections for robust optimization. A novel temperature decay schedule balances exploration and convergence, with production shifts when no improvement occurs. This approach exploits O's scale-invariance and scales better with large n (4806) by concentrating gradient computations. The main limitation is the use of a fast surrogate gradient, which might be noise-prone, but priority is given to computational efficiency within the time budget.

## Mathematical specification

### Variables
- Input: Sequence a, a list of non-negative coefficients, implicitly with sum >= 0.01 (clipped/detected by evaluator).
- Output: Sequence b that minimizes the exact AC1 objective via `evaluate_sequence(b)`.

### Objective
Minimize O = \frac{2 \cdot n \cdot \max(\text{convolve}(a, a, \text{mode}='full'}}{\left(\sum(a)\right)^2}, where convolve is linear convolution yielding 2n-1 terms.

Constraints:
- Non-negativity: All elements >= 0.
- Upper bound: All elements <= 1000 (evaluator clipped, overridden for safety).
- Sum: Sum must be at least 0.01 (evaluator returns inf otherwise).
- Invariance under scaling: O is invariant to positive scaling factor ν, i.e., O(a) = O(ν * a).

Access to the objective via `evaluate_sequence` or fast surrogate for gradients.

### Formulation for optimization
- Use `grad_smooth` for gradient estimation based on the full convolution spectrum. This computes an approximate gradient for the maximum point in the convolution, leveraging FFT convolutions for low overhead.
- Alternatively, `grad_argmax` is a heuristic method but may not capture complex behaviors, so it is reserved for fallbacks.

### Direction and step control
- Direction vector d is computed for each dimension based on gradient information via simplex reflection or grid descent.
- Step size via line search to decrease O.

### Solver requirements
For O gradient estimation:
- `grad_smooth` uses CVXPY or explicit gradient calculation via FFT.
