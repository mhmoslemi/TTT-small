1. Approach and Rationale
The core strategy involves using a genetic algorithm to iteratively refine a population of coefficient sequences, minimizing the AC1 upper bound. Unlike gradient-based methods, this approach explores a broad search space through crossover and mutation operators, making it suitable for complex, non-convex optimization problems. While gradient descent might converge faster in smooth regions, a genetic algorithm is chosen to avoid local optima and leverage its implicit parallelism. The primary challenge is balancing computational efficiency with search quality.

2. Mathematical Specification
Variables:
- Length `n` = 4806
- Sequence `a = [a₀, a₁, ..., a_{n-1}]`, where `a_i ∈ [0, 1000]` and `∑ a_i ≥ 0.01`

Objective Minimization:
- AC1 score = `2 * n * max(convolve(a, a, mode='full')) / (∑ a)^2`

Operators:
- Crossover: Uniform crossover between two parents to generate a child.
- Mutation: Gaussian mutation with bounded clamping to maintain feasibility.

Fitness Evaluation:
- Uses the provided `evaluate_sequence` function for elaborate filtering and scoring.
