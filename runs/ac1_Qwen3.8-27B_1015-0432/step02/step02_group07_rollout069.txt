The key: the score is 2*n*max(f*f)/(sum(f))^2. For uniform, max(f*f) = sum(f)^2/n (since (f*f)[n-1] = sum f[i]^2 = n*(1/n)^2 = 1/n when sum=1, and this is the max by symmetry and positivity).

Can we make max(f*f) < 1/n (with sum=1)?

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality: (f*f)[n-1] = sum f[i]*f[n-1-i] >= (sum f[i])^2/n = 1/n (by CS, treating (f[i]) and (f[n-1-i]) as two vectors with the same norm).

Wait, that's not right. CS says: (sum a_i b_i)^2 <= (sum a_i^2)(sum b_i^2). Here a_i = f[i], b_i = f[n-1-i]. sum a_i b_i = (f*f)[n-1]. (sum a_i^2)(sum b_i^2) = (sum f[i]^2)^2 (since f[n-1-i] is a permutation of f[i]).

So (f*f)[n-1]^2 <= (sum f[i]^2)^2, i.e., (f*f)[n-1] <= sum f[i]^2.

That's the upper bound, not lower. We want a lower bound on max(f*f).

By the AM-QM inequality: sum f[i]^2 >= (sum f[i])^2/n = 1/n. And (f*f)[n-1] <= sum f[i]^2. But (f*f)[n-1] could be much smaller than sum f[i]^2 if f is "anti-correlated" with its reverse. For nonnegative f, the minimum of (f*f)[n-1] = sum f[i]*f[n-1-i] given sum f=1 is... by rearrangement, it's minimized when f[i] is increasing and f[n-1-i] is decreasing, i.e., f is monotonically increasing (or decreasing).

For f monotonically increasing with sum=1: f[i] = (i+1)/(n(n+1)/2).
(f*f)[n-1] = sum f[i]*f[n-1-i] = sum [(i+1)/(n(n+1)/2)] * [(n-i)/(n(n+1)/2)]
= 4/(n^2(n+1)^2) * sum (i+1)(n-i)
= 4/(n^2(n+1)^2) * n(n+1)(n-1)/6
= 4(n-1)/(6n(n+1)) ≈ 2/(3n) < 1/n

So the center value is less than 1/n! But the max might be elsewhere.
