### Strategy to Minimize AC1 Upper Bound

The goal is to minimize the AC1 upper bound, defined as \( \frac{2n \cdot \max(\text{convolve}(a, a, \text{mode}='full')}{(\sum a)^2} \), where \( n \) is the sequence length and \( a \) is a vector of nonnegative coefficients up to 1000. The optimization uses gradient smoothing and sparse initialization to handle the non-differentiable max operation and large sequences efficiently.

#### 1. Approach and Rationale
The optimization involves approximating the non-differentiable convolution maximum using exponential smoothing. A smooth approximation \( M_\epsilon = \log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2n-1} \exp\left( \frac{c_i}{\epsilon} \right) \right) \) replaces the max, where \( c \) is the full convolution result. This smoothing allows gradient computation via log-sum-exp tricks. Initializations include dense uniform, tapered steps, exponential pulses, and stationary blocks. The L-BFGS-B optimizer with gradient projection enforces bounds and guides near optima. The implementation uses FFT for efficient convolution and gradient updates.

#### 2. Mathematical Specification
**Variables**: 
- \( n \): Sequence length (retained from evaluation)
- \( a \): Coefficient vector of length \( n \)
- \( \epsilon \): Smoothing parameter (fixed to 1 during optimization)

**Objective**:
- Smoothed objective: \( f(a, \epsilon) = \frac{2n \cdot M_\epsilon}{(\sum a)^2} \)
- Smoothed max: \( M_\epsilon = \log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2n-1} \exp\left( \frac{c_i}{\epsilon} \right) \)

**Constraints**:
- \( 0 \leq a_i \leq 1000 \) for all \( i \)
- \( \sum a \geq 0.01 \)

**Gradient**:
- Computed using log-sum-exp and linear convolution gradients:
