    if tau < 1e-12:

    d = np.maximum((c - cm) / tau, -50.0)
    e = np.exp(d)
    s = e.sum()
    return cm + tau * np.log(s), e / s

5. So the subgradient of $\max(c)$ is $2 \cdot \sum_{k \in S} \alpha_k \cdot \text{shift}(f, k)$ for some $\alpha_k \geq 0$, $\sum \alpha_k = 1$.
6. To minimize $\max(c)$, we want to move in the direction $-\nabla \max(c)$.
7. The best choice of $\alpha$ would weight the "deepest" peaks more.

This is essentially what the softmax smoothing does - it assigns weights $\alpha_k \propto \exp(c_k / \tau)$ to each lag $k$, and the gradient is $\sum_k \alpha_k \nabla c_k$.
