For nonnegative f, the DC component is fixed (sum=1), and we want the other frequency components to be as small as possible. This is related to the "Wiener-Khinchin" theorem.

Actually, that's a great insight! By Wiener-Khinchin:
- FFT of conv(f,f) = |FFT(f)|^2 (in the appropriate normalization)
- We want the IFFT of |FFT(f)|^2 to be as flat as possible
- Equivalently, we want |FFT(f)| to be as flat as possible

For a nonnegative sequence, the DC component (FFT at frequency 0) is sum(f) = 1. We want all other frequency components to be as small as possible. But we can't make them zero (that would require f to be a delta, which makes the time-domain convolution a delta too).

This is related to the "minimum peak autocorrelation" problem in radar/signal processing.

Let me also think about whether the gradient is correct. The issue is that we're computing:
- b = conv(f, f) (the autocorrelation)
- w = softmax(beta * (b - max(b))) (weights on the peaks)
- c = conv(f, w[::-1]) (cross-correlation)
- g = 2 * c[n-1:2n-1][::-1] (extracted gradient)

The factor of 2 comes from the fact that b[k] = sum f[i]*f[k-i] depends on f twice. The gradient of b[k] w.r.t. f[j] is f[k-j] + f[j-k] (if both valid), which is effectively 2*f[k-j] (by symmetry of the valid range... actually not exactly, but approximately for the central part).

The cross-correlation conv(f, w[::-1])[k] = sum_i f[i] * w[n-1-(k-i)] = sum_i f[i] * w[n-1-k+i].

For k = n-1+j: = sum_i f[i] * w[j+i].

So g[j] = 2 * sum_i f[i] * w[i+j] for j=0,...,n-1.

This is approximately 2 * sum_k w[k] * f[k-j] (the cross-correlation of f with w), which is the "blur" of f by w. This makes sense as a gradient: if w has mass at positions where b is large, then the gradient points in the direction of reducing f at positions that contribute to those peaks.
