For a uniform sequence, the convolution is triangular with peak at center = S²/n. Score = 2.
To get below 2, we need to "flatten" the convolution. This means we need the sequence to be shaped so that the convolution is flat.

Consider a sequence that's high in the middle and low at edges. The convolution at the center would be lower (less overlap of large values), and at the edges it would also be lower. The convolution might be more flat.

Actually, for the specific problem of minimizing max convolution / (sum)², the optimal shape might be related to the inverse of the triangular kernel. If c[k] should be constant, then we need a * a = constant, which means a should be the "square root" of a flat function in convolution space. In Fourier space, |A(ω)|² should be flat, so |A(ω)| = constant, meaning A(ω) is a rectangle in frequency domain, which corresponds to a sinc in time domain. But sinc changes sign, and we need nonnegative.

The constraint a >= 0 makes this more complex. The optimal shape is likely a "bell" or "U" shape.
