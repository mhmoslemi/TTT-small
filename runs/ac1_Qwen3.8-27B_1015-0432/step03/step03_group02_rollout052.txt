Let me also think about whether there's a closed-form optimal. The problem is:
minimize max_k (f*f)[k] subject to sum f = 1, f >= 0.

This is equivalent to: minimize M subject to (f*f)[k] <= M for all k, sum f = 1, f >= 0.

This is a quadratic program (the constraints are quadratic). Not trivially solvable in closed form.

However, there's a connection to polynomial multiplication. If f is the coefficient vector of a polynomial P(x) = sum f[i] x^i, then (f*f)[k] is the coefficient of x^k in P(x)². We want to minimize the max coefficient of P(x)² subject to P(1) = 1 and f >= 0.

This is related to the problem of finding polynomials with small coefficients, which is a classic problem in discrete mathematics.

For the specific case, I don't think there's a simple closed form. The iterative approach is the way to go.

def fft_conv(a, b):
    """FFT-based linear convolution."""
    n, m = len(a), len(b)
    size = 1 << (n + m - 2).bit_length()
    result = np.fft.irfft(np.fft.rfft(a, size) * np.fft.rfft(b, size))
    return result[:n + m - 1]
