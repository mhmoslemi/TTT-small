I think the key insight for large n is:
- The peak of the convolution is at the center (k = n-1) for "reasonable" (not too asymmetric) sequences
- The center value is sum_j f[j]*f[n-1-j], which is the "flip inner product" of f with itself
- To reduce this, we want f[j] and f[n-1-j] to be "anti-correlated" (one large, one small)
- But this creates peaks at other lags

The optimal solution likely has a "sawtooth" or "staircase" shape where f increases roughly linearly from one end to the other. This spreads the convolution peak across multiple lags.

For a linear ramp f[j] = j+1 (j=0,...,n-1):
- sum = n(n+1)/2
- conv[k] = sum_j (j+1)(k-j+1) for valid j
- The center value conv[n-1] = sum_j (j+1)(n-j) for j=0,...,n-1
  = sum_j j(n-j) + sum_j (n-j) = n*sum(j) - sum(j^2) + n^2 - n*sum(1)/...
