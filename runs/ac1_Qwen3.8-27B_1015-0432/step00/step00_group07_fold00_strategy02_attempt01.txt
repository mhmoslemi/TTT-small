To address the problem of minimizing the AC1 upper bound (defined as \( \frac{2n \cdot \max(\text{convolve}(a, a))}{(\sum(a))^2} \)) for a sequence of coefficients, a combination of gradient-based optimization and adaptive perturbation strategies is employed. The approach leverages the gradient information from a carefully targeted evaluation to refine the sequence, aiming to reduce the maximum autocorrelation peak while maintaining a feasible sum. The complexity of the gradient function is handled by discrete subgradient estimates, ensuring numerical stability and efficient convergence within the time budget.

**1. Approach and rationale**

The core idea is to use a gradient-descent-like method with adaptive steps to minimize the AC1 objective. The gradient at the current sequence point guides the perturbations to move towards potentially lower scores. This avoids costly evaluations for each coordinate in a naive Gauss-Newton style and helps escape local minima by switching between local exploration and global escape modes. The strategy starts with the provided parent sequence (`height_sequence_1`), which serves as a warm-start. Gradient information is approximated by self-perturbation at a low rate, reducing the total number of full evaluations needed. The method is chosen for its balance between computational efficiency and improvement potential, as it adaptively refines the sequence without requiring dense matrices or excessive evaluations.

**Comparison to previous approach:** While the previous strategy used simulated annealing with gradient-based perturbations, this approach incorporates a more structured gradient handling that dynamically adjusts based on directional sensitivity. It improves upon the parent by making more information-efficient updates, reducing the long-term evaluation cost.

**Key limitations and assumptions:** Gradient estimation is approximate, so the method may not achieve theoretical optima, and convergence depends on the sequence of evaluations and the score landscape. The algorithm assumes that smaller gradients correspond to beneficial directions for score reduction. The clamping bounds (0-1000) are enforced during and post-optimization, accepting their numerical implications.

**Bottleneck addressed:** The previous score of 2.0 can be improved by switching the search from local annealing to gradient-guided refinement, which is more targeted. This approach reduces wasted energy in less consequential dimensions.

**Drawbacks:** If the initial sequence is poor, improving moves may be slow. Gradient estimation introduces noise, potentially oversmoothing good improvements or overemphasizing bad ones.

**2. Mathematical specification**

- **Representation**: A sequence \( a \) is represented as a real-valued vector of length \( n = 6733 \) with components \( a_i \in [0, 1000] \).
- **Objective**: Minimize \( E(a) = \frac{2n}{(\sum a_i)^2} \cdot \max_{k=0}^{2n-2} |\text{convolve}(a, a, \text{mode}="full")[k]| \), where \( \text{convolve}(a, a, \text{mode}="full") \) produces a sequence of length \( 2n - 1 \). The minimization must ensure \( \sum a_i \geq 0.01 \) to avoid division by zero after evaluator clipping.
- **Constraints**: \( a_i \geq 0 \) for all \( i \) and \( a_i \leq 1000 \) for all \( i \).
- **Gradient analysis**: For a small localized perturbation \( \delta i \) at index \( i \), the directional derivative is approximated as:
  - \( \frac{\partial E}{\partial a_i} \approx \frac{E(a + \delta \epsilon \delta i) - E(a)}{\delta \epsilon} \), where \( \epsilon \in \{-1, +1\} \) and \( \delta i \) is a small block mask (e.g., indices \( i \pm 5 \)), ensuring \( |\delta \epsilon| < 0.1 \) for stability.
  - This avoids the undefined max gradient by using one-sided finite differences.
- **Optimization formulation**: Minimize \( E(a) \) subject to \( 0 \leq a_i \leq 1000 \) using a gradient-based descent with an adaptive step, initially \( \beta = 0.15 \).

**PCP subsampling**: To reconcile the full convolution, we approximate the gradient with a subsampled local computation. The subproblem is:
- Evaluate \( E \) once per coarse block per optimization step.
- Use \( \beta \) perturbation adjustment: \( a \leftarrow a - \beta \cdot \text{sign}\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial a}\right) + \text{noise for diversity} \), but the full evaluation is costly, so we model it differently.
