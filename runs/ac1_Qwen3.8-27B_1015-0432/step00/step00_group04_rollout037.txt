Actually, I recall that for the "peak-to-average" ratio problem in autocorrelation, sequences with "low peak factor" are desirable. In OFDM and related fields, this is the "peak-to-average power ratio" (PAPR) problem.

For our problem, we want to minimize max(c[k]) / (S/n)² (since S/n is the average). Actually our objective is 2n*max(c)/S², and S/n is the mean, so S²/n² is the mean-squared. So max(c)/(S/n)² = n*max(c)/S² and our objective is 2*(max(c)/(S/n)²).

So we want to minimize 2 * (peak / mean²) of the autocorrelation.

For a random sequence (iid exponential), the peak of the autocorrelation is approximately (n²/n) * log(n) or something... actually for a random sequence with mean μ and variance σ², c[n-1] ≈ n*μ² + n*σ² = n(μ²+σ²) and other c[k] ≈ n*μ². The peak/mean² ≈ (μ²+σ²)/μ² = 1 + (σ/μ)².

For an exponential distribution, σ/μ = 1, so peak/mean² ≈ 2, giving score ≈ 4. Worse than uniform.

For a Bernoulli(p) sequence, σ/μ = sqrt((1-p)/p), and peak/mean² ≈ 1 + (1-p)/p = 1/p. Score ≈ 2/p. For p near 1, score ≈ 2. For p = 0.5, score ≈ 4.

So uniform (p=1 effectively) is the best among "random" sequences.
