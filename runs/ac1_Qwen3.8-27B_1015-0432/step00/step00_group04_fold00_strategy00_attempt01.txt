We use a hybrid candidate generation strategy that uses a surrogate (FFT-based) objective to repeatedly perturb a block of the current best candidate. The key idea is to use the speed of the FFT convolution to guide the search toward lower AC1 bounds without incurring the heavy cost of the exact convolution for every candidate. 

The algorithm starts with the parent sequence and alters it by block-wise perturbations, which include random rupture, multiplicative noise, and reflection. These small, targeted changes explore the solution space locally, ensuring we do not deviate too far from good candidates. The exact evaluator is used sparingly to validate candidates that show promise, thus balancing between exploration and exploitation. This approach ensures that we minimize the AC1 upper bound effectively while adhering to the memory and time constraints.

We choose FFT convolution for its speed compared to direct convolution, which is O(n^2), and we hope that the local search will soon find a candidate that, after exact evaluation, yields a lower AC1 bound.
