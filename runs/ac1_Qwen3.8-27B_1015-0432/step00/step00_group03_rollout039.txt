Actually, for a symmetric U-shape: $f_j = f_{n-1-j}$. Then $(f*f)_{n-1} = \sum_j f_j^2$. By Cauchy-Schwarz, this is minimized when $f$ is uniform. So a U-shape INCREASES the center value! That's the wrong direction.

For a symmetric "n-shape" (low at edges, high in center): $f_j = f_{n-1-j}$, center high. Then $\sum f_j^2$ is also larger (more concentrated). Also bad.

So for symmetric sequences, the uniform is optimal for the center value. We need asymmetry!

**Key insight: Asymmetry helps.**

If $f$ is such that $f_j$ is "anti-correlated" with $f_{n-1-j}$, then $(f*f)_{n-1} = \sum f_j f_{n-1-j}$ is small.

- $S = n/2$

$(f*f)_{n-1} = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} f_j f_{n-1-j}$.

For $j < n/2$: $f_j = 1$, and $n-1-j > n/2-1$, so $f_{n-1-j} = 0$.
For $j \geq n/2$: $f_j = 0$.

So $(f*f)_{n-1} = 0$. The center is 0!
