Let me try a different approach: use a multiplicative update or a "water-filling" strategy.

Actually, here's a key insight: the problem is equivalent to:
minimize max_k conv(a,a)[k]
subject to sum(a) = n, a >= 0

At the optimum, by the KKT conditions, the gradient of the max should be constant on the support of a (where a > 0). Specifically, if S is the set of active constraints (k where conv(a,a)[k] = max), then:
(1/|S|) * sum_{k in S} grad_k(a) = lambda * 1 (constant on support)

And for a[j] > 0: (1/|S|) * sum_{k in S} 2*a[k-j] = lambda (roughly)
For a[j] = 0: the same expression >= lambda

This suggests an iterative water-filling:
1. Compute conv(a,a)
2. Find active set S (k where conv is at maximum)
3. Compute the "pressure" at each j: p[j] = (1/|S|) * sum_{k in S, valid} 2*a[k-j]
4. Update: a[j] += eta * (lambda - p[j]) where lambda is chosen to maintain sum(a) = n
5. Clip to [0, ∞) and renormalize

This is essentially the projected subgradient method, but the water-filling interpretation makes it clearer.
