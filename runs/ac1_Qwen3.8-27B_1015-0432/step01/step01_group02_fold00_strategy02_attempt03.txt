To solve this problem, we need to minimize the AC1 upper bound for a given sequence of non-negative coefficients. The AC1 upper bound is defined as the maximum of the autocorrelation of the sequence divided by the square of the sum of the sequence. The challenge is to find a sequence that minimizes this bound efficiently and effectively.

### Approach
1. **Initialization**: Start with the provided parent sequence (or a uniform distribution if no parent is available). Ensure the sequence is clipped to [0, 1000] and adjust its length if necessary.

2. **Gradient Descent with Progressive Projection**: Use a gradient-based method to iteratively update the sequence. The gradient is approximated by focusing on the top K lags with the highest autocorrelation values. The update direction is derived from the negligible effect on these lags, ensuring non-negativity and boundedness of coefficients.

3. **Adaptive Parameters**: Adjust the step size (alpha) and the number of top lags (topK) dynamically based on the improvement in the score. If the score plateaus, increase alpha and topK to explore different lags.

4. **Fallback Handling**: If the sequence becomes invalid (sum is too low or contains NaNs/INFs), repair it by perturbing and recomputing.

5. **Termination**: The algorithm stops when the time budget is exceeded or after a specified number of iterations, returning the best valid candidate found.

1. **Initialization**: The algorithm starts by loading the initial sequence (either provided parent or uniform distribution). The sequence is normalized by clipping coefficients to [0, 1000] and adjusting the length to the required size.

2. **Gradient Descent**: For each iteration, the algorithm computes the full convolution to determine the top K lags. The gradient vector is updated based on the contributions from these lags, weighted by their convolution values. The sequence is then updated in the direction of the gradient, clipped to [0, 1000] to maintain feasibility.

3. **Adaptive Adjustments**: If the score does not improve for a certain number of iterations, the step size (alpha) and the number of top lags (topK) are adjusted to explore different regions of the search space.

4. **Fallback and Validation**: Invalid sequences (sum < 0.01 or out-of-bounds) are repaired by scaling and perturbation. The best candidate is retained based on the lowest AC1 score.

5. **Termination**: The algorithm exits when the time budget is depleted, returning the best candidate found. This ensures efficient use of computational resources.
