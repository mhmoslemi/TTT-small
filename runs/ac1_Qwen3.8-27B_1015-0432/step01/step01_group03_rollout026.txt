The condition max_c < 1/n (for score < 2) means that for all k, sum_j a[j]*a[k-j] < 1/n.

This is a system of 2n-1 quadratic inequalities. For large n, most of these are "easy" (the sum has few terms), but the middle ones (near k = n-1) have ~n terms.

For the middle lags, the constraint is: sum_j a[j]*a[n-1-j] < 1/n, sum_j a[j]*a[n-j] < 1/n, etc.

The first one is the "reversal correlation" and the others are "near-reversal" correlations.

By the rearrangement inequality, for fixed multiset {a[j]}, the sum sum a[j]*a[n-1-j] is maximized when a[j] and a[n-1-j] are similarly sorted. So to minimize this, we want a[j] to be "anti-correlated" with a[n-1-j].

But we also need the OTHER lags (n-2, n, n-3, n+1, etc.) to be small. This creates a complex set of constraints.

I think the optimal solution is a sequence that oscillates with a period related to n/2, creating destructive interference in the autocorrelation.

For example, a[j] ≈ cos(πj) (alternating) would give zero autocorrelation at odd lags, but the even-lag autocorrelation would be large. With non-negativity constraint, the best we can do is a[j] = (1+cos(πj))/n = (1+(-1)^j)/n, which is 2/n for even j and 0 for odd j. This is a "comb" function.

For the comb: a is nonzero at n/2 positions.
c[k] = sum_j a[j]*a[k-j]. For even k=2m: c[2m] = (2/n)^2 * (number of pairs) = 4/n^2 * (m+1) for m=0..n/2-1.
