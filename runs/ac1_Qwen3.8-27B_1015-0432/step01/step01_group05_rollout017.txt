OK so now I understand the problem better. The key is that `np.convolve(a, a)` is NOT the autocorrelation in the usual sense. It's the convolution, and the "center" (lag n-1) is where the full overlap happens.

So the objective is to minimize `max_k sum_j a[j]*a[k-j]` relative to `(sum a)^2/n`.

This is equivalent to minimizing the maximum coefficient of the autocorrelation polynomial. For a "flat" spectrum, these would all be equal to `(sum a)^2 / (2n-1)` roughly, but that's not quite right either.

Actually, for a binary sequence with entries in {0,1}, the problem becomes: choose a subset S of {0,...,n-1} to minimize `max_k |{j : j in S and k-j in S}|` / `(|S|)^2 * 2n`.

This is related to "difference sets" or "Golomb rulers" - we want the number of representations of each k as j + (k-j) with both j and k-j in S to be as uniform as possible (and as small as possible).

For the continuous case, we want to find nonnegative weights that minimize the peak of the "autocorrelation".
