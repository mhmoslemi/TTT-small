Let me think of another approach: **Use a smooth surrogate for max**. Replace max(c) with a soft-max: `soft_max(c, tau) = tau * log(sum exp(c[k]/tau))`. For small tau, this approximates max. The gradient of soft_max is well-defined everywhere.

Gradient of soft_max(c, tau) w.r.t. p[i]:
d(soft_max)/dp[i] = (1/tau) * sum_k exp(c[k]/tau) * c_k' * tau / sum_k exp(c[k]/tau)
= sum_k softmax_k * c_k' / sum_k exp(c[k]/tau) * sum_k exp(c[k]/tau)
= sum_k softmax_k * 2*p[k-i] (for valid k-i)

This is a weighted average of the subgradients from all k, weighted by a softmax. This gives a smooth gradient!

- Minimize soft_max(convolve(p,p), tau) subject to sum(p)=1, p>=0
