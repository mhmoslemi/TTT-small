So for peak at position $k_j$, the contribution to $h[i]$ is $w_j \cdot f[k_j - i]$ for valid $i$. This is essentially: if we increase $f[i]$ by $\delta$, the peak at $k_j$ increases by $\delta \cdot f[k_j - i]$ (to first order). So $h[i]$ is the weighted sum of "gradients" at the peaks.

Then the update is $a \leftarrow a \cdot \exp(-\alpha \cdot h / h_{max})$, which multiplicatively reduces elements that contribute more to the peaks.

This is similar to "mirror descent" or "multiplicative weights" for the problem.

Improvements I can think of:

1. **Use log-sum-exp instead of top-K**: Instead of discretely selecting top-K peaks, use a soft version: $w_k = \exp(\beta \cdot c_k) / \sum \exp(\beta \cdot c_l)$. This gives a smooth gradient.

2. **Normalize differently**: The current code normalizes by $h_{max}$. Maybe we should normalize by the sum or mean.

3. **Use the exact subgradient**: Instead of just the contribution at peaks, use the full "pressure" from all convolution lags, weighted by how much they exceed the target level.

4. **Bipartite update**: Update in a way that preserves the sum more naturally.

5. **Annealing**: Start with a large $\alpha$ (aggressive) and decrease it.

6. **Multiple scales**: Run the optimization at a coarser resolution first, then refine.
