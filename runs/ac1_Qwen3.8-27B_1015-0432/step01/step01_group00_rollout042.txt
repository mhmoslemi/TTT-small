Let me also think about the "LP update" from the problem statement. The idea is:
1. Given current $f$ and $M = \max(b)$, solve:
   $$\max \sum_j g_j \quad \text{s.t.} \quad (f \star g)_k \leq M, \quad g_j \geq 0$$
2. Rescale $g$ to $\sum g = 1$.
3. Update $f \leftarrow (1-t)f + tg$.

This is a "best response" step: given the constraint $(f \star g)_k \leq M$, find the $g$ that maximizes the sum (i.e., the "widest" sequence that fits under the $f$-convolution bound).

The dual of this LP is:
$$\min M \sum_k \lambda_k \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \sum_k \lambda_k (f \star e_j)_k \geq 1 \quad \forall j, \quad \lambda_k \geq 0$$

where $e_j$ is the $j$-th standard basis vector.

$(f \star e_j)_k = f_{k-j}$ (when valid). So the constraint is:
$$\sum_k \lambda_k f_{k-j} \geq 1 \quad \forall j$$

This is $\lambda \star f \geq 1$ (element-wise), where $\lambda$ has length $2n-1$ and $f$ has length $n$.

The objective is $M \sum_k \lambda_k$.

This is a dual NP-hard problem in general, but the structure (convolution) might help. However, implementing this efficiently is complex.

$$\delta b_k = \sum_i \frac{\partial b_k}{\partial f_i} \delta f_i = 2 \sum_i f_{k-i} \delta f_i$$

$$2 \sum_i f_{k_j - i} \delta f_i = M - b_{k_j} \quad \text{for } j = 1, \ldots, K$$
