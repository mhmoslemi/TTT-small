    n = len(a)

Yes, for a signal of length N with unit sampling rate, `rfftfreq(N)` returns `[0, 1/N, 2/N, ..., N/2/N]` = `[0, 1/N, 2/N, ..., 1/2]`. These are in cycles per sample (or cycles per unit, since the sampling rate is 1).

The Gaussian window in the spatial domain: $g[i] = \exp(-i^2 / (2\sigma^2))$ centered at $i=0$.

Its DFT: $\hat{g}[k] \approx \sigma\sqrt{2\pi} \cdot \exp(-2\pi^2 (k/N)^2 \sigma^2)$

where $k/N = f_k$ is the frequency in cycles/sample.

So the damping factor is $\exp(-2\pi^2 f^2 \sigma^2)$ where $f = k/N$.

With `fr = np.fft.rfftfreq(N)`, `fr[k] = k/N`. So `dp = np.exp(-2*np.pi**2 * sig**2 * fr**2)` is correct. ✓
