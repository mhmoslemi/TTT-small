1. **Approach and rationale**

This approach uses a surrogated-based convex optimization strategy with a warm start from the parent sequence, combined with stochastic gradient descent for exploration. The central idea is to approximate the costly exact evaluation (full convolution) with a fast, spectral-based surrogate that leverages the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) for efficient gradient estimation. This reduces the per-candidate evaluation time, allowing more aggressive search within the 1100-second budget. The method addresses the need for matrix-free formulations by avoiding explicit dense storage and instead using implicit handles for the autocorrelation computations. By decoupling sequence updates from the expensive convolution checks, we can explore the space more widely and converge to a lower AC1 bound, potentially breaking the previous 2.0 ceiling through better normalization and adaptive confidence setups.

Key limitations:
- The surrogate_estimation may introduce bias or variance, especially if the sequence is not smooth, potentially wasting search budget on inaccessible directions.
- Reliance on DFT for gradient estimation assumes the sequence is approximately frequency-based, which may not always hold, and Manhattan regularization might penalize structured sequences inconsistently.
- Not exhaustive or guaranteed to find the global optimum.
- This is a heuristic method; performance depends on parameter tuning. Despite these, it offers theoretical novelty by embedding a projected gradient step post-surrogate check.
