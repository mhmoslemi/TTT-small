1.Approach and Rationale
The central idea is to optimize a non-negative real-valued sequence to minimize the AC1 upper bound by leveraging the piecewise constant nature inherent in step-function representations. This approach closely resembles a step-function with discontinuous heights at specific breakpoints, reducing the high-dimensional space (n coefficients) to a lower-dimensional manifold defined by segment heights and positions. Drawing from atomic norm concepts, this formulation enforces sparsity in the optimization space by minimizing a proxy of the autocorrelation norm, which aligns with the objective function. While the risk of early termination due to local minima exists, the algorithm incorporates adaptive cooling for gradients and boundary recomputation to manage this risk. Iterative refinement ensures convergence or near-optimum, and re-normalization prevents premature convergence to invalid states. This method improves upon the parent program by explicitly adhering to step-function constraints, which reduces computation steps and avoids poorly performing coordinate-wise updates.

2.Mathematical Specification

- **Breakpoints (b)**: Define segments on the index domain [0, 4497]. Represented as a sorted list (numpy array) of length k, with 0 ≤ b[0] < b[1] < ... < b[k-1] < 4498.
- **Heights (h)**: Non-negative real numbers defining constant values on each segment. Represented as a list (numpy array) of length k, with h[i] ∈ ℝ^+ for i from 0 to k-1, and h[i] ≤ 1000 (clamped).
- Total sum: ∑_{i=0}^{k-1} (b[i+1] - b[i]) * h[i] / total length, capability to maintain values.

Constraints:
- Segment boundaries b must be strictly increasing: b[i+1] > b[i].
- Heights h must be non-negative and not exceed 1000.
- Sum of coefficients must be ≥0.01 (handled by scaling).
- Autocorrelation constraint implicitly enforced by score.
