One approach: think of the problem in the Fourier domain. Let A(ω) = FFT(a). Then the convolution a*a has DFT A(ω)^2. The max of a*a is bounded by ||A(ω)^2||_1 / N (by the Hausdorff-Young inequality or just the triangle inequality).

To minimize max(a*a), we want A(ω)^2 to be as "spread out" as possible in the frequency domain, which means A(ω) should be as "flat" as possible. But A(0) = S is fixed, and A(ω) for ω ≠ 0 represents the "non-DC" components.

For a = ones(n), A(0) = n, A(ω≠0) = 0. The convolution is triangular.
For a = random, A(ω) ≈ flat. The convolution has a peak at lag 0 but is "noisy" elsewhere.

The optimal is somewhere in between: a smooth sequence with specific oscillations that make A(ω)^2 as flat as possible.
