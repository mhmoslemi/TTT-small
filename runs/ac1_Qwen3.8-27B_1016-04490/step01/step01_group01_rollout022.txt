Let me also think about the gradient more carefully.

The objective: $L = 2n \cdot M / S^2$ where $M = \max_k c_k$ and $c_k = (a*a)[k]$.

Subgradient of $L$ w.r.t. $a_j$:
$\partial L / \partial a_j = 2n [ (\partial M / \partial a_j) / S^2 - 2M / S^3 ]$

$\partial M / \partial a_j = \sum_k w_k \cdot 2a_{k-j} \cdot \mathbf{1}[0 \leq k-j < n]$

where $w_k$ are weights on the active peaks (summing to 1).

This can be computed efficiently using FFT: for each peak $k$, the contribution to the gradient is $2 \cdot a$ shifted and reversed, weighted by $w_k$.

    S = a.sum()
    mean_a = S / n
    peak_idx = np.argpartition(b, -n_peaks)[-n_peaks:]
    pv = b[peak_idx]

This computes $\sum_k w_k (a_{k-j} - \text{mean})$ for the valid range of each peak. The subtraction of the mean accounts for the $-2M/S^3$ term (since $\partial S / \partial a_j = 1$ and the mean of 1 over $n$ is 1, but... hmm, it's not exactly right, but it's a reasonable approximation).

Actually, the exact gradient should be:
$\partial L / \partial a_j = 2n/S^2 \cdot \sum_k w_k \cdot 2a_{k-j} \cdot \mathbf{1}[0 \leq k-j < n] - 2n \cdot 2M/S^3$

$= 4n/S^2 \cdot \sum_k w_k \cdot a_{k-j} \cdot \mathbf{1}[0 \leq k-j < n] - 4nM/S^3$

The second term is a constant (doesn't depend on $j$), so for gradient descent (especially multiplicative), it just shifts the whole gradient uniformly. The previous code subtracts `mean_a` which is $S/n$, not the exact constant, but it's a reasonable approximation.
