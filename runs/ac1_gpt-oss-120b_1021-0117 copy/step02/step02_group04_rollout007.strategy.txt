1. Approach and rationale
We address the problem by performing a constrained optimization to minimize the AC1 upper bound for a nonnegative step function. Unlike the initial program that uses a stochastic search with various update rules, our approach relies on a linear programming (LP) relaxation that ensures theoretical progress towards the optimal solution. The core idea is to maintain a normalized sequence with unit sum (to account for the scale invariance of the AC1 bound) and iteratively refine it by solving an LP that projects the sequence onto a convex region defined by the convolution constraints.

The bound to minimize is:
   score = (2 * n * max(convolve(f, f))) / (sum(f))²
where n is the sequence length, f is the sequence, and the convolution is the full convolution (mode="full") of order 2n-1.

Our method has three main components:
- A normalized representation where sum(f) = √(2n) (ensuring scale invariance)
- An LP that projects f onto the set {g | ||f * g||_∞ ≤ M} (where M is the current condition number)
- A projected gradient step that updates f using the solution of the LP

This approach offers guarantees of progress and convergence in the direction of improved AC1 bounds, unlike heuristic methods. The main limitations are the inability to dynamically adjust the dimension and the potential high computational cost of forming the sparse constraint matrix.
