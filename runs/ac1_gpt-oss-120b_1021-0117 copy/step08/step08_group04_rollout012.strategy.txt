  Approach and rationale
  The goal is to minimize the AC1 upper bound for a sequence of nonnegative coefficients. The previous approaches used gradient-based optimization, which could get stuck in poor local optima or be computationally expensive for large vectors. This new method employs a **stochastic greedy coordinate descent** search that explicitly accounts for the objective's discreteness. Instead of optimizing with fixed constraints, we sample components conditionally and projects directly onto the feasible set, ensuring meaningful steps that reduce the maximum auto-correlation while scaling adaptively.

  The key innovations are:
  1. **Conditional component replacement**: At each iteration, we sample a new coefficient by retaining probability mass near the current value while occasionally exploring new regions via exponentials and logarithms that preserve the simplex structure.
  2. **Adaptive scaling**: The standard deviation of coefficient updates adapts inversely to the norm of the gradient and convexity measure, ensuring efficient exploration.
  3. **Multi-gradient descent**: We combine full-gradient trust-region steps with stochastic local moves to ensure both neighborhood coverage and systematization.
