### Strategy Explanation

We minimize the AC1 upper bound score by optimizing a non-negative sequence with sum constrained to 1 (simplex projection). Our approach uses iterative projected gradient descent with exponential gradient estimation to handle non-differentiability. Key innovations include:

1. **Projection to Simplex**: Optimizations are performed by projecting onto the simplex to ensure non-negative sequences with sum=1, which simplifies the objective since scaling preserves the score.

2. **Exponential Gradient Estimation**: Instead of directly computing the gradient (which is challenging due to the max operator), we use exponential temperature weighting to approximate the subgradient. This accounts for peaks in the convolution and balances exploration.

3. **Efficient Gradient Calculation**: We compute the gradient as the compact dot product of a windowed weight vector (from exponential weighting) with the current sequence, avoiding expensive matrix operations.

4. **Temporally Decaying Annealing**: Parameters are adapted during optimization: high initial temperature for exploration, followed by slow cooldown for refinement, and step size reduction for stability.

5. **Fallback to Best Candidate**: Maintain the historically best solution since the objective is non-convex and early runs may fall into local minima.

### 1. Approach and Rationale

**Goal**: Optimize a non-negative sequence with sum=1 to minimize its AC1 upper bound (2*n*max(convolution length))/(sum(sequence))^2). The AC1 upper bound depends on the maximal autocorrelation value.

**Challenges**:
- The score involves a costly linear convolution.
- The optimization domain is high-dimensional (n=2535) with non-convex structure.
- The objective's non-differentiability at maxima requires approximation.

**Solution Strategy**:
- **Gradient-Free with Estimation**: Use exponential smoothing to approximate gradients, turning the problem into a bounded-optimization variant.
- **Projected Gradient Descent**: After each gradient step, project into the simplex to maintain constraints.
- **Simulated Annealing**: Incorporate stochastic acceptance for uphill moves to escape local minima.

**Motivation**:
- By leveraging exponential weighting, we trade weak-gradient signals for computational resilience.
- The simplex projection ensures valid candidates.
- Temperature decay adapts to feature scales, enabling tighter convergence.

### 2. Mathematical Specification

**Variables**:
- **Input/Output**: A simplex-normalized sequence, `a ∈ ℝⁿ⁺` (sum=1, aᵢ ≥ 0).
- **Objective**: Minimize score(a) = (2*n*∥a* a ∥∞)/1 (since ∑a=1)  
  (where ∥a* a ∥∞ is the maximal element of linear convolution).

**Constraints**:
- Non-negativity: aᵢ ≥ 0 for all i.
- Simplex: ∑aᵢ = 1, hence score is independent of a's scaling.

**Gradient Estimation**:

- Gradient: `gᵢ = ∑ weight_k * a[k-i]` over relevant indices.

**Energy**: Score is the energy to minimize; derivatives lead to optimization updates.

**Optimization Requirements**:
- Gradient estimates used via linesearch and step size annealing.
- Iterations persist until timeout or negligible improvement.
