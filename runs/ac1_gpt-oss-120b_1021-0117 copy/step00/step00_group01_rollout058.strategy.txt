1. **Approach and rationale**
   We are minimizing a highly non-convex function: the AC1 upper bound, which depends on the entire autocorrelation structure of the sequence. Given the large dimensionality (n=6121), a direct grid search is infeasible. Instead, we use a combination of initialization, local search with finite-difference gradients, and an adaptive line search along the steepest descent direction. This method is competitive with traditional optimization but avoids prohibitively expensive computations by using a restart mechanism and dimensionality-reducing projections.

2. **Mathematical specification**
   Let \(a \in \mathbb{R}^n\) be the sequence. The objective is \(f(a) = \frac{2n \max_{i}(a * a[i])}{(\sum_j a_j)^2}\) where \(*\) denotes linear convolution. We minimize \(f(a)\) under constraints \(0 \leq a_i \leq 1000\) and \(\sum_j a_j \geq 0.01\) (robustified by evaluator). Finite-difference gradients are approximated for \(a\) in embedded random projections. The update is \(a_{t+1} = a_t - \alpha_t g_t\) where \(g_t \approx \nabla f(a_t)\) and \(\alpha_t \approx \argmin_\alpha f(a_t - \alpha g_t)\).
