## 1. Approach and Rationale

The central idea is to minimize the AC1 upper bound using a coordinate-wise optimization method combined with line search. Given the non-differentiability of the max function in the objective, we employ a stochastic gradient approximation via coordinate-wise perturbations. This method efficiently navigates the highly nonlinear search space while respecting the sparsity constraints. Although the problem is nonconvex, the combination of dimensionality invariance and local line searches ensures plausible solutions within computational limits.

Key limitations include: 1) potential local optima due to coordinate-wise updates, 2) sensitivity to line search parameters, and 3) heavy reliance on gradient approximations. However, the method leverages the self-convolution structure and uses FFT-based operations for efficient computation.

## 2. Mathematical Specification

### Representation
The candidate is represented as a half-normalized random walk signal:
`a = [w0, w1, ..., w_{n-1}]`

### Variables
Length $n$ coefficients. Each represents a transform coefficient from a random walk process.

### Objective
Minimize:
`score = (2*n) * max(conv(a, a)) / (sum(a))²`

### Constraints
- $0 \leq a_i \leq 1000$ for all $i$
- $sum(a) \geq 0.01$
- Self-convolution length: $2n-1$

### Surrogate Gradient
Approximate gradient components via coordinate-wise line search:
`∂score/∂a_i ≈ α_i * sign(-score_change)`
where $α_i$ is the optimal step size.

### Random Walk Projection
Quantize coefficients via:
$a_i = floor(exp(N(0,2.0)))$
followed by simplex projection.
