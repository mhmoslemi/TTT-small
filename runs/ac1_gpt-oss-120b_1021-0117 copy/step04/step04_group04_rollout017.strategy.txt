   The central idea is to use a step-function-based optimization strategy to minimize the AC1 upper bound. This approach constructs the sequence from a few contiguous intervals (steps) where each step has a constant value. By doing so, we can focus optimization on the separation and amplitude distribution of these steps, which directly influences the autocorrelation's maximum value. The rationale is that isolated pulses (sharp changes in the sequence) tend to produce smaller maximum autocorrelation compared to smooth distributions, addressing the gradient-based methods' limitation of getting stuck in local optima. This method initializes candidates from a uniform parent sequence and improves them through amplitude variations. An unavoidable limitation is that this does not explore all sequence layouts but focuses on structured solutions that can be refined efficiently.
