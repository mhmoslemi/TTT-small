2. Mathematical specification
Defin the representation: since the AC1 upper bound is minimized by sequences that are approximately harmonic, the formulation leverages complex Fourier modes parameterized by real coefficients for energy distribution. For a sequence $a = (a_k)_k$, define $a_k = r_k e^{i\theta_k}$, with $r_k \geq 0$ and $\theta_k \in [-\pi, \pi)$. However, this complex parameterization is unsuitable for direct numerical optimization due to scale and phase issues.

Instead, use the following real-valued parameterization: 
- $a_k = \sum_{i=1}^P w_i \phi_i(k)$, 
where $\phi_i(k)$ are base functions (trapezoidal profiles centered at grid points), $w_i \geq 0$ are weights, and $P$ is the number of active components.

The optimization variable is a vector $w \in \mathbb{R}^P$ with $P = 2541$ (fixed grid size) or less depending on the support constraints induced by $\mathcal{\alpha}$.

The exact objective is:
\mathit{AC1}(a) = 2n \max_{k=0}^{2n-2} |(a \star a)[k]| / (\|a\|_1)^2 \]

Since $\|a\|_1$ scales quadratically with the components under fixed $P$, a normalized form is used:
\mathit{AC1\_norm}(a) = \max_{k} (a \star a)[k] / (\|a\|_1)^{2/}n

Under this normalization, minimizing $\mathit{AC1\_norm}$ corresponds to minimizing the AC1 bound, as the ratio is invariant to the overall scaling.
