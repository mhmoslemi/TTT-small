What sequences have a peak/average ratio close to 1? Those with nearly flat autocorrelation.

In the theory of spread spectrum, "Barker sequences" have the property that the aperiodic autocorrelation (excluding the peak) is bounded by 1. But those are ±1 sequences.

For non-negative sequences, one approach is to use a "random" sequence. If a[j] are i.i.d. with mean μ and variance σ², then:
- E[c[k]] ≈ n * μ² (for small k) or (n-|k|) * μ² (for the linear convolution)
- The peak c[0] = sum a[j]^2 ≈ n*(μ² + σ²)
- The center c[n-1] ≈ n * μ² (by CLT, it's close to the mean)

So for a random sequence, the edge values (c[0], c[2n-2]) are large (proportional to n*(μ²+σ²)), while the middle values are close to n*μ².

If σ² is small compared to μ² (i.e., the sequence is nearly constant), then c[0] ≈ n*μ² and the convolution is nearly flat. This is the uniform case.

If σ² is large, c[0] = n*(μ²+σ²) is large, which is bad.

So for a "random" approach, we want low variance, which pushes us back to uniform.
