Let me also think about a **spectral approach**:

The convolution `a*a` can be computed as `IFFT(FFT(a)^2)` (with appropriate padding). The peak of `a*a` is related to the "time-domain" peak of the energy spectrum `|A(f)|^2`.

If we parameterize `a` as `a = h + p` where `h = 1` (uniform) and `p` is a perturbation with `sum(p) = 0`, then:
`a*a = h*h + h*p + p*h + p*p`

The uniform part `h*h` has a triangular shape with peak n. The perturbation parts can help flatten this.

In the frequency domain:
`FFT(a*a) = |FFT(a)|^2 = |FFT(h) + FFT(p)|^2 = |FFT(h)|^2 + FFT(h)*FFT(p) + conj(FFT(h))*FFT(p) + |FFT(p)|^2`

`FFT(h)` for h = [1,1,...,1] (n ones, zero-padded to 2n-1) is the Dirichlet kernel, which has a peak at f=0 and sidelobes.

The perturbation `FFT(p)` can be designed to cancel the sidelobes of `|FFT(h)|^2`, making the total spectrum flatter, which reduces the time-domain peak.

This is analogous to window design in FFT! We're designing a "window" (the sequence a) that has a flat squared magnitude spectrum (in the baseband).
