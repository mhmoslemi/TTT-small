It seems like uniform is optimal for small n. For large n, maybe we can do slightly better by having a non-uniform distribution that reduces the peak at the center.

For a sequence a of length n, conv(n-1) = sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a[i]*a[n-1-i].

If a is symmetric (a[i] = a[n-1-i]), then conv(n-1) = sum a[i]².
By Cauchy-Schwarz: sum a[i]² ≥ (sum a[i])²/n.
So for symmetric sequences: score ≥ 2.

For non-symmetric sequences: conv(n-1) = sum a[i]*a[n-1-i].
By Cauchy-Schwarz: sum a[i]*a[n-1-i] ≤ sqrt(sum a[i]²) * sqrt(sum a[n-1-i]²) = sum a[i]².

But we want a LOWER bound on the max. The max could be at any lag, not just n-1.

For non-symmetric sequences, the peak might shift away from the center, and the value at the center might be lower. But the peak at some other lag might compensate.

Actually, I think the key insight is:

For ANY sequence a (not just symmetric), we have:
conv(a,a)[n-1] = sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a[i]*a[n-1-i]

And by the AM-GM inequality: a[i]*a[n-1-i] ≤ (a[i] + a[n-1-i])²/4

So: conv(n-1) ≤ sum (a[i] + a[n-1-i])²/4 = (1/4) * sum (a[i] + a[n-1-i])²

If the sequence is symmetric, this equals (1/4) * sum (2a[i])² = sum a[i]².

For non-symmetric, it could be different.

But we need a LOWER bound, not upper.
