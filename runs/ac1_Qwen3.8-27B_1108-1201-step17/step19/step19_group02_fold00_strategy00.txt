Approach and rationale
We aim to minimize the evaluation function even further by implementing a multi-resolution coordinate descent method. 
The idea is to first optimize a sequence at a particular length, then use wavelet transforms to analyze and transfer mass away from indices with high contribution to the convolution maximum.
At each level of resolution, we iteratively find the coefficient that contributes most to the current maximum convolution value and shift its mass to the least contributing symmetric index, while maintaining non-negativity and normalization.
This approach carefully handles boundaries of the convolution calculation to ensure correctness.

Mathematical specification
We consider a normalized non-negative sequence `a` with length `n` (sum(a) = 1) for optimization.
The objective is to minimize `J(a) = 2 * n * max_k`(linear_convolve(a)[k])` 
where linear_convolve(a)[k] = Σ_{i+j=k} a[i] * a[j] for k in [0, 2*n-2]
We represent the sequence as a weighting functional over the unit interval [0,1] with `n` points, ensuring that our updates maintain this structure.

Key components:
1. Wavelet-like mass Transfer: At each resolution, identify heavy contributions and counteract them symmetrically.
2. Renormalization in Higher Resolutions: Use interpolation to increase resolution, then optimize further.
3. Boundary Analysis: Handle indices near the edges, where the convolution endpoints have fewer terms.
