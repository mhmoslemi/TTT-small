The problem is that for a sequence that is NOT symmetric, the convolution has a "ramp" on one side (from k=0 to k≈n/2) where the values grow, and this ramp can have values larger than the "center" value.

To minimize the max, we need to flatten ALL parts of the convolution, not just the center.

This is a much harder problem. The goal is to make the entire convolution as flat as possible.

For a symmetric sequence (a[i] = a[n-1-i]), the convolution is symmetric and the peak is at the center (k=n-1). The constraint is b[n-1] = sum a[i]^2 >= (sum a)^2/n (by Cauchy-Schwarz), so objective >= 2.

For a non-symmetric sequence, the convolution is not symmetric, and the peak can be off-center. The challenge is to make the peak as low as possible.

The key insight: we want a sequence where the convolution is as flat as possible. This is the "minimum peak-to-average" criterion for the autocorrelation.

For complex sequences, this is well-studied (Barker sequences, ULB sequences). For real non-negative sequences, it's less studied but related.
