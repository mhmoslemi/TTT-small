- j in [max(0, k-n+1), min(k, n-1)] and k-j in [0, n-1].
- k-j in [0, n-1] means j in [k-n+1, k].
- j in [0, n-1] (always true for our purposes).

So the condition is: j in [max(0, k-n+1), min(n-1, k)] (which is S_k).

And a[k-j] is valid (in [0, n-1]) when k-j in [0, n-1], i.e., j in [k-n+1, k].

The intersection of [max(0, k-n+1), min(n-1, k)] and [k-n+1, k] is just [max(0, k-n+1), min(n-1, k)] (since [k-n+1, k] contains [max(0, k-n+1), min(n-1, k)]).

So: ∂conv[k]/∂a[j] = 2 * a[k-j] for j in [max(0, k-n+1), min(n-1, k)], and 0 otherwise.
