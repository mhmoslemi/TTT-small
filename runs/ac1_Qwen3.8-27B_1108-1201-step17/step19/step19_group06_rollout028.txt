For the U shape, a[i] ≈ 1+cos(πi/n) and a[n-i] ≈ 1+cos(π(n-i)/n) = 1+cos(π-πi/n) = 1-cos(πi/n).
So a[i]*a[n-i] ≈ (1+cos(πi/n))(1-cos(πi/n)) = 1-cos^2(πi/n) = sin^2(πi/n).
sum = sum sin^2(πi/n) ≈ n/2.
conv[n] ≈ (n/2)/n^2 = 1/(2n).

But what about k = n-1? conv[n-1] = sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a[i]*a[n-1-i].
a[i]*a[n-1-i] ≈ (1+cos(πi/n))(1+cos(π(n-1-i)/n))
= (1+cos(πi/n))(1+cos(π - π(i+1)/n))
= (1+cos(πi/n))(1-cos(π(i+1)/n))

For large n, cos(π(i+1)/n) ≈ cos(πi/n) - (π/n)sin(πi/n).
So this is approximately (1+cos(πi/n))(1-cos(πi/n) + (π/n)sin(πi/n))
≈ (1-cos^2(πi/n)) + (π/n)(1+cos(πi/n))sin(πi/n)
= sin^2(πi/n) + O(1/n)

So conv[n-1] ≈ (1/n^2) * n/2 = 1/(2n), same as conv[n].

What about k = n-2, etc.? By similar reasoning, they should all be approximately 1/(2n).

So the U shape gives an approximately flat convolution with value ≈ 1/(2n), leading to score ≈ 1!

But this is in the asymptotic limit. For finite n, there will be deviations near the edges (k=0, k=2n-2, etc.) where the convolution is small (only 1 or 2 terms contribute).
