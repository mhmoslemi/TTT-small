The issue is that for a linear ramp, the peak doesn't stay at the center - it shifts to the right (for an increasing ramp) and the value at the shifted peak is actually HIGHER than the center.

This makes sense: the product of two large numbers (from the right side of the ramp) is larger than the product of one large and one small number (at the center).

So a simple linear ramp is NOT the way to go. We need a different asymmetry.

Let me reconsider. The issue is:
- At the center (k=n-1): we're multiplying a[i] with a[n-1-i]. For a ramp, these are "anti-correlated" (large × small + small × large), which is good.
- At k > n-1: we're multiplying a[i] with a[k-i] where both i and k-i are on the "large" side of the ramp. So we get large × large, which is bad.

The solution: we need a sequence where the "large" values are concentrated on ONE side only, so that for k > n-1, at least one of the two factors is small.

This suggests a sequence like: large values in the first half, small values in the second half.
a = [1, 1, 1, ..., 1, 0, 0, 0, ..., 0]

But we saw this gives J = 2n/k > 2 for k < n.
