The issue is that for asymmetric sequences, the convolution peak can be much higher than for symmetric ones. The optimal sequence needs to balance between:
1. Making the convolution as flat as possible
2. Ensuring the peak at the "off-center" lags isn't too high

For a symmetric sequence (a_i = a_{n-1-i}), the convolution is symmetric and the peak is at k=n-1, where conv[n-1] = sum(a_i^2). By Cauchy-Schwarz, this is at least S^2/n, giving J ≥ 2.

For an asymmetric sequence, the convolution is not symmetric, and the peak can be elsewhere. But as I just showed, for a linear sequence, the off-center peak can be quite high.

So the optimal strategy is more subtle. We need a sequence that:
- Is somewhat asymmetric to reduce the "overlap" at k=n-1
- But not so asymmetric that the off-center peaks become too large

This is a well-studied problem in sequence design. The optimal shapes are related to "Golay sequences" or "CAZAC" (Constant Amplitude Zero Autocorrelation) sequences, but with the non-negativity constraint.

Given the target of 1.5027 and the previous best of 1.504, we're in a regime where the sequence is close to optimal. The key is to find the right shape.
