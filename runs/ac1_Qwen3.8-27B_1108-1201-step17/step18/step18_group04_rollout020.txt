The key is: for conv[k = n+m] (m > 0), the "truncation effect" (fewer terms on the left) vs. the "value effect" (larger values on the right).

For the sum sum_{i=m+1}^{n-1} (i+1)^α (n+1-m-i)^α:
- The number of terms is n-1-m (decreases as m increases)
- The peak value of each term: at i ≈ (n-m)/2, value ≈ ((n-m)/2)^(2α)
- The "effective width" of the peak: ~sqrt(n)/sqrt(α) for the Gaussian-like shape

For m << n:
- Number of terms: n-m ≈ n
- Peak value: ((n-m)/2)^(2α) ≈ n^(2α)/2^(2α) (slightly smaller)
- Width: roughly the same

So for m << n, conv[n+m] ≈ conv[n-1] * (1 - m/n)^(2α) * (1 - m/n) (the last factor from fewer terms)
≈ conv[n-1] * (1 - m/n)^(2α+1)

This is decreasing in m! So for m > 0 and m << n, the convolution is decreasing.

But for m close to n (i.e., k close to 2n), we're in a different regime where very few terms contribute, and each term is large (because it involves f[n-1] which is the largest element).
