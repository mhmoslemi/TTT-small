The key point is: for a two-level sequence with the first half at $c_1$ and the second half at $c_2$ (with $c_1 \neq c_2$), the convolution peak is NOT at the center. The center value is $n c_1 c_2$ (approximately), while values near the edges are $O(m c_1^2)$ or $O(m c_2^2)$.

If $c_1$ is large and $c_2$ is small:
- Center: $\approx n c_1 c_2$ (moderate)
- Left edge: $\approx m c_1^2 = (n/2) c_1^2$ (large if $c_1$ is large)

So the peak would be at the left edge, not the center. This means the max is $(n/2) c_1^2$, and:
$J = 2n \cdot (n/2) c_1^2 / (n(c_1+c_2)/2)^2 = 2n \cdot (n/2) c_1^2 / (n^2(c_1+c_2)^2/4) = 4 c_1^2 / (c_1+c_2)^2$

For $c_2 = 0$: $J = 4c_1^2/c_1^2 = 4$. (Worse than 2!)
For $c_2 = c_1/3$: $J = 4/(1+1/3)^2 = 4/(16/9) = 9/4 = 2.25$.
For $c_2 = c_1$: $J = 4/4 = 1$. But this is the uniform case where the peak is actually at the center with value $n$, giving $J = 2$.
