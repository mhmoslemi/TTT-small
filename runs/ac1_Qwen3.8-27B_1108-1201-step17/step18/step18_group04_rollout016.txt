So the strategy is: make the sequence "anti-symmetric" in the sense that large values at one end pair with small values at the other end in the center convolution. This reduces the peak.

More generally, for the center term:
c[n-1] = sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a[i] * a[n-1-i]

We want this to be small. By the rearrangement inequality, to minimize sum a[i]*a[n-1-i], we should arrange a so that large values are paired with small values. The minimum is achieved when one is increasing and the other is decreasing (i.e., a is either increasing or decreasing).

But wait, c[n-1] is determined by the sequence, not by how we arrange it. The sequence IS the arrangement. So we want a[i] to be large when a[n-1-i] is small, i.e., a should be monotone (either increasing or decreasing).

But we also need to worry about other conv values, not just the center. For a monotone sequence, the other peaks might be at the edges.

The insight is: we want to REDUCE the center peak (c[n-1]) while not increasing other peaks too much. This means we want the sequence to have a "valley" in the middle (when viewed from the center outward) or more precisely, we want a[i] to be small when i is near the center and a[n-1-i] is also near the center.

Wait, c[n-1] = sum a[i]*a[n-1-i]. The middle terms (i near n/2) contribute a[i]*a[n-1-i] where both i and n-1-i are near n/2. So if the middle of the sequence is small, the center of the convolution is reduced.

But the edge values of the convolution (c[0] = a[0]^2, c[2n-2] = a[n-1]^2) are determined by the endpoints. And the near-edge values (c[1] = 2*a[0]*a[1], etc.) depend on adjacent elements.

So the optimal structure seems to be: high values near the ends, low values in the middle. Like a "U" shape or "dumbbell" shape.

Let me think about this differently. The problem is essentially:
- We have n non-negative numbers summing to 1
- We want to minimize the maximum of the convolution
- The convolution has 2n-1 values

For large n, what's the theoretical minimum?

Consider the DFT perspective. Let A(f) = sum_i a[i] * e^{-2πifi/n} (DFT of a). Then:
- The convolution a*a has DFT A(f)^2
- max_k (a*a)[k] is related to the maximum of the inverse DFT of A(f)^2
- sum(a) = A(0)

Actually, the peak of the convolution is bounded below by the DC component of the convolution divided by the length:
max_k (a*a)[k] >= (1/(2n-1)) * sum_k (a*a)[k] = (1/(2n-1)) * (sum a)^2 = 1/(2n-1)

So J >= 2n/(2n-1) ≈ 1 for large n. But this bound is very loose.

A tighter bound: by Parseval, sum_k (a*a)[k]^2 = (1/(2n-1)) * sum_f |A(f)|^4 (with appropriate normalization). And (a*a)[n-1] is the "peak" that we're trying to minimize.

Actually, I think the right framework is:
- We want to minimize max_k (a*a)[k] subject to sum(a) = 1, a >= 0
- This is related to the "sidelobe level" of the autocorrelation
- For non-negative sequences, it's harder than for complex sequences (where you can use Barker codes, Zadoff-Chu, etc.)
