**Another idea: "Away-step" Frank-Wolfe**

The standard Frank-Wolfe can be slow to converge because it can't decrease the value of coordinates that are already at their lower bound (0). The "away-step" FW allows moving away from vertices:
- Compute the standard FW direction: v = argmin_{x in C} <g, x>
- Compute the away direction: u = argmax_{x in S} <g, x> where S is the set of vertices in the current decomposition
- Take a step in the direction v - u (if it reduces the objective)

For the simplex, this means:
- v = e_j where j = argmin_i g[i] (the simplex vertex with the smallest gradient)
- u = e_i where i = argmax_{i in support} g[i] (move away from the support element with the largest gradient)

The away-step FW is known to converge linearly (vs. sublinear for standard FW).

But in our case, we're not doing standard FW on the objective. We're doing LP-FW where the "vertex" is the LP solution, not a basis vector. So the away-step doesn't directly apply.

However, we can still try: after computing the LP direction g_dir, also consider moving away from the current a in the direction of the "worst" coordinates (those contributing most to the peak autocorrelation).
