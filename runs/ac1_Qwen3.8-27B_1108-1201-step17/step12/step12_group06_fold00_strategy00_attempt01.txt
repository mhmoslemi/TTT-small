### Approach and Rationale  
The key idea is to consider sequences as functions with specific frequency responses. The convolution of a signal with itself resembles an autocorrelation, and we wish to minimize the maximum peak of the autocorrelation. This is equivalent to minimizing the maximum value in the product of two consecutive batches of the signal, which happens in sequences that avoid consecutive high values and have a distribution that "cancels" itself at larger lags.  

For example, a sequence constructed as `0.5` raised cosines placed periodically might yield an autocorrelation that never grows too large. The total length is selectable, and we can perturb the sequence to trade-off between avoiding high autocorrelation and maintaining a high peak in the convolution to enforce realism.  

### Mathematical Specification  
We model the sequence as:  
```math  
a_i = f(x_i)  
```  
where `x_i` are positions equally spaced from `0` to `1`, and `f` is a periodic function chosen from a library. The convolution is computed via FFT for efficiency, and the goal is to minimize the `max_b` after normalization.  

The sequence generation uses a `FourierLUT` (Look-Up-Table) to interpolate between precomputed raised cosine components. This allows flexibly constructing sequences of any length.
