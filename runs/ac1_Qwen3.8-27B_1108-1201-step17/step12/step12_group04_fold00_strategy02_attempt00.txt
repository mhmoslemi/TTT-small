# Coarse-to-Fine Hierarchical Optimization for Peak-Reducing Sequences

## Approach and Rationale

We need to find a sequence of non-negative heights that minimizes the evaluation function: 
2n × max(convolution) / (sum(a)²)

This is a high-dimensional optimization problem with several important characteristics:
- The evaluation depends on the central tendency and peakedness
- The solution space requires non-negativity and zero/max bounds
- The metric is the ratio of scale information to peakedness

I propose a hierarchical, multilevel optimization strategy that progressively refines search candidates from coarse to fine resolution. This approach combines:

1. A convolution-aware initialization
2. Strategic length selection
3. Fourier-series guided optimization
4. Progressive refinement in temperature space
5. Focused optimization cycles

The hierarchical approach allows us to efficiently escape local minima while converging toward the global optimum with increasingly precise checks.

## Mathematical Specification

We'll optimize the sequence on three scales:
- Low resolution: 120 samples (minimal length, fastest computation)
- Medium resolution: 150 samples (adaptive length)
- High resolution: Initial parent length (typically 1600 samples)

For each level:
**Input**: Sequence of length n, window size w, temperature t
**Output**: Optimized sequence with normalized sum

The objective function at level i is:
score_i = (2n × max_k autocorrelation(a)[k]) / (sum_i(a)²)

We use a temperature transformation that smooths the optimization landscape:
a_normalized = a_clip / (sum(a_clip) + ε)   [safeguard against zero sum]

And the convolution-smoothing constraints:
∀k: (a_normalized * a_normalized)[k] ≤ M(t)

M(t) is progressively tightened across optimization stages.
