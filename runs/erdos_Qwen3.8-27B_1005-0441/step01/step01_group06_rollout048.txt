Even simpler: use a penalty. In the 1D problem, add penalty * (sum(h) - n/2 + t - old)^2. But this makes the 1D problem no longer just a max of (quasi-)linear functions.

Let me just use the projection approach. It's simple and works in practice.

Actually, here's another idea: instead of projecting after each sweep, I can fix one coordinate (say h[0]) and only optimize the other n-1 coordinates freely. Then h[0] = n/2 - sum(h[1:]). This automatically satisfies the sum constraint. The bounds on h[0] impose a constraint on sum(h[1:]), but I can handle that by checking at the end.

This is cleaner! Let me do this:
- Fix h[0] = n/2 - sum(h[1:])
- Optimize h[1], h[2], ..., h[n-1] via coordinate descent (each in [0,1])
- After convergence, check if h[0] ∈ [0,1]. If not, project.

            m2 = (idx2 >= 0) & (idx2 < n)
