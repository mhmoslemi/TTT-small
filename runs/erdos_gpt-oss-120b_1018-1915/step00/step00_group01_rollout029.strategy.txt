### Approach and Rationale  
The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks to find a step function \( h(x): [0, 2] \to [0, 1] \) with \( \int_0^2 h(x) \, dx = 1 \) that minimizes the maximum overlap integral \( C_5 = \max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1 - h(x+k)) \, dx \). The initial solution at \( n = 47 \) has a bound of \( C \approx 0.38085 \). This strategy uses a combination of gradient-based optimization and symmetry exploitation to explore higher resolutions (up to \( n = 500 \)) and find improvements. The core idea is:  
1. **Gradient-Based Refinement**: Use constraints to dynamically adjust variables while respecting the integral and bounds.  
2. **Symmetry Exploitation**: Assume the optimal solution is symmetric and periodic, reducing the search space.  
3. **Resolution Progression**: Test increasing resolutions (47, 94, 188, 375, 500) and reuse parent solutions to aid convergence.  
Key limitations:  
- The objective is non-convex, and symmetries are assumed rather than verified.  
- Gradient computations may be unstable, so we include fallback mechanisms.  

### Mathematical Specification

\[
\min_{\mathbf{h} \in [0, 1]^{n_{\text{points}}}, \sum_i h_i \cdot dx = 1 \quad \max_k \left| \int_0^2 h(x)(1 - h(x+k)) \, dx \right|
\]
