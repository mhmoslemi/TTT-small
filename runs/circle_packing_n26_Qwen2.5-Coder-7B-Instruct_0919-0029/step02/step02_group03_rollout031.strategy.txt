### 2.  Detailed Algorithm Flow

1. **Initialisation**  
   - Generate a *reasonable* guess for the centre positions: a hexagonal lattice with 5 rows alternating 5/4 columns (23 circles) plus three random points inside the square (uniform on `[0,1]^2`).  
   - Compute the associated radii via the inner LP to get an initial `sum_radii`.

2. **Outer Optimisation Loop**  
   The loop iterates until convergence or a maximum number of restarts is reached.

   a. **Call `scipy.optimize.minimize`**  
      - **Input**:  
        * `x0 = centres.flatten()` (52‑element vector).  
        * **Bounds**: `[0,1]` for every coordinate.  
        * **Function**: `obj_fun` that reshapes the vector, calls `compute_sum_radii`, negates the result.  
        * **Gradient**: `grad_fun` that calls `compute_sum_radii` on perturbed vectors to build the numerical gradient.  
      - **Method**: `'L-BFGS-B'` with `maxcor=10`, `ftol=1e-8`, `gtol=1e-8`.  
      - **Tolerance for stopping**: relative change in objective < `1e-7` or max 200 gradient evaluations.

   b. **Accept the result**  
      - If the objective improved by at least `1e-6` compared with the best seen, update best centres and radii (`compute_sum_radii` on the returned centres).  
      - If the objective stagnates, perform a *restart*:
        * Add a Gaussian jitter with std `1e-3` to every coordinate.
        * Clip to `[0,1]`.
        * Re‑run the optimiser from this perturbed start.

3. **Post‑processing**  
   - After the outer loop, recompute the radii from the best centre set using the inner LP (makes sure we tie the radii exactly to the best positions).  
   - Clip any very small negative radii to zero.  
   - Ensure all boundaries are respected (`np.clip` to `[0,1]` for coordinates, `max(0,r)` for radii).

4. **Return**  
   Output `(centres, radii, sum_radii)` where
   - `centres` has shape `(26, 2)`;
   - `radii` has shape `(26,)`;

### 5.  Why This Is Materially Different

1. **Directed Optimisation** – Unlike the evolutionary or purely stochastic annealing strategies, the L‑BFGS‑B optimiser uses a *finite‑difference gradient* to follow the steepest descent, giving much more efficient navigation of the 52‑dimensional search space.

2. **Exact Inner Radius Computation** – Every call to the objective or gradient resolves the *exact* optimal radii for the current centre configuration via a convex LP, ensuring that the outer optimiser receives a high‑fidelity objective signal.

3. **Controlled Perturbations & Restart Strategy** – The algorithm deliberately perturbs the solution between restarts rather than relying on random mutations of centres. This systematic perturbation helps escape shallow local traps while preserving good structure.

4. **Computational Simplicity** – Only standard SciPy objects (`linprog`, `minimize`) are used; no custom meta‑optimisers or implicit penalty terms are required.
