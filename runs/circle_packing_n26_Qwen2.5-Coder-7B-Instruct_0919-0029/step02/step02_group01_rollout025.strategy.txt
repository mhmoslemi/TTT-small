## 1.  Mathematical model

Let  

\[
p=\bigl[x_1,\dots,x_{26},\ y_1,\dots,y_{26},\ r_1,\dots,r_{26}\bigr]\in\mathbb{R}^{78}
\]

be the optimisation vector.

### Objective  
Maximise total radius:

\[
\max_{p}\quad S(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{26} r_i
\]

We minimise \(-S(p)\) in the solver.

### Constraints  

1. **Bounds on positions**  
   \[
   0 \le x_i \le 1,\qquad
   0 \le y_i \le 1
   \]

2. **Radius bounds** (linear, easy to give as `LinearConstraint`)  

   \[
   r_i \le x_i,\;\; r_i \le y_i,\;\; r_i \le 1-x_i,\;\; r_i \le 1-y_i
   \]

   This guarantees that circle \(i\) stays inside the unit square.

3. **Pairwise non‑overlap** (non‑linear; 325 constraints)

   For all \(\{i,j\}\) with \(i<j\):

   \[
   g_{ij}(p)=
   \sqrt{(x_i-x_j)^2+(y_i-y_j)^2}\;-\;(r_i+r_j)\;\ge 0
   \]

   The left‑hand side is the Euclidean distance between centres minus the sum of radii; it must be non‑negative.  
   The constraints are *convex* in the sense that each \(g_{ij}\) is a concave function of the decision variables, but together they form a non‑convex feasible set; nevertheless, `trust‑constr` can handle them as *nonlinear inequality* constraints.  

   **Gradient** of \(g_{ij}\):
   \[
   \frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial x_i}= \frac{x_i-x_j}{\sqrt{(x_i-x_j)^2+(y_i-y_j)^2}},
   \quad
   \frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial y_i}= \frac{y_i-y_j}{\sqrt{(x_i-x_j)^2+(y_i-y_j)^2}},
   \]
   \[
   \frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial r_i}= -1,\qquad
   \frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial r_j}= -1,
   \]
   all other partials zero.  
   These expressions can be vectorised for efficient evaluation.

### Feasible region
The domain is a compact hyper‑rectangle defined by the bounds; the nonlinear constraints carve out a subset.  
Because all equality/inequality constraints are continuous, the feasible set is closed, and a maximiser exists.

## 2.  Algorithmic approach

1. **Initial configuration** – a *fast feasible seed*

     (x,y)=(0.1+0.2 i,\,0.1+0.2 j),\quad i,j=0,\dots,4

     \[
     f(p)= -\sum_i r_i \;+\; \lambda \sum_{\text{violations}} \bigl(\text{negative parts of }g_{ij}\bigr)^2

   - Store the best individual \(p_{\text{DE}}\).

3. **Local refinement with `trust-constr`**  
   - Initialise the solver with \(p_{\text{DE}}\).
   - Supply:
     * Linear constraints for the radius bounds (via `LinearConstraint`).
     * Nonlinear constraints \(g_{ij}(p)\ge 0\) for every pair (via `NonlinearConstraint`).
     * Bounds on variables directly through `Bounds`.

| **Analytic Jacobians** | Reduce iteration count by providing exact gradients | Saves computation time compared to finite differences |

## 4.  Anticipated failure modes & remedies

| Failure | Cause | Mitigation |
|---------|-------|------------|
| **Solver diverges / stagnates** | Ill‑conditioned constraints or poor initial guess | Switch to the penalty‑DE stage or restart with a new random seed; adjust λ |
| **Residual constraint violation** | Tolerance too high in `trust-constr` | Tighten `tol` to 1e‑8, use a feasible starting point, or increase the penalty |
| **Numerical overflow in penalty** | λ too high **and** many violations | Monitor the penalty term; if overflow occurs, reduce λ or scale the variables |
| **Long runtime** | Too many generations in DE or too many pairwise constraints fed to solver | Reduce population size, limit generations, or use the vectorised distance matrix to compute gradients efficiently |
| **Infeasible initial guess** | Random perturbations that put a circle outside the square | Clip immediately after perturbation; enforce bounds in DE solver |

Each of these failure modes has a straightforward counter‑measure that can be encoded automatically (e.g., a retry count with exponential back‑off).
