**Goal**  
Pack 26 circles inside the unit square so that the sum of their radii is as large as possible (target ≥ 2.637).  
The proposed plan uses a *global exploration* of circle centre positions combined with an *exact radii maximisation* for each configuration.  This is a qualitatively different approach from the fixed‑grid, linear‑programming method that was used previously.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1. Core mathematical formulation
For a given set of centres \(\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^{26}\) the radii that maximise the sum under the non‑overlap and boundary constraints are obtained by solving the following linear programme:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{maximize}   & \displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{26} r_i                                   \\
\text{subject to} & r_i \;\le\; x_i ,\quad r_i \;\le\; 1-x_i, \\
                   & r_i \;\le\; y_i ,\quad r_i \;\le\; 1-y_i , \quad i=1,\dots,26 ,\\
                   & r_i + r_j \;\le\; \sqrt{(x_i-x_j)^2+(y_i-y_j)^2}\quad
                     \forall\,1\le i<j\le26 ,\\
                   & r_i \;\ge\; 0 .
\end{aligned}
\]

All constraints are linear in the radii once the centres are fixed; note that the square‑root appears only in the right‑hand side so the LP‑subproblem is standard.  
The objective value of this LP is exactly the maximum possible sum of radii for the specified arrangement of centres.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2. Exploration of centre positions – simulated annealing

1. **Initial population**  
   * Start from a coarse, feasible lattice (e.g. the 5 × 5 grid of radius 0.10 plus the central circle of radius ≈ 0.18284) – call this **seed S**.  
   * Generate a *set* of \(K\) random perturbations of **S** by adding to each centre a small vector in \([-p,\,p]^2\) (with \(p=0.02\)), then clipping to \([0,1]^2\).  
   * For each perturbed layout compute the LP radii. Keep the configuration with the highest objective value; denote it **current best**.

2. **Annealing loop** (time‑budget controlled, e.g. 2000 iterations)  
   * At each step choose **one** circle uniformly at random.  
   * Propose to move it to a new position \((x_i',y_i') = (x_i, y_i) + \delta\), where \(\delta\) is a 2‑D random vector drawn from a zero‑mean Gaussian with standard deviation \(\sigma_t\).  
   * Clamp to \([0,1]^2\).  
   * Call the LP routine to obtain new radii and the corresponding objective value \(E_{\text{new}}\).  
   * **Acceptance test**  
     * If \(E_{\text{new}} > E_{\text{curr}}\), accept the move (update current best).  
     * Otherwise accept with probability \(\exp[(E_{\text{new}}-E_{\text{curr}})/T_t]\), where \(T_t\) is the annealing temperature at iteration \(t\).  
   * Decrease the standard deviation \(\sigma_t\) and the temperature \(T_t\) according to a schedule, e.g. \(\sigma_{t+1} = \sigma_t \times 0.99\), \(T_{t+1} = T_t \times 0.99\).  
   * Record the best configuration encountered so far.

3. **Multiple restarts**  
   * After finishing one annealing run, repeat the whole process with different random seeds \( (K\approx 5\text{–}10)\).  
   * Take the best record from all runs as the **proposed final configuration**.

### 3. Local deterministic refinement

The stochastic phase often leaves the configuration close to a local optimum.  A short deterministic improvement step can push the objective further:

1. **Coordinate ascent over centres**  
   * For each circle \(i\) in turn:  
     * Perform a 2‑D hill‑climbing search around its current coordinate using a grid of \(m \times m\) small perturbations (e.g. \(m=5\), step size \(\epsilon = 1e-4\)).  
     * For each candidate position evaluate the LP radii and keep the one giving the highest objective.  
   * Repeat the cycle until no improvement exceeds a tiny threshold (e.g. \(1e-6\)).

2. **Optional radius‑only re‑optimisation**  
   * After the last coordinate ascent step, re‑run the LP with the final centres to correct any small numerical drift and to guarantee that every circle has the maximal allowable radius for those points.
