**New idea** – Replace the continuous‑gradient / penalty approach of earlier strategies with a **mixed‑integer linear programming (MILP) formulation that fixes the centre positions on a coarse discrete grid**.  
The problem then becomes a classic 0‑1 optimisation problem for the choice of 26 grid‑points, coupled with continuous radii variables, all subject to linear constraints.  
Because the constraints are linear and the objective is a linear sum of radii, state‑of‑the‑art MILP solvers (CBC via `pulp`) can solve the problem in a fraction of a second when the grid is modest.

### 3.  Linear constraints

1. **Exactly 26 circles**

   \sum_{k=0}^{P-1} y_k = 26
   \]

2. **No‑overlap** – for every unordered pair `(k,l)` with `k<l`  

   Let `d_kl` be the Euclidean distance between the two grid points.  
   The constraint that the two circles are disjoint is
   \[
   r_k + r_l \;\le\; d_{kl}
   \]
   If either rectangle is not selected, the inequality cannot be violated regardless of the radii.  
   A standard “big‑M” linearisation is therefore used:
   \[
   r_k + r_l \;\le\; d_{kl} \;+\; M (\,1-y_k\,) \;+\; M (\,1-y_l\,)
   \]
   where `M = 0.5` (larger than any feasible radius).
