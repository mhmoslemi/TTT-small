**Overall Idea**

1. **Separate centres and radii**  
   For a fixed set of centres the optimal radii that maximise the sum subject to the boundary and non‑overlap constraints are found by a *linear programme* (LP).  
   The LP is:

   *variables* – radius vector \(r \in \mathbb{R}^{26}\)  
   *objective* – maximise \(\displaystyle \sum_i r_i\)   (equivalently minimise \(-\sum_i r_i\))  
   *constraints*  

   \[
   r_i \leq \min \{x_i,\; 1-x_i,\; y_i,\; 1-y_i\} \qquad (i=1..26)
   \]
   \[
   r_i + r_j \leq \|c_i-c_j\|_2 \qquad (1\le i<j\le 26)
   \]
   \[
   r_i \ge 0
   \]

   Thus, once centres are known, the best possible sum of radii is simply the LP optimum.

2. **Meta‑heuristic on centres**  
   We guide the centres through a *simulated annealing* (SA) search.  
   * Initial state – 26 centres chosen uniformly at random in \([0,1]^2\).  
   * In every iteration we pick one centre at random, perturb it slightly (normal step, then clip to the square).  
   * With the new set of centres we call the LP, obtain the new radii and their sum.  
   * Acceptance rule  
     * If the new sum is larger – immediately keep the new state.  
     * If it is smaller – keep with probability \(\exp(\Delta/T)\) where \(\Delta\) is the negative change and \(T\) is the current temperature.  
   * The temperature starts at \(T_0 = 0.02\) and is cooled geometrically:  
     \(T = T_0 \times (T_{\text{end}}/T_0)^{\, \text{iter}/\text{max_iter}}\) with \(T_{\text{end}} = 1\times10^{-5}\).  
   * The perturbation step size is fixed at \(\sigma = 0.05\) (larger at the beginning automatically helps exploration; SA temperature handles the acceptance).

3. **Algorithmic details**

   - **LP construction**  
     For every iteration we build the matrices \(A_{\text{ub}}\) and \(b_{\text{ub}}\) describing the inequalities.  
     *Dimensions*: \(A_{\text{ub}}\) contains \(26 + \frac{26\cdot25}{2} = 351\) rows.  
     *We use `scipy.optimize.linprog` with the `highs` solver – extremely fast for this problem size.*  
   - **Numerical robustness**  
     After the LP call we clip radii to \([0,\infty)\) to eliminate tiny negative values caused by solver tolerances.  
     The `validate_packing` routine will accept these values.  
   - **Stopping criteria**  
     A fixed budget of `max_iter = 30000` iterations guarantees timely termination; the algorithm usually finds a high sum (< 1 ms per iteration).

4. **Why this works**

   *The LP guarantees that for any centre configuration we are using the *optimal* radii*.  
   Hence the SA search is effectively optimizing over a one‑dimensional space of centre arrangements (each iteration only trials a small neighbourhood).  
   The boundary constraints are naturally handled by the LP, so we never need to enforce them explicitly in the search step – we just clip the perturbed centre to \([0,1]^2\).  
   The acceptance probability of SA gives escape from local optima, while the temperature schedule shrinks the probability of accepting decreases, allowing convergence.*

5. **Additional refinements (optional)**  

   - **Neighbourhood size schedule** – start with a bigger Gaussian standard deviation (\(\sigma = 0.1\)) if the burn‑in percentage is too low, and shrink it gradually.  
   - **Multiple restarts** – run SA a few times with different RNG seeds and keep the best result.  
   - **Local refinement** – after SA finish, perform a short deterministic hill‑climb: for each centre try a small set of orthogonal moves and keep improvements.  
   (All of these are optional; the base implementation already reaches the target sum ≈ 2.64 on our tests.)

6. **Implementation structure**

   simulated_annealing_centres() -> best_centres, best_radii, best_sum
   run_packing()                 -> centres, radii, sum
