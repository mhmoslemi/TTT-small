**High‑level plan**

1. **Two‑stage optimisation**  
   *Stage 1 – Global search over centres & radii* : Use a *differential evolution* (DE) algorithm to explore the full 52‑dimensional parameter space  
   \((x_1,y_1,\dots,x_{26},y_{26}, r_1,\dots,r_{26})\).  
   The objective is the *negative* sum of radii with a huge penalty that forces feasibility: no overlap, no outside‑square positions, and no negative radii.

2. **Stage 2 – Exact radius refinement**  
   For the best centre configuration produced by DE, solve a small linear programme (LP) that maximises the radii under the true linear constraints.  
   This guarantees the returned packing is optimal for those centres and is fully valid.

The two stages are conceptually distinct from “LP + SA on centres” because the global optimisation handles *both* geometry and sizing simultaneously and the penalty formulation does not rely on linearising pairwise constraints—allowing the solver to respect the non‑linear geometry during exploration. Only after a promising set of centres is found do we invoke the exact LP.

### Stage 1 – Differential Evolution over 52 variables

#### 1.1 Variable bounds  

- Positions: \(x_i, y_i \in [0, 1]\).  
- Radii: \(r_i \in [0, 0.25]\).  
  (0.25 is safely larger than any feasible radius in a unit square with 26 mutual neighbours but keeps the search space compact and avoids large penalties.)

#### 1.2 Penalty‑based objective  

For a candidate vector \(v=(x,y,r)\) the DE objective is

\[
F(v) = -\sum_{i=1}^{26} r_i \;+\; \lambda \, P(v)
\]

where \(\lambda = 10\,000\,000\) and the penalty \(P(v)\) aggregates every type of geometric violation:

| Violation | Penalty term |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Boundary**: circle extends outside the square | \(\displaystyle \sum_i \Bigl[\max\bigl(0,-x_i\bigr)^2 + \max\bigl(0,x_i-1\bigr)^2 + \max\bigl(0,-y_i\bigr)^2 + \max\bigl(0,y_i-1\bigr)^2\Bigr]\) |
| **Negative radius** | \(\displaystyle \sum_i \max\bigl(0,-r_i\bigr)^2\) |
| **Overlap**: pair \(i<j\) is too close | \(\displaystyle \sum_{i<j} \max\bigl(0,\, r_i+r_j - d_{ij}\bigr)^2\) where \(d_{ij} = \sqrt{(x_i-x_j)^2+(y_i-y_j)^2}\) |

Because the penalty terms are squared, any non‑zero violation incurs a large cost that dominates the objective. Small, residual numerical errors after refinement are harmless.

#### 1.3 DE configuration  

- **Population size**: 50 (balances diversity against evaluation cost).  
- **Strategy**: `'intermediate'` – a good trade‑off between exploration and exploitation.  
- **Cross‑over probability**: 0.7 (standard).  
- **Mutation factor**: 0.8 (common in DE).  
- **Maximum generations**: 2000 → ≈ 100 000 objective evaluations total (each evaluation loops over 325 pairwise distances, trivial for a CPU core).  
- **Control**: run DE twice with independent seeds (e.g., 42 and 51) and keep the configuration yielding the largest LP‑optimised sum.

#### 1.4 Numerical aspects  

- All vector operations use NumPy’s ufuncs; the pairwise distance loop can be written with broadcasting or pre‑computed midpoint arrays.  
- The penalty weight \(\lambda=10^7\) ensures that even a single metre of overlap gives a penalty of \(10^{14}\), vastly exceeding the possible objective magnitude (≈ 6.5).  
- No explicit clipping is required beyond the DE bounds; the penalty alone will steer the population back inside the feasible set.

#### 1.5 Failure modes & mitigations  

- *“DE converges to a configuration with very slight violations”* – the final LP step will automatically shrink overlapping radii to zero, producing a perfectly feasible packing.  
- *“Degenerate pairs of centres with identical coordinates”* – DE’s stochasticity usually avoids exact duplicates; if it occurs, the overlap penalty will be astronomically high and the candidate will be discarded.  
- *“Numerical overflow in penalty calculation”* – with the chosen bounds the maximum penalty per pair is \((0.25+0.25)^2 = 0.25\), so even a 325‑term sum cannot exceed \(81\); the multiplier 10⁷ keeps the total in the 10⁸–10¹⁰ range, well below the double precision overflow threshold.

### Stage 2 – Precise radius optimisation via Linear Programming

#### 2.1 LP formulation (for fixed centres)

- **Variables**: \(r_i,\ i=1..26\).  
- **Objective**: maximise \(\sum_i r_i\) (equivalently minimise \(-\sum_i r_i\)).  
- **Constraints**  
  1. Boundary limits: \(r_i \le \min\{x_i,\;1-x_i,\;y_i,\;1-y_i\}\).  
  2. Pairwise non‑overlap: \(r_i + r_j \le d_{ij}\) for all \(i<j\).  
  3. Non‑negativity: \(r_i \ge 0\).

These are all linear constraints; the LP is non‑degenerate and solvable in milliseconds.

#### 2.2 Solver choice  

- Use `scipy.optimize.linprog` with `method='highs'` (the default modern solver).  
- Supply bounds \([0,\,\infty)\) automatically; no additional bounds required because the boundary constraints handle the upper limits.  
- After the solver, clip any tiny negative components to zero (due to numerical tolerance).

#### 2.3 Why the LP improves the solution  

The DE phase may have produced centres that are suboptimal for radius growth (e.g., too close to a boundary or to another circle). The LP adjusts all radii simultaneously to their theoretical maximum given these centres. This can increase the total radius by a few hundredths, a noticeable gain that the naïve DE objective might miss due to its penalty‑based approximation.

#### 2.4 Failure modes

- *"LP yields infeasible solution"* – impossible, since setting all radii to zero always satisfies the constraints.  
- *"Solver returns a very small negative radius due to tolerance"* – we clip to zero, preserving validity.  
- *"Exceeded time limit"* – the LP is tiny; even with a 10 ms per call, the entire routine completes in less than a second.

### Full algorithmic workflow

1. **Run DE twice**  
   For each seed:  
   - Initialise a random population within bounds.  
   - Run DE for 2000 generations with the penalty‑based objective.  
   - Record the best candidate vector \(v_{\text{best}}\) (centres + radii).  

2. **Select most promising centre layout**  
   For each of the two DE results, solve the LP for the recorded centres; keep the configuration that returns the larger LP sum.  

3. **Return final packing**  
   - **centres** = the chosen centre matrix (26×2).  
   - **radii** = the LP output radius vector.  
   - **sum_radii** = sum(radii).  

All steps are fully deterministic apart from the two random seeds, ensuring repeatability on the judge platform.
