### 1.  Core Idea

Treat the 26 circles as *slow‑growing pollutants* inside a rigid unit square:  
at any instant each circle has a concrete centre \(c_i=(x_i,y_i)\) and a radius
\(r_i\).  Initially all radii are zero.  In each iteration

1. **Simultaneous growth** – every circle expands a tiny amount, all at the
   same *growth speed* \(g\).
2. **Collision reaction** – if an expansion would violate a boundary or
   create an overlap, the offending circle is immediately *shaken* out of
   collision by moving its centre in the direction that locally maximises
   the distance to the nearest contact (wall or neighbour).  
   After the move the radius is recomputed as the *maximum allowed* for the
   new configuration.
3. **Synchronization** – after every small move the whole system is checked
   for any remaining inactive constraints; if none exist the circles have
   reached a *local equilibrium*, i.e. a configuration where no radius can
   be increased without violating a constraint.

Because the growth step is *proportional* to the same \(g\) for all circles,
the algorithm automatically respects the *sum‑of‑radii* objective: the total
increase in the objective at each iteration is simply \(26\cdot g\).  Thus,
maximising the final sum of radii is equivalent to letting the system evolve
until no circle can grow further.
