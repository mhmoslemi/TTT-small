### Approach and Rationale

I'll develop a constructive heuristic based on periodic patterns to find a better upper bound for the Erdős minimum overlap constant. The core idea is to leverage the observation that optimal solutions often exhibit repetitive structure, and we can systematically parameterize families of periodic patterns to minimize the maximum overlap. This approach balances exploration of promising candidate structures while maintaining conformance to the integral constraint.

The method provides an improvement over random search by:
1. Targeting natural periodic structures that minimize high overlap
2. Using explicit parameterization rather than random sampling
3. Guaranteeing exact satisfaction of the integral constraint

This contrasts with the previous simulated annealing approach, which explores the solution space stochastically but risks missing structural patterns. The pattern-based approach provides more direct guidance toward relevant configurations and reduces the search space dimensionality effectively.

### Mathematical Specification

We discretize the problem using `n_points` samples on the interval [0,2]. The integral constraint becomes `sum(h) = n_points/2`. Our solution h will be periodic with a chosen period `p` (divisor of `n_points`). The solution vector of length `n_points` is determined by repeating the periodic pattern `n_points` times and discarding the initial `n_points % p` elements.

#### Discretization:
- Domain: [0,2] partitioned into `n_points` intervals
- Values: h ∈ [0,1]
- Integral: ∫h(x)dx = 1 ≈ `sum(h) * dx = sum(h) * 2/n_points = 1` ⇒ `sum(h) = n_points/2`

#### Representation:
h will be defined by its values at indices `0 ≤ i < p`, which are repeated throughout the domain.

#### Objective:
Minimize C₅ = `np.max(np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")) * (2/n_points)`

#### Structure:
The solution is piecewise constant, with changes only at integer multiples of the period length `Δx = p / (n_points/p)` (where `n_points/p` is the number of repetitions). This structure ensures minimal high overlap configurations.
