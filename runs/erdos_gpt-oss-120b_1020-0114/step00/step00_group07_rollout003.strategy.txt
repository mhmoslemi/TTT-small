1. Approach and rationale:
We tackle the Erdős minimum overlap problem by using a combination of spectral analysis and constrained optimization. The core idea is to represent the step function h using a Fourier series expansion and then optimize the coefficients subject to integral and boundedness constraints. This approach leverages the symmetry observed in optimal step functions and reduces the dimensionality of the problem.

The key innovations are:
- Using a spectral representation to parameterize step functions
- Applying projected gradient descent to enforce feasibility
- Employing multilevel optimization by increasing resolution while preserving structure

This method efficiently explores the solution space despite the non-convex nature of the problem. The multilevel aspect prevents premature convergence while the spectral representation captures the essential features of optimal solutions.

def project_coefficients(coeffs, n):
    # Enforce |irfft(coeffs)| <= 1
    h = np.real(np.fft.irfft(coeffs))
    for i in range(len(h)):
        if h[i] > 1:
            coeffs = coeffs - (h[i] - 1) * (np.angle(np.fft.rfft(h)[i]) / (2*np.pi))
        elif h[i] < 0:
            coeffs = coeffs + (-h[i]) * (np.angle(np.fft.rfft(h)[i]) / (2*np.pi))
    return coeffs

def clamp_and_normalize(h, dx):
    total = dx * np.sum(h)
    if abs(total - 1) > 0.001:
        factor = 1 / total
        h = h * factor
    h = np.clip(h, 0, 1)
