1. **Approach and Rationale**  
   We address the Erdős minimum overlap problem using a **Fourier-based parametrization** and **projected gradient descent**. The key idea is to represent the step function as a Fourier series and minimize the maximum overlap under the given constraints. This allows us to leverage the global structure of the function while local optimization refines the solution. The approach differs from previous methods by:  
   - Using a spectral representation to capture periodic components (expected in optimal step functions).  
   - Implementing a multilevel strategy that exploits resolutions from coarse to fine for faster convergence.  
   - Combining conjugate gradient steps with stochastic gradient-based searches to balance exploration and exploitation.  

   However, Fourier-based methods can lead to Gibbs phenomena (oscillations near discontinuities) and may not fully satisfy the step function constraint (values must be in {0,1}). Our repair mechanism ensures feasibility post-optimization.

2. **Mathematical Specification**  
   Let the step function \( h: [0,2] \to [0,1] \) be represented by its Fourier series:  
   \[
   h(x) = \frac{c_0}{2} + \sum_{k=1}^{n} \left( a_k \cos(k\pi x) + b_k \sin(k\pi x) \right)
   \]  
   where coefficients \( a_k, b_k \) are scalars. The integral constraint \( \int_0^2 h(x)  dx = 1 \) restricts the DC component:  
   \[
   \int_0^2 h(x)  dx = \frac{c_0 \cdot 2}{2} + \sum_{k=1}^{n}, a_k \cdot 0 + b_k \cdot \int_0^2 \sin(k\pi x)  dx = c_0 + 0 = 1,
   \]
   so \( c_0 = 1 \). The overlap integral is:  
   \[
   C_5 = \max_k \int_0^2 h(x) (1 - h(x+k))  dx.
   \]  
   We optimize the Fourier coefficients to minimize \( C_5 \) subject to \( a_k, b_k \geq 0 \) (ensuring non-negative Fourier components for step-like functions) and enforce \( h(x) \in \{0,1\} \) post-recovery.
