1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea  
The task is to minimize the exact discrete maximum
\[
C_5(h)=\max_{r\in\{0,\ldots,2n-2\}} \left[\text{dx}\cdot S_r(h)\right],
\quad \text{dx}=2/n,
\]
where \(S_r(h)\) are the entries of `np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")`. Rather than using previous linearized momentum, Fourier evolution, simulated annealing, or basis-coefficient searches as the central update mechanism, this plan optimizes an epigraph problem directly as a smooth nonlinear programming problem:
\[
\min_{h,\tau}\ \tau \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \tau-S_r(h)\ge 0\ \forall r,\quad \sum_i h_i=n/2,\quad 0\le h_i\le 1,\ \tau\ge 0.
\]
The variable \(\tau\) is treated as an explicit continuous maximum envelope. Every overlap constraint is retained as an analytic inequality with an exact Jacobian computed from the bilinear terms. SciPy's stable SLSQP solver, which supports smooth nonlinear inequality/equality constraints and bounds, is used for local nonconvex epigraph solution. This is substantive: the optimizer is allowed to move all coordinates simultaneously to reduce the own maximum residual, using the true quadratic products in the active/nonactive constraint surface, rather than only following one linearized direction, one smooth surrogate, or local pair-transfer directions.

1.2 Why this can improve the parent  
The parent and its predecessors are heavy on active-set LP directions, smoothed gradients, Fourier genes, annealing, and pair polishing. Such mechanisms can stall once the current active envelope becomes locally rigid. The full epigraph nonlinear solver performs a sequence of QP-like local models over the entire constraint system. It can shift fractional mass in the all-true bilinear constraints in small steps, allowing tiny improvements near the parent point after the global barriers are slightly relaxed. Because a very small improvement is still included (record is 0.380892; target 0.3808), direct simultaneous constraint satisfaction is crucial.

1.3 Global and non-global components  
The epigraph solver is local. Thus global basin escape must come from a seed factory and mutation process. These do not claim global optimality. Every result is judged by the actual evaluator, not solver objective or constraint values. If an epigraph solution is inaccurate or violates the constraint violation tolerance sufficiently that its premise differs, rejection is safe; the objective is recomputed exactly.

1.4 Scope of mathematical claims  
The bilinear constraints are not convex. Therefore no KKT point or SLSQP local result is a global upper-bound theorem. The returned claim is merely the exact bound of a valid produced vector. The method is heuristic but changes existing low-dimensional/design trials with mandatory exact acceptance.
