1.1 Goal and main bottleneck
The evaluation is the nonconvex finite-dimensional minimax problem

C5(h) = max_{r=0,...,2N-2} dx * corr(h, 1-h, mode='full')[r],

where x = dx * h, dx = 2/N, h_i in [0,1], and sum_i h_i = N/2. The parent construction close to the current record is already close to a local optimum in a broad full-space perturbation search. A purely one-directional linearized or coordinate-descent method is expected to make tiny progress because it treats the maximum locally along a fixed direction, while a true local optimum of the minimax problem forces many near-active lag constraints to move together.

The central proposed change is: solve soft target problems on a reduced feasible variable space, not on h directly, and repeatedly accept only candidates that improve the authoritative evaluator. The reduced space is the complementary/mirror family h_i + h_{N-1-i} = 1 (or h and mirrored ones paired over exactly half the positions). This representation exactly satisfies the interval integral constraint constructively, avoids repeated projections during every surrogate objective call, and cuts the number of optimization variables by about two. Then, instead of minimizing the sharp maximum directly by epigraph LP descent, the main search tries to make a list of candidate bounds B attainable by minimizing a smooth sum of violations of all lag overlaps against B. Several smoothing widths and target levels are used, including a smooth logarithmic-penalty variant, an epigraph-like full-LSE smoothing pass, and a bounded multi-seed global search over the feasible complementary manifold.
