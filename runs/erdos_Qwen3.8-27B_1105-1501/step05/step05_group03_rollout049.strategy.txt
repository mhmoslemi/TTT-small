1. Approach and rationale
1.1 Central idea
The objective is a finite-support max of bilinear overlap functions

    C5(h) = max over all lags d of (2/n) * sum valid i h_i * (1 - h_{i+d}),

with h_i in [0,1] and sum_i h_i = n/2. The previous parent code makes heavy use of linearized LP directions, gradient/epigraph-style local moves, Fourier evolutions, and binary/continuous pair polish. This plan replaces the primary optimization layer with a different search space: a sum-zero rectangular-block basis followed by an exact quantile projection. A candidate is produced from a small parameter vector y as

    raw(y) = 0.5 + B @ y,
    h(y) = P(raw(y)),

where every column of B has exact zero sum and P maps any real vector to a feasible [0,1] vector with exact target sum by solving a threshold equation. The search occurs in the low-dimensional y-space using robust derivative-free global/search methods (differential evolution plus coordinate pattern searches), and every candidate is judged only by the exact task objective. This avoids relying on derivatives, epigraph solvers, LP-subproblem bundles, or binary MILP models as the main mechanism.

1.2 Why this helps
The record construction is already close to a local optimum of the full n-dimensional continuous problem. The bottleneck is not trivial coordinate release but combining structured multi-square/block changes that preserve the exact integral constraint and exploring several discrete resolutions. Low-dimensional sum-zero basis search can make large coherent shape changes, including block depletions/fillings and local transitions, without clipping-then-rescaling. Projection is exact and jointly enforces bounds and sum. Differential evolution provides believable basin escape. Multi-resolution processing is supported because the evaluator naturally uses dx = 2/n, and a shorter resolution with a better discrete overlap profile is a legitimate tighter bound; longer resolutions may also improve due to stabilization of transitions. A final full-dimensional random pair-transfer polish gives a fallback safe exact local improvement method after the basis dimension is left behind.
