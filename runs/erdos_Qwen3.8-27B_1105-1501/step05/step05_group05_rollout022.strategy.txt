1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The supplied construction is already a good local minimum of the discrete overlap objective at about 0.380893. The objective, however, is not a convex minimization problem: each score for a lag is the finite-support quadratic overlap

    f_r(h) = dx * sum_i h_i(1-h_j)

and the maximum over all 2n-1 linear lags is a nonconvex epigraph constraint. Therefore the method should not be presented or treated as a convex program or a proof of optimality. It should be treated as a high-quality local mechanism that can move a continuous feasible vector to a lower exact objective, with every accepted accepted candidate re-scored by the exact evaluator.

The proposed main method is different from the earlier linear-transfer/gradient/LP/local-pair approaches because its dominant local engine is an exact epigraph-based constrained nonlinear solver. It directly optimizes the variables

    minimize t subject to f_r(h) - t <= 0, sum(h)=n/2, 0<=h_i<=1,

using an analytic Jacobian for the active top lags. Instead of optimizing only along one predefined direction, or representing the maximum by a log-sum-exp penalty, the epigraph variable is kept in the optimization. The optimizer can therefore coordinate simultaneous reductions in several top lags, which is the practical bottleneck for lowering the maximum by only a few 1e-5. This principal active-set SLSQP/nonlinear-epigraph layer is the new mechanism; preceding and surrounding phases are exact verification and auxiliary corrections.

The method uses:

- A dynamic active-set epigraph SLSQP layer based directly on the original objective.
- Analytic exact gradients for the binary-dependent top scores (without approximating products with linear constraints).
- High-resolution transfer from the supplied parent, because discretization noise and boundary effects can mask the continuous optimum.
- Fallback exact feasible line-search search along min-norm active-constraint and smooth-supergradient directions, used to push very-small candidate improvements or recover if the SLSQP layer stalls.
- Multi-resolution and multi-seed restarts, with exact acceptance only by the official metric. No surrogate is used for final acceptance.
- A strict exact-validity barrier: only finite, bounded, mass-preserving arrays that pass exact evaluator evaluation are stored and returned.

The method remains local/heuristic. It can plausibly move the parent to 0.3808 by locally reducing the active-petal plateau using a second-order/enactive constrained-search engine at higher and lower resolutions, and by combining active-set epigraph corrections with finite exact steps.

1.2 What bottleneck is addressed

In a minimax problem after many local methods, usually:

1. Several distinct lag overlaps are very close to the maximum.
2. Pure gradient descent or unilateral coordinate transfer often lowers one top lag while increasing another.
3. LP/linearized penalty methods struggle because the constraints are bilinear and become nonconvex; treating them only linearly can stall.
4. One-resolution search can get stuck in discretization artifacts or local basin structure at fixed n=122.

The new primary epigraph phase solves a small reduction of active constraints as a direct constrained optimum (not by smoothing or pair partition) and can find a direction that jointly lowers a group of active scores. The high-resolution transfer layer can then step into a different discretization representation, from which the physical better lower value may be reachable, then passes down to coordinates.

1.3 Limitations and factual status

Facts from the problem and the evaluator:

- The official objective is `max(np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")) * (2/n)` over the full finite-support lag set. It is linear correlation with a finite overlap support, not a periodic/circular correlation, and should not be replaced by an unverified periodic convolution identity.
- The constraints are 0<=h_i<=1 and sum(h)=n/2. Feasibility must be joint, because projection may require a joint water-filling method rather than clipping then scaling.
- The objective is polynomial and generally nonconvex due to products `h*(1-h)`, so a direct unchanged convex CVXPY problem cannot give global optimum. The workflow avoids doing that.

- The parent is not a global optimum and a small constrained Newton/gradient improvement exists at same or finer resolution.
- High-resolution representation and a local epigraph truncation may help escape a coarse local basin.
- No proof is given, so no optimality claim will be made. The best candidate is only called improved when the actual objective strictly decreases.
