1. Approach and rationale

The core idea is to change the search representation from a full vector with a global-sum constraint to a hard complementary-pair subspace and then attack the minimax objective through soft target-margin feasibility problems instead of directly solving an epigraph or linearized direction problem.

Two structural changes are made relative to the previous parent algorithm:

- Complementary-pair parameterization. Every candidate is represented by an array `a` of length `m = floor(n/2)`, with the full vector reconstructed as `h_i = a_i` for the first `m` positions and `h_{n-1-i} = 1 - a_i` for the mirrored positions; for odd `n`, the center entry is fixed to `0.5`. This hard-ensures `0 <= h <= 1` and `sum_i h_i = n/2` without projection, penalty drift, or sequential clipping/rescaling. Feasibility is exact by construction.

- Soft feasibility/margin minimization. For a requested target bound `B` below the current known objective, the optimizer solves a smooth soft-inequality subproblem that penalizes only lags whose discrete overlap values exceed `B`. This is closer to the task of proving `C_5 <= B` than simply minimizing a smoothed maximum: when `B` is just below the current value, inactive constraints are downweighted, allowing movement specifically on the near-maximal lags, while active constraints are pushed down together. Moreover, every candidate is accepted only if its exact authoritative evaluator improves the stored best.

A smoothed maximum scalarization is also used as an exploration phase because it provides a differentiable surface and can move through weak plateaus. However, the central new search phase is not the LP linearization or epigraph scalar `t` descent of the previous approach; it is a sequence of hard-symmetric soft-margin feasibility solves at prescribed candidate bounds.

Why this could improve the supplied parent:

- The parent is numerically near a local optimum in a full-dimensional local search. The feasible complement of zero-sum perturbations can be hidden by nearly duplicate/half-active lags and projection drift.
