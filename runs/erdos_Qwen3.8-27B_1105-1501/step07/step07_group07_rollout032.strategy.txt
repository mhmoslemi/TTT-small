   1.1 Central idea

   Replace the prior linearized/epigraph local-descent strategy with a continuous annealed-smoothing of the finite-support maximum. Instead of constructing trust-region linear directions from a subset of active constraint rows, solve a sequence of problems whose objective is a logarithmic-sum-expential approximation of the maximum lag score. For a current raw lag-score vector, this is a convex smoothing of the maximum. Each smoothed problem is solved by a constrained local optimizer that enforces the sum constraint and the bounds at the niveau. The smoothing parameter is decreased step by step, so that the optimizer first avoids premature concentration on one or two active lags and later approaches the nonsmooth maximum objective.

   This changes the mathematical algorithmic structure in a substantive way: the method operates directly on a smooth upper approximation of the original maximum of all lag scores. It does not choose a set of active top constraint rows for a linear programming direction, it does not use a differentiable detour into a small subset of neighborhood scores as the primary search engine, and it does not rely on a combination of row truncation plus projected gradient line search as the control mechanism. The smoothed full-maximum subproblem has a nonconvex feasible region because of the bilinear objective, but the smoothed scalar objective is differentiable and its gradient can be computed from the exact row gradients.
