1.1 Central idea

The proposed improvement is built around an exact two-coordinate continuous line-search minimizer for the discrete Erdős overlap objective, augmented by active-lag-guided random jumps and multi-resolution candidate comparison. The principal engine is a pair-pivot local search in which, at every stage, the current vector `h` satisfies the box and mass constraints. For a pair of indices `(i,j)`, the only search variable is a scalar `alpha` in the feasible interval generated by

    h' = h + alpha * (e_i - e_j).

The exact discrete correlation vector for `h'` is a quadratic polynomial in `alpha`, so the value

    C5(h') = dx * max_r corr(h', 1-h', mode="full")[r]

can be evaluated along the entire feasible segment without constructing many new vectors. A dictionary of pairs is selected from the currently most active correlation lags and their exact analytic gradients. Candidate directions are therefore not random perturbations but low-dimensional displacements across the same tangent directions and constraints that govern the observed peak overlap.

The method differs substantially from the previously suggested subgradient/LP/smooth-max/annealing methods:

- It does not minimize a smooth surrogate as the objective for the final move.
- It does not search only along unsdivided subgradient gradients or full LP directions.
- It does not merely swap bits at random.
- It uses the original evaluator as the sole acceptance certificate for each accepted step.
- It incorporates escapes from local optima through structured random critical-lag directions and restart basins, then accepts only exact improvements.

1.2 Why this can address the parent optimum

At the supplied parent near `C5 ~= 0.380889`, the maximum overlap is likely attained by several different lags. A one-variable engineering direction may produce an immediate reduction for one lag but create a new maximum at another. The pair-pivot algorithm performs very cheap and exact pairwise line searches over the entire segment, preserving the exact sum and box constraints at every point. Because it explicitly chooses index pairs linked by the active and nearly-active correlations, it moves simultaneously in theカラ highest-adversarial coordinate directions while every accepted candidate is judged on the initial discrete evaluator. This makes it especially suited to small improvements in a near-optimal plateau where local curvature and maximum overlap constraints simultaneously hold.

Importantly, the method is local and nonconvex. The exact-pair-streaming is a local optimizer in a restricted family of feasible two-coordinate directions. It is not logically justified towards global, nor toward re-attaining the current record. However, the method's chaotic restarts and conservative exact acceptance make it a robust way to extract remaining numerical slack.

1.3 Distinguishes objective / constrained generation from acceptance

All proposed candidate moves are evaluated with the same evaluator as the harness:

    exact_C5(v) = max(np.correlate(v, 1-v, mode="full")) * (2 / len(v)).

Targets such as pre smoothed correlations, gradient temperatures, or active lag weights are solely computational heuristics; they are never allowed to replace the real objective.
