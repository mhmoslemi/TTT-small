1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea
The parent search was built around many stochastic line-searches over a soft-max surrogate of the objective. The proposed replacement is a deterministic “feasible-edge minimax polishing” engine. Its main candidate direction is found by solving a small convex linear program that represents the first-order decrease of the largest few raw overlap constraints, but the actual accepted move is always a one-dimensional line segment between two points that are exactly feasible. This avoids projection stability problems, preserves the integral constraint by construction, and forces directions that reduce the currently worst overlaps more aggressively than random Fourier, single-pair, or purely projected gradient directions.

The second idea is that the parent is not the only useful attractor. For binary threshold, modular/low-discrepancy, and spectral fluctuation constructions, the same engine can gradually open gaps and fractional thicknesses that have correlations matching the active lags. Even if an initial candidate is worse than the parent, its basin may contain a better local optimum. Thus the full program maintains a small pool of feasible candidates and runs it with round-robin allocations of time.

1.2 Why this addresses the known bottleneck
The task’s objective is a maximum over 2n−1 finite-support correlations. The smooth gradient toggles the largest lag but is conservative: it uses one aggregate descent direction and often rotates if many tied lags are present. A maximization problem can stall because a first-order aggregate cannot reduce every top raw curve simultaneously. The proposed linear program solves the simultaneous top rows at once, producing a direction that decreases their first-order values until the minimum top-row slope becomes as large as possible under box and integral walls. Then the exact nonlinear correlation path confirms the candidate. This spoils primarily the stagnant “all top overlaps are equally high but a single gradient or random direction fails to lower them collectively” regime.

The steps are not convex over the original bilinear problem. The LP is a linearized convex direction subproblem. Acceptance is only after exact re-evaluation with the evaluator metric, so there is no global optimality claim. The search may still reach a local optimum; however, unlike previous random parameter search, the chosen direction optimizes every selected active first-order reduction under simultaneous feasibility bounds.

1.3 Main limitations
The objective is nonconvex. The LP only optimizes a linearized active-set model and can underuse second-order curvature. Exact lines can be rejected because constraints causing their lines become continuously active. Fractional variables near 0 and 1 make the box-tangent cone narrow. The method is therefore local/pooled, not a guaranteed global minimizer. Nevertheless, it explores locations away from the parent and gains more systematic control of the active constraints that determine the exact C₅ score.
