1.1 Central idea

The supplied evaluator is a finite-support minimax objective. For a one-dimensional array `h` of length `n`,

`dx = 2.0 / n`  
`target = n / 2`  
raw profile `r(h) = np.correlate(h, 1 - h, mode="full")`  
`C5 = dx * max_l r_l(h)`

subject to

`0 <= h_i <= 1`, `sum_i h_i = target`.

The previous style of solver mainly builds one-dimensional directions from smoothed gradients or isolated coordinate/pair moves and then line-searches them. That remains useful, but it is vulnerable to getting stuck in the local correction polygons of the heavy boundary and finite-support lags.

The proposed primary update is materially different: solve a small linear program whose variables are the whole vector `h` (plus a scalar minimax level `t`), minimizing a one-sided linearization of the overlap constraints for the top lag set inside a trust box around the current feasible point. The one-sided surrogate is exact at the base point, but not a global upper bound on the true bilinear objective. Therefore every LP proposal is judged by the exact discrete objective before it can be kept or used as a direction.

The LP gives a whole-vector extremal move aimed at lowering many active lag constraints simultaneously. After an LP proposal is obtained, we perform an exact finite-support line search along the resulting zero-sum direction. The accepted current point is updated only from exact evaluator values, so an invalid or overoptimistic LP move cannot corrupt the saved construction.

The method combines:

- exact finite-support evaluation for every candidate;
- a joint feasible projection mechanism that enforces bounds and sum simultaneously;
- whole-vector constrained LP updates rather than only smooth-gradient or single-pair moves;
- exact one-dimensional refinement along LP directions;
- multi-resolution and seeded candidate-state scheduling;
- a robust parent fallback.

The LP subproblem is convex. The original product `h_i * (1-h_j)` remains generally nonconvex. No global optimality is claimed. Every retained step is conditional on the original evaluator.

1.2 Why this can improve the parent

At a near-optimal vector, the bottleneck is typically an active set of several lagged overlap profiles. Single-coordinate or low-rank pair moves adjust only a few coordinates and may leave one of the active constraints unchanged. The LP update changes all coordinates to the vertex or boundary chosen by the constrained linear minimax surrogate. That is a natural first-order truncation for minimizing the maximum of many affine lag estimates. The exact finite-support line search then chooses whether the LP's suggested full jump, a partial jump, or no jump is globally accepted.

The schedule also transfers the parent construction to nearby and shorter resolutions. Since the evaluator is exact on the returned vector sequence, a different resolution can expose a different local basin while retaining a valid fallback.

1.3 Important limitations

- The LP uses the one-sided surrogate `correlate(x, 1 - h_base)` and its corresponding active lag constraints. The true objective is `correlate(x, 1 - x)`. Hence the LP can be optimistic.
- A separate backward one-sided constraint is optional because `correlate(h_base, 1 - x)` is also linear in `x`, but neither form proves an upper bound on the true objective.
- The exact line search and exact candidate acceptance ensure we never retain a worse exact candidate as the global best.
- The method is a heuristic local/nonconvex search, not a global proof.
- The smaller sequence is preferred only as a tie-break near the exact best score. Lower exact `C5` is the primary criterion.
