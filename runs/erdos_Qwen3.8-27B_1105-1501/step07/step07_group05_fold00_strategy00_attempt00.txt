1. Approach and rationale

The core difficulty is that the authoritative objective is a nonconvex maximization of the linear-correlation sum
`max_l Raw[h,1-h](l) * 2/n`, over all `2*n-1` linear lags. The previous parent value is close to the target; the most plausible way to go below it is a highly parallel/serialized randomized global search over the same feasible space, but using absolutely exact objective changes for each proposed move so that no nuisance from correlated arrays or smoothing dominates the search.

The proposed method replaces generic full-array correlation evaluations inside local updates by an exact algebraic update for finite exchanges of two (or more) coordinates. For any step direction whose support consists of a small list of indices, the change in all `2*n-1` raw counts is known analytically as a quadratic polynomial in the step size `alpha`. Since each proposed move is a zero-sum exchange, the integral constraint is handled exactly. Box constraints are handled through the corresponding exact polynomial bounds. This permits a very dense exchange search: a randomized simulated annealing phase may take uphill proposals, while a repeated exact-pair line-search phase can monotonically descend from any candidate along the strongest local exchange direction. These two search modes are complementary: annealing escapes local minima imposed by (a) discrete holes in binary moves, (b) exchange matching, or (c) misleading active-lag weighting; exact line search then harvests the local opportunity discovered by the annealer without smoothing errors.
