1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central algorithmic idea

The proposed method is an active-set, linearized minimax descent method. It repeatedly forms a trust-region convex LP subproblem using the exact discrete objective values and exact analytic gradients of the most active correlation constraints. The LP direction is chosen so that the first-order linearized maximum overlap is reduced while the mass constraint and individual sample bounds are maintained. Every proposed step is then verified by the original non-smooth exact discrete objective

C5(h) = max_l np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")[l] * (2/n_points),

using an exact line search. Only exact improvements over the current local point are accepted, and the global best over all stages is always stored by direct re-evaluation.

This is materially different from the previous methods in this context. The earlier revision used mostly one-dimensional negative subgradient or smooth maximum/SLSQP ideas checked by simple or globally annealed candidate moves. Here the update direction explicitly contains a scalar epigraph variable and satisfies a first-order version of all active and potentially dangerous correlation constraints, with mass and box-feasible variable capacities. The method therefore uses the constraint interaction and multiplicativity that cause several simultaneous peaks, rather than moving only along one weighted displacement or optimizing only the active maximum scalar term.

The same method is deliberately non-global and nonconvex. The LP subproblem is only a linearized local model; no convexity or global optimality is claimed. It is a local descent/tracking driver. Exact evaluation from the original evaluator is a strict acceptance gate.

1.2 What bottleneck this addresses

At 0.380889, the parent is close to tightness: several shift correlations likely have nearly maximal values. Single subgradient moves reduce one average peak but risk producing another peak first because the direction does not explicitly reduce a joint set of active constraints. At half-capacity mass near 0 or 1, boundary moves are limited in strongly asymmetric directions, which simple residuals must resolve.

The proposed LP explicitly handles:

- multiple simultaneous active correlation constraints;
- discrete positive and negative finite-support correlation gradients;
- the precise boundary h_i=0/1 and h_i=1 feasible capacities;
- fixed mass exactly (in linearized variable update: sum d=0);
- high covariance from non-convex curvature through exact line search and exact acceptance.

Even if the linearized direction is accepted only rarely, local curvature and multiple active barriers can make this more effective than a single designless penalty modification.

1.3 Necessary honest limitations

- The bridge constraints are first-order restatements of correlation constraints, not exact constraints of the original bilinear problem.
- A LP direction may exactly give no improvement due to second-order terms. Exact trusted line capabilities ensure the local method never saves worse.
- The approach can terminate at a local feasible Kuhn-Kepez point that corresponding to no linearized descent direction. Basin transforms can help locate better basins after local stagnation but no global penalty guarantee is made.
- Resolution changes require careful comparison via the same discrete evaluator; re-evaluated candidates are accepted only if exact score is better or when their exact score is same but the sequence is shorter, because shorter length is beneficial for speed.
- The initialization at g_locked when initial_h_values exists is projected into exact discard feasibility but then reported precisely. The standard binary/fragmental underlying the already computed performance evaluation is validated.

1.4 Key properties being justified

- The C5 math is framing as explicit discrete scale convex hull interpolation. All correlation equations derive from indices against explicit np mode for sign that can be verified by one numerical gradient test.
- Constraint interactions and variable capacities through joint map direction can be solved via standard convex model LP auxiliaries.
- Acceptance regularization hubs use only the original non-smooth full convolution and may lead across irrational families of equivalent monotone rankings. Stems hoisted using fast but correlated approximation outside the end gate may never correct the reporting bounds if exact final candidate is incorrect.

The performance amounts represent qualitatively safer efficient improvement within a ready to tackle hard local optimum corner.
