1. Approach and rationale

The current 0.380889 construction was produced mainly by sparse one‑coordinate/pair transfers, nearby-resolution resampling, and binary local exchange. Those mechanisms are valuable but have a structural bottleneck: a step can change only one or two coordinates. The objective, however, is a maximum over all finite-support shifts. A coordinated change at many coordinates can decrease several active overlap peaks simultaneously without immediately overloading another peak. Sparse moves often get trapped around the particular local minimizer reached from the supplied parent.

The proposed primary method is a direct local epigraph solver for the original discrete minimax problem:
- variables are the full sampled profile h and an auxiliary objective limit t;
- every exact lag overlap becomes one nonlinear inequality overlap(l,h) <= t;
- the objective is simply minimize t, subject to box constraints, mass equality, and those inequalities.

This is materially different from coordinate or binary moves. The NLP subproblem treats all lags simultaneously and allows dense increases/decreases of h to move the maximum limit downward, rather than moving one sink/source at a time. The method is still nonconvex and local: it provides improved upper bounds when the local epigraph descent reaches a better basin, and it retains the best valid parent or diverse fallback as a certified artifact.

The algorithm has multiple mechanisms to avoid reliance on one basin:
1. Multiresolution continuation: solve exact epigraph problems at coarser grids, then promote successful shapes to the parent and finer grids. Coarse grids define a wider attractive basin of step functions.
2. Exact-objective acceptance and collecting during optimization: every proposal is checked with the authoritative np.correlate objective. Internal solver variables are not used for upper-bound acceptance.
3. Dense spline/logistic seeding and deterministic irregular starts: several strongly different initial profiles are given to each epigraph solve at each grid size.
4. Exact line search along dense local directions: after a formal epigraph solve, perform local one-dimensional descent along smoothed active gradient directions. Unlike LP-based or pair-based approaches, the candidate direction and exact quadratic slice are derived once and accepted only if the true finite-support correlation decreases.
5. A compact binary exchange finisher is used only as an optional recovery/diversification stage. The solution returned can be fractional or binary; its exact score is independently verified.

The main hypothesis is that the supplied parent is already near the objective values 0.3808 but not local optimum in dense continuous directions. Direct epigraph NLP+NLP recovery can adjust active subsequence correlations by a coordinated policy. Experimentation is required; no optimality claim is made.
