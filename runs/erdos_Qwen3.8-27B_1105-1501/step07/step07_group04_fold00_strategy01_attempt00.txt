1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central different idea

The main method is an exact incremental combinatorial optimizer over balanced binary step sequences, driven by the active maximum lags of the true discrete correlation. It is different from the previous parent-generation algorithm in two essential ways:

- It maintains the full raw correlation vector for the binary state and updates it exactly under one or more single-bit switches/preservatives with an O(n) exact delta formula, rather than recomputing the full nonlinear correlation for every trial move. This allows a much larger number of directed, feasibility-preserving trials within the same budget.
- It generates trial moves directly from the currently active lag contributions. For every correlation lag that is near the current maximum, it proposes weight-preserving swaps that can decrease or rearrange the contributing 1/0 pairs at that lag. This makes the local search aware of the actual bottleneck lags rather than relying only on random or subgradient-like moves.

The search hierarchy is:

1. Keep a valid fallback parent and exact scored candidate throughout the run.  
2. Convert candidate sequences to exactly balanced binary states as far as possible.  
3. Run the primary combinatorial search using exact incremental correlation updates and active-lag compositions.  
4. Transfer improved states to nearby and smaller/even samples, compare them with the original evaluator, and retain only improvements in original C5.  
5. As a secondary polish, apply a short exact line-search/subgradient-like continuous witness polish to best states. Final acceptance is always made on the object exactly as produced by a call to the declared objective `max`.

No candidate found by a surrogate or inexact incremental formula is ever returned without a final exact recomputation using a usual evaluator “max(correlate) * dx”.

1.2 Why this addresses the bottleneck

The current parent is already very close to the reported record. The remaining gap is on the order of 1e-4. A purely subgradient continuous method or a coarse annealing method changes scores in long, flat plateaus. The likely bottlenecks are:

- candidate proposal quality: random moves rarely target the exact lag indices whose maxima are active;
- feasibility handling: clipping/rescaling may leave exact constraints unstable, reducing constructive tests;
- search fanout: the exact sequence combinatorics cannot be explored deeply if every move re-evaluates the full quadratic correlation;
- scale sensitivity: time continuity vs. exact finite-difference sensitivity near a terminal value creates variance and delays/loor test.

The proposed combinatorial algorithm attacks these directly. It proposes targeted pattern-rearrangements at active lags, does exact testing fast with incremental correlation arrays, and keeps returned scores coherent under the specially specified constraints via special binary or calibrated projection. Since the discrete objective is inherently a maximum of products, no global optimality by repeated convex methods is claimed. Improvement is empirical.
