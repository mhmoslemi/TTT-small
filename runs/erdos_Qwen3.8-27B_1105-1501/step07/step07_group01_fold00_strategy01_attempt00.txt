1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

Construct the final step function by optimizing a globally scoped, smooth surrogate of the exact minimax overlap function, then polish the resulting point with exact finite-correlation line moves. The primary optimization engine is a projected Frank-Wolfe trust-region method over the feasible polytope

P = {h : 0 <= h_i <= 1, sum_i h_i = n/2}.

For each lag r = 0,...,2n-2, the evaluator's raw overlap function is

Q_r(h) = np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")[r].

The final objective is

C5(h) = max_r Q_r(h) * dx, dx = 2/n.

The method does not optimize one lag at a time. It replaces max_r Q_r(h) by an annealed log-sum-exp function

L_beta(h) = (1/beta) log(sum_r exp(beta * (Q_r(h) - m(h)))) + m(h),

where m(h) = max_r Q_r(h) and beta controls the smoothness width in raw-correlation units. As beta increases, L_beta converges from a broad averaged energy toward the exact maximum. The key design choice is to optimize L_beta over the full feasible box with a Frank-Wolfe linear-minimization oracle. The Frank-Wolfe direction chooses a vertex or fractional vertex of P that is globally best for the current linearized smooth objective. This gives large, constraint-respecting mass transfers along the grid while preserving the sum and bounds automatically through convex combinations of feasible points.

After the smooth annealing, exact scalar line searches through candidate transfer directions of the form d = e_i - e_j improve the nonsmooth exact objective. Every move during this stage is scalar over a feasible segment, so no ad hoc projection is used during core updates. Since the exact evaluation is cheap at n around 180, all candidates for acceptance are stored only after exact re-evaluation. The output is always the smallest exact C5 bound found and returned, not just a surrogate score.

1.2 Why this is materially different from the previous parent

The parent construction indexes coordinates over a feasible h, and uses pointwise coordinate descent, active-set linearized LP steps, simulated annealing over binary moves, and small local perturbations. Those methods primarily work by nearby pointwise changes or top-k linear local subproblems. The present method is different in three substantive ways.

First, it uses a homotopy over the whole max functional beta from broad smoothing to sharp max smoothing. It is aimed at reducing an entire neighborhood of near-max lags simultaneously before forcing sharpness. This avoids coordinate moves that are locally beneficial for one max lag while generating a new nearby max.

Second, the primary update is Frank-Wolfe over the polytope P. Its linear oracle globally sorts the current gradient and allocates the full required mass to the best coordinates. This direction can be a coordinated transfer, unlike a single-coordinate step.

Third, the core samples maintain feasibility by convex combination, without repeated projection. The exact projection is reserved only for initialization and resolution transfer. Subsequent feasible switching is contracted through exact convexity of P, not waterfilled repeatedly. This avoids losing small cooperation contrasts across the h variables.

No global optimality is claimed. The objective is nonconvex because the overlap functions contain products -h_i h_j. Multiple starting points, scaled resolutions, and exact line polishing make the method more likely to discover a lower point. The quality report must not use surrogate C5; it uses exact C5 returned from the evaluator function as implemented.

This plan is a highly tuned local and global-screening optimization strategy, not a global certificate. Smoothing homotopy and Frank-Wolfe can still stop in local basins. Different permutations or random shifts can yield different basins. The final answer is only the best exact objective found.
