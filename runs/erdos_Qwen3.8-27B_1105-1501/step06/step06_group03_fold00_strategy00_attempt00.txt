1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea  
The problem is a discrete nonconvex minimax problem:

\[
C(h)=\max_r \sum_j h_j(1-h_{j+\ell_r})\,dx,
\]

where \(r\) runs over all finite-support lag terms (positive, negative, and zero lag). The matrix from the candidate samples is a low-dimensional assignment of the continuous overlap integral, but the objective is exactly a maximum of quadratic expressions in \(h\). The difficulty is that linearizations of the active constraints can declare a local optimum even when there exists a nonconvex, curvature-driven feasible move that reduces the maximum. Additionally, active constraints are not known exactly in continuous nonconvex minimax; moving near an inactive constraint can quickly make it active.

The proposed program combines three complementary mechanisms:

1. A deterministic trust-region active-set linear-imax direction very similar to the parent family, but with:
   - a much better equality interpretation of the overlap terms,
   - a robust direct formula for all lag values and gradients,
   - variable trust radii and expanded active neighborhoods on stagnation,
   - exact single-line polynomial line search using the full objective.

2. A coordinate-space generalization of pairwise moves called “block-wave and low-frequency basis moves”. These are still only a scalar line search in one feasible direction but use higher-dimensional supports to shift curiously-coherent wave-shaped mass (small adjacent blocks, sign patterns, and low-frequency harmonics). The combination is intended to escape tiny-neighborhood stagnation caused by the linearized active-set method.

3. Stochastically rounded “fraction-parent binary trials”. If the supplied parent \(h\) is fractional, round it to plausible balanced binary words using permutations of h-dependent scores, and apply local two-flip/interior-descent improvements. If such a binary vector produces a lower exact score, seed the continuous optimizer from it and then allow interior relaxation by directed line moves.

4. A multi-resolution fallback: generate/resize neighbors of the returned-best resolution around \(n=122\), use feasibility-preserving interpolation plus exact-first scoring, and run the same optimizer on lower-dimensional problems for a fraction of the time. Smaller \(n\) is often faster and can expose structures not represented at a single discretization; it is also explicitly preferred when scores are comparable.

1.2 Why this is different and potentially better  
The previous program used linearized trust-region directions and pair moves. New side aspects:

- exact direct overwrite terms rather than relying only on the linear rows produced from generic prior code. This helps LP gradient weights match the actual max-metric row family.
- block and wave supports of small contiguous windows, not just endpoint pairs; this captures coherent raising of one window and lowering of another.
- independent low-frequency modes are entirely different (near-smooth perturbations may reduce many nearmany interaction peaks simultaneously, while a pure active-set LP direction can be overly fragmented).
- Multiple thresholdings of parent into binary balanced vectors make no uncontrolled long literal array and can branch to a fundamentally different local basin.
