1.1 Central idea

Use the supplied parent only as a safe feasible fallback, then search the feasible polytope by a different route than the previous trust-region/local-linear-gradient method. The new primary exploration engine is a relaxed complementary-coordinate linear existemovement (RCCLM): temporarily separate the vector \(h\) and its complementary partner into two auxiliary balance-constrained vectors \((a,b)\). For a fixed partner \(b\), the a开场phanian/correlation objective is exactly linear in \(a\). Then solve the symmetric LP in \(b\) for fixed \(a\). 1.2 Why this addresses the bottleneck

The previous plan relied mostly on local first-order approximations of the true bilinear maximum and on small-radius LP directions. This restricts motion to a very local neighborhood and can stall when a genuinely promising descent requires simultaneous rearrangement of many coordinates or swapping sequential blocks.

The proposed method replaces each local linearization with a **global linear minimax decision** against a chosen fixed partner:
- With the partner temporarily held, each correlation value is a linear form.
- Minimizing the largest such form is an LP with the only constraints being \(0\le x_i\le1\) and the required sum.
- The LP maximally “warps” the vector into positions supported by the partner, often hitting boundary extreme points, thus producing non-small moves not just perturbations along the previous simulated accuracy-equivalent gradients.

1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

Retain the supplied parent as an immediately valid fallback, but explore the feasible set through a new mechanism: a relaxed complementary-pair linear-oracle search. Introduce supplementary balance-constrained vectors \(a\) and \(b\), each in the original box
\[
0\le a_i\le1,\quad 0\le b_i\le1,\quad
\sum_i a_i=\sum_i b_i={n\over2}.
\]
They are not constrained to satisfy \(b_i=1-a_i\), but each is feasible for the original constraint set separately. For a fixed partner \(b\), the formal correlation vector \(dx\,corr(a,b)\) is exactly linear in \(a\). Therefore
\[
\min_{a:\; \sum a=n/2,\;0\le a\le1}\max_q dx\,corr(a,b)
\]
is a global linear minimax LP, not merely a local linearized direction. Then do the mirrored LP in \(b\) for fixed \(a^\star\). The two LP variables yield a complementary endpoint pair
\[
u_1=a^\star,\qquad u_2=1-b^\star,
\]
both of which satisfy the target sum, and whose convex interpolations
\[
u(\alpha)=(1-\alpha)u_2+\alpha u_1,\qquad 0\le\alpha\le1,
\]
are also feasible under the original constraints.

This procedure differs fundamentally from a trust-region/first-order local method because every coordinate move is selected by solving a minimax problem globally over the whole balance-feasible box, relative to a chosen partner. The procedures can push many coordinates to extreme/saturated positions and rearrange the array globally. However, the exact nonconvex objective is never replaced: all candidate endpoints and interpolated points are scored by the evaluator formula before acceptance.

1.2 Why it could improve the supplied parent

The parent appears to be near a stall of local algorithms. - a linear oracle can choose nonlocal heat-of-compression positions globally across all constraints instead of marginalizing gradients one-by-one;
- endpoints can become structurally different but remain feasible for interpolation to a balance feasible extreme control point, with an exact objective check avoiding unsafe acceptance;
