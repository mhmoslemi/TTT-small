1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

Treat the supplied parent vector as a valid, immediately returnable fallback, then run a deliberately different multistart finite-dimensional optimization cascade whose primary engine is a smooth, constraint-respecting norm minimization of the correlation vector \(S(h)\), followed by exact non-smooth polishing and resolution transfer. The exact objective is never replaced by a claimed equivalent; every live candidate is judged by the evaluator metric

\[
C5(h) = \max_{q} S_q(h) = \max_q \left[ \mathsf{np.correlate}(h, 1-h,\text{"full"})\times \frac{2}{n} \right].
\]

1.2 Why this may help

The previous program relied heavily on a linearized trust-region LP and small coupled coordinate moves. That can be appropriate, but it can stagnate when the maximum correlation has several nearly tied active lags whose interaction is only visible through curvature. A finite-\(p\)-norm surrogate and successive \(p\)-sweeping naturally shifts pressure onto the largest lags while providing smooth gradients. Since the feasible set

\[
\mathcal{H}_n=\{h\in\mathbb{R}^n:0\le h_i\le1,\ \sum_i h_i=n/2\}
\]

is convex, this smooth constrained search is a different local topology from the previous L∞/LP approximation. The scheme also deliberately searches several resolution levels by interpolation, feasible projection, and exact re-evaluation, because the discretization length itself can change and lower finite-sample penalty without changing the interval constraint.

1.3 Limitations and status of claims

- The bilinear objective \(h_i(1-h_j)\) and its maximum make the problem nonconvex. The proposed staged nonconvex method does not establish global optimality.
- A finite-\(p\)-norm reduction does not guarantee exact \(L_\infty\) reduction, so exact acceptance is mandatory.
- The performance gain over \(0.380891\) is systematically plausible with many starts, increasing \(p\)-sweeps, and resolution transfer, but not mathematically guaranteed.
- Parent availability alone guarantees at least a valid fallback near the current record.

2. Mathematical specification

2.1 Discrete representation and exact objective

Let sample length be \(n\) and

\[
\Delta x = 2/n.
\]

The sampled vector is \(h=(h_0,\ldots,h_{n-1})\). Constraints are:

1. \(0\le h_i\le1\) for all \(i\).
2. \(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i = n/2\).

The exact correlation vector is defines\(m\) as

\[
S(h) = \Delta x\,\operatorname{corr}(h,1-h),
\]

where \(\operatorname{corr}\) is NumPy’s linear correlation in mode “full”, a real vector of length \(2n-1\). The exact score is

\[
C5(h)=\max_q S_q(h).
\]

Do not use circular correlation or cy tax and do not combine lag signs except as produced by the evaluator.

2.2 Exact Jacobian matching the evaluator

For direct correlation experiments, define an explicit index offset consistent with NumPy linear correlation. For row \(q\), define

\[
s=q-(n-1).
\]

Equivalently:

- If \(q=n-1\), the objective is \(\sum_i \Delta x\,h_i(1-h_i)\).
- If \(q<n-1\), let \(r=n-1-q>0\) and use the nonnegative offset \(r\). The contribution is

\[
S_q(h)=\Delta x\sum_{i=0}^{n-r-1}h_i(1-h_{i+r}).
\]

Then

\[
\frac{\partial S_q}{\partial h_i} = 
\begin{cases}
+\Delta x(1-h_{i+r}), & 0\le i\le n-r-1,\\
-\Delta x h_i, & i\text{ here belongs to } \{i+r:0\le i\le n-r-1\},\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

- If \(q>n-1\), let \(r=q-(n-1)>0\) and use

\[
S_q(h)=\Delta x\sum_{i=0}^{n-r-1}h_{i+r}(1-h_i).
\]

Then

\[
\frac{\partial S_q}{\partial h_i} = 
\begin{cases}
-\Delta x h_i, & 0\le i\le n-r-1,\\
+\Delta x(1-h_{i-r}), & i\in\{i+r:0\le i\le n-r-1\},\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

These formulas are given to prevent mismatched sign conventions. The program may implement them directly or may rely on the same expressions only when verified against numerical finite differences.

2.3 Feasible set and two candidates in subdivision dimension

At a new length \(n'\), violateary candidate \(\tilde h\) is feasible as long as

\[
0\le \tilde h_i\le1,\quad \sum_i\tilde h_i=n'/2,
\]

with tolerance not worse than about \(10^{-9}\). The exact metric remains computed with \(2/n'\). Interpolation may create fractional sums; feasible projection restores them algorithmically before exact scoring.

2.4 Smooth finite-\(p\) surrogate

For fixed candidate \(h\), compute \(S_q\ge0\). For \(p>1\), define

\[
\Phi_p(h)=\left(\sum_q S_q(h)^p\right)^{1/p}.
\]

This is only a surrogate, not an exact upper bound to \(C5\). The relation

\[
\|\Phi_p(h)\|_{\infty}/(2n-1)^{1/p}=\Phi_p/hน่าจะ อย่างมาก<2n-1}S_q? = C5(h)
\]

occurs asymptotically as \(p\to\infty\), but all acceptances below still use exact \(C5\), not \(\Phi_p\).

A stable gradient for large \(p\): set

\[
m = \max_q S_q,\quad r_q = S_q/m.
\]

Then

\[
U = \sum_q r_q^p,
\]

\[
\Phi_p = mU^{1/p},
\]

\[
\nabla_h\Phi_p = U^{1/p-1}\sum_q r_q^{p-1}\nabla_h S_q.
\]
