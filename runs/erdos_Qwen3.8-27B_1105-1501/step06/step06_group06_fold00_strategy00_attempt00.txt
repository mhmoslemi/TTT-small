1. Approach and rationale
1.1 Central idea
The parent construction is already close to a discretized local optimum, so pure SLSQP or active-gradient descent gives only tiny movement. The main bottleneck is that the optimizer is repeatedly confined to the basin of one fixed sample length and, in many iterations, to a small active set of lags.

The proposed method is a primal multi-resolution minimax search that alternates two ideas:

- A **full-lag sequential linearization / trust-region solver**: at each current vector `h`, build a linearized model for **all** overlap lags, not just the visible maxima. Solve an LP with the variables transformed so that every trial move stays inside `[0,1]` and preserves the mass constraint up to the LP numerical tolerance. Then perform a rigorous exact line search on the original discrete lower bound. The LP supplies a common descent direction for the first-order linearization; the exact evaluation decides acceptance.

- A **multi-resolution memetetic loop**: repeatedly create trial vectors at smaller, equal, and finer cell counts from the best vector in terms of exact evaluator cost. Coarsening gives smaller sequences and can reveal a better discretization; refinement creates additional degrees of freedom and can break discretization stagnation. Only exact evaluator scores are used for acceptance.

1.2 Why this can improve the parent
The evaluator is a maximum over many nonconvex bilinear constraints. If only active constraints are optimized, a first-order direction often rotates from one active set to another without lowering the envelope of the curve `score(lag)`. Linearizing the whole lag family forces the direction to reduce not only the current active lags but also the near-active lags, which is the correct first-order surrogate for the max objective and for extrapolating curvature through line search. You can exploit exact symmetry of the correlation: the overlap contains both positive and negative lags, and the highest maximum is often distributed over several lags.
