**Key idea: Use the structure of the correlation to design better moves.**

**Another key idea: Use the Fourier transform.**

The correlation C(d) = sum_k h[k](1-h[k+d]) is related to the autocorrelation of h. By the Wiener-Khinchin theorem, the autocorrelation is the inverse Fourier transform of the power spectrum. So:

C(d) = sum_k h[k] - sum_k h[k]h[k+d] = (number of ones in overlap) - R_h(d)

where R_h(d) is the autocorrelation of h at lag d.

For binary h, R_h(d) = sum_k h[k]h[k+d]. The power spectrum is |H(f)|^2 where H is the DFT of h.

So minimizing max_d C(d) is related to making the power spectrum as "flat" as possible (low autocorrelation at all non-zero lags). This is the "barker sequence" / "low autocorrelation sequence" problem!

Barker sequences and related constructions (like M-series, Golay sequences) are designed to have low autocorrelation. We could use known low-autocorrelation binary sequences as starting points.
