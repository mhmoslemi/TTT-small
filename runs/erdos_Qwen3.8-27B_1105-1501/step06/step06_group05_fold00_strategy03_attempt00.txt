1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central method and what this changes

The proposed method is a constrained population-based stochastic search on finite step functions, followed by an exact greedy pair-exchange polish. The search operates directly on feasible objects, not on unconstrained parameters. It maintains the original finite correlation objective, uses exact correlation scores for objective acceptance, and uses a temperature-controlled soft-max surrogate for population selection.

This is materially different from the supplied program because the search state is not created by a single local line-search loop over active gradient-like directions. Instead:

- Candidate proposals are drawn from zero-sum Gaussian offsets applied to a feasible mean. Because the offset sums to zero, the pre-projection proposal preserves the required sum. The only remaining feasibility issue is box bounds, which is corrected by a genuine fixed-sum monotone projection plus a deterministic equality repair. This is a robust joint feasibility mechanism rather than clipping followed by arbitrary rescaling.
- The best elite proposals feed back through a mean and diagonal variance update. This gives adaptive, multiple, correlated observation of low-overlap regions, rather than repeatedly inducing a single local direction.
- Ranking uses a smoothed maximum-overlap surrogate with annealed temperature. This lets poor-but-structured correlations propagate across the active finite correlation rows in a way that hard-max line-search may miss. All actual archive updates, final candidates, comparisons between resolutions, and returned values are based on the original exact evaluator F(h)=dx*max(np.correlate(h,1-h,mode=full)).
- Exact local pair-exchange moves are strongly weld based two coordinates exchanging mass: they preserve sum exactly and saturate coordinate bounds. The local polish is purely exact (no surrogate acceptance), and it handles finite objective moves that hard-max constraints often have at coordinate box bounds.
- Different possible lengths are explored by legal resampling. Lower exact scores remain valid proof objects; shorter arrays are only favored under nearly equal scores to respect the preference for smaller faster samples. Older valid scores are not contaminated: archive selection always resolves by exact score first.

The bottleneck it addresses is local-minimum entrapment of active-lag gradient/line-search methods. By annealing smoothing over all finite correlation rows, maintaining covariance over coordinate directions with exact-sum mutations, and applying exact coordinate-bound exchanges, it investigates basins and edge vertices a simple local method cannot readily reach. The design is heuristic. No global optimum is assumed or claimed. A backstop fallback and exact revalidation ensure that a feasible candidate is returned before the deadline.

Reasonable assumptions and limitations:
- The exact evaluator and problem constraints are nonconvex. The method is a constructive minimizer, not an exact solver or proof of optimality.
- Covariance population ranking with a surrogate can sometimes be misled by rows whose linearized transition没见过. exact archive only or objective evaluation candidate realized; asynchrony objective final ensures specific formal proof consistency.
- Searching nearby resolution differences is safe as an upper-bound proof because each returned vector is evaluated by the task formula. Dimension changes are not assumed monotone; the accept/reject rule is exact numerical improvement on returned vectors, not shared adjusted scores.
- Numerical equality comparison of floating sums cannot be double-level exact for arbitrary arrays. The feasibility design includes deterministic repair and revalidation. If a transformation would leave a non-finite or outside bounds output, the exact evaluator substitutes fallback rather than emitting impossible output.
