1.1 Central idea

The supplied parent construction is a feasible point in a compact polytope

    P_n = { h in [0,1]^n : sum_i h_i = n/2 }.

The scored objective is the exact evaluator maximum

    C_5(h) = dx * max_r  np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")[r],
    dx = 2/n.

The new search is built around one exact mathematical fact and a two-tier local strategy.

First, for any feasible direction vector V satisfying

    sum_i V_i = 0,

the family of feasible steps

    h(alpha) = h + alpha V

forms the entire raw score vector as an exact quadratic function of one scalar variable alpha:

    y(alpha) = np.correlate(h + alpha V, 1 - h + (-alpha V), mode="full")
             = y(0)
               + alpha * ( np.correlate(V, 1-h, "full")
                           - np.correlate(h, V, "full") )
             + alpha^2 * ( - np.correlate(V, V, "full") ).

Only the exact task evaluator pattern, ordinary linearity plus one bilinear term, is involved. There is no finite-difference approximation, no separate surrogate, and no missed boundary contributions as long as np.correlate is the same correlation evaluator used by the scorer. This gives the ability to solve a highly accurate one-dimensional section-of-the-original-problem step along any zero-sum direction.

Second, almost all local non-smoothness of the exact max objective comes from several lag scores competing for the maximum. Coordinate-preserving pair transfer steps

    h(p) += alpha,
    h(q) -= alpha

are especially natural because they preserve both the bound sum and the total integral exactly except for harmless roundoff. They also imply that nearly every geometrically useful small change can be approached by one or several exact one-dimensional pair sections. A pair section uses a V vector with only two non-zero entries, hence it is cheap and highly interpretable. Powerful local progress is then made by cycling through pair sections, multi-coordinate zero-sum sections, and exact direction line searches. These are combined with a sparse full-lag linearization primal model when necessary.

The second strength is basin exploration. The old solution is projected into families with enforced or encouraged symmetry, monotone intensity transforms, balanced discrete sequences, and multiple resolutions. Single coordinate descent and single direction line search can easily stall on a boundary or on a very flat active-lag subset. These transformations do not replace the final exact objective; all candidates must still pass the actual task evaluator.

1.2 Computational tennis with full-lag structure

At a local point, several scores are active means near the maximum one; moving only according to a subset of active lags can cause a previously inferior score to become active suddenly. The single-line traversing search captures this issue because every one-dimensional sample of the entire score vector

    z(alpha) = raw corr(h(alpha), 1 - h(alpha), "full")

is sufficiently mined over the gravity sampler within each feasible alpha interval. It may not analyze the max of all possible quadratic sections globally, but the local rigorous for the closed quadratic scalar returned after solving is the raw score function.

Pair lines provide complete local `x` and `y` endpoints surface. They do not guarantee a global h descent, but set/update up function f can mostly non-conv penalty certificate target.
