1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The plan replaces the previous smooth-p-norm / top-lag linear interpolation exploitation by a **principled exact pair-coordinate descent**. The discrete objective is

    C(h) = dx * max_r np.correlate(h, 1-h, "full")[r],  dx = 2/n.

A prior pipeline focused on smoothed norms of scores or on first-order linearizations of a small subset of scores. Here, the fundamental search unit is a strict two-coordinate mass transfer of the form

    h_j <- h_j + delta,   h_k <- h_k - delta,

with delta chosen automatically within the feasible interval. This preserves the required integral exactly and never violates the box constraints when delta is chosen in the derived bounds.

The mathematical reason this is powerful is that, for a fixed pair (j,k) and all other components fixed, the score curve as a function of delta is not merely approximated by a line: it is exactly a max over a vector-valued quadratic curve. For each lag r there are scalars A_r, B_r, and a sparse quadratic coefficient vector Q_r such that

    S_r(delta) = A_r + B_r * delta + Q_r * delta^2,

where S_r is the exact evaluator discretization. For sparse pair moves, Q_r is nonzero only at the zero lag and at two lag positions corresponding to the separation |j-k|. This makes the one-dimensional optimization easy to evaluate very cheaply and consistently with the evaluator.

The algorithm then accepts a pair move only if its exact evaluated score improves. Because every accepted move is a real feasible move judged by the official objective, no consistency between internal formulas and numerical errors can pollute the final best score. The internal algebra is solely a search accelerator, while the acceptance test remains exact.

This is a materially different construction machinery from the earlier strategy:
- previous programs used smoothed `p`-norm reductions;
- previous LP moves used only first-order models;
- previous pair polishing used blind ternary scanning without exploiting the exact quadratic structure;
- this plan uses an exact pair-line model for all scores and iteratively refines the pair optimum with golden-section refinement.

The mechanism is also deterministic-predictable under a fixed seed and finite search caps, which improves reproducibility while still allowing global exploration when needed.

2.1 Exact objective and definitions
