1. Approach and rationale

The supplied parent is already close to a strong local optimum on the official discrete grid. Therefore the algorithm should not rely only on another smooth first-order descent or a single LP/epigraph relaxation; those all tend to chase the same active correlations and then stall on a locally flat plateau. The substantive change is to combine an active-guided stochastic relaxation with exact coordinate-transfer polishing. The search basis consists of unit-sum-preserving mass transfers between two positions, which are precisely the moves that preserve the global integral and make it easy to test thousands or hundreds of thousands candidate modifications with the original evaluator.

The method explores this bi-neighborhood with a Metropolis annealer. The proposal distribution uses a simple weighted active-lag gradient: among the currently near-maximal internal scores, the algorithm estimates which positions can decrease the concentrated maximization objective by transferring mass from a donor with negative or high sensitivity to a receiver with low sensitivity. This proposal guidance remains a heuristic and need not deform the feasible set or provide a relaxation.

After or during annealing, the exact objective is tested with the specified finite correlation metric. If a window of no improvements occurs, top records are deterministically polished by two-sided exact pair line search over a sequence of candidate direction transfers, each direction chosen from active-lag gradient information or fallback random feasible candidate pairs. This exact line-search treatment captures real non-smooth minima behind active constraint levels and permits small fractional moves that may never be granted by the previous parent's arithmetic.

Facts relied upon:
- The official objective and feasible sum/box condition are exact and cheap to test.
- For any feasible h and a feasible coordinate transfer h_p += delta, h_q -= delta, feasibility and sum preservation remain exact within floating tolerance.
- The internal split score formulation produces the same multiset as the direct evaluator's output. All final performance decisions accept or reject only exact `np.correlate`.
- The method is nonconvex. It constructs feasible h vectors as upper-bound witnesses where they are found and does not claim a proof of global optimality, feasibility of thresholds, or convexity anywhere.

Why this can improve supplied parent:
- The stochastic guided family can escape a plateau where active signs transmit not strict local complementary slackness; deterministic parent gradient/LP methods stay there.
- Direction method moves two finite coordinates and immediately follows with exact optimization along that line. This directly attacks several weak-correlation toggles and can balance more active maxima than a single weighted soft-gradient update does.
- Certain alternate discretizations and balanced enumerations are explored algorithmically not parent-transform only (and hence can find smaller privileged settings).

Important limitation:
- The final score cannot be guaranteed strictly oval to numbers below 0.3809 until computed exactly; random proposing depends on budget scaling, feasibility handling, and the local state pool. Parent fallback remains feasible for a guaranteed return.
