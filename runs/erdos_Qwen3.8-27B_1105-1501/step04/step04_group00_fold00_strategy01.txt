1. Approach and rationale

The supplied parent construction is a feasible locally optimized vector. The central remaining problem is that the current objective is a finite-support minimax functional: the maximum is achieved simultaneously by a group of shifted overlap constraints, and progress is limited unless all of those active constraints are decreased together. The previous family of methods used linearized linear programs, random metaheuristics, Fourier pools, and soft-gradient steps. Those are useful but do not directly solve the nearby active-constraint equality problem.

This plan uses an active-constraint equality-seeking sequential quadratic/trust-region method as the primary redraw of the parent basin. The idea is to identify the currently largest overlap constraints and to construct a move that makes them closer together by solving a small weighted least-squares system involving only the gradients of those active constraints, while imposing the global mean constraint exactly and controlling move length with an exact objective line search. This is substantively different from an LP line search because it seeks approximate equality among active constraints, not merely any feasible linear decrease of all constraints or a one-sided weighted gradient step.

At mathematical optimality, the active correlation rows are typically nearly tied at the maximum. A constrained linearization that targets all active players toward a common lower level is therefore often the correct local direction when it exists. The direction generation is local and nonconvex requirements are honored: no candidate is accepted unless the exact evaluator admits an improvement. The method is not claimed to be globally convergent, but it is a disciplined local method often better suited to the tie structure of a minimax correlation objective.

To avoid inheriting a single parent basin, the full orchestration repeats the same core optimizer on several compact resolutions and on several diversified starting vectors. Every returned candidate is evaluated with the exact evaluator taken directly from `np.correlate`, after an exact feasibility repair. The parent is never overwritten until it is beaten by a fully validated strict lower score.

Limitations:
- The problem is nonconvex; successful local equalization of active constraints does not guarantee global optimality.
- A weighted least-squares direction may violate the finite-state nonlinearity and fail the exact line search. In that case it is rejected.
- High dimensions make dense linear systems slower. This plan caps dimensions at compact values and uses active rows only.
- The equality direction may be poorly conditioned if active constraints are numerically redundant. Regularization and a fallback weighted-gradient direction handle this.
