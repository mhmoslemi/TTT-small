Actually, let me reconsider the golden section approach. The function f(alpha) = max_k (s_k + a_k*alpha - b_k*alpha²) is NOT necessarily unimodal. It's the max of concave quadratics, which can have multiple local minima.

Example: s_1 + a_1*alpha - b_1*alpha² peaks at alpha = a_1/(2*b_1), and s_2 + a_2*alpha - b_2*alpha² peaks at alpha = a_2/(2*b_2). If these peaks are at different alpha values, the max function has a "valley" between them, and the golden section might converge to a local minimum.

However, in practice, for small alpha ranges (which is what we're dealing with since the feasible range is typically [-0.1, 0.1] or so), the function is often unimodal or nearly so.

To be safe, I'll use a hybrid approach:
1. Grid search with 20 points over the feasible range
2. Golden section in the best interval from the grid
3. This gives a good approximation even if the function is not perfectly unimodal
