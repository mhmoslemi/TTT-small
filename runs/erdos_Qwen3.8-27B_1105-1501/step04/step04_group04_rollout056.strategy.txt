1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

Treat the discretized Erdős maximum-overlap problem as a nonsmooth max-of-bilinear objective constrained to a truncated sum simplex:
C(h) = max_r F_r(h), subject to 0 ≤ h_i ≤ 1 and ∑_i h_i = n/2,
where F_r(h) = (2/n) times the r-th finite-support cross-correlation coefficient between h and 1-h, with the exact lag alignment of np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full").The supplied parent h is retained as the primary valid fallback. The optimization pipeline is deliberately not a single convex solver call: the true objective is nonconvex, so every proposed update is judged by the original exact evaluator.

The primary search is a high-resolution, feasibility-preserving continuation method. It smooths the max with log-sum-exp, uses exact finite-support gradients of the shifted correlation terms, and projects every trial vector back onto the exact box/sum feasible set. A final exact coordinate-transfer and randomly perturbed group-line phase attacks the nonconvex nonsmooth neighborhoods that pure gradient or convexified methods are likely to miss. Where feasible, candidate vectors are transferred among nearby resolutions, but the exact discrete score is always recomputed before acceptance.

1.2 Why this is chosen over another convex subproblem or direct CVX model

A direct convex formulation of max_r ∑ h_i(1-h_{i+d}) over a free h in [0,1] is not available: the products make each F_r bilinear, and the maximum of bilinear forms is generally nonconvex. Therefore, CVX or interior-point convex modeling is only useful as a local heuristic at best and can immediately be rejected or become misaligned. The proposed strategy uses convex smooth max estimates only to generate candidate directions; it then checks the true objective for each trial point.

1.3 Key advantage over a previous try-again local LP/trust-region scheme

The earlier gradient-like path retained prior approximately linear trust steps. The new scheme differs in three concrete ways:

1. a symmetric log-sum-exp smooth max continuation uses current exact values rather than progressively pushing into a previous linearization;
2. exact active-correlation group line search samples and golden-section refines directions computed from the active correlation weights, and starts only from an exact current feasible h, with projection after each trial move;
3. the final phase performs exact maximum-of-quadratic coordinate transfer (internally cost-awarely nonconvex, evaluated exactly on a finite grid!), rather than a shallow random delta-only search.

The combined method is designed to reduce the final discrete C₅ not necessarily from a proven global optimum, but by freeing the optimizer from purely monotonically local linear models and driving through true nonconvex kinks.

1.4 Stage-hierarchy validation

The returned bound is exact relative to the proposed discretization: h_values is the stored vector, it is stored with reconstructed final projection, and c5_bound is computed as the maximum of np.correlate(h, 1-h). The parent is validated initially; if it is finite, box-valid, and has sum close enough to its required n/2, it is eligible and made feasible. If absence or corruption prevents it, a focused deterministic feasible construction is used. If a new vector is returned that is more precise, it independently projects and is exactly scored by internal `evalC5(h)` once the algorithm finishes. This guarantees validation of both the feasible box-constraint and sum constraint as returned, compared with exactly 1 carry good summaries.

All gradients, local flow, projected steps, transferring between grid sizes, and pair or group search are heuristic reductions or local direction-generating devices, not globally exact proofs of minimality in the nonconvex problem.
