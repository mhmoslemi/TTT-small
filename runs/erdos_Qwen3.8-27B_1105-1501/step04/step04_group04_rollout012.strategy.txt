1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The proposed search is a hybrid, low-dimensional-plus-refinement method whose primary novelty is a feasible-coordinate parametrization by smooth harmonic/logistic generators, combined with exact finite-support objective evaluation and exact acceptance. Instead of starting from a convex linearization or a generic subgradient descent on the full 128-dimensional vector, we search in a global 15–26-dimensional exponentiated-harmonic family that can approximate almost-binary arrays by saturating the logistic map. The objective is never replaced by a relaxed objective; every projected feasible point is evaluated by the original `np.correlate(h, 1-h, "full") * dx` objective before it can be chosen as an incumbent. After a good global point is found, a second stage performs carefully prioritized nonconvex finite-support line transport: simultaneous active-lag linearized descent, exact two-coordinate exchange line minimization, and transfer to nearby resolutions. This attacks both the coarse geometry, where a low-dimensional harmonic family can make large nonconvex moves, and the local nonsmooth geometry, where exact line moves and active-lag directions can squeeze out small improvements.

The parent solution, when available, is used as a hard valid fallback and as a source for the parent-derived resolution to be optimized. The same exact objective and feasibility projector are used for every candidate. I do not claim that any stage gives a proof of global optimality; the parent remains valid, and the objective score returned is always the exact value of the returned projected vector.

1.2 Why this can help where prior trust/LP/SGD approaches failed

High-dimensional trust-region linearizations and smoothed gradient steps are very good at polishing an existing solution but are intrinsically weak at finding a new basin whose active constraints require a correlated sill of amplitude across multiple lag constraints. An exponentiated-harmonic parametrization provides a flexible low-dimensional manifold that includes both smooth and almost-binary h values (`expit(z)` saturates to 0/1) and enforces area afterward by projection. A global stochastic optimizer on that manifold can rapidly relocate phase spacing and boundary locations that are hard to reach with sequential directions of one gradient. If the resulting h is below the parent, it immediately provides a child for a precise active two-point line descent. This combination directly attacks the local exact zeros of lag maxima rather than only minimizing a surrogate.

1.3 Critical distinction from prior proposals
