1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

Use an exact-feasible, active-set, minimax descent loop whose key step is different from a soft-max optimizer, a general epigraph nonlinear solver, and simple two-coordinate exchange: generate trial directions by solving a small, strongly regularized convex penalty subproblem in the tangent-feasible direction space. At each iterated point h, compute the exact evaluator scores for all 2n-1 lags. Build a set of near-active score rows. For each selected row r, linearize its score around h and demand roughly that the score remain at or below a target level by enforcing

    grad_r · p <= allowed_r

The violation of this linear demand is penalized quadratically. The direction-search objective is

    Phi(p) = 0.5*mu * ||p||^2 + 0.5*gamma * sum_{r in A} w_r * max(0, grad_r·p - allowed_r)^2

and it is minimized over p constrained to (approximately) zero-sum directions and safe local one-sided movement bounds. The subproblem is convex because maximally a squared positive-part affine function is convex and the squared norm and linear equality constraints are convex. It is not a claim that the original problem is convex; it is only a convex mechanism to propose useful zero-sum descent directions. Every command direction is then accepted only after exact ray evaluation using the official evaluator:

    raw scores = np.correlate(h, 1-h, "full")
    c5 = max(scores) * 2/n

so there is no surrogate contamination in the returned best vector.

This directly attacks the common bottleneck near a local plateau: the complete max is nonsmooth, and active constraints are unevenly coupled. Rather than averaging a smooth surrogate over all lags, the method focuses on robust, exact linear models of the currently near-maximum constraints, regularized so that candidate directions are not degenerate, and uses exact line minimization to decide whether that direction can actually lower the raw global maximum.

1.2 Distinctive mathematical logic from other proposals

This proposal is materially different from both global low-dimensional basis optimization, epigraph SLSQP/trust-constr on the true raw objective, and population two-coordinate transfer:

- The direction program is not trying to solve a full minimax model. It only asks for zero-sum directions satisfying active constraints with a minimum-shear regularization plus soft violation. It produces categories of bounded model directions, and the objective is evaluated exactly along complete feasible rays.
- The penalized subproblem is solved in a projected tangent subspace. This prevents tiny equality errors from accumulating; direction correction and exact ray endpoint formulas enforce preservation of the equality integral constraint in accepted candidates.
- The active rows are dispersed by raw score and by score index separation, avoiding redundant or locally identical active requirements that can make a linear model artificially conflict.
- A local “group completion” mechanism tests if the current point is topology inconsistent rather than line하던근 부경 search. If no direction improves a baseline, prominent sampling and daughter matrices proceed from transpose to Antwort; a stagnated vector becomes retrieval regenerating.

1.3 Limitations

The full optimization remains nonconvex because h_i(1-h_j) terms are products of h variables, and the penalty relaxation is approximate. No proof of global optimality exists. Heuristic claim: active penalty-divergent ray directions can find very small reductions in the exact maximum objective over existing N=64 solutions by finding near-KKT achievable belief corrections. All statements regarding solution values seek confirmation only through the actual formula and require not treating a surrogate as true. A vector cannot possibly produce an erroneously too low bound, as new best updates recompute exact results and the final output re-validated.
