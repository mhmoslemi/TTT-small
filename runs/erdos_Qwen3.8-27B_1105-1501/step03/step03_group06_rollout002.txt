1. The minimum must occur either at a vertex of one of the quadratics, or at an intersection of two of the functions (linear-linear, linear-quadratic, or quadratic-quadratic).

2. For the quadratics with Q_d = 1: vertex at t = -P_d/(2*Q_d) = -P_d/2. Value at vertex: c_d - P_d²/4.

3. For linear functions: no vertex, they're monotone.

4. The minimum of max of these is at a point where at least two functions are equal and at their maximum.

In practice, the simplest approach is:
- Sample t densely (say 200 points) in the feasible range
- Find the best sample
- Refine with golden section search
This is simple and effective for n=64.

But we can do better: since the function is the max of a small number of quadratics and many linears, we can find the exact minimum:
- Compute all pairwise intersection points
- Check if each intersection is a valid candidate (both functions are ≥ all others there)
- Also check vertices of the quadratics
- Evaluate at the best candidate
