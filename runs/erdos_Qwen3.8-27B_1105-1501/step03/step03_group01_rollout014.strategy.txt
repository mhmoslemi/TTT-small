1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The construction must be judged by the exact discrete evaluator

    C5(h) = dx * max_k corr(h, 1-h, mode="full"),    dx = 2.0 / n,

under 0 <= h_i <= 1 and sum_i h_i = n/2. The previous parent has C5 about 0.380917 and is already a high-quality local point. The remaining gap to the desired threshold near 0.3808 is very small (roughly 1e-4), so a purely global probabilistic search is unlikely to succeed by itself. The adopted strategy is a deterministic and stochastic local method that directly minimizes the nondifferentiable max-over-lags objective, but does so by exact orthogonal line filters in a rich family of mean-preserving directions.

The central mechanism is:

- For any direction D with sum_i D_i = 0, the path h(t) = h + t D stays mass feasible for all t.
- Every correlation entry is a bilinear function of h, so along such a path every discrete row of np.correlate(h(t), 1-h(t)) is exactly a quadratic polynomial in t.
- Therefore the exact maximum-over-lags function along the line is a one-dimensional maximum of quadratic functions.
- The line objective can be globally studied and bracketed by evaluating the quadratic coefficients once per direction. Then the chosen candidate is accepted only if the original evaluator evaluates it as a strict improvement.

A variety of direction families are tried:

- weighted gradents from top active constraints,
- sign/block transfer directions derived from the gradients of near-active overlaps,
- low-frequency smooth mean-changing directions,
- random localized transfers,
- small dimensional perturbations and resolution changes.

The method is fundamentally different from using a full epigraph optimization, a smooth-max SLSQP problem, or a linearized subproblem only. Here the dominant update engine is exact directional quadratic line search under a box-feasibility interval, wrapped in a stochastic membrane around the current local solution. It seeks narrow, non-smooth valleys in the exact max objective that a smoother surrogate can miss.

1.2 Why this can improve the parent

At a local optimum of a max-of-quadratics problem, ordinary descent may fail because continuing a single descent direction immediately makes another lag become the maximum. Exact quadratic line formulas let us search the full feasible interval of each direction, so a direction can move a current active constraint below another previously inactive constraint and reduce the overall maximum if the bracketing is good. Block-sign directions and top active-gradient directions target the most important finite set of lags, while line filtering prevents accepting statistical noise from smoothing.

The construction is designed to return a valid vector with the parent as safe fallback. It is not claimed to be globally optimal. Improvement is a heuristic consequence of:

- using the exact C5 for all acceptance and storage,
- preserving joint mass-and-bound feasibility exactly or to a safeguarded precision,
- never returning a worse-than-parent construction,
- sampling many local directions from several angle bases,
- allowing small dimensional changes that can reduce quantization resonance.

1.3 Important limitations

- The objective is nonconvex, and the line function can have multiple local minima. The line search is not guaranteed globally in one step.
- The direction generator is finite and heuristic.
- Small floating-point projections can alter a candidate, so the final best value is recomputed under the original evaluator and loser candidates are rejected.
- The parent can remain the returned construction if the local pops do not clean because the Euclidean optimum has much lower norm but not lower exact max.
- No success or optimality guarantee is claimed.
