1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The supplied parent construction is already very close to a continuous local optimum, and the previous algorithm spent most of its time on a smoothed maximum surrogate, L-BFGS on that surrogate, and linearized LP-trust-region polishing. That is a common approach, but it has a structural bias: the smooth surrogate optimizes a weighted average of all correlation constraints, and the linearized LP uses only first-order approximations followed by ordinary line search on the true objective. Consequently, if the exact bottleneck robustly depends on curvature or discontinuities along a line, the direction can be attenuated or discarded before the exact kink minimum is seen.

The proposed algorithm changes the main optimization primitive. It treats the exact evaluator itself, with all finite interval boundary terms preserved, as a scalar one-dimensional piecewise-quadratic minimax problem along candidate lines. For any zero-sum direction d (so that the integral constraint is exactly preserved on the unprojectioned line segment), the official correlation vector along h(t)=h+t d has an exact closed form as a quadratic polynomial in t for every lag. Therefore the exact score along that feasible segment is

    C_n(h+t d) = max_r  Q_r(t)

where r runs over all 2 n - 1 lags and every Q_r(t) is a quadratic. The line minimum is the minimum of a piecewise-quadratic nonnegative-max scalar function. The algorithm constructs many candidate directions, performs dense multi-stage global scans of this exact univariate objective, and accepts improvements only after the exact task evaluator is re-run on the resulting vector.

This approach is materially different from the parent algorithm:

- The smoothed LSE maximization surrogate is used only to generate descent directions, not as the scalar function to be minimized by L-BFGS.
- The main descent procedure optimizes the exact evaluator along full feasible line segments using closed-form quadratic coefficients, not linearized max constraints and not a smoothed averaged objective.
- The local move set includes exact coordinate-pair line minimizations h+t(e_j-e_i), which give fully feasible bounded transfers between any two sample coordinates. This is absent from the very truncated LP direction polishes of the parent and gives a much better local method for a construction that may have many near 0 or 1 entries.
- Several candidate line proposals at a time are evaluated exhaustively at every accepted-perturbation level; if none of them improves after dense scanning, the search tries random perturbations, coordinate exhaustive sweeps, smooth active-set directions, and macro transformations at several resolutions. The exact evaluator remains the only acceptance judge throughout.
- The method explicitly respects finite support: the line coefficients come from the exact full `np.correlate` bilinearity, not from a periodic autocorrelation formula that would drop interval boundary terms.

This approach is plausible to add improvement because the parent breakthrough of about 0.3809 is only a few inactive-grid target gaps into a bound of about 0.38085. A method that can make sure to examine exact curvature along bounded pair transfers and active-correlation directions may find changes that the parent’s smooth linear system missed, especially if the local problem has active constraints with nonsmooth curvature. This is not a proof of global optimality and makes no such claim.
