1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The discrete objective is a nonsmooth maximum of 2n_points-1 finite-support shifted-overlap terms. The supplied parent algorithm is dominated by subgradient-like descent, local coordinate search on the full vector, random perturbations, and resolution sweeps. Those methods look at a value surface and choose a local move; they do not explicitly solve the natural first-order tangent problem for the maximum of all active overlaps. The proposed primary algorithm makes that tangent problem explicit and solveable:

At a feasible candidate, compute every signed shifted raw overlap value and its exact gradient with respect to h. Consider a tangent-plane line h + alpha d with d preserving the required sum and respecting the tangent cone of active bound constraints. Use a bounded linear program to predict a feasible tangent direction whose predicted maximum (over all selected lag inequalities) is below the current maximum. Then use exact official evaluation on lines restricted to the feasible interval. If the exact metric increases along that tangent direction, reject and retry; if it decreases, accept the exact best point along the line. This gives exact-objective improvement while solving a first-order approximation of the full max over lags.

To escape genuine local minima (the exact tangent plane may show no linear improvement), the program uses two complementary escape mechanisms, but returns only exact-assessed candidates:
