1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central algorithmic idea

The proposed search is centered on an exact one-dimensional global line search along feasible two-coordinate transfers
    h_i -> h_i + alpha,  h_j -> h_j - alpha.
The crucial mathematical observation is that, along such a transfer, the exact finite-support correlation used by the evaluator is not merely locally linear: it is a simple quadratic expression whose quadratic coefficients are independent of the current coordinates and known explicitly from the lag offsets. Therefore one can search the whole feasible alpha-interval with the exact objective value, rather than optimizing a smoothed surrogate or taking only local derivative-based lines.

For fixed current vector h, define
    F(alpha) = max_r [ S_r + alpha * (G[:,i]_r - G[:,j]_r) + alpha^2 * Q_{ij,r} ] * (2/n),
where S is the actual raw np.correlate score vector at alpha = 0, G is the exact raw Jacobian of that score vector, and Q has only three non-zero entries: a negative diagonal correction and two positive off-diagonal corrections. Every candidate generated by the line formula is then tested after writing the two-coordinate move explicitly and re-computing the authoritative evaluator. Acceptance is only allowed if the exact objective is strictly better than the current local point and the global best is updated only if the exact objective is better than the incumbent global construction. This is not a mathematical reduction of the original nonconvex problem to a convex one. It is a restricted but exact family of two-point descent moves, combined with global/independent starting points, feasible transformations, mixtures, and optional resolution transfer.

This idea is materially different from the previously proposed pipelines because the central local operation is neither a smoothed exponentiated max, a full-dimensional linear convex trust region, nor a generic LP/SLSQP attempt. The core descent primitive solves a genuine exact one-dimensional nonconvex/max-quadratic objective on a mass-preserving one-dimensional slice, by coarse global line testing and local refinement. It corrects numerical smoothing mistakes in the previous program and can find side-sliding rebalanceings that smooth gradients cannot because the exact two-coordinate quadratic envelope is retained.

1.2 Why this may improve the supplied parent

The current best construction is already a local optimum at one resolution, but lies extremely close to transition constraints for a small number of active lags. Pairwise exact attractor search has two direct advantages:
- it decreases the objective by the largest possible amount on all cheap two-directory mass transfers;
- it does not rely on a bad step-size adaptation across a smoothed objective, because the full objective over the line is used.

Pair moves alone are not globally sufficient. Therefore the search wraps this exact descent primitive inside:
- a deterministic fallback based on the supplied parent;
- feasible rotations/reversals/mixtures of known endpoints;
- seeded random feasible transfers and fully repaired random proposals;
- a population of verified feasible [[c5, vector, n]] records;
- optional exact cell-average resolution transfer.

The global soundness is absolute: no returned candidate is trusted from a surrogate, final projected heuristic, or multiline producer without re-validating the original evaluator and feasibility conditions.

1.3 Limitations

- The problem remains nonconvex. A locally minimal point with respect to all pair-transfers may still be suboptimal globally. Multi-start and random restarts are heuristics, not a proof of global optimality.
- Exact line search minimization over several pieces of quadratics may miss an isolated narrow minimum if the coarse sampling is too sparse. We mitigate this with adaptive-common grid intervals, local refinement, and final direct exact validation. The pair line is searched only on a subset of all feasible directions.
- Reconstruction to a different n is not equivalent to original discretization. It is accepted solely as a new valid feasible discretization if its exact evaluator is smaller. It is never used as a proof that a transformed bound came from the parent without checking.
- The supfinder algorithm is fit for a CPU-only interior approximately and does not make guarantees faster than available single CPU.
- Some proposals generated by random transfer may be adversarial toward the offspring search behavior. Uniform policies control but need exploration not prove any values.
