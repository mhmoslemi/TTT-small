1. Approach and rationale

Core change from the supplied parent program

- The parent uses a smoothed maximum surrogate and then a linearized trust-region LP/direction search. The smoothed surrogate wants to reduce an exponential aggregate of all correlation values, which can bias the direction away from the exact bottleneck because its gradient is a normalized weighted average over a soft maximum. The parent also explores mostly by small Gaussian perturbations and linear directions.
- The proposed program instead makes the bottleneck itself the decision variable. It works in epigraph form:
  minimize the scalar T (in raw correlation units) subject to T >= every full correlation value, the box constraints, and the exact integral constraint.
- This formulation is still nonconvex and nonquadratic-convex, so a local solver does not prove global optimality. However it is algebraically closer to the evaluator's max-objective: active-lag constraints sit directly in the working QP, allowing the solver to move in directions that reduce the active bottleneck rather than an aggregate average.
- Principal numerical solver: scipy.optimize.minimize(method="SLSQP") on the epigraph problem. The objective is almost trivial (just T), and the difficult work is done by the nonlinear inequality constraints. Custom analytic gradient routines are not trusted in the final decision step; instead, a verified Jacobian method supplies gradients of the full correlation vector. If the analytic gradient cannot be produced or validated, fall back to vectorized finite differences. This removes orientation/indexing ambiguity about the exact np.correlate row convention.
- Search topology is constrained but not pointwise random-only: several compact, deterministic/global analytic transforms of the parent construction are supplied to the same local epigraph optimizer. These include complement-reflection/reflection symmetrizations and low-magnitude active-gradient perturbations. All accepted candidates are checked with the exact evaluator, and the unoptimized parent is retained as a valid fallback.
- This is materially different from the previous algorithm because the central mathematical machinery is different: direct epigraph constrained optimization over the exact all-lag max inequality, versus smoothed aggregation plus linearized LP smoothing plus random restart polishing.

Why this may improve the parent

- If the parent is close to a local smooth minimax point, SLSQP can reduce the few active/near-active lags more efficiently than optimizing an exponential mean.
- If the parent objective is flat but exact active constraints are locally reducible, SLSQP can exploit quadratic curvature in the correlation inequalities.
- Exact validation at every candidate stage ensures that the returned bound is never worse than the retained best valid construction. If the epigraph optimizer fails, the valid parent is returned.

Important limitations

- The problem is not convex. A direct local minimization of epigraph constraints can converge to a local rather than global optimum.
- SLSQP's raw variable and nonlinear constraints may require restarts and scaling/control to avoid stalling. The implementation below addresses this with slack, iteration limits, deadline checks, repeated short hot-restarts, and exact retention.
- The finite-difference Jacobian path is slow but robust for n around 64, and can be limited to active/treated constraints for higher dimensions.
