1. Approach and rationale

The central idea is to treat the feasible set as a union of many exact mass-preserving straight-line segments, and to make that geometry the primary optimization mechanism. For any two coordinates \(i\) and \(j\), fixing their sum \(h_i+h_j\) gives a one-dimensional feasible segment in the intersection of the box constraints and the total-mass constraint. The algorithm repeatedly performs exact numerical line searches on such segments. When no such segment improves the current solution, it uses additional exact mass-preserving block perturbations to make basin-hopping transitions. Every routine is evaluated with the authoritative discrete-overlap objective; no surrogate value, relaxed value, or solver-reported value is ever used to accept a point.

This is materially different from approaches based primarily on smoothed maxima, LP tangent directions, SLSQP over the full correlation bundle, repeated random gradient steps, or binary annealing. Here the optimization is organized around exact feasible one-dimensional bisector/coordinate-pair geometry. It does not rely on a global descent direction, does not solve a convex relaxation, and does not require SLSQP or logistic regularization. It combines two useful facts:

- The objective is exact and continuous in every coordinate-pair line that preserves mass, so it is very effective to make the strongest possible local improvement along all available mass-preserving segment directions. This can discover reductions missed by step-size heuristic global directions.
- When a point is a local minimum with respect to all one-coordinate-pair linear alternatives, basin hopping using exact zero-sum block moves can move mass around a wide neighborhood without violating the cycle-box and total-intervation constraints, and can move to another basin whose pairwise line descent reaches lower points.
