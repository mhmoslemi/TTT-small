For n=63, D has 63 entries. Computing D[j] for each j requires summing over up to 2*min(j+1, n-j) lags. Total work is O(n²) ≈ 4000.

3. Compute D (O(n²))
4. Best pair is (argmin D, argmax D) (O(n))
5. 1D line search for that pair (O(n) per evaluation, ~30 evaluations = O(n))

Total per iteration: O(n²) dominated by step 1 and 3.

∂r(k)/∂h[j]:
- h[j] appears as h[n] when n=j, and j must be in [k, n-1]:
  contributes (1 - h[j-k]) if j ≥ k
- h[j] appears in (1-h[n-k]) when n-k=j, i.e., n=j+k, and j+k must be in [k, n-1]:
  contributes -h[j+k] if j+k ≤ n-1, i.e., j ≤ n-1-k
