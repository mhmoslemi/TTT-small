1.1 Central idea

The optimization problem is a finite-dimensional constrained nonconvex minimax problem:

- objective: the maximum over all `2*n-1` finite-support correlation entries,
- feasible set: a bounded `L1`-type slice: `0 <= h_i <= 1` and `sum(h_i)=n/2`.

The supplied or default parent construction is already very good: it only has to be moved downward by a few `1e-4` or less. The previous natural strategies tend to spend most iterations on one of two things:

- a smoothed surrogate for the maximum, whose exact objective can waste effort when it no longer matches the actual active lag;
- random local coordinate changes or latent gradients that do not directly produce exact global improvement along a feasible path.

This plan uses an entirely different primary mechanism: **exact feasible transport-line descent**. It repeatedly chooses feasible directions that move mass while preserving the integral constraint, and for each chosen direction it performs a small but structured one-dimensional search on the *exact discrete overlap objective*, not a surrogate, not a mere gradient step, and not an optimizer call that separates the objective from feasibility. Every intermediate point on a chosen line is exactly result-checked with the official metric after joint feasibility repair. This gives a directly goal-adjustable local message/discovery operator: whenever a selected line contains any feasible vector with smaller C₅ among the explored candidates, the algorithm can find and accept it.

The line directions are not arbitrary unconstrained directions. They are low-order feasible mass-transport directions:

1. **Coordinate-pair transport** `_delta_ij`: move a small amount from coordinate i to coordinate j, or vice versa, for a family of pairs selected by active-correlation activity.
2. **Balanced random transport** directions: generated as zero-sum noise, which can move mass through many coordinates at once.
3. **Quasi-uniform carousel transport** directions: smooth sinusoidal or plateau-like feasible directions, useful for smoothing or boundary-redistribution improvements a single pair direction cannot capture.
