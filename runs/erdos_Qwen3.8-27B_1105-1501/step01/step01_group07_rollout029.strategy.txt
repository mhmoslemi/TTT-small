1. Approach and rationale

The supplied parent is already close to the numerical target, and the primary obstacle is no longer a simple lack of small moves. The previous parent program mostly performs (i) a soft-max gradient walk and (ii) random one/two-coordinate moves. In the finite-sample objective

C₅(h) = max_k np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")[k] * dx,

the maximum is selected by many interacting lag terms. A random coordinate move or a soft-max gradient step changes every subtangent score including inactive near-max lags without explicitly satisfying the coupled constraint “do not make any other lag exceed the current maximum.” Because the objective is a maximum of bilinear functions over the box plus a conservation sum constraint, local progress at a structured parent often requires a coordinated update that reduces the currently active overlap constraints while not allowing a near-active lag to break through.

The proposed primary method is materially different because it solves, at each feasible center, a trust-region linear programming surrogate of the full finite maximization. Let h* be the current feasible vector and dx=2/n. For every finite lag k in 0, …, 2n-2, use the evaluator’s exact local score vector s_k(h*) and exact local gradients J_{k,j}(h*) so that

s_k(h*+z) ≈ s_k(h*) + Σ_j J_{k,j}(h*) z_j.

Then for a small candidate y=h*+z, define a scalar y-score surrogate M satisfying all local constraints

s_k(h*) + Σ_j J_{k,j}(h*) ψ_j ≤ M    for all k,

    minimize M

                  Σ_j(h_j+ψ_j)=n/2,
                  handler? same variables maybe M free in [0,1].

It replaces single-coordinate trial-and-error with a linear program whose constraints are exactly the finite lag constraints used by the evaluator. Acceptance is then decided by the original evaluator, never by the LP surrogate.
