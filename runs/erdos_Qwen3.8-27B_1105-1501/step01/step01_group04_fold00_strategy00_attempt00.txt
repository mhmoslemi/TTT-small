1. Approach and rationale

The central idea is to treat the problem as a non-smooth linearized minimax problem and repeatedly take exact, feasible, verified descent steps away from the current maximum-overlap constraints. The previous smoothed projected-gradient search retains useful global-search qualities, but it optimizes a surrogate and its step choices are not directly derived from the real active maximum constraints. A tiny improvement from roughly 0.3811 or 0.38085 to near or below 0.3808 is most likely a local minmax-improving direction that the previous surrogate step misses because the active overlaps are not explicitly coordinated.

The primary algorithm is therefore:

1. Validate and repair the supplied `initial_h_values` parent. Use it as a guaranteed fallback.
2. Repeatedly compute all exact overlap coefficients from the authoritative evaluator formula.
3. Select the active or near-active overlapped lags.
4. Solve a small linear program whose variables are a feasible tangent step in the h-cube and a scalar estimated common relative decrease. The LP tries to make several active lag gradients simultaneously negative while preserving the sample sum exactly.
5. Convert the LP step into a feasible exact-vector update by bounded line search and evaluators of the original max-correlation objective.
6. Keep improvement according to the original exact `np.correlate` objective, so no surrogate can cause a worse final answer.
7. Use secondary diverse searches only as trickle-through exploration while always retaining the best valid candidate found so far.

This addresses the bottleneck directly: in a near-optimal sequence many unfortunate shifts have almost identical maximum overlap. Reducing just the current largest lag creates a new maximum immediately. The LP explicitly creates a direction reducing a coordinated set of nearly active lags.

Important limitations and justified facts:

- The exact objective is piecewise differentiable and nonconvex. The bilinear product `h_i(1 - h_j)` has an indefinite quadratic component. A global convex solver cannot be used on the full problem. The LP direction is only a local linearized subproblem, not a proof of global optimality.
- Candidates are accepted only after exact evaluation with `np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full") * dx`. Hence every final bound returned is authoritative.
- If no improvement to target is found in the budget phase, the returned parent remains valid and has the prior bound. This gives fail-safe ledger behavior.
- Smaller sample counts are preferred but only when the exact bound is not materially worse. Best-candidate ranking is therefore secondary: first lower C5; on near equality, smaller `n_points`.
