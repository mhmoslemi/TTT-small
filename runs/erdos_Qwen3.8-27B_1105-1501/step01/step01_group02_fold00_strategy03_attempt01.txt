1.1 Central objective and central idea

The task is to minimize the exact finite-window max-overlap

C5(h) = max_{r=0,...,2n-2} dx * [np.correlate(h, 1-h, "full")[r]],

where dx = 2/n and h satisfies 0 <= h_i <= 1 and sum_i h_i = n/2. Any proposal that changes the objective, discards lag directions from "full", uses circular correlation, or accepts a surrogate value as the returned bound would be scientifically and procedurally invalid. Therefore the returned bound must always be recomputed using this exact evaluator.

The proposed method is materially different from a temperature/smooth-max gradient sweep followed by coordinate or pair moves. Its primary search engine is a nonsmooth steepest-descent mechanism based on an exact active-set linear program in the l1 metric. At each step:

1. The exact correlation vector is computed.
2. The active or near-active lags that currently attaining or nearly attaining the maximum are identified.
3. For those constraints, the first-order directional derivative is linearized.
4. A linear program chooses a constraint-sum-preserving direction that minimizes the maximum first-order derivative over the selected active set and the Hessian-free l1-normalization ball.
5. The chosen direction is tested by an exact one-dimensional line search using the original evaluator, never by the linearized objective alone.
6. A candidate is accepted only if the exact evaluator gives a smaller feasible value.

This is different from previous-style approaches because it does not use smooth-max averaging and naive projected-gradient or coordinate moves as the main search engine. It directly attacks the nonsmooth max at the maximum by seeking a feasible direction that simultaneously reduces the currently binding and nearly binding lags. Nonsmooth objectives commonly have directions that reduce the maximum even when a single smoothed gradient direction is poor or blocked.

Justified facts:

- The exact evaluator is finite and includes all 2n-1 linear-correlation lags from np.correlate(..., mode="full").

- A direction vector with zero sum changes coordinates under a pair while maintaining the equality constraint algebraically. - The linear program is a convex, linear subproblem over the normalized direction. Its constraints match the linearized first-order model of the selected active lags.
- The method never accepts a candidate by solving the linearized problem; every rigorous accept decision uses the original C5 evaluator.

Primary heuristic hypotheses, explicitly not guaranteed:

- The chosen top active subset approximates the effect of the truly infinite active-or-generation near-optimal active set.
- The local linearized steepest descent speed is recoverable by shrinking active probabilities and l1 step sizes for micro-overpayments in fixed-curvature nonsurface regimes.
