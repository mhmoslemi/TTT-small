        h[i] = orig_i + a2
        h[j] = orig_j - a2
        s2 = score_exact(h)
        if s1 < s2:
            hi2 = a2
            a2 = a1
            a1 = lo2 + (1.0 - gr) * (hi2 - lo2)
        else:
            lo2 = a1
            a1 = a2
            a2 = lo2 + gr * (hi2 - lo2)
    
    best_t = (lo2 + hi2) * 0.5
    h[i] = orig_i + best_t
    h[j] = orig_j - best_t
    return score_exact(h)

The previous code's main issue was probably:
1. Not enough time spent on local optimization (the random pair search is inefficient)
2. Not trying enough different global starting points

My new strategy:
1. **Phase 1 (30% time)**: Intensive systematic pair optimization at the initial resolution
   - Shell-based ordering
   - Full convergence (no improvement for a full shell cycle)
   - This should extract all the local improvement available

2. **Phase 2 (30% time)**: Global exploration
   - Generate diverse starting points using Fourier parametrization
   - For each, do a short local optimization (few passes of pair descent)
   - Keep the best

3. **Phase 3 (20% time)**: Resample to higher resolution and optimize
   - Take the best solution from phases 1-2
   - Upsample to 2x resolution
   - Do systematic pair optimization at the new resolution

4. **Phase 4 (20% time)**: Final refinement
   - Alternating between pair optimization and small perturbations
   - Try to squeeze out the last bit of improvement

Let me think about the Fourier parametrization for global exploration:

h(x_i) = sigmoid(c + Σ_{k=1}^{K} (a_k cos(2πkx_i/2) + b_k sin(2πkx_i/2)))

where x_i = 2*i/n are the grid points.

After computing h, apply make_feasible to ensure the integral constraint.

For K=5, we have 11 parameters (c + 5 a's + 5 b's). We can use:
- Random sampling (Latin hypercube)
- Differential evolution
- CEM (Cross-Entropy Method)

Let me use a simple approach: generate many random Fourier patterns, project to feasible set, evaluate, and keep the best few. Then do local optimization from the best ones.

Actually, I realize there's a subtlety. The sigmoid parametrization might not be flexible enough to represent the optimal solution. The optimal solution might have sharp transitions (like a step function). Let me use a higher temperature sigmoid or a different activation.

Alternative: use a "soft step" function:
h(x) = clip(0.5 + Σ (a_k cos(...) + b_k sin(...)), 0, 1)

This is more flexible because it can represent sharp transitions when the Fourier coefficients are large.
