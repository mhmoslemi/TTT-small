1. Approach and rationale

The goal is to return a feasible discrete vector `h` of length `n` with
`0 <= h_i <= 1` and `sum_i h_i = n/2`, minimizing the exact evaluator
objective

`C5(h) = (2/n) * max_k np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")[k]`.

The method proposed here is materially different from simple smoothing or a
single high-dimensional NLP/epigraph solve. It is a two-part local-global
method built on:

1. exact finite-support correlation objectives and gradients, including both
   lag directions;
2. convex local linear mini-max directions obtained by solving a bounded linear
   program on the first-order Taylor model of all or the most important lag
   constraints;
3. exact acceptance: every LP direction is tested with the original,
   nonsmooth correlation objective by a one-dimensional line search;
4. mass-preserving pair transfers, which are exact continuous local search
   directions on the feasible manifold and are especially efficient when `h`
   is nearly binary or nearly a binary sequence with one half-entry;
5. a deterministic diversity/pool search and an optional resolution-transfer
   stage.

This design addresses the bottleneck in nonconvex min-max overlap optimization.

- A direct high-dimensional SLSQP solve on the exact max or on smoothed max
  can become unstable because the active set changes abruptly.
  The first-order LP directions are explicit and give a fallback secant
  direction for each active constraint.
- The two constraints `h_i in [0,1]` and `sum h_i = n/2` are coupled. They are
  handled jointly by construction: line directions keep mass exactly and stay in
  bounds, and all other candidates are projected through a dedicated bounded
  half-mass simplex projection.
- The LP is a relaxation/solvent: a convex linear programming subproblem that
  approximates `max_k C_k(h)` by first-order Taylor expansion around the
  current point. It can exploit small n, especially the supplied `n=45`
  (89 lag constraints and 45 `d` variables + a scalar epigraph variable).
- Pair transfers guarantee exact preservation of the mass and bounds during a
  move by transferring mass from one coordinate to another. They are likely to
  find small improvements missed by the linearized step because the true
  bilinear dependence is evaluated exactly along the move.

No global optimality is claimed. All used LP/SOCP-like constraints are purely
local approximations and candidates are judged only by the exact discrete
evaluator.

The search keeps a validated best candidate at all times. The parent
`initial_h_values`, if supplied, is always validated and retained as a fallback.
