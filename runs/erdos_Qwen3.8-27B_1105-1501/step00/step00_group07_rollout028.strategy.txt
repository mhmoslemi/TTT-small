1. Approach and rationale

    1.1 Central objective

    The task is to return a finite vector h with entries in [0,1], discrete integral exactly one, i.e. sum(h) = n_points/2, whose exact evaluator score

        C5 = max(np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")) * (2.0 / n_points)

No valid reduction to a simpler circular autocorrelation, ordinary convolution, or complement-overlap identity is being used; the method repeatedly computes the same objective as the authoritative metric.

    1.2 Proposed primary method

    The proposed method is not another essentially random single pairwise perturbation/simulated annealing search. Its primary descent mechanism is a deterministic sweep over unordered coordinate-pair channels. For every unordered pair (a,b), it optimizes the one-dimensional path

        h_a(t) = h[a] + t
        h_b(t) = h[b] - t
        h_i(t) = h[i] for i not a,b

    over the whole feasible interval for both signs of t. This path preserves the sum exactly and remains inside the box when t lies in its derived interval. Each candidate t is re-evaluated by the exact evaluator formula, and a move is accepted only when its repaired, exact score improves the current vector. After repeated pair-channel descent, a secondary linearized minimax-gradient step is attempted as a local all-coordinate descent direction; it is also accepted only after projection to exact feasibility and exact scoring. This creates two complementary descent channels that are absent from a simple single random stationary-move search:

    - exhaustive pair-transfer optimization, which directly explores the strongest two-dimensional mass-transfer directions that are feasible within the current state, and
    - an active raw-gradient linear minimax direction, which can move many coordinates simultaneously while the strongest active correlations are linearized and mass is removed only after exact verification.

    These methods are followed by deterministic transformations to alternative lengths, rank/model perturbations, and seeded pair-walk perturbations to escape local coordinate-pair local minima. Every final returned state is valid and scored with the exact supplied metric. There is no claim of global optimality; accepted improvements are exact local improvements.
