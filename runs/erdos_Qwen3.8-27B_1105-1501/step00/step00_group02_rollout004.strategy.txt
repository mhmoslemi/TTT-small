1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The supplied parent is a finite vector `h` of length `n_ref` (normally 47) that is already near a disciplined local optimum. The task is nonconvex and a general full-dimensional move set can spend a lot of time repairing feasibility or wandering through flat plateau regions caused by the maximum over many lags. Instead of treating all `n` coordinates independently, the proposed program optimizes a small number of smooth, zero-mean perturbation modes anchored at the parent construction. This is a materially different route from pair-coordinate transport, full-coordinate simulated annealing, binary word reordering, or full-dimensional line polishing: the search lives in a low-dimensional function class `h_parent + B z`, where columns of `B` are centered modes, and every proposed raw point is converted to a perfectly feasible vector by an exact projection mechanism. The exact original evaluator is always used for acceptance.

The mode family is built algorithmically from grid indices (discrete Fourier, symmetric cosine, or wavelet-like centered functions), not copied from the parent table. Starting at `z = 0` reproduces the parent on its original grid, so the method has a built-in fallback and can seek small smooth changes that reduce the maximum overlap. A global continuous allocator (bounded differential evolution) specialists in the low-dimensional space, and auxiliary deterministic/randomized line search methods polish around promising coefficients. Optional resolution transfers can test whether the same smooth family sampled on a slightly finer grid obtains a smaller exact discrete value; this decision is always made by the exact evaluator, not by geometric intuition.

1.2 Why this may improve the parent

- The exact objective is the maximum over `2n-1` truncated bilinear terms. A tiny increase in coordinates appearing in the currently active lag penalizes that lag directly and may also increase symmetric neighboring lags. Low-dimensional modes are global and smooth, able to redistribute small wins or losses across many microregions rather than attempting cornering in independent sparse coordinate swaps.
- A near-optimal construction should often have multiple near-tied active lags. A few carefully tuned smooth modes can equalize and lower such coupled modes more effectively than many sparse coordinate transfers.
- Parent grid `n=47` discretization error may prevent a slight lowering even if the continuous optimum is lower. Simulating smooth constructions at a slightly larger slice resolution can effectively remove this discretization “tooth” while still producing a legitimate exact return for that new length.
