1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

Implement a differentiable feasible search inside the original discrete constraint set by representing every candidate as a logistic vector whose sum is forced by solving one scalar monotone equation. Let raw unconstrained variables be a real vector of length n-1. They are completed to an n-vector, then a single common shift is chosen so that the sum of sigmoid entries is exactly the required mass. This gives an exact joint mechanism for the two hard constraints: the sigmoid bounds ensure 0 <= h_i < 1, and the scalar root ensures sum_i h_i = n/2 up to the chosen floating tolerance. On that mapped manifold, replace the original nonsmooth minimax objective by a log-sum-exp smoothed version and run gradient-based local optimization with randomized raw perturbations. A high-resolution dyadic snap and an exact pairwise lattice polish produce a final returned vector that satisfies the box and sum constraints to floating arithmetic tolerance and is judged by the untouched np.correlate objective.

1.2 Bottleneck addressed

The parent construction is already close to a local optimum. Ordinary random or Euclidean steps in raw h are awkward because a change in h can break either 0 <= h_i <= 1 or sum(h)=n/2, and repairs tend to move distance away from the current objective. Pairwise symmetric transfer lines avoid the mass disturbance but move only along one-dimensional lines. This plan instead makes the box and mass constraints intrinsic to the unconstrained variables, then uses gradient information from a smooth majorant of the minimax objective to move through many coupled h coordinates at once. It differs substantively from earlier pair transport and low-dimensional Fourier-mode plans: the decision variables are unconstrained logistic coordinates on the full feasible interior, the feasibility application is one monotone scalar shift, and the local model is an analytically differentiated log-sum-exp surrogate.

1.3 Why it could improve the parent

The objective is the maximum of many truncated bilinear products h_i(1-h_j). Improving the optimum usually requires balancing several active overlapping diagonals. A logit map can change all h coordinates, including allocating mass between under-loaded and over-loaded positions, by smooth gradient or L-BFGS steps on a one-inequality-smoothed surrogate. Starting multiple perturbations around the supplied parent, the optimizer can discover new near-tied active-lag equilibria. Final snap plus exact pairwise transfer regains precision and respects strict arithmetic constraints.

1.4 Honest limitations

This is a nonlinear, nonconvex, internal heuristic. It does not guarantee the global discrete optimum. The logistic representation cannot represent h_i exactly 0 or 1 without an infinity norm; the dyadic snap afterward can produce exact endpoint multiples including 0 and 1, but sometimes final postprocessing changes the raw optimizer's candidate. A smooth majorant can converge to an artificial smoothed minimum. The exact returned C5 value is always obtained by the authorizing evaluator; no claim of global optimality is intended.
