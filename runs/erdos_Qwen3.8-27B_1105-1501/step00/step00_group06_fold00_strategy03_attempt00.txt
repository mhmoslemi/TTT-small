1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The returned solution must be judged by the exact finite-support linear-correlation evaluator

    C(h) = dx * max_t np.correlate(h, 1 - h, "full")[t],
    dx = 2 / n,

with h having length n, entries in [0, 1], and mass sum(h) = n/2. The task is therefore a nonsmooth minimax finite-dimensional problem over the box-mass polytope. Because products h_i h_j occur in every autocorrelation term, no convex program gives the exact upper bound without either a nonconvex formulation or a relaxation that does not certify the final value. The proposed method keeps the original evaluator as the only acceptance test and attacks the problem in three layers:

1. Global or diffuse stochastic exploration over several feasible resolutions, including transfers of the supplied parent between resolutions, but using the exact evaluator for every candidate.
2. A smoothing-active-gradient phase. A log-sum-exp smoothing of all exact correlation values is used repeatedly to obtain tangent zero-sum descent directions. The direction itself is never accepted by its smoothing value; the algorithm performs exact one-dimensional line searches along the direction using the original max-correlation objective.
3. A strict pair-transfer/pair-rotation polish on the best working point. Move directions of the form e_i - e_j preserve mass exactly and generate small-scale exact feasible moves across bounds. If sufficiently much time remains after mandated polishing, all pair directions are tried once, otherwise a deterministic randomized subset is tried.

The method is deliberately not a single one-dimensional random line walker and is not a linearized epigraph LP method. Its differentiating structure is a multi-resolution exact scoring population and then smoothing-active log-sum directions generated by consistent finite differences of a smooth surrogate built directly from the official evaluator's correlation array. The smoothing only proposes directions; no smoothing value is used to update or defend the bound.

1.2 Why this can improve the parent

A supplied parent at n = 70 is likely very close to a local optimum. To reduce such a point from approximately 0.38085 to the target 0.3808, the required movement is extremely small and often involves simultaneous changes in the active or nearly active lags. Several near-worst constraints usually tie or nearly tie. A log-sum-exp gradient does not move along one hard-coded active lag; it forms a weighted sum of all constraints, with weights controlled by the smoothing parameter. Low, medium, and high smoothing give directions that either smooth over many constraints or focus on the current peak. The exact line search then tests the true nonsmooth penalty over a feasible segment. Pair rotations are useful at the boundary of 0 and 1, because exact feasible moves then are simple coordinate transfers and the exact allowable interval is finite and easy to compute. The population phase builds starting points not reachable by purely local parent-walking and prevents collapse onto the supplied support.

1.3 Main known facts and hypotheses

- Fact: The official objective is the maximum over all 2n-1 linear-correlation output entries multiplied by 2/n. No circularity or periodic identification is used. The algorithm therefore evaluates this exact expression for every accepted candidate.
- Fact: The smoothing surrogate is not equivalent to the exact best value. It is only a nonconvex objective defining candidate directions.
- Fact: Exact finite lines h + lambda d with sum(d)=0 preserve the mass exactly before correction, and the coordinate constraints confine lambda to a computable interval. The final feasible point in the line search is still post-processed with an exact-sum feasibility helper and then rescored.
- Hypothesis: Combining population diversity with smoothed active-set directions and mass-preserving pair rotations can move a tight parent across a local saddle to a better feasible value within 1100 seconds on one CPU at small n. This is not guaranteed.
- Limitation: There is no claim of global optimality. The bit-difference required, about 0.00005, may not be attainable from this stochastic family within the budget. Therefore the algorithm always returns the best feasible candidate found and at least the reconstruction safely derived from the supplied parent.
