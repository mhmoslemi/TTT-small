1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central method

The proposed search is an exact-metric, feasibility-preserving local/vector descent method with multi-start resolution transfers and active-constraint directional line search. It is materially different from a globally smoothed NLP/SLSQP or convex-relaxation strategy. The method keeps every move in the feasible tangent space of the exact constraints:

- bounds \(0\le h_i\le 1\),
- exact sum \(\sum_i h_i=n/2\),

and it measures every proposed point with the official task evaluator

\[
C_5(h)=\frac{2}{n}\max_{r=0,\ldots,2n-2}\operatorname{np.correlate}(h,1-h,\text{full})_r .
\]

The optimization never optimizes circular correlation, never uses an autocorrelation identity that replaces boundary terms, and never reliably optimizes only a smooth surrogate for the final decision. Surrogate information is used only to move proposals; all acceptance is judged by the exact evaluator after the candidate is restored to feasibility.

The main algorithmic objects are:

1. A zero-sum feasible manifold. A perturbation vector \(d\) satisfies, to numerical tolerance,
   \[
   \sum_i d_i=0.
   \]
   Then \(h+\lambda d\) has the same sum as \(h\). Bounds are enforced through a scalar line interval
   \[
   0\le \lambda\le \lambda_{\max}(h,d)
   \]
   rather than by a blind post-hoc clip-and-rescale step after every tiny move.

2. An exact one-dimensional objective formula. For fixed \(h,d\), define the raw full-correlation arrays
   \[
   A_r=\sigma(h,1-h)_r,
   \quad
   B_r=\sigma(d,1-h)_r-\sigma(h,d)_r,
   \quad
   Q_r=-\sigma(d,d)_r,
   \]
   using the same \(\sigma=\) self-/\(\texttt{np.correlate}\)-\ldots naming convention for all three arrays and the same lag dimension prefix. Then the exact scaled objective of the line point \(h+\lambda d\) is
   \[
   F_h^d(\lambda)=\frac{2}{n}\max_r\left(A_r+\lambda B_r+\lambda^2Q_r\right).
   \]
   This follows from bilinearity of the correlation vector and does not require circular convolution. It allows many line samples to be evaluated with pure vectorized arithmetic.

3. Multiple active-constraint descent direction generators. At a current feasible \(h\), we construct plausible negative-gradient directions from the exact correlation objective and its derivative, then project them to the zero-sum tangent, clip or bound through the line interval, and perform exact line samples. Top-lag weighted-gradient directions, finite-difference-gradient variants, equal-zero-sum effective-lag directions, variably averaged top-lag gradient directions, and random tangent directions are all available.

4. Basin-changing transformations and resolution transfers. The initial construction is transformed under mirror-complement, symmetric mirror average, small random feasible perturbations, thresholdlike topology changes, and rescaling to another \(n\). Resolution transfer is a genuinely different representation test because the lag length changes and orthogon况 may change even when exact sum constraints are maintained.

5. Best-candidate safety discipline. Global acceptance occurs only after the candidate has been enforced into valid-size-feasibility with a joint shift/coordinate repair operation. The parent, which is retained as a feasible construction, is the only non-improvement fallback. The output is the exact evaluator recomputed from the retained candidate.

The requested change is numerically tiny: from about \(0.38085\) toward \(0.3808\). - It does not improve distorted cyclic or boundary-replaced objectives.
- Every proposed line interval is exact; no proposal can violate \(0\le h_i\le1\) because directions defining interval ends are sorted exactily.
- It can increase the objective temporarily (in a limited controlled basin-change stream) while never moving global best lower.
