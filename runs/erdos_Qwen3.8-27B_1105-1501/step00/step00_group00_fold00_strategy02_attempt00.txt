1. Approach and rationale

Primary method. The search treats the discrete problem as a finite nonconvex minimax optimization over  
`h ∈ R^n`, with feasible set  
`F_n = {0 ≤ h_i ≤ 1, sum(h_i) = n/2}`, and exact finite-support evaluator  
`C5(h) = dx * max_k np.correlate(h,1-h,mode='full')`, `dx = 2/n`. The method couples:

- exact evaluator scoring after every proposal;
- robust joint feasibility projection using a scalar shift and box clipping;
- smooth-max gradient descent through SLSQP or projected rapid local updates;
- active-set epigraph local refinements over a small set of active correlation lags;
- deterministic feasible perturbations, including resampled parent-like constructions;
- bounded line searches along feasible coordinate-pair perturbations, evaluated using the exact evaluator.

The nonconvex product constraints are not replaced by a convex solver. The problem is treated as local, heuristic, non-global optimization, with no claim of global optimality. This plan is independent of earlier unverified plans and defines all math, formulas, feasibility repair, indexing, scheduling, and robust handling.

Why it is expected to improve. To reduce all correlation maxima, one must push zero-lag values toward binary structure while reducing shifted overlaps. Gradient methods using overlapping-detected differentiable substitutes provide effective descent directions, exact finite-dimensional correspondence, projection, and exact objective san来处理. However, the local method may stand at stationary points.
