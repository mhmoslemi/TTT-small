1. Approach and rationale

The supplied construction is already very close to the published upper bound, so the final target is a tiny refinement. The main engineering bottleneck is not smoothing the maximum: the construction lives on a small high-dimensional feasible polytope with 0<=h_i<=1 and the exact finite-support maximum of 2n-1 correlation outputs. Small unprojected infeasible moves are dangerous, but blindly projecting/clipping leads to repeated recomputation of an infeasible vector and loss of the careful balance already present in the parent. 

The proposed method is a deterministic plus stochastic pair-direction descent in the feasible manifold. A pair change
h_i <- h_i + d, h_j <- h_j - d
preserves the equality mass exactly and stays in the joint box for a signed 1D interval of d. Along every such line, each overlap functional is completely described by the current objective vector, a directional slope obtained from the exact finite-support derivatives, and a very simple bilinear curvature pattern. This gives a 1D polynomial model—still nonconvex because of the zero-lag h_k(1-h_k) terms—but it is an exact description of the local pair-restricted overlap objective (up to the same manual correlation convention as the evaluator). The method uses this exact model to propose a scarcity of highly promising feasible pair moves, then accepts or accepts warm-restart candidates using the actual task evaluator.

This is materially different from the prior smoothing, active-epigraph, or diagonal projection proposals: there is no scalar smoothing of the max and no epigraph solve using a relaxation of all pair constraints. The search geometry is based on mass-preserving two-coordinate lines that are automatically feasible and on a closed-form polynomial model of exactly the local neighborhood constrained to that line. It is a carefully constrained exact-manifold nonlinear neighborhood search, rather than a generic unconstrained smooth descent followed by projection.

Important honest limitations:
- The global problem is nonconvex. No global optimality, nor optimality of pair local search, is claimed.
- A pair-move line is not globally convex: zero-lag overlap terms h_i(1-h_i), h_j(1-h_j) are concave in the line parameter. Thus the line optimizer will be a practical global 1D maximin optimizer based on full interval sampling and local refinement, not a golden-section convex proof.
- The returned bound is the newly evaluated exact C5 value of an individually reconstructed candidate h, never an auxiliary smoothing or epigraph value.
- If a parent exists, it is always retained as a valid fallback and retained as the incumbent unless an exact evaluator says otherwise.
