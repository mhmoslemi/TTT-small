1. Approach and rationale

The central idea is to attack the minimax problem with a sequence-aware trust-region sequence of linearized epigraph programs. For each current feasible vector h, the exact score is the maximum of the 2n−1 finite-support quantities induced by the official correlation formula. Around h, every one of those quantities has an explicit gradient with respect to h. Therefore a small trust-region subproblem is a linear program whose epigraph variable t estimates the maximum score of a proposed vector h'. Because the underlying objective is bilinear/nonconvex, the linear model is not globally valid; however each proposal is accepted only if the exact task objective, recomputed with the official evaluator, is strictly darker. The small linear LP is excellent for moving in promising directions, while the exact evaluation and trust-region backtracking make safety absolute: a bad proposal is simply rejected, and the previously validated vector is retained.

The method contributes useful diversity beyond one continuity descent on the supplied parent:
- it acts on all full-correlation lags, not only two-index lines,
- it maintains the total sum and box constraints exactly enough through a convex feasibility repair,
- it combines two different local methods: an LP-like trust-region model and a smooth-max gradient epoch,
- it seeds that local machinery from the parent and from algorithmically transformed variants lying in related discretization lengths,
- it attempts exact binary or half-integer post-polishes so that the exact sum feasibility is at least as credible as with floating repair.

This is materially different from generic pair-line or pair-only exhaustive probing. The core update is a small sized LP reasoning on the full vector and all lags, not coordinate pairs. The use of the exact evaluator after every candidate remains the formal acceptance mechanism. The method is a search heuristic; it does not claim global optimality. The hypothesis is that the nonconvex objective in the neighborhood of an already high-quality parent can be improved by a direction found from linearizing its active overlap constraints. Justified facts are the exact difference and gradient formulas below; the actual convergence to a particular score is empirical.
