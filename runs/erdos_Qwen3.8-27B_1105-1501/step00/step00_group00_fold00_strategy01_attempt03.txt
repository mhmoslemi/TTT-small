1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The proposal combines two search mechanisms that are both driven directly by the exact finite-support evaluator, not by a circular approximation or a commonly assumed autocorrelation surrogate:

- a bit-packed discrete phase for feasible even-length binary vectors `h`, with exactly half the entries equal to `1`, using the bitwise form of the evaluator;
- a continuous phase based on a linearized active-overlap subproblem over the feasible hyperplane `sum(h)=n/2` together with safe, bounded coordinate moves. Every step produced by the linearized subproblem is immediately scored with the original objective.

The discrete phase exploits one important structural special case: when `h` is binary and `n` is even, every finite overlap term in `np.correlate(h, 1-h, "full")` is a nonnegative integer count. For a fixed lag `p`, the evaluator divided by `dx = 2/n` is the number of ordered pairs `(i, i+p)` satisfying the relevant directed mismatch. This allows an extremely fast exact evaluation and very rapid generation of many valid candidate starting points. The continuous probability is that some of these point designs, possibly after small fractional perturbation in the continuous phase, can improve on the supplied parent.

The continuous phase avoids formulating or pretending that the full problem is convex. For a fixed stored vector `h`, only the active overlap constraints near the current maximum are used. Around the current point, the objective is locally nonsmooth, constrained but with bounds and a linear normalizer. If the exact evaluator is finite and robust, random/reactive coordinate tries should find locally feasible directions that do not increase the active overlap. Because each exact step is verified with the original objective before saving, even if the model is inaccurate, the best vector is solely based on real objective evaluations. Therefore no incorrect upper bound is produced.

1.2 Why this is materially different

The expected representative approaches are smooth-max or nonconvex epigraph solvers. This plan shifts the principal work:
