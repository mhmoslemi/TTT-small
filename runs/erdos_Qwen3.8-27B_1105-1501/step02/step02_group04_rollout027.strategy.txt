1.1 Central idea

The previous program primarily uses pairwise coordinate transfers: change two coordinates, keep all others fixed, and solve/line-search for the best value of that one pair. This only explores a very restricted set of directions. A better local direction often requires moving many coordinates simultaneously, reducing several near-tie maximal lags at once.

The main method below is therefore a nonconvex active-lag linearized descent method:

1. At the current h, identify the upper band of lags whose corr values are close to the current maximum.
2. Use the exact bilinear objective to build the exact first-order Jacobian of only these active or near-active lags.
3. Solve a small linear program to find a feasible simultaneous direction d, with sum(d)=0 and 0 allowed, and maximize a linearized decrease theta of the active-lag values.
4. Accept the move only after evaluating the original exact evaluator along the full line h + alpha*d; theta is only safest for finding a promising direction, not for acceptance.
5. Add periodic full coordinate pairwise line-polishing and stochastic kicks as safe local search. The pairwise moves are exact convex one-dimensional subproblems, but they are only a fallback and a final polisher, not the primary mechanism being tested.

The important difference from the previous plan is that the primary step direction is chosen from an LP over many simultaneous coordinates that affect the upper edge of the correlation vector. The previous plan searched one pair at a time, so it may stagnate whenever any single pair transfer increases the maximum because several lags need to be lowered together.

This is not guaranteed to find a global optimum. J(h) is bilinear and thus nonconvex. All improvements are accepted only by exact re-evaluation of the original np.correlate formulation.
