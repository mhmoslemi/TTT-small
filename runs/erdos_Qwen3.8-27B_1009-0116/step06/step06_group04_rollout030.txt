Let me think about the L-BFGS-B approach more carefully. The issue is that L-BFGS-B doesn't handle equality constraints directly. I'll use a penalty:

penalty = lambda_val * (sum(x) - n/2)^2

With lambda_val large enough (say 1000-10000), this effectively enforces the constraint while keeping the problem unconstrained (only bounds).

One issue: with a large penalty, the Hessian approximation in L-BFGS-B might be poorly conditioned. I'll use a moderate penalty and increase it over iterations.

Actually, a better approach: parameterize one variable as a function of the others. Let x[n-1] = n/2 - sum(x[0:n-1]). Then I only optimize over n-1 variables with bounds, and the last variable is determined. But this last variable might go out of [0,1] bounds, making it tricky.

Actually, the simplest approach that works well in practice: use L-BFGS-B with a large penalty and then repair. The penalty keeps the sum close to n/2, and the repair fixes any tiny residual.

def _repair(h, n):
