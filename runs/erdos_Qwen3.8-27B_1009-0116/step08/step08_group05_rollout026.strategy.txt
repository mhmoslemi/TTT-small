1. Approach and Rationale
The Erdős minimum overlap problem requires finding a step function h that minimizes the maximum overlap with its complement when shifted. This is an instance of the minimization of the maximum autocorrelation value, which is a challenging optimization problem due to its non-convexity. Our approach combines gradient-based optimization (to refine structure) with randomized initialization and patch swapping (to explore diverse solutions) and evolutionary strategies (to enhance diversity and prevent local minima). It uses explicit gradient information for local refinement but focuses the search on promising regions identified by randomized exploration. Unlike previous methods, we:

- Use a gradient-aware ranking of shifts to prioritize relevant constraints
- Combine continuous optimization with binary-like flipping at key points
- Implement safety checks to maintain the integral constraint
- Use a multi-stage optimization that balances between global exploration and local refinement

The approach is designed to escape local minima by: 
  - Using multiple independent initializations
  - Swapping patches between candidate solutions
  - Applying intelligent gradient projection
  - Maintaining a memory of promising regions

2. Mathematical Specification
Variables:
  - h: n-dimensional vector representing the step function samples
  - Parameters: 
      n = number of samples (initially 560)
      score(h) = max_{k} ∫ h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx, computed via the discrete convolution
      0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1 for all i
      Σh[i] = n/2 exactly

Operators:
  - Score(complete(h), k): discrete overlap at shift k
  - TopK(shifts): k's with top overlap scores
  - BlockGradient(shift, h): gradient w.r.t h that penalizes high overlap
  - FlipBlock(h, size, position): random flip of contiguous block
  - Repair(h): enforces integral constraint and bounds [0,1]

Constraints Enforcement:
  - The repair function uses iterative adjustment to meet Σh[i] = n/2
  - All flips and perturbations have their integral adjusted
  - Objective computation always includes max over all 2*n-1 shifts
