1. Approach and rationale
We address the Erdős minimum overlap problem by improving upon the given initial construction using a multi-objective optimization strategy. The central idea is to simultaneously optimize the step function while considering multiple lags that contribute to the maximum overlap. This differs from the previous approach that primarily focused on local refinement after initial global exploration. 

The key bottlenecks in the existing solution include:
- Over-reliance on gradient-based methods that may get trapped in local optima
- Limited exploration of functions with periodic or structured patterns
- Insufficient consideration of functions with multiple valleys and peaks

We introduce a multimodal optimization strategy that combines global exploration using adaptive repair mechanisms with local refinement using sensitivity-weighted perturbations. This approach should better balance the trade-off between function smoothness and the avoidance of large local maxima in the overlap function.

2. Mathematical specification
Our formulation maintains the original problem: minimize max_k ∫h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx subject to sum_i h_i = n/2 and 0≤h_i≤1. We discretize with samples at equiprobable points x_i = i*dx, dx=2/n.

For optimization, we define:
- P(u) = probability mass at u (discretized uniform measure)
- Energy functional E(h) = max_k[min(|∫h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx - C|, 1)] for C∈ℝ
- Repair function R(h) such that R(h) clips values to [0,1] and adjusts h to satisfy ∑h = n/2

The objective is to find h that satisfies the constraints and minimizes E(h). We use a surrogate approximation for the max operation with a linear penalty.
