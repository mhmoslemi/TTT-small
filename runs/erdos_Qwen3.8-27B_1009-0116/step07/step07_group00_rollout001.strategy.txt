## 1. Approach and Rationale

The current approach combines multiple optimization techniques but may struggle with non-convex optimization due to its heavy reliance on iterative refinement. This new strategy introduces:

- **Multi-scale neighborhood exploration**: Uses local neighborhoods of varying lengths (2,3,5,7) to systematically discover improvements
- **Dual-peak analysis**: For each candidate solution, evaluates and compares both its primary and secondary peaks to ensure balanced optimization
- **Graph-based validation**: Creates a response surface map and validates improvements via a directed acyclic graph (DAG) to ensure convergence

This addresses the problem's non-convexity by:  
a) Using wider exploration (more sample points)  
b) Ensuring both negative and positive change detection  
c) Implementing adaptive termination criteria

The key innovation is the systematic approach to neighborhood variation and validation rather than random mutation. This reduces the risk of getting stuck in shallow local optima while still exploring promising candidates.

## 2. Mathematical Specification

The optimization problem:
