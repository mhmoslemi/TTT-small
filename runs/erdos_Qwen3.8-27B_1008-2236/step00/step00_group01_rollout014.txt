For a periodic extension, at large n, the correlation at shift s ≈ (n/|p|) * count_per_period(s mod p).

But for finite n, the boundary effects matter. The key is that at large |s|, there are few terms, so the count is small.

For the C₅ computation, what matters is the MAX over all k. For a periodic pattern with period p and n = m*p points:
- At shift s (for s < p): count ≈ m * count_per_period(s)
- At shift s (for s ≥ p): the periodic pattern wraps, but the finite sequence doesn't. So at shift s, we have n-s terms.

Actually, for a truly periodic pattern (repeated m times), at shift s:
- Count = sum_{j=0}^{n-1-s} h[j](1-h[j+s])
- Since h is periodic with period p, h[j] = h[j mod p] and h[j+s] = h[(j+s) mod p]
- Count = sum_{j=0}^{n-1-s} h[j mod p](1-h[(j+s) mod p])
- ≈ (n-s)/p * count_per_period(s mod p)

So C₅ ≈ max_s [(n-s)/p * count_per_period(s mod p)] * 2/n
≈ max_s [count_per_period(s mod p) * (1-s/n)] * 2/p

For large n, this approaches (2/p) * max_s count_per_period(s mod p).
