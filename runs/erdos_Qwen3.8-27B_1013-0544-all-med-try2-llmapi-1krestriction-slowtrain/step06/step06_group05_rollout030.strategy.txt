# Improved Erdős Minimum Overlap Problem Algorithm

## 1. Approach and Rationale

We propose a multi-resolution optimization approach using adaptive basis pursuit for the step function representation. Unlike previous methods that relied on mesh-based optimization, we decompose the function into basis components (rectangular pulses) that can be efficiently manipulated. This approach balances exploration and exploitation by:

- Using a coarse resolution for global optimization
- Transferring promising solutions to finer resolutions for refinement
- Employing an adaptive basis representation that automatically adjusts to the solution structure
- Using a combination of local and global search strategies to escape local optima

The key innovation is the basis-pursuit representation which allows for compact, interpretable solutions while maintaining mathematical rigor. This method directly addresses the combinatorial nature of the problem while retaining the mathematical exactness required for the Erdős integral calculations.

This approach can achieve a better bound than previous solutions by:
- More effectively exploring the solution space through structured basis manipulation
- Maintaining strict adherence to constraints through basis decomposition
- Significantly reducing the number of evaluations needed for both feasibility and optimization
- Allowing arithmetic operation time complexity independent of grid resolution

The primary challenges are maintaining basis consistency across resolutions and efficiently transferring solutions between scales.

## 2. Mathematical Specification

### Representation
A solution h:[0,2]→[0,1] is represented as a sum of basis functions:
$$
h(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{m} w_i \cdot \mathbf{1}_{[a_i,b_i]}(x)
$$

where $w_i \in [0,1]$ are weights, and the intervals $[a_i,b_i]$ are disjoint and cover $[0,2]$. 

### Variables
- The number of nonzero basis components $m$
- The interval boundaries $a_i, b_i$ (with $a_1=0, b_m=2$)
- The weights $w_i$

Constraints:
1. $h(x) \in [0,1]$ $\forall x$
2. $\int_0^2 h(x) dx = 1$

### Objective
Minimize
$$
C_5 = \max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1 - h(x+k)) dx
$$

### Formulation
The problem is transformed into a basis-pursuit perspective:
1. Choose a coarse grid of potential interval boundaries
2. Select m intervals from the grid
3. Assign weights to satisfy the integral constraint
4. Minimize the correlation overlap cost

This formulation allows dimension reduction and vectorized implementations.
