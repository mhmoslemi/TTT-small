### Approach and Rationale
The current computational time is dominated by the expensive correlation calculations. This strategy addresses this bottleneck by using spectral methods to replace direct correlations with efficient FFT-based calculations. The core idea is to represent the step function in the frequency domain, where correlation operations translate into simple multiplications. This reduces the computational complexity from O(n^2) to O(n log n), enabling faster optimization.

Key advantages:
1. **Efficiency**: FFT-based methods speed up correlation calculations by 10-100x, allowing more aggressive optimization within the time budget.
2. **Smooth Optimization**: The Fourier basis provides smooth, differentiable representations that mitigate the harsh constraints of max-overlap functions.
3. **Better Convergence**: Spectral methods naturally smooth abrupt changes, enabling optimizers to escape shallow local optima.

Limitations and Constraints:
- Uses a polynomial-based spectral representation (Chebyshev series) for numerical stability.
- Ensures feasibility via iterative projection after optimization.
- Spectral methods may underrepresent abrupt features unless sufficiently high degrees are used.
- We must match the evaluation method exactly, so subsequent re-projection verifies scores.

### Mathematical Specification
We parameterize the step function \(h(x)\) on domain \([0, 2]\) using a truncated Chebyshev series expansion:

\[
h(x) = \frac{1}{2} + \sum_{k=1}^K T_k(x) \cdot a_k
\]

where \(T_k(x)\) is the \(k\)-th Chebyshev polynomial and \(a_k\) are coefficients to be optimized.

The constraints are:
1. \(0 \leq h(x) \leq 1\) for all \(x \in [0, 2]\)
2. \(\int_0^2 h(x) \, dx = 1\)

The objective is:

\[
\min \max_{\tau} \int_0^2 h(x) (1-h(x+\tau)) \, dx
\]

**Surrogate Objective**:
To avoid the expensive max operation, we use a penalty-based approximation:

\[
f(\mathbf{a}) = \max_{\tau} \left| C_{\tau} \right| + \lambda (1 - \sum_{k=1}^K a_k^2)
\]

This penalizes large coefficients that might violate bounds.

**Projection Operator**:
After optimization, project coefficients to enforce bounds:

1. Scale coefficients to satisfy \(0 \leq h(x) \leq 1\)
2. Adjust coefficients to meet integral constraint
3. Use volume preservation for coefficient adjustments
