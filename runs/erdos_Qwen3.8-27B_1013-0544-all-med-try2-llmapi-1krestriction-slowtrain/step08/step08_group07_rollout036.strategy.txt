1. **Approach and Rationale**:
   The current solution employs a heuristic-based optimization with multi-stage refinement. However, non-convex optimization problems like this often succumb to local optima. We propose a population-based optimization strategy using cooperative coevolution, which dynamically delegates different parts of the optimization to distinct subpopulations. The key innovation is **spatial segmentation** of the optimization domain, where segments of the grid are trained on specialized fitness landscapes, followed by periodic exchange of high-performing candidates across segments. This mimics natural selection's diversity maintenance and exploits parallelism in optimization.

   We address three main bottlenecks:
   - Local minima escape: By evolving diverse candidates across subpopulations, we increase the basin of attraction for the global minimum
   - Feature emergence: Specialized segments can develop distinct patterns optimized for different functional regions of the objective
   - Computational efficiency: Breaking the optimization into independent subprocesses allows parallel execution and time adaptive resource allocation

   However, this approach makes two significant trade-offs:
   - It requires careful coordination of interaction protocols between subpopulations
   - It may struggle with many-to-one mappings in segmentation
