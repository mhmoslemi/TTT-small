The key is to find binary (or non-negative) sequences where the convolution is as flat as possible. This is related to the "minimum peak" problem for the aperiodic autocorrelation.

Actually, I realize I should think about this differently. The full convolution a*a is NOT the same as the aperiodic autocorrelation. The aperiodic autocorrelation at lag τ is sum_i a[i]*a[i+τ]. The full convolution at position k is sum_i a[i]*a[k-i], which is a "reversed" correlation.

For a real sequence, the full convolution a*a at position k equals the aperiodic cross-correlation of a with its reverse, at offset k-(n-1). So:
(a*a)[k] = sum_i a[i] * a[k-i]

If we define b[i] = a[n-1-i] (the reverse of a), then:
(a*a)[k] = sum_i a[i] * b[n-1-k+i]

This is the cross-correlation of a and b at offset n-1-k.

So the problem is equivalent to: find a non-negative sequence a such that the cross-correlation of a with its reverse is as flat as possible.

For a symmetric sequence (a = b), this is the autocorrelation, and we've shown the score is ≥ 2.
For an asymmetric sequence, we might be able to do better.
