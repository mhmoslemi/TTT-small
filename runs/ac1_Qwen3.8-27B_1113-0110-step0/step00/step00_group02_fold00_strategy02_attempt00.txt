We are given an initial sequence (height_sequence_1) of length 6962. The goal is to find a sequence of non-negative coefficients that minimizes the evaluation function: 2 * n * max(conv(a,a)) / (sum(a))^2.

Since the evaluation function involves a convolution and is non-convex, we need to use an optimization approach that can navigate the search space efficiently. The key is to sample sequences that reduce the gradient of the objective, which is dominated by the corners of the 2D autocorrelation.

### Strategy
The optimized solution will use gradient ascent to gather the mass in the center of the autocorrelation. The intuition is that by concentrating the coefficients in the middle, the diagonal products (which dominate the autocorrelation in the corners) can be reduced.

- **Gradient Estimation:** For a candidate sequence, estimate the gradient by computing the impact of each coefficient using numerical differentiation. Use a small step size and update the sequence by reducing the corners and increasing the center.
- **Regularization:** To prevent over-concentration and ensure diversity, apply a regularization term that limits the ratio of consecutive coefficients.
- **Efficiency:** Use a fixed number of evaluations per sequence to ensure computations stay within the time limits. Given the constraints (n=6962, O(n^2) per evaluation), we allow for 1800 seconds and limit the hyper-parameter sweeps accordingly.

### Mathematical Specification
We consider a sequence \( a \) of length \( n=6962 \).

The objective to minimize is:
\[ \text{objective}(a) = \frac{2 \cdot n \cdot M}{S^2} \]
where \( M = \max(\text{convolve}(a,a,\text{mode}='full')) \) and \( S = \sum_i a_i \).

We use numerical gradient estimation for each index \( i \):
\[ \frac{d\text{objective}}{da_i} \approx \frac{\text{objective}(a + \epsilon e_i) - \text{objective}(a)}{\epsilon} \]
then clamp the updates so we don't reduce below 0 or increase above 1000.

To ensure diversity and prevent degradation, add a regularization term:
\[ \text{gradient}_i^- = \text{objective}(a) + \|\text{regularization}\| \cdot (a[i-1] - a[i+1])^2 \]
where the regularization prevents consecutive coefficients from being too extreme.

### Implementation Procedure
1. **Initialization:**
   - Read the initial sequence from `height_sequence_1`.
   - Clip values to [0, 1000] and ensure the sum is at least 0.01.

2. **Gradient Estimation and Update:**
   - For each candidate, compute M (max convolution) and S.
   - Estimatthe gradient for each index \( i \) by slightly perturbing the coefficient \( a_i \) (if not at boundary).
   - Update the candidate sequence in the direction of increasing negative gradient.

3. **Run-time Control:**
   - Allow up to 1800 seconds of wall-clock time.
   - Schedule the evaluations so that even though the gradient estimation is expensive, we continue until timeout.

4. **Fallback:**
   - If no improvement is found within the time, return a sequence of all ones (adjusted by the time remaining) or the original sequence.

### Search Choices and Useful Variations
- **Hyper-parameter Sweep:** We use three values for the step size \( \epsilon \): 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, and two values for the regularization weight: 0.01 and 0.05.
- **Step Size \( \epsilon \):** Controls the magnitude of the gradient steps.
- **Regularization Weight \( \lambda \):** Regularizes the coefficients to avoid extreme changes (oscillation is bad for diversity).

### Computebudget and Robustness
We expect O(1000) gradient updates and evaluations per second. In 1800 seconds, we can do up to 3 million updates. However, the first few hundred updates will stabilize the sequence.

### Validation and Return
- After each update, check the objective and replace the current sequence if it's finite and improved.
