**Approach: Iterative water-filling / gradient descent**

The idea: start with a sequence, compute its convolution, identify the "hot spots" (peaks), and redistribute mass from positions that contribute to peaks to positions that don't.

More concretely:
1. Start with $a$ (e.g., a geometric or exponential decay, or a random sequence)
2. Compute $c = a * a$
3. The "excess" at position $k$ is $e_k = \max(0, c_k - \bar{c})$ where $\bar{c} = S^2/(2n-1)$
4. The "pressure" at position $j$ (how much it contributes to the excess) is related to the gradient
5. Reduce $a_j$ where it contributes most to peaks, increase where it contributes to low regions
6. Renormalize and repeat

Let me think about the gradient more carefully.

$\frac{\partial c_k}{\partial a_j} = 2a_{k-j}$ (for $j \neq k/2$) or $a_{k/2}$ (for $j = k/2$, even $k$)

where the indices are understood to be 0 if out of range.

To reduce the peak, we should DECREASE $a_j$ where this gradient is large. The gradient is large when $a_{k^*-j}$ is large, i.e., when $j$ is near $k^*$ (in the reflected sense).

- Find $k^* = \arg\max c_k$
- For each $j$: $a_j \leftarrow \max(0, a_j - \eta \cdot 2 a_{k^*-j})$ (with appropriate boundary handling)
