**Goal** – Produce 26 circles inside the unit square whose radii sum exceeds 2.636 while satisfying the strict non‑overlap and boundary tolerances required by `validate_packing`.

**Core idea – “force‑directed, variable‑radius optimisation with global radius budget”**  
We treat every circle as a particle that repels the others and the walls. Each particle carries a *radius variable* that we try to enlarge under a global budget. The algorithm alternates between (a) moving centres to reduce overlap forces, (b) redistributing a fixed total amount of radius among the circles according to a profitability measure, and (c) a final post‑processing shrink to guarantee the 1e‑12 margin. This is a completely different paradigm from lattice‑initialisation, MILP, or Voronoi‑iteration; it is a physics‑inspired global optimisation that works directly with continuous variables.

---

### 1. Mathematical formulation

Variables  
- \(c_i = (x_i,y_i) \in [0,1]^2\) – centre of circle *i* (i = 0,…,25).  
- \(r_i \ge 0\) – radius of circle *i*.  

Constraints (hard)  
- **Wall feasibility**: \(r_i \le x_i,\; r_i \le 1-x_i,\; r_i \le y_i,\; r_i \le 1-y_i\).  
- **Non‑overlap**: \( \|c_i-c_j\| \ge r_i+r_j \) for all \(i<j\).

Objective (soft, to be maximised)  
- Maximise the total radius sum \(\displaystyle S = \sum_{i=0}^{25} r_i\).

Because the constraints are non‑convex, we embed them in a *penalty energy* that is zero when all constraints are satisfied and grows smoothly otherwise. The optimisation will minimise the energy while keeping a fixed total radius budget; after convergence we will have the maximal feasible sum.

---

### 2. Energy model

Define three separate energy components:

1. **Wall repulsion**  
   \[
   E^{\text{wall}}_i = \bigl[\,\max(0,\; r_i - x_i)\bigr]^2
                    + \bigl[\,\max(0,\; r_i - (1-x_i))\bigr]^2
                    + \bigl[\,\max(0,\; r_i - y_i)\bigr]^2
                    + \bigl[\,\max(0,\; r_i - (1-y_i))\bigr]^2 .
   \]

   This penalises any radius that would exceed the distance to a side.

2. **Pairwise overlap**  
   For each unordered pair \((i,j)\) let \(d_{ij}= \|c_i-c_j\|\).  
   \[
   E^{\text{pair}}_{ij}= \bigl[\,\max(0,\; r_i+r_j - d_{ij})\bigr]^2 .
   \]

3. **Radius‑budget penalty** – we enforce a global target sum \(B\) (initially a large over‑estimate, later decreased).  
   \[
   E^{\text{budget}} = \lambda\bigl( B - S\bigr)^2 ,
   \]
   where \(\lambda>0\) is a weighting factor that encourages the optimiser to use as much of the budget as possible.

The **total energy** is the sum of all components:
\[
E = \sum_i E^{\text{wall}}_i + \sum_{i<j}E^{\text{pair}}_{ij} + E^{\text{budget}} .
\]

When \(E=0\) the configuration is feasible and the sum of radii exactly equals the current budget \(B\).

---

### 3. Global optimisation loop

We will start with a *generous* budget \(B_0\) (e.g. \(B_0 = 3.0\), larger than any attainable sum). The algorithm repeatedly:

1. **Gradient descent / L‑BFGS step** – minimise \(E\) with respect to all \((x_i,y_i,r_i)\) using a robust quasi‑Newton method (SciPy’s `minimize` with `L-BFGS-B`). Bounds are set as:
   - \(0 \le x_i,y_i \le 1\)
   - \(0 \le r_i \le 0.5\) (the absolute maximum in a unit square).

   The gradient of each term is analytic:
   - For wall terms, derivative w.r.t. \(x_i\) is \(-2\max(0, r_i - x_i)\) when \(r_i > x_i\), else 0; derivative w.r.t. \(r_i\) adds \(2\max(0,r_i-x_i)\) etc.
   - For pair terms, if \(r_i+r_j > d_{ij}\) the gradient w.r.t. \(c_i\) is \(-2(r_i+r_j-d_{ij}) (c_i-c_j)/d_{ij}\) and w.r.t. \(r_i\) is \(2(r_i+r_j-d_{ij})\). When the pair is non‑overlapping the gradient is zero.
   - For the budget term, \(\partial E/\partial r_i = -2\lambda (B - S)\).

   Supplying these exact gradients prevents the optimizer from stalling.

2. **Check feasibility** – after the descent, compute the maximal violation:
   \[
   v_{\max}= \max\bigl\{\,\max_i\bigl(r_i-x_i,\,r_i-(1-x_i),\,r_i-y_i,\,r_i-(1-y_i)\bigr),\;
                     \max_{i<j}\bigl(r_i+r_j-d_{ij}\bigr)\bigr\}.
   \]
   If \(v_{\max} \le 10^{-13}\) we have a feasible configuration for the current budget.

3. **Budget adaptation** – if feasible, *increase* the budget: set \(B \leftarrow B + \Delta\) with a small positive increment \(\Delta\) (e.g. \(5\times10^{-4}\)). If not feasible, *decrease* the budget: \(B \leftarrow B - \Delta\).  
   This binary‑search‑like update converges to the largest budget for which a feasible solution exists.

4. **Termination condition** – stop when the step \(\Delta\) falls below \(10^{-6}\) or after a maximum number of outer iterations (e.g. 40). The final feasible \(B\) is the best achievable sum of radii.

Because the energy is smooth everywhere (the max‑functions are piecewise linear with smooth squared penalties) the L‑BFGS optimizer can handle the high‑dimensional problem (78 variables) efficiently, and the outer budget adaptation guarantees we approach the true optimum from below.

### 4. Initialisation

A good starting point accelerates convergence:

1. **Seed centres** – generate a low‑discrepancy Sobol sequence of 26 points in \([0,1]^2\).  
2. **Initial radii** – set each radius to the minimum of the distances to the four walls *and* half the distance to its nearest neighbour (computed from the Sobol points). This yields a feasible configuration whose sum is ~2.3, comfortably below the target, guaranteeing that the first outer iteration (with a large budget) will be infeasible and will trigger a reduction.

3. **Flatten vector** – pack the 78 variables into a 1‑D NumPy array for the optimiser.

### 5. Gradient implementation details (no code)

- Compute the full distance matrix \(D\) once per energy/gradient evaluation (cost \(O(n^2)\) with \(n=26\)).  
- For each pair, evaluate the overlap amount \(o_{ij}= \max(0, r_i+r_j-d_{ij})\).  
- Accumulate gradient contributions:
  - To \(r_i\): add \(2 o_{ij}\) (and a symmetric term from the pair where \(i\) is the second index).  
  - To \(c_i\): if overlapping, subtract \(2 o_{ij} (c_i-c_j)/d_{ij}\).  
- Wall contributions are handled similarly with per‑circle checks.

All operations are vectorised with NumPy; the gradient function returns a flat array of length 78.

### 6. Post‑processing & validation safety margin

Even though the outer loop stops when the energy is essentially zero, floating‑point rounding can leave a violation of order \(10^{-13}\), which is still within the validator’s tolerance of \(10^{-12}\). To be absolutely safe:

1. **Shrink every radius** by a tiny epsilon `eps = 5e-10`:  
   \[
   r_i \leftarrow \max(0,\; r_i - \text{eps}) .
   \]
   This guarantees strict interior placement.

2. **Clamp centres** to \([\text{eps},\,1-\text{eps}]\) to keep them away from the walls.

3. **One‑pass repair** – iterate over all pairs, recompute distances, and if any overlap remains (should not happen), reduce the larger radius by half the overlap plus `eps`. Because the initial shrink already provides a margin, this loop will run at most a few iterations.

After repair, recompute `sum_r = np.sum(radii)`. This value will be at most `eps*26 ≈ 1.3e-8` less than the theoretical optimum found by the outer loop, well above the required 2.636.

The force‑directed dynamics naturally push circles toward corners (where they can be larger) and spread interior circles, achieving a near‑optimal distribution without any explicit lattice or MILP construction.

### 8. Failure‑mode analysis & mitigations

| Failure mode | Cause | Detection | Remedy |
|--------------|-------|-----------|--------|
| Optimiser stalls at a local minimum with many tiny circles | Initial budget too high, causing many overlaps that the gradient cannot resolve | Energy stops decreasing while \(v_{\max}>10^{-9}\) | Reduce the budget step \(\Delta\) temporarily, restart the inner optimisation from the last feasible configuration. |
| Division by zero in pair gradient when two centres coincide | Overlap penalty pushes centres together | `d_{ij}` exactly zero | In the gradient code add a tiny regulariser: use `d = sqrt(dx^2+dy^2) + 1e-12`. |
| Radii exceed 0.5 (theoretical bound) due to numerical drift | Budget increase after feasibility mis‑detected | `r_i > 0.5 + 1e-9` | Clip radii to 0.5 after each inner optimisation step; this does not affect feasibility because a radius larger than 0.5 would automatically violate wall constraints. |
| Final sum falls short of 2.636 after shrink | Chosen outer tolerance too aggressive | `sum_r < 2.636` after repair | Re‑run the outer loop with a slightly larger initial budget or with a smaller shrink epsilon (e.g. 1e-10). The algorithm is cheap enough for a few retries. |
| Runtime exceeds limits due to excessive outer iterations | Very small `Δ` leading to many budget adjustments | Iteration count > 100 | Cap outer iterations; if not converged, report the best feasible sum encountered so far (still likely >2.636). |

All mitigations are deterministic and cheap; they do not require random restarts, preserving the “materially different” nature of the approach.
