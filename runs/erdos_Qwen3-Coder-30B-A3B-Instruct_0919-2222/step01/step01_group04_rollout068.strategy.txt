**Goal:** Construct a step‑function h on [0,2] (discretised to N points, N≈70) with values in [0,1] and total integral 1 that minimizes  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{k}\int_0^2 h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx .
\]

Because the integral is approximated by a discrete correlation, the problem can be rewritten as  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{k}\;{\rm corr}_k\bigl(h,\,1-h\bigr)\,\Delta x ,
\qquad
\Delta x=\frac{2}{N},
\]

where  

\[
{\rm corr}_k(h,1-h)=\sum_{i\in I_k} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+k}\bigr)
\]

and \(I_k\) is the index set where the two shifted vectors overlap.  Since \(\sum_i h_i=\frac{N}{2}\) is fixed,  

\[
C_5(h)=1-\min_{k}\sum_{i\in I_k}h_i h_{i+k}\,\Delta x .
\]

Thus minimising \(C_5\) is *equivalently* the **maximin autocorrelation** problem: maximise the smallest overlap
\(\displaystyle R_k=\sum_{i\in I_k}h_i h_{i+k}\) over all non‑zero shifts k.

The proposed solution proceeds in three conceptual stages, each exploiting a different perspective on the problem and together forming a search strategy that is fundamentally different from the earlier L‑BFGS/Nelder‑Mead routine.

### 1. Discrete binary “seed” via max‑min overlap hill‑climbing

### 3. Continuous optimisation via projected Adam on the max‑overlap subgradient

The objective \(C_5(h)=\max_k f_k(h)\) with  

\[
f_k(h)=\sum_{i\in I_k} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+k}\bigr)
\]

is piecewise smooth: the gradient is the gradient of the *active* lag(s) at the current point.  A first‑order method with explicit constraint projection is both simple and robust for modest dimensions.

#### 3.1 Subgradient computation

For a given lag \(k\) (positive or negative) the gradient of \(f_k\) w.r.t. h is:

```
if k ≥ 0:
    for i = 0 … N‑k‑1:
        ∂f_k/∂h_i        = 1 – h_{i+k}
        ∂f_k/∂h_{i+k}    = – h_i
else (k < 0):
    let ℓ = –k
    for i = ℓ … N‑1:
        ∂f_k/∂h_i        = 1 – h_{i‑ℓ}
        ∂f_k/∂h_{i‑ℓ}    = – h_i
```

All other components have zero derivative.  The stencil can be implemented with two vectorised slice assignments, costing O(N) per lag.

When several lags achieve the same maximal value (common when the landscape is flat), average their individual gradients to obtain a legitimate subgradient of the max‑function.

#### 3.2 Adam update with projection

1. Compute the subgradient \(g_t\) as above.
2. Update moments:

3. Bias‑correct:
   * \(\hat m_t = m_t/(1-\beta_1^t)\)

4. Perform an **unconstrained** step:
