Approach and rationale\n\nOptimize the finite sequence at the runtime parent’s resolution using projected gradient descent on a smooth approximation to the maximum over all correlation lags. At each temperature, form a log-sum-exp of the exact finite-support lag values, compute its analytic gradient, and take a backtracked step followed by Euclidean projection onto the capped simplex. Decrease the smoothing temperature in a fixed continuation schedule. Keep and rank returned candidates only by the exact evaluator score, not by the smoothed objective.\n\nThis is materially different from a direct epigraph/SLSQP approach: it introduces no epigraph variable and calls no constrained nonlinear-program solver. Its update is a first-order projected step for a differentiable surrogate, with the box and sum constraints enforced jointly by projection. This avoids asking a generic solver to handle the original nonsmooth maximum directly.\n\nThe finite-lag correlation formulas, their gradients, the log-sum-exp approximation bounds, and the capped-simplex projection are justified mathematically. The nonconvex products in the lag values mean the smooth objectives are also generally nonconvex. Continuation, seeded starts, and perturbations are heuristics for exploring useful basins; they do not certify a global optimum or guarantee a particular target score. Retain a validated parent as a fallback and do not claim an unmeasured improvement.\n\n2. Mathematical specification\n\nFor n samples, let h be a real vector of shape (n,), dx = 2.0/n, and S = n/2. These are exactly the terms in np.correlate(h, 1−h, mode='full'), possibly written in the evaluator’s lag order. Every nonzero lag has only n−s terms.
