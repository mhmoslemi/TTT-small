At each feasible vector, construct a convex quadratic upper bound for every signed-lag correlation, minimize the maximum of those bounds over the box-and-mass set, then accept a proposal only after evaluating its exact discrete score. This addresses the nonsmooth maximum without differentiating through an active-lag choice and without asking a solver to handle the original nonconvex quadratic constraints.\n\nThis is materially different from directly solving the nonconvex epigraph, smoothing the maximum and taking projected-gradient steps, exact one-pair line minimization, or maintaining an evolutionary population. Each MM step solves a derived convex subproblem in the full sample vector; multiple algorithmic starts and occasional incumbent perturbations are used only to explore separate basins. The exact lag formula, feasibility conditions, and upper-model derivation below are mathematical facts. That repeated MM steps, numerical solvers, and multiple starts find a lower score is a heuristic expectation, not a guarantee. The objective is generally nonconvex, so the program must make no global-optimality or target-attainment claim.\n\n2. Mathematical specification\nFor a positive integer n, represent the samples by h ∈ R^n, let dx = 2/n and m = n/2, and require 0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1 and sum_i h[i] = m. This includes odd n, where m is a half-integer.\n\nFor every signed lag k from −(n−1) through n−1, define\nq_k(h) = dx · sum_{i=max(0,−k)}^{min(n−1,n−1−k)} h[i+k](1−h[i]).\nThe authoritative score is C(h) = max_k q_k(h). The vector q, in this same lag order, is `np.correlate(h, 1.0-h, mode='full') * dx`; lag k is at index n−1+k. For p>0, q_p sums h[i+p](1−h[i]) over i=0,…,n−1−p and q_−p sums h[i](1−h[i+p]) over that same partial overlap. q_0 sums all n products. Include both lag directions and zero; do not wrap indices, replace a partial-overlap sum with the full mass, or use an autocorrelation shortcut.\n\nThe lag gradients have shape (2n−1,n), with rows in the order returned by `np.correlate`. For k=±p, its only nonzero entries are −dx at symmetric positions (i,i+p) and (i+p,i); each row has absolute sum at most 2dx. Therefore the largest Hessian eigenvalue is at most 2dx for every lag. The convex MM subproblem is\nminimize t,\nsubject to −x ≤ d ≤ 1−x, sum_i d[i]=0, s≥||d||², and q + Gd + dx*s*1 ≤ t*1.\nHere 1 is the length-L all-ones vector. The bounds and equality are exactly h=x+d ∈ K. Since dx>0, an optimum uses s=||d||². The model value at d=0 is C(x); its minimized value is no greater, and each lag model upper-bounds its exact lag. Thus an exact solution of the subproblem cannot increase C. Numerical solver proposals must nevertheless be checked using the exact score.\n\nFor starts and repairs, use the Euclidean projection onto the capped simplex: P(y)[i]=clip(y[i]−λ,0,1), with λ chosen so the sum is m.
