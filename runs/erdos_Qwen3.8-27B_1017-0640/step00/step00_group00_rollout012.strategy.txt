Mathematical specification\nFor a selected positive integer n, let h be a real vector of shape (n,), dx = 2/n, and m = n/2. The feasible set is K = {h: 0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1 for every i and sum_i h[i] = m}. Thus the discrete mass is exactly the required half-interval mass, including when n is odd and m is a half-integer.\n\nFor signed lags k = -(n-1), …, n-1, define\nq_k(h) = dx · sum over i=max(0,-k) to min(n-1,n-1-k) of h[i+k](1-h[i]).\nThis is the finite, non-circular correlation. In particular, for p=1,…,n-1, q_p = dx · sum from i=0 to n-1-p of h[i+p](1-h[i]), and q_-p = dx · sum from i=0 to n-1-p of h[i](1-h[i+p]). Also q_0 = dx · sum_i h[i](1-h[i]). The array `np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode='full') * dx` is ordered by k from -(n-1) through n-1, so k is at array index n-1+k. Include every entry. Do not wrap indices, omit either lag direction, substitute a full-interval mass at nonzero lags, or use an autocorrelation shortcut. The authoritative objective is C(h) = max_k q_k(h), in the evaluator’s units.\n\nFor a temperature τ>0, use the surrogate F_τ(h) = τ log(sum_k exp(q_k(h)/τ)). It satisfies max_k q_k ≤ F_τ ≤ max_k q_k + τ log(2n-1), and F_τ tends to the exact maximum as τ decreases to zero. Compute it stably: let Q=max(q), set e_k=exp((q_k-Q)/τ), w_k=e_k/sum_j e_j, and F_τ=Q+τ log(sum_j e_j). The weights w_k sum to one; underflow of very small exponentials to zero is acceptable because at least one e_k equals one. These temperatures and the surrogate are in the same units as C(h). Always rank candidates by C(h), never by F_τ.\n\nFor the lag gradients, use array index n-1+k for lag k. For coordinate r, the zero-lag derivative is ∂q_0/∂h[r] = dx(1-2h[r]). For p>0,\n∂q_p/∂h[r] = dx(1_{p≤r≤n-1}(1-h[r-p]) - 1_{0≤r≤n-1-p}h[r+p]),\n∂q_-p/∂h[r] = dx(1_{0≤r≤n-1-p}(1-h[r+p]) - 1_{p≤r≤n-1}h[r-p]).\nIf both terms apply to a coordinate, add both. Therefore ∇F_τ(h) = sum_k w_k ∇q_k(h). A memory-efficient implementation can accumulate this vector directly: initialize g = dx*w[n-1]*(1-2h); for p from 1 to n-1, let wp=w[n-1+p] and wm=w[n-1-p], then add dx*wp*(1-h[:-p]) to g[p:], subtract dx*wp*h[p:] from g[:-p], add dx*wm*(1-h[p:]) to g[:-p], and subtract dx*wm*h[:-p] from g[p:].
