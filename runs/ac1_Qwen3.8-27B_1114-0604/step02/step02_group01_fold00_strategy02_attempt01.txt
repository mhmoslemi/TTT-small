# Central Idea: 
We combine random initialization with a gradient-based coordinate-descent optimization technique. The method involves:

1. **Random Walk Initialization**: Starting from random points to build diverse candidate sequences.
2. **Gradient Descent Polishing**: Refining each candidate by iteratively moving mass away from the highest contributions in the convolution and towards the lowest contributions.
3. **Adaptive Step Sizing**: Balancing large and small steps during optimization based on progress.
4. **Structured Perturbations**: Adding random, sinusoidal, or exponential perturbations to escape local minima.

The rationale is to improve the explormation-exploitation balance of the original approach, which primarily used single-gradient steps. By introducing structured perturbations and better maintaining the invariant between sequence length and evaluation, we expect to escape local minima more effectively.

## Mathematical Representation
The search space is defined as: 
  - Sequences of length `n` (will be same as parent context)
  - All coefficients are floats between 0 and 1000 (inclusive)
  - Sum of coefficients >= 0.01

### Objective Function (Score)
We minimize: 
  score = (4.0 * n * max_conv) / (sum_sq - 0.01 + 0.01*0.01)   [prevent division by zero]
where:
  max_conv = maximum of the full-linear convolution (all 2*n-1 values)
  sum_sq = (sum_of_sequence)^2

Why this is significantly different:
  - Factor of 4: We use this to mitigate the impact of initializing with a normal sum (~ sqrt(n)) rather than the fixed normalization (sqrt(2n)), and to fit our geometric searching without affecting actual metric.
  - Denominator: We avoid numerically unstable divisions by using a safe formula with soft constraint.

Compare to official: 
  evaluate_sequence(seq) => (2*n * max_conv) / (sum_sq with sum>=0.01)

Our score is: 
  score = 4.0 * (formula of official)/ (adjustment from soft constraints)

But soft constraint doesn't change the actual minima, just controls stopping condition.
