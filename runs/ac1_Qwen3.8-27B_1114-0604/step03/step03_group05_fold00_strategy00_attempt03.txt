### Strategy 1: Approach and Rationale
We conduct a **hybrid optimization** strategy that combines multiple search techniques to find high-quality coefficient sequences efficiently. The central idea is to balance exploration through global search methods (like simulated annealing) with local refinement using gradient-based optimization. Given that our previous program achieved 1.5093, we aim to improve upon that by using a more sophisticated exploration mechanism.

The key bottleneck has been the limited exploration due to the wrapped nature of the FFT-based convolution and gradient evaluation. To address this, we will implement a **multi-armed bandit approach** using gradient bands. This means we will maintain a collection of "blueprints" (templates) for generating sequences. Each blueprint includes:
- Initial sequence generation method (random, structured perturbation)
- A scaling factor to meet the normalization requirements
Each time, we will evolve a candidate sequence within a chosen blueprint by using its gradient information. We will track the fitness of all active candidates and replace the highest scoring one with one generated by a different blueprint to ensure diversity.

### Strategy 2: Phase 1 - Variance Flattening
We begin by generating an initial population using two methods:
1. **Random Initialization**: Each coefficient is randomly drawn from a uniform distribution between 0 and 0.9. This ensures non-negativity and controlled normalization.
2. **Segmented Flattening**: We use predefined segment templates that often yield sequences with moderate variance.

We collect all initial candidates and apply the variance flattening objective, defined as:
```latex
variance\_score(a) = 2n \frac{\sum_{j} (convolve(a,a)[j])^2}{(\sum_{j} a[j])^4}
```
Using this, we compute the variance score for each candidate and identify the **superiority gap**:
- The minimum variance score among all candidates.

We schedule this phase to use a budget of 120 seconds, then quantify the time used.

### Strategy 3: Phase 2 - LSE Bandit with Local Searches
Next, we deploy **bandit scheduling**: We set a learning rate and determine the direction for gradient adjustment. We calculate the gradient for each candidate sequence using:
```latex
gradient = \frac{\partial \variance\_score}{\partial a} = 4 \frac{\sum_{j} (2 \cdot (convolve(a,a))_{k} \cdot convolve(a,a)_{k}^{\text{diff}}) }{(\sum_{j} a[j])^5} - \frac{4 \cdot variance\_score}{(\sum_{j} a[j])^4} \cdot \sum_{j} a[j]
```
This allows us to track the **effective score** over time and converges when the improvement becomes vanishingly small.

In the third phase, we introduce **bandit-averaged restarts**: every 100th candidate is replaced by a new one generated from a different structure.
