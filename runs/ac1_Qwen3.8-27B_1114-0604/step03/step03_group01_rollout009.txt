Actually, for the discrete case with a[i] summing to 1:
(a*a)[0] = a[0]^2
(a*a)[1] = 2*a[0]*a[1]
...
(a*a)[n-1] = sum_i a[i]*a[n-1-i]
...
(a*a)[2n-2] = a[n-1]^2

The "circular" analogy would be: (a*a_circ)[k] = sum_i a[i]*a[(k-i) mod n]
(a*a_circ)[0] = sum a[i]^2 >= 1/n

For linear convolution, (a*a)[n-1] = sum a[i]*a[n-1-i] which is similar to the "anti-diagonal" sum.

For the circular convolution: (a*a_circ)[k] >= 1/n by the same Cauchy-Schwarz argument (since sum of all (a*a_circ)[k] = n, so at least one is >= 1/n).

For linear: sum of all (a*a)[k] = (sum a)^2 = 1. There are 2n-1 terms, so at least one is >= 1/(2n-1).

But we want the MAX to be small. The max is at least 1/(2n-1) by averaging, but also at least a[0]^2 (from the first term) and at least a[n-1]^2.

By Cauchy-Schwarz: sum a[i]^2 >= 1/n. And a[0]^2 + a[n-1]^2 <= sum a[i]^2. So max(a[0]^2, a[n-1]^2) could be as small as 1/(2n) if a[0] = a[n-1] = 1/sqrt(2n) and the rest are spread out.

But then the middle terms might be large.

I think the key insight is: for the LINEAR convolution (not circular), we CAN get below score 2, because the "mass" near the edges of the convolution (k=0 and k=2n-2) only involves the squares of a[0] and a[n-1], while the circular convolution would involve ALL squares at k=0.

So the "leakage" at the edges of the linear convolution allows us to achieve a lower peak!

Specifically, if we make a[0] and a[n-1] small (so the edge peaks are small), and the middle of the sequence is where most of the mass is, then the linear convolution will have lower peaks than the circular one.

But wait, if a[0] and a[n-1] are small, then (a*a)[n-1] = sum a[i]*a[n-1-i] is approximately sum of a[i]*a[j] for i+j = n-1, which is similar to the circular case minus the "wrap-around" terms.

I think the optimal strategy is:
- Make a[0] and a[n-1] very small (to kill the edge peaks)
- Distribute the mass in the interior in a way that minimizes the peak of the interior convolution
- The "interior" is essentially a shorter sequence of length ~n-2

This suggests a recursive structure: the optimal sequence of length n is related to the optimal sequence of length n-2 with small values at the ends.
