For a better sequence, we want to flatten this triangle. The optimal sequence should have a shape that makes the convolution as flat as possible.

One approach: think of it as an optimization problem. We want to minimize max_j (a*a)[j] subject to sum a = 1, a >= 0.

This is a convex optimization problem (the min of a max of linear functions is convex). We can use subgradient methods or other convex optimization techniques.

The subgradient of max_j (a*a)[j] with respect to a is:
- At the maximizing lag j*, the gradient is 2 * (shifted a) (the vector with a[j*-i] at position i).
- If there are multiple maximizers, any convex combination.

So a subgradient descent step would be:
a_new = a - step_size * 2 * (shifted a at peak lag)
Then project onto {a >= 0, sum a = 1}.

This is essentially what the MMD method does, but with the softmax approximation to the max.

Actually, let me reconsider the problem from scratch. The key insight I've been missing:

The score is `2*n*max_b / sum_a^2`. For a normalized sequence (sum=1), this is `2*n*max_b`.

The max of the convolution is at some lag j*. At that lag, the convolution value is:
(a*a)[j*] = sum_i a[i] * a[j*-i]

This is an inner product of a with a shifted version of itself. By Cauchy-Schwarz:
(a*a)[j*] <= sqrt(sum a[i]^2 * sum a[j*-i]^2) = sum a[i]^2

So the max convolution is at most sum a[i]^2 (the energy of the sequence). And sum a[i]^2 >= 1/n (by Cauchy-Schwarz on the sum).

But the max convolution could be less than sum a[i]^2 if the peak is not at lag 0. For the full linear convolution, the peak is often at the center (lag n-1), not at lag 0.

At lag n-1: (a*a)[n-1] = sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a[i]*a[n-1-i], which is the inner product of a with its reverse.

So we want to minimize the inner product of a with its reverse (and all other shifted versions).

This is a different problem from minimizing the energy! We want the sequence to be "anti-correlated" with its shifts.
