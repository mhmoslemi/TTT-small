Now, the "second peak" is at $k = n-2$ and $k = n$: $(a*a)_{n-2} = (n-1)/n^2$.

So the ratio of second peak to first peak is $(n-1)/n \approx 1 - 1/n$.

For a non-constant sequence, we might be able to reduce the center peak while the "second peak" also changes. The question is whether we can make ALL peaks below $0.751/n$.

For $n = 3809$, $0.751/n \approx 0.751/3809 \approx 1.97 \times 10^{-4}$.

The center of the constant sequence is $1/n \approx 2.63 \times 10^{-4}$.

So we need to reduce the peak by about 25%. That's a significant reduction.

I think the key insight is that we need a sequence where the "effective bandwidth" of the autocorrelation is wider. The constant sequence has a triangular autocorrelation (peaked at center). We need something closer to a rectangular autocorrelation (flat).

This is the classic "minimum peak sidelobe" (MPSL) problem, but for non-negative sequences. For complex sequences, the optimal MPSL sequences have a peak-to-average ratio of about 4-6 dB (which corresponds to a score of about 1.16-1.23). For real non-negative sequences, it's likely worse.

A score of 1.5027 corresponds to a peak-to-average ratio of $1.5027 \cdot (2n-1)/(2n) \approx 1.5027$ (for large $n$). That's about 1.76 dB. That seems achievable.
