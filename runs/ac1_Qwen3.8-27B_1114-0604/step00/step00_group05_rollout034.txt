Wait, but this is essentially gradient descent on the peak, which might converge slowly. Also, the "gradient" I'm using (s_j = a_{k*-j}) only accounts for the peak at k*, not for preventing other positions from becoming the new peak.

Let me think about a better approach.

**Better approach: Combine gradient step with "peak awareness"**

At each step:
1. Compute b = a*a
2. Find the top K positions where b is large (K = 10 or so)
3. Compute the combined gradient: s_j = sum_{k in topK} a_{k-j} (weighted by how close b_k is to the max)
4. Take a step in the -s direction (projected)

This is like a "top-K subgradient" method.

Alternatively, I can use a smooth approximation of the max: instead of max(b), use the log-sum-exp: LSE(b) = -t * log(sum exp(-b_k/t)) for small t. This is differentiable and approximates max for small t.

The gradient of LSE wrt b_k is exp(-b_k/t) / sum exp(-b_j/t), which is a softmax over the b values. For small t, this concentrates on the max.

Then the gradient wrt a_j is sum_k softmax_k * 2 * a_{k-j}.

This gives a smooth optimization problem that we can solve with standard methods.
