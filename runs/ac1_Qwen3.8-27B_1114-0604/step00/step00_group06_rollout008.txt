Actually, I think the key insight is:

For a sequence concentrated in the middle (like a tent or Gaussian), the self-convolution at $k=0$ is $\sum a_i^2$, which is large, but the self-convolution at the center $k \approx n/2$ involves products of $a_i$ with $a_{n/2 + i}$ which might be smaller.

Wait no, for `np.convolve(a, a)`, the element at index $k$ is $\sum_i a[i] a[k-i]$. At $k = n-1$ (the center), this is $\sum_i a[i] a[n-1-i]$.

If $a$ is concentrated in the middle (say around index $n/2$), then $a[i]$ is large when $i \approx n/2$ and $a[n-1-i]$ is large when $n-1-i \approx n/2$, i.e., $i \approx n/2 - 1$. So at the center of the convolution, we're multiplying the "middle" of $a$ with the "middle" of reversed $a$, which is still the middle. So the peak at the center would be large.

But at $k=0$: $(a*a)[0] = a[0]^2$, which would be small if $a$ is concentrated in the middle.
At $k = n/2$: $(a*a)[n/2] = \sum_i a[i] a[n/2-i]$. If $a$ is concentrated around $n/2$, then $a[i]$ is large for $i \approx n/2$ and $a[n/2-i]$ is large for $n/2-i \approx n/2$, i.e., $i \approx 0$. So we're multiplying the "middle" with the "edges" which are small. This could be small!

So the peak might not be at the center. It might be at some offset.
