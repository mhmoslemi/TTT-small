This is great! Now I understand the problem. The key is to find sequences where the convolution is as "flat" as possible (all values close to the maximum), which minimizes the maximum.

This is related to the concept of "perfect" or "near-perfect" sequences in signal processing, where the autocorrelation has as few non-zero values as possible. But here we want the convolution to be as flat as possible.

For the constant sequence, the convolution is a triangle (peaked). For the optimal sequence, we want the convolution to be as flat as possible.

This is actually a well-studied problem! It's related to "low peak-to-average power ratio" (PAPR) sequences, or more specifically, to the problem of finding sequences whose autocorrelation (or in this case, self-convolution) is as flat as possible.

The key difference: in standard PAPR problems, we deal with complex sequences of unit magnitude. Here, we deal with non-negative real sequences.

For non-negative sequences, the problem is:
Minimize max_k (a*a)[k] subject to sum a = 1, a >= 0.

This is equivalent to finding a probability distribution on {0, ..., n-1} that minimizes the maximum of its self-convolution.

I recall that for continuous distributions, the uniform distribution minimizes the maximum of the convolution (since the convolution of two uniform distributions on [0,1] is a triangle, and the uniform distribution is the only one that gives a "nice" shape). But for discrete distributions, the optimal might be different.
