Actually, let me reconsider. The key issue is: what sequence shapes have low peak convolution?

Let me think about it from the perspective of the "energy" distribution. The convolution c = a*a has total energy S^2. We want this energy to be spread as evenly as possible over 2n-1 positions.

For a uniform sequence, c is triangular (concentrated in the middle), giving max = S^2/n = n (for S=n). So max/S^2 = 1/n, and objective = 2n/n = 2.

For a "flat" c, we'd want c[k] ≈ S^2/(2n-1) for all k, giving max/S^2 ≈ 1/(2n-1), and objective ≈ 2n/(2n-1) ≈ 1.

The question is: can we find a non-negative a such that a*a ≈ constant?

This is equivalent to finding a sequence whose DFT has constant magnitude (a "flat" spectrum), but with the constraint that a is real and non-negative.

In the frequency domain: if A(ω) = FFT(a), then the magnitude of the zero-padded FFT of a determines the convolution. Specifically, the zero-padded FFT of c = a*a is |A_padded(ω)|^2.
