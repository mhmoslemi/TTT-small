**Thought: Connection to Turán's problem / Singer's construction**

The problem of minimizing the peak autocorrelation is related to the "minimum distance" problem in coding theory. There are known constructions (like Barker sequences, Zadoff-Chu, etc.) that achieve near-optimal peak-to-average ratios for certain lengths.

For our problem (non-negative sequences, linear convolution), the connection is:
- We want conv(a,a) to be as flat as possible.
- The "ideal" flat value is (sum a)^2 / (2n-1).
- The "excess" at each lag is conv[k] - (sum a)^2/(2n-1).

A sequence that achieves conv[k] = (sum a)^2/(2n-1) for all k would be "perfect" and give score = 2n/(2n-1) ≈ 1. Such sequences are called "perfect" or "Burrows-Wheeler" sequences, and they're known to exist only for very limited lengths (n = 2, 4 for binary sequences).

For non-perfect sequences, we want to minimize the maximum excess. This is the "low autocorrelation" problem.

**Known results:**
- For binary sequences (±1), the best known peak autocorrelation is O(sqrt(n)) (Barker's bound), giving a PAR of O(sqrt(n)).
- For general real sequences, better results are possible.
- For non-negative sequences, the problem is slightly different.
