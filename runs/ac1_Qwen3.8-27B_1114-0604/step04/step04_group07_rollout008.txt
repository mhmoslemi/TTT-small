This is a crucial insight! The palindrome constraint is HARMFUL - it limits the score to >= 2. We should focus entirely on non-palindromic optimization.

Now, for non-palindromic sequences, the center of the autoconv is `sum_i a[i]*a[n-1-i]` which can be much less than ||a||_2^2. In fact, by the rearrangement inequality, if a is increasing and a[n-1-i] is decreasing, their dot product is minimized.

So the strategy should be:
1. Make the sequence "monotone" (or close to it) so that the center of the autoconv is small.
2. But also make sure the other lags don't have large peaks.

This is a much more interesting optimization problem!

Let me think about what the optimal non-palindromic sequence looks like.

For a strictly increasing sequence a[0] < a[1] < ... < a[n-1]:
- b[n-1] = sum a[i]*a[n-1-i] (small by rearrangement)
- b[k] for k < n-1: these involve "less mixed" products
- The peak might shift to k > n-1 (right of center)

Actually, for an increasing sequence, let's think about what happens:
b[k] = sum_j a[j]*a[k-j]. For k > n-1, the sum involves larger indices (larger values of a), so b[k] tends to be larger for k > n-1.

For a linearly increasing sequence a[i] = i+1 (i=0,...,n-1):
b[k] = sum_j (j+1)*(k-j+1) for valid j.
This is a cubic polynomial in k. The peak is at k = n (one past the center).
