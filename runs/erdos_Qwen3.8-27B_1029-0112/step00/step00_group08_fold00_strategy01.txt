1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The task is to find a discrete vector h of length n_points on [0,2] with discrete integral 1, i.e. sum(h*dx)=1 or equivalently sum(h)=n_points/2, that minimizes

    C5(h) = max_lag contribution.

The evaluator is directional:

    C5(h) = max(np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")) * (2.0/n_points).

This is a nonlinear, nonconvex min-max problem over a finite-correlation representation. The approach proposed here is materially different from a weighted maximum or annealed simulated subgradient strategy: formulate and locally solve the epigraph nonlinear program

    minimize alpha
    subject to 0 <= h_i <= 1,
               sum(h_i) = n/2,
               for every finite-support correlation offset d:
                   F_d(h) * dx <= alpha.

Here F_d(h) denotes one full-offset sum of products. Since the maximum over offsets is exactly represented by imposing upper-bound constraints for all offsets, the epigraph directly couples alpha with the entire active offset set. Concurrently, the derivative information points in directions that reduce the bottleneck set, not just the single largest lag. This is the primary proposed mechanism.

1.2 Why this could improve the parent or break plateaus

A parent construction that is already near the record value often sits on a local surface where the maximum is owned by several offsets. Aggregating only the dominant gradient can waste movement in variables that do little for the whole constraint family. The epigraph model supplies Lagrange-like equations for the active constraint set instead of an artificial scalar objective, and repeated constrained fixes can shift h over a wide family of offsets simultaneously. It remains only a local method, but it is expected to refine local regions more systematically because every offset is a constraint rather than only the largest one. It becomes the deterministic top-level mechanism for the returned candidate, with multiple starts and limited further support, and it can be executed using scipy and numpy alone.
