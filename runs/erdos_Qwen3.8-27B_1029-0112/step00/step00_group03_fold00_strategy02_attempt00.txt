1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The supplied `initial_h_values` construction is a strong local solution, so the most useful next step is a local minimax refinement, not a wholesale situation replaced. The central algorithm proposed here is an exact-feasible active-constraint least-squares/linearized-descent engine. In each iteration it:

1. evaluates the exact discrete objective;
2. identifies the currently largest overlap lags (the active constraints);
3. builds exact first-order derivative rows for those lag forms;
4. solves a small convex subproblem whose variables are the coordinate displacements `d` that keep `sum(d)=0` and the candidate within [0,1], and whose constraints make the linearized scores of the active lags closer equally to a target reduction;
5. tests the resulting displacement only through the true objective;
6. records the candidate only if the true objective has actually decreased by the specified tolerance.

The key mathematical difference from a global smooth-max projected-gradient method is that the direction is not a single generalized subgradient line, nor a single-pair mass move. The subproblem seeks a simultaneous displacement that linearly reduces all important lags whenever that is possible, treating the most active constraints in one linearized system. It is also distinct from a naive LP descent in its treatment of objective contact: here the excess of each chosen active row is made explicit as a nonnegative slack and the displacement is balanced by a quadratic regularization, followed immediately by exact evaluation and a line search. The soft nonconvex product terms remain only in the exact score.

The methodology is local: the exact objective is bilinear/nonconvex and there is no proof of global optimality. All accepted results are certified only by re-evaluating the exact objective on the returned array. This is primarily designed to chip away at a tight parent boundary and to offer a second route to improvement through related resolutions or local seeks when the parent’s low-resolution basin is narrow.

1.2 Why this could help

The current bound is around 0.38085 and the target is 0.38080, a difference of only ( about 5). At this level, progress normally needs multiple active constraints to be reduced together. Giving all high-lag rows local target equalization tends to avoid repeatedly uncapping the old determining lag or replacing it with a lower lag one step later. The box-preserving equality displacement treats sum of values as a conserved mass, so mass can be shifted simultaneously in many coordinates without projection. Borrowing exact/smoothlog-max smooth relaxation among other methods as initial backups helps get a richer candidate reservoir, but the goal remains efficient exact improvement.
