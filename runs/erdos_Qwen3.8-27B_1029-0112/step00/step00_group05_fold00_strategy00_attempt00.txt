1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea.
The task is a finite-dimensional constrained nonconvex minimax problem. The objective is the maximum over all linear/cyclic-looking lags of a bilinear autocorrelation-like quantity, while the decision vector h must satisfy zero bounds and one exact linear equality. The plan is to solve this directly in the discrete representation used by the authoritative evaluator. It does not approximate the evaluator or replace the objective by a surrogate that changes the boundary terms. Approximate/smoothed objectives are used only to generate candidate feasible vectors; every accepted candidate is judged by the evaluator-equivalent exact objective.

The practical bottleneck is that ordinary unconstrained smoothing then clipping/rescaling can destroy the exact integral constraint, and that the max-over-lags objective is sharp and nonconvex. The main search therefore maintains feasibility after every composed update using a joint projection to the feasible set

F = { h in R^m : 0 <= h_i <= 1, sum_i h_i = m/2 }.

The search combines:
- a parent-preserving multiresolution constructor;
- smooth L∞ approximations, specifically log-sum-exp smoothed maxima, to move toward a flatter cost tables;
- local constrained nonlinear minimization of the smoothed objective with simple bounds and one equality;
- an explicit exact min-max formulation where an auxiliary scalar t is reduced subject to constraints t >= c_ell(h); this is used as a local polish, not as a global guarantee;
- final repair and re-evaluation with the real correlation evaluator.

1.2 Why this can improve.
The supplied parent is already a high-quality local construction. The gap to 0.3808 is very small relative to 0.38085, so the solver must find a precise local descent in the active constraints. Log-sum-exp and auxiliary t formulations can represent the same active constraint set that limits the max, but they are more differentiable and less degenerate than raw max. Multi-resolution transfers, especially integer-repeat parent transfers, expose new discrete lags and weaken boundary artifacts without destroying the area constraint. Local projected movers and exact min-max constraints then make small improvements that a direct projection followed by rescaling cannot recover reliably. The method is heuristic for the global problem: no method below is claimed globally optimizing the nonconvex discrete problem unless it is validated by exact evaluator output.

1.3 Limitations and claims.
- The discrete feasible set is convex, but the objective is nonconvex because of h_i h_{i+d} terms.
- A smoothered log-sum-exp objective is not exactly the evaluator objective, only a smooth approximation with adjustable sharpness.
- The auxiliary scalar formulation is a precise smooth representation of the minimization of max_l c_l(h) as minimize t subject to t >= c_l(h), but the feasible region is nonconvex. It provides local search, not global optimality.
