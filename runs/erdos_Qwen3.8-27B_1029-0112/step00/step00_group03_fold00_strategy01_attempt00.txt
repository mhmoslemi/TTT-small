1.1 Central idea

Use a Multi-Start Smooth-Max Projected Gradient Descent (MM-PGD) method, followed by exact-scoring acceptance and a small deterministic coordinate-exchange polish.

The exact objective is a maximum over a finite set of bilinear overlap constraints. The objective is nonsmooth and nonconvex. The central difference from a pure coordinate/LP-search approach is to replace the maximum by a smooth log-sum-exp surrogate,
\[
F_\beta(h)=\frac{1}{\beta}
\log\left(\sum_{\ell} \exp(\beta s_\ell(h))\right),
\]
where \(s_\ell\) are the exact unormalized shifted overlap scores in the evaluator’s units. Then \(F_\beta\) is smooth and its gradient is a convex combination of the gradients of the active lexicographic constraints, with softened weights. By running a projected gradient line search over the exact discrete feasible set, each smoothed step proposes simultaneous feasible mass redistribution across many coordinates, not merely one-direction pairwise transfers. Only exact objective evaluations using the authoritative evaluator determine whether a candidate is kept.

A refined parent construction is obtained through the following primary mechanisms:

- The supplied parent, if present, is directly used as the main starting point. It is repaired to exact feasibility by a projection onto the box/simplex set, not by clipping and independent rescaling.
- Temperature annealing: start with a low inversion parameter \(\beta\), approximate a broad subgradient descent, then increase \(\beta\) to approximate the true maximum more sharply.
- Multivariate projected line search: every trial step is projected into the exact feasible set. Both bounds and the mass constraint are satisfied before scoring.
- Multi-start and replication: explore perturbed parents, mirrored parents, complements, and finite-resolution duplicates/inteprolations. Any returned dimension is acceptable if exact evaluation is performed with its own \(dx\).
- Exact acceptance: no candidate is deduced from the smooth objective or from any convex surrogate for final selection. The only reported bound is the exact score from the official evaluator or this implementation’s validated equivalent.
