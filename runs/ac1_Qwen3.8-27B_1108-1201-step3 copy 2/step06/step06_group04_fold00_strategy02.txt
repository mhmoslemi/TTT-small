# Approach and Rationale

The central idea is to optimize a step function with non-negative heights that minimizes the evaluation function \( \frac{2n \cdot \max(\text{convolve}(a, a))}{(\sum a)^2} \). We start from a known initial sequence (the modular OK result) and systematically improve it by adding energy sinks (coefficients set to 0) that specifically target the maxima in the convolution. This culling strategy ensures the step function doesn't boost undesirably at any rate by deliberately dampening key indices.

This approach is chosen for its guaranteed descent property and explicit manipulation of high-frequency components. However, it's heuristic and might get trapped in local minima. The complexity analysis of the evaluation function provides a solid starting point for descent.

# Mathematical Specification

- \( n \): sequence length
- \( \text{convolve}(a, a) \): full convolution (non-circular) of sequence with itself
- \( M = \max(\text{convolve}(a, a)) \)
- \( s = \sum a \)

The objective to minimize: \( \frac{2n \cdot M}{s^2} \)

We represent sequences as vectors of non-negative reals with a defined length. The search space is continuous, with constraints:
