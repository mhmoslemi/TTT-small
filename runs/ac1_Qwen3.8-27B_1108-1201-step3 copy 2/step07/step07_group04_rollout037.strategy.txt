# Enhanced Step Function Optimization

## Approach and rationale
The optimization problem is to find a sequence of non-negative numbers that minimizes the objective function $2n \max(\text{convolve}(a,a))/(\sum(a))^2$, where convolve is in full mode. Previous approaches used linear programming best-response followed by damped LP refinement, but we note that these methods may get stuck in local optima for large search spaces. 

We decide to use a more exploratory approach based on:
1. **Genetic algorithms**: These can combine good segments of sequences found earlier, helping to spread excellent traits across the search space.
2. **Simulated annealing**: Allows controlled acceptance of worse states to escape local minima, while still preserving the best candidates found.
3. **Component-wise adaptation**: Focus optimization on the most critical position indices that drive the convolution maximum, enabling more compute-efficient targeted searches.

The key idea is to evolve sequences using a genetic algorithm-like approach but with two enhancements:
- We will use **asynchronous mutation strategies** to modify only the components contributing to the maximum convolution value, significantly reducing computation time per generation.
- We will include **simulated annealing steps** where the probability of accepting worse states decreases exponentially with the generation number, helping to converge faster while maintaining diversity until the optimum is found.

## Mathematical specification
Our candidate representation is a sequence $a = (a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n)$ of $n$ non-negative floats.

We define:
1. The **objective function**:
   $$
   \text{score}(a) = \frac{2 \cdot n \cdot \max\left(\text{convolve}_{\text{full}}(a, a)\right)}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\right)^2}
