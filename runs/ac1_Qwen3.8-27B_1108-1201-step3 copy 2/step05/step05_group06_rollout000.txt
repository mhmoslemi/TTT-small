For the optimization, the key challenge is that the objective is non-smooth (max of linear functions). The log-sum-exp smoothing is a standard approach, but the temperature schedule is crucial.

Let me also think about a different optimization approach: **mirror descent** or **exponential weighting**.

Since we're optimizing over the simplex (after normalization), we can use mirror descent with the negative entropy as the mirror map. The update would be:
`a_new[i] ∝ a_old[i] * exp(-lr * grad[i])`

This is related to "multiplicative weights" and might work well for this problem.

Another idea: **block coordinate descent**. Instead of optimizing one coordinate at a time, optimize a block of coordinates jointly. For the objective `max_k conv[k]`, if we fix all but a block of coordinates, the max is a convex function of the block, and we can optimize it directly.

Actually, let me think about a more specific approach. The objective (with sum=1) is:
`J(a) = 2n * max_k conv(a,a)[k]`

The max is achieved at some set of indices S = {k : conv[k] = max}. At the optimum, |S| >= 2 (otherwise we could adjust a single coordinate to reduce the max).

If we know S, we can formulate a quadratic program: minimize M subject to `conv[k] <= M` for k in S, `sum(a) = 1`, `a >= 0`. The Lagrangian gives us the optimality conditions.

For a practical approach, let me implement:
1. **Smoothed optimization with adaptive temperature**: Use log-sum-exp with a carefully tuned temperature schedule. The key is to start with a high T (to explore) and gradually decrease (to exploit).
2. **Active set identification**: Track which lags are at the max, and focus optimization on making them more equal.
3. **Coordinate descent with needle search**: For each coordinate, find the optimal value via 1D search.
4. **Pairwise blocks**: Update pairs of coordinates together.
5. **Multi-start**: Try different initializations and keep the best.
