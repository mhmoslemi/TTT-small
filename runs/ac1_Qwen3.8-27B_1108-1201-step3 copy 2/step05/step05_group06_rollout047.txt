Let me think about it from a different angle. We want to minimize max_k c[k] subject to sum a = 1, a >= 0.

This is equivalent to: find a probability distribution a on {0, ..., n-1} that minimizes the peak of the convolution a*a.

The convolution a*a at lag k is the probability that X+Y = k where X and Y are independent with distribution a. So we want a distribution whose sum X+Y has the minimum peak probability.

This is a problem in probability theory! We want a distribution on {0,...,n-1} such that the distribution of X+Y (where X, Y are iid) has minimum point mass.

The maximum possible "spread" of X+Y is on {0, ..., 2n-2}. The minimum peak of a distribution on 2n-1 points is 1/(2n-1) (uniform). But we can't achieve uniform for X+Y by choosing a suitable distribution for X (in general).

For the uniform distribution on {0,...,n-1}, X+Y has a triangular distribution on {0,...,2n-2} with peak at n-1 of value n/(n^2) = 1/n. So the "cost" (in our normalized terms) is 1/n, giving overall cost = 2.

For a "concentrated" distribution near one end, X+Y is also concentrated, giving a high peak.

The question is: what distribution minimizes the peak of X+Y?
