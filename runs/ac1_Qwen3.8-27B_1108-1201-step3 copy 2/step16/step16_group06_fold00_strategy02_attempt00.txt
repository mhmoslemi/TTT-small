In this solution, we address the problem of finding a step-function sequence that minimizes a specific evaluation function. The evaluation function is defined as:
\[ J = \frac{2 \times n \times \max(\text{convolve}(a, a))}{(\text{sum}(a))^2} \]
where \( n \) is the length of the sequence, and "full" linear convolution produces \( 2n-1 \) coefficients.

Our approach is to optimize the sequence by iteratively adjusting its position and orientation in the spatial domain, using a projection strategy onto normalized vectors. This method increases the diversity of patterns explored during optimization, avoiding local minima that arise from relying on a single starting configuration.

### Approach and Rationale
We start by converting the initial numerical sequence into complex exponential form (\( e^{i\theta} \)) to access phase information. This transformation allows us to explore sequences through rotations (\( \theta \) from 0 to \( 2\pi \)) and translations (subset indices). We alternate between reducing the sequence length and expanding it to cover more potential minimal configurations efficiently.

### Mathematical Specification
The optimization process uses Heron's formula on normalized \( l2 \) vectors. The projection \( J \) is computed using real-valued coarsening to maintain feasibility. The solver iteratively updates vectors with defined upper bounds and checks for improvements, adjusting length or phase, and refines the sequence through matrix-free operations.
