But wait, for discrete sequences, there are discretization effects. For larger n, we can better approximate the "ideal" continuous shape. So there might be a small improvement with larger n.

Let me also think about what the "ideal" continuous shape is. We want a non-negative function f on [0,1] such that the convolution (f*f)(t) = ∫_0^1 f(s)f(t-s) ds is as flat as possible.

The "flattest" convolution is a constant, which requires f*f = c for all t. In the Laplace/Fourier domain, F(s)^2 = c * (1 - e^{-s})/s (for the Laplace transform of a constant on [0,1]... this is getting into the weeds).

For the Fourier transform: if F(ω) = ∫_0^1 f(x) e^{-iωx} dx, then the Fourier transform of f*f is F(ω)^2. We want F(ω)^2 to have a flat inverse Fourier transform, i.e., F(ω)^2 should be a constant (in the appropriate sense). But F(ω)^2 being constant means |F(ω)| is constant, which means f is a "minimum phase" sequence with flat magnitude... this is the "flat-top" window problem.

The optimal flat-top window (in the sense of minimizing the peak of the convolution) is known to be related to the "Kaiser-Bessel" or "Dolph-Chebyshev" windows, but those minimize the peak sidelobe of the Fourier transform, not the convolution.

For our problem (minimizing peak of f*f), I believe the optimal f is close to a constant (uniform) with small corrections. The corrections should be designed to "fill in" the valleys of the triangular convolution shape.

The triangular convolution (from uniform f) has shape: min(t, 2-t, 1) for t in [0,2] (approximately). The peak is at t=1 (center). To flatten this, we need to reduce the center and increase the edges. This suggests making f larger near the edges and smaller in the center - i.e., a "U-shaped" or "bimodal" distribution.

But wait, if f is U-shaped (large at edges, small in center), then f*f at the center (t=1) involves f(s)*f(1-s) for s in [0,1]. If f is large at s=0 and s=1, and small at s=0.5, then f(0.5)*f(0.5) is small, but f(0.1)*f(0.9) is large. So the center of f*f might still be large.

Actually, for a symmetric U-shaped f (f(x) = f(1-x)), the center of f*f is (f*f)(1) = ∫_0^1 f(s)f(1-s) ds = ∫_0^1 f(s)^2 ds (by the symmetry). This is the L2 norm squared of f, which is minimized by uniform f. So making f U-shaped increases the center of f*f!

This confirms that the uniform sequence is locally optimal in some sense. The improvement must come from a more subtle shape.
