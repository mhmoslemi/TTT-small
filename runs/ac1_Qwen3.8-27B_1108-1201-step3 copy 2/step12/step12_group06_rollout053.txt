Let me think about one more thing: the geometric average of iterates.

In the Nesterov LP, the iterates a_1, a_2, ..., a_T are generated. The "averaged" iterate is:
a_avg = (sum_{t=1}^T w_t * a_t) / (sum w_t)

where w_t are weights. A common choice is w_t = t (linear weights) or w_t = 1 (uniform weights).

The theory says that the averaged iterate has a better bound than the last iterate. Specifically, for the O(1/t) method:
F(a_avg) <= F(a*) + O(1/T)

while the last iterate satisfies:
F(a_T) <= F(a*) + O(1/T) (same rate, but potentially worse constant)

In practice, the geometric average (w_t = gamma^t for gamma close to 1) often gives the best results because it gives more weight to recent iterates (which are closer to the optimum) while still benefiting from averaging.

Let me use a geometric average with gamma = 0.999 (gives effective window of ~1000 iterates).
