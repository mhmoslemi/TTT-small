For the second order at the center:
eps^2 * (q*q)[n-1] = eps^2 * sum_j q[j]*q[n-1-j]

This can be positive, negative, or zero depending on q. If q is symmetric (q[j] = q[n-1-j]), then (q*q)[n-1] = sum q[j]^2 > 0. If q is anti-symmetric (q[j] = -q[n-1-j]), then (q*q)[n-1] = -sum q[j]^2 < 0.

So by choosing an anti-symmetric perturbation, the center value DECREASES to second order! This means the uniform vector is NOT a local minimum of the center value.

But we care about the MAX of all convolution values, not just the center. An anti-symmetric perturbation might reduce the center but increase some other lag.

Let me try a concrete example. n=4, p* = [0.25, 0.25, 0.25, 0.25].
Anti-symmetric perturbation: q = [1, -1, -1, 1] (sum = 0).
p = p* + eps*q = [0.25+eps, 0.25-eps, 0.25-eps, 0.25+eps]

conv[2] = 2*(0.25+eps)^2 + (0.25-eps)^2 = (0.25+eps)^2 + (0.25-eps)^2 + (0.25+eps)^2
Wait let me redo.
conv[2] = p[0]*p[2] + p[1]*p[1] + p[2]*p[0] = (0.25+eps)(0.25-eps) + (0.25-eps)^2 + (0.25-eps)(0.25+eps)
= 2*(0.0625 - eps^2) + (0.0625 - 0.5*eps + eps^2)
