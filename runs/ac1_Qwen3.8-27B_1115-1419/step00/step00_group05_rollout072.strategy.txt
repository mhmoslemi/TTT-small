**1. Approach and Rationale**
The central idea for this improvement is inspired by harmonic analysis and frequency domain optimization. We will treat the step function coefficients as time domain signals and analyze their spectral content to lower the maximum convolution value. The provided evaluate_sequence function computes a ratio that depends on the L-infinity norm of the convolution and the L2 norm of the sequence, scaled by the length. 

In signal processing, non-negative signals with limited autocorrelation often have concentrated frequency content. This approach optimizes the spectral distribution to flatten the autocorrelation, reducing its maximum. Unlike the previous block perturbation, which works in time domain space, spectral optimization allows us to model the constraints and objectives more directly.

The key innovation is to relax the problem slightly by allowing optimization of the sequence's volume rather than fixed length, and to ensure that we maintain the required nonzero sum even after clipping operations. We will search for sequences that minimize the L-infinity norm of the convolution while keeping the masses distributed in a way that avoids large time domain coefficients exceeding 1000 (which the evaluator will clamp). 

This spectral method ensures more direct convergence to the mathematical minimum rather than relying on iterative refinements that might get stuck in local minima. The primary operations will include:

- Fast Fourier Transform-based autocorrelation computation (much faster than direct convolution)
- Gradient-based spectral optimization using analytical differentiation
- Non-negative transformation back to time domain via inverse FFT
- Adaptive mutation rates based on progress

**2. Mathematical Specification**
We will represent a candidate sequence as `x = [x0, x1, ..., xn-1]` of length n (initially set as 512, a power-of-two value commonly used in FFT algorithms). 

The objective is: `O(x) = 2n * max(|X[k]| for k=0 to n-1) / ((sum(xi))^2)`

- `x` is the sequence [x0, x1, ..., xn-1]
- `X = np.fft.fft(x) / sqrt(n)` (normalized by n^0.5 to ensure Parseval)

- `sum(xi)` is adjusted to align with the 0.01 threshold in case of aggressive optimization

- Energy consrvation adjustment post-clamping
