**Key insight: The problem is related to "minimum peak-to-average power ratio" (PAPR) or "minimum autocorrelation" sequences.**

In radar/communications, the analog problem is: find a sequence (possibly complex) that minimizes the peak-to-mean ratio of its autocorrelation. For real non-negative sequences, the problem is more constrained.

One approach used in the literature is **successive interference cancellation (SIC)** or **temporal SLS (Search for Least Squares)** methods:
1. Start with an initial sequence
2. Identify the peak in the autocorrelation
3. Subtract a "replica" of the peak from the sequence
4. Repeat

But this is for complex sequences. For non-negative sequences, we can't subtract.

**Another idea: Use the Fourier domain**

The autocorrelation R(k) is the inverse DFT of |A(ω)|^2 (for circular correlation). For linear correlation, it's similar but with windowing effects.

If we want to minimize max R(k), we want |A(ω)|^2 to be as "spread out" as possible in the time domain, which means A(ω) should have a "flat" magnitude in the frequency domain... but that's for complex sequences.

For non-negative sequences, A(ω) = sum a[i] * e^{-iωi}, and |A(ω)|^2 = A(ω)*A(-ω) (for real a). The constraint a >= 0 means A(ω) has the form of a "minimum phase" spectrum or similar.
