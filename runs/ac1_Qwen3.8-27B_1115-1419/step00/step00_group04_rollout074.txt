Another idea: use a "wrapped" version. In the circular convolution, we can have perfectly flat spectra (using roots of unity). The linear convolution is related to the circular convolution of a "zero-padded" version.

Actually, let me think about this differently. The problem is equivalent to: find a polynomial A(z) = sum a[i] z^i with a[i] >= 0 such that the coefficient of z^k in A(z)^2 is minimized for all k.

This is related to "Giacoletto" or "low autocorrelation" sequences. For complex sequences, there are perfect sequences (Golay, Barker), but for non-negative reals, it's more constrained.

For the immediate problem, I think the best approach is:
1. Use the central binomial construction for various n
2. Apply a local search to improve on it (particularly targeting the tail b[n]...b[2n-2])
3. The local search should be able to squeeze out a bit more

For n around 50-200, the central binomial gives score ≈ 1.58-1
