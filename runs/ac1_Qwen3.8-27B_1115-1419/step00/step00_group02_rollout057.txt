The objective is $f = 2n \cdot \max(b) / S^2$. Let's denote $t = \max(b)/S^2$, so $f = 2nt$.

However, there's a key observation: if we fix $t$ and consider the problem of finding $a$ that minimizes $\max_k b_k$ (i.e., the Chebyshev approximation problem in the space of convolutions), we can use iterative methods.

Let me try a different approach: **iterative proportional fitting (IPF)** or **multiplicative weights**.

The idea: Start with a feasible $a$. At each step, find the $b_k$ that is at the maximum. Then, scale down the $a_i$ that contribute most to that $b_k$, and scale up the $a_i$ that contribute least. This is similar to the "lasso" or "coordinate descent" approach.

More concretely:
1. Compute $b = \text{conv}(a, a)$.
2. Find $k^* = \arg\max b_k$.
3. For each $i$ that contributes to $b_{k^*}$ (i.e., $a_i \cdot a_{k^*-i} > 0$), reduce $a_i$ slightly.
4. Redistribute the "mass" to the $a_i$ that don't contribute to $b_{k^*}$.
5. Repeat.

This is essentially what the gradient descent does, but in a more structured way.

Let me also consider the **Frank-Wolfe (conditional gradient)** method:
- At each step, solve a linear program: minimize $\langle g, v \rangle$ subject to $v \geq 0$, $\sum v = 1$, where $g$ is the subgradient.
- The LP solution is $v = e_j$ where $j = \arg\min g_j$ (the vertex of the simplex that minimizes the linear objective).
- So the Frank-Wolfe update is: $a \leftarrow (1-t)a + t \cdot e_{j^*}$ where $j^* = \arg\min g_j$ and $t$ is the step size.

This is essentially a "move a small amount of mass to the position with the most negative gradient" algorithm.

The step size $t$ can be chosen via line search or the standard FW schedule $t_k = 2/(k+2)$.
