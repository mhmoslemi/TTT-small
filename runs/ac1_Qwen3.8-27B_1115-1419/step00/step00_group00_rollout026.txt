That's terrible! The geometric progression is bad because the last element dominates.

The issue is that for very asymmetric sequences, the self-product of the largest element (at the last lag) dominates the convolution.

So we need a balance: asymmetric enough to reduce the center peak, but not so asymmetric that the self-product of the largest element dominates.

Let me think about this differently. The constraint is:
- conv[0] = a[0]^2
- conv[2n-2] = a[n-1]^2
- conv[n-1] = sum a[i]*a[n-1-i]

All of these must be small relative to sum^2.

So we need: max(a[0]^2, a[n-1]^2, sum a[i]*a[n-1-i], and all other lags) to be small relative to sum^2.

In particular, a[0]^2 and a[n-1]^2 should be small, meaning a[0] and a[n-1] should be small. But then the sum is dominated by the middle elements.

This suggests a "bell-shaped" sequence might be good: small at the edges, large in the middle. But that's symmetric, and for symmetric sequences, conv[n-1] = sum a[i]^2 which is the peak.

Unless... the sequence is "anti-symmetric" in a non-negative sense: large on one side, small on the other, with a smooth transition.
