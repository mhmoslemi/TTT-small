Actually, let me think about it in Fourier domain. The auto-convolution in time domain corresponds to |FFT(a)|² in frequency domain (for circular convolution). For linear convolution, it's a bit different, but the idea is similar.

If we want the auto-convolution to be flat, we want |FFT(a)|² to be related to a specific pattern. A flat auto-convolution would correspond to a specific frequency response.

But for LINEAR convolution, the relationship is:
FFT(convolve(a,a)) = FFT(a) * FFT(a) (with zero-padding to length 2n-1 or more)

So if we want conv(a,a) to be constant (= sum_a²/(2n-1)), we need |FFT(a)|² to be the FFT of a constant sequence of length 2n-1, which is a delta at frequency 0. That means FFT(a) should be a delta at frequency 0, i.e., a is constant. But a constant has a triangular auto-convolution, not flat! Contradiction.

The issue is that linear convolution of length n with itself gives length 2n-1, and the "flat" target of length 2n-1 has a specific FFT that can't be decomposed as a product of two identical shorter FFTs (except the trivial constant case).

So the optimal is NOT a perfectly flat convolution. It's the closest we can get.
