Actually, this is exactly the problem of designing a sequence with low peak-to-sidelobe ratio in its autocorrelation (or in this case, its full convolution, which is related).

The classical solution is to use "low autocorrelation" sequences like Barker sequences, but those are for ±1 sequences. For non-negative sequences, the problem is different.

One approach that might work: Use a "cosine-tapered" sequence. The idea is:
a[i] = A * cos(pi * i / (2*n)) for i = 0, ..., n-1

This gives a window function that tapers to zero at the edges. The convolution of a cosine window with itself has known properties - it's a raised-cosine, which has lower sidelobes than a rectangular window.

Actually, let me think about this more carefully. The convolution of a sequence with itself is related to the squared magnitude of its Fourier transform (by the convolution theorem):
conv(a,a) = IFFT(|FFT(a)|^2)

So minimizing max(conv(a,a)) is equivalent to making |FFT(a)|^2 as flat as possible (in the frequency domain). This is the same as making the power spectral density flat!

For a non-negative sequence a, its FFT is A[k] = sum_i a[i] * e^{-2*pi*i*k/n}. The constraint |A[k]|^2 ≈ constant for all k is the "flat spectrum" condition.

But we also have the constraint that a >= 0, which in the frequency domain means that the inverse FFT of |A[k]|^2 must be non-negative. This is a difficult constraint.

However, this gives us a useful reformulation: We want to find a non-negative sequence whose power spectral density (PSD) is as flat as possible. The objective is then:
2*n*max(conv)/(sum)^2 = 2*n*max(PSD_inverse)/ (sum)^2
