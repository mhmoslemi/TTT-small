I think the fundamental issue is: for any real sequence a, the circular autocorrelation at lag 0 is sum(a^2) >= (sum(a))^2/n by Cauchy-Schwarz. And the linear convolution at the center is close to the circular autocorrelation at lag 0 for large n.

Wait, that can't happen for non-negative sequences, right? The center convolution has the most terms (n terms), and all terms are non-negative, so it should be the largest.

Actually no! The center convolution is sum_j a[j]*a[n-1-j]. This has n terms but each term is a[j]*a[n-1-j]. An off-center convolution, say at lag 2, is sum_j a[j]*a[n-1+2-j] = sum_j a[j]*a[n+1-j]. This also has n-2 terms (fewer). But if the sequence has a spike, the center value could be small while an off-center value captures the spike.

Example: a = (0, 0, ..., 0, M, 0, ..., 0) (spike at position p).
conv[n-1] = sum_j a[j]*a[n-1-j] = M^2 (only when p = n-1-p, i.e., p = (n-1)/2).
If p ≠ (n-1)/2, then conv[n-1] = 0 (the spike doesn't meet itself).
But conv[2p] = M^2 (the spike meets itself at lag 2p).
And conv[2(n-1-p)] = M^2 at the other end.

So for a spike NOT at the center, the peak of the convolution is at an off-center lag, and it's M^2. The center value is 0. S = M. Score = 2n*M^2/M^2 = 2n. Bad.

For a spike AT the center (p = (n-1)/2 for odd n), conv[n-1] = M^2, score = 2n. Also bad.

So single spikes are bad. We need spread-out sequences.
