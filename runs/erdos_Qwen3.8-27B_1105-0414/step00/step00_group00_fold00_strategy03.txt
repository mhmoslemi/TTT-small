1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea and why it can improve the supplied construction
The authoritative objective is the maximum finite-support overlap of sampled values of h and sampled values of 1-h. It is a sharp maximum of bilinear quantities and is nonconvex in the constraints 0 <= h_i <= 1 and sum h_i = n/2. A straight convex relaxation does not certify the returned construction, and a circular autocorrelation surrogate ignores the left and right boundary terms used by the evaluator. The proposed algorithm therefore optimizes the exact evaluator end to end.

The central algorithm is a hybrid exact integer transport and global low-dimensional parameterization method:

- All construction candidates are represented on a finite grid as q_i / S, where q_i is an integer and S is a fixed power of two. This representation makes feasibility exact and makes objective comparisons exact relative to the same discretized finite-support model.
- To explore globally, a coefficient vector z generates a smooth profile on the sample grid through a normalized Chebyshev/Cosine basis. The smooth profile is then forced into the box and mean constraints by joint projection, and then quantized to the integer feasible representation. Every candidate is scored only with the exact finite-support evaluator, upgraded or downgraded by exact integer correlation.
- To exploit locally, a pair-transport local descent transfers mass between coordinates while preserving the integer sum exactly. This works directly on the discretized representation and can improve a candidate without leaving the feasible set. Basin randomization connects local descents and avoids immediate plateaus.
- The parent construction, if supplied through initial_h_values, is used only as an executable numeric parent and benchmark. It is validated and coerced when needed; the final returned array is never constructed from a literal table and is always rebuilt or repaired using the algorithm.

This is materially different from the earlier circular smooth-max and direct Boolean-swap digests. The optimization here is over a low-dimensional family of smooth profiles mapped to a jointly feasible integer sample vector, followed by exact metal transport. It uses the exact objective and preserves both bounds and sum by a joint continuous projection followed by integer round-off correction, not clipping plus rescaling.

1.2 Bottlenecks addressed
- Objective sharpness: The hard maximum over lags causes nonsmooth stalls. The integer grid and low-dimensional random global phase find alternative basins; the exact evaluator prevents merit function mistakes from wasting time.
- Feasibility: Continuous clipping and renormalization separately usually break either the box or the sum. The exact box/mean projected continuous projection and integer sum correction provide a joint feasible repair formula.
- Boundary terms: Finite-support correlation is used unchanged for scoring. No circular-periodic or omitted-boundary surrogate is used.
- Nonconvexity: The method is a certified heuristic. It does not claim global optimality. A final candidate must be valid and the reported bound is the authoritative objective or an upper bound strictly not smaller than that objective.
