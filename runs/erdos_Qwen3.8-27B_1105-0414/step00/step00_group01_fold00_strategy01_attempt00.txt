1. Approach and rationale
--------------------------
The supplied parent construction is already near the current record. The intended improvement is therefore a high-precision local minimax refinement of an already good feasible vector, not a new coarse global construction. The main difficulty is that the true objective is a finite maximum of terms that are not convex in h, because each term contains products h_i(1-h_j). Consequently, a direct convex-programming approach would generally not solve the requested objective exactly. The proposed algorithm uses the original evaluator exactly for every score that can be returned; solvers are used only as local search devices and their outputs are always re-judged with the evaluator metric after feasibility repair.

The central scientific idea is to solve the finite-dimensional discretized minimax problem:
minimize over h and auxiliary scalar t the value t subject to t being at least every allowed lag overlap, subject to box constraints and the exact mass constraint. This is a smooth but nonconvex nonlinear program. We use two complementary local formulations:

1. An exact minimax SLSQP formulation that introduces t as a decision variable and imposes one inequality per lag. Its objective is linear: minimize t. This formulation tends to balance the currently active worst lags.

2. An adaptive smoothing formulation that minimizes a soft maximum of the lag costs over h alone. This has far fewer constraints and is used to create escape directions from the parent. The soft objective is deliberately not the final acceptance criterion; it only generates candidate vectors that are then scored by the exact evaluator.

The search is multi-start with parent-centered, smoothed, harmonic-perturbed, active-gradient-perturbed, pairwise averaged, and coarse-resolution candidates. The parent construction is always kept as the fallback candidate. No claim of global optimality is made; all returned values are exact evaluator recomputed scores.

The key implementation bottleneck to address is threefold:
- Maintain the nontrivial boundary-aware correlation objective exactly, including both positive and negative lags and the finite-support sum counts.
- Maintain the mass constraint while respecting the box constraint, because clipping and rescaling separately can destroy feasibility.
- Guarantee the final returned vector has exact floating-point sum equal to n_points / 2 and finite bounds, while still retaining the tiny numerical improvement needed to cross the target threshold. The final return mechanism performs dyadic quantization with integer count repair, so that every returned scalar is a dyadic rational and the exact vector sum is an exact float.

The method improves over a naive one-start solver by using high-precision objective definitions, gradient information, reconciliation between exact and smooth objectives, and specially designed candidate domains that preserve the parent structure. 2. Mathematical specification
-----------------------------

  For q ≥ 0:
    F_q(h) = Σ_{i=0}^{N-1-q} h_i (1 - h_{i+q}).

    Let m = -q. Then valid pairs are j = i - m for i = m, ..., N-1:
    F_{-m}(h) = Σ_{i=m}^{N-1} h_i (1 - h_{i-m}).

The tested objective is:
