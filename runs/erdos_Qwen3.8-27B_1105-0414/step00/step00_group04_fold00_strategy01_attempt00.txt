1. Approach and rationale

Core idea. Treat the discrete problem as a nonsmooth constrained minimization over a finite-dimensional vector. The prior strategy mentioned in the task was to smooth the maximum and apply log-sum-exp/SLSQP-style epigraph optimizations. This plan is deliberately different. It uses an exact finite-time algorithm based on deterministic convex one-dimensional subproblems over pairs of coordinates, combined with exact dyadic feasibility, incremental pair merit evaluation, and randomized perturbation restarts.

The central observations are:

1. The evaluator objective is the exact nonnegative maximum over all full linear correlations between the vector and its pointwise complement. It is nonconvex because of bilinear terms h_i(1-h_j), but it is nonsmooth max of correlations.

2. If the total mass of two entries is held fixed and only those two entries move, each affected correlation term becomes either constant, affine, or a convex quadratic in the moving coordinate. Hence the maximum over the correlations is convex along a fixed-mass two-coordinate pair move. The exact one-dimensional minimum of that subproblem can be found reliably without allowing the vector to leave bounds or violating the mass constraint.

3. The hard constraints can be enforced exactly, essentially alone, using a dyadic integer grid h_i = y_i/D with y_i integer. For even n_points=70 the mass target is an exact integer target K=n*D/2. All accepted moves keep the current vector at the correct mass and inside [0,1]. This avoids the common trap of clipping and rescaling separately and gives exact feasibility without using a parent table literal.

4. The search remains local in principle. It does not claim global optimality. It improves the supplied parent if the pair-coordinate space contains an improvement accessible from the parent basin. It escapes local plateaus only through bounded random mutations and restarts, not dedicated convex global optimization. The original evaluator is used for every published solution, so surrogate and convex claims never affect the accepted reported bound.

Bottleneck addressed. Smoothed maximum methods can get stalled near a smooth surrogate-optimal point where the actual evaluator changes because of active nonsmooth constraints, and continuous feasibility manipulation can be disturbing. This method evaluates and improves the actual nonsmooth objective exactly, preserves both feasibility constraints exactly via integer mass target K, and uses the only distinctive mathematical property available: fixed-pair coordinates are guaranteed univariate convex subproblems.

Choices and hypotheses. Justified: univariate pair objective convexity, exact evaluator reuse, integer feasibility mechanism, deterministic fallback. Heuristic: whether random restarts find a better basin than direct pair descent from the parent, and what denominator and mutation strength are best. The method provides no proof of solving the original nonconvex finite-dimensional problem globally.
