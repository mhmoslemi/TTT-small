1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central mathematical difficulty. We are minimizing an L-infinity-type functional
    M(h) = max_s dx * sum_i h[i]*(1-h[i+s]),
    s = n_points-1, n_points-2, ..., -n_points+1,
    over the nonconvex hypercube/affine intersection {0 <= h[i] <= 1, sum(h)=n_points/2}. The objective is highly nonsmooth and, due to products h[i]h[j], nonconvex. This is why we do not treat CVXPY once and assume a global optimum. We use the exact evaluator only for acceptance of candidates.

1.2 Chosen primary approach. The main mechanism is a feasible, exact-acceptance, smooth-plus-active-set local optimizer:
    (a) use a stable log-sum-exp surrogate for max_s R_s(h) to get dense informative gradients;
    (b) move in projected feasible directions preserving the sum and box constraints;
    (c) whenever the current point is nonsmooth or locally difficult, solve a linear program (LP) on the exact-active linearizations of many or all shift values to generate a primal steepest-like descent direction;
    (d) every proposed point is repaired to feasible and judged by the authoritative exact score. A candidate is returned only if it is valid and gives a smaller exact score than the current best. No global optimality is claimed.

1.3 Why this should help the parent construction. The parent is presumably close to best known value; improvements of order 5e-5 are possible only by resolving active lag conflicts. The LP on active lag linearizations directly targets the active restrictions sooner; log-sum-exp and stochastic variation permits escaping small local minima around parent, and exact local searches/P/S-metric with moment composition retains old validity.

1.4 Startup treatment. When `initial_h_values` exists it is read as parent and optimized at its native length.
