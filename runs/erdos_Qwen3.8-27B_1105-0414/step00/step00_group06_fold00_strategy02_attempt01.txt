1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

Use the exact evaluator throughout the search, but optimize the hard maximum with a chain of smooth log-sum-exp models in the original grid space. For each candidate vector, the returned upper bound is always the exact evaluator value of a feasible vector, never the value of a smoothed surrogate. A structured multi-resolution search produces diverse feasible grid candidates. Selected candidates are then polished with a multidimensional analytic-gradient chain using SLSQP: at stage `tau` the objective is the smooth logarithm of the sum of exponentiated raw correlation values. Increasing `tau` progressively approaches the exact max objective while retaining smooth analytical derivatives. Every optimized vector is finally given an exact original-objective pair-transfer line polish to remove small residual oversharges.

This is materially different from active-set LP/epigraph loops that construct a linear trust-region feasibility problem, and it is different from a pure stochastic or population search. The proposed method uses a globally available smooth approximation model with analytical gradients, makes the non-differentiable maximize problem locally traversable, and then validates only by the authoritative evaluator.

1.2 Why it can improve the supplied construction

The supplied construction is already close to the lower target. The required reduction is very small, typically `3.8085e-1` down to at most `1.1e-4`. Such a reduction is often not available through coarse random moves, but it can be available by moving mass in the most active overlap lags, if max activity is identifiable. Smooth log-sum-exp reveals a diagonalizable stochastic gradient on a neighborhood of near-maximum lags. A high `tau` stage approximates a binding constraint gradient while preserving equality and box information in SLSQP. Exact pair-line polish then corrects any error made by the smooth model or cuts small overshoot near maximum constraints using only transfers that are exactly sum-and-box feasible.

The primary bottlenecks addressed are:

- `np.correlate` includes a maximum over all `2 n - 1` lags. A direct gradient of `max` loses information for near-binding lags; log-sum-exp at moderate `tau` gives them partial gradient weight, avoiding loss of one-dimensional movement or parent basin.
- `sum(h)=n/2` is an equality. Simple clipping/rescaling violates feasibility. Pair transfers and monotone root-based box projection keep sum and bounds feasible.
- High-stage max approximations are non-smooth or under-differentiable; a staged `tau` sequence lets SLSQP make useful feasible steps before the final exact maximum is invoked.
- The local optimum in the parent is close to feasibility tightness. Selected candidate diversity avoids detecting an assignment but no modifications, using nearby transformed parents plus mathematical seed families.

- The original accepted objective is exact `max_j dx*np.correlate(h,1-h,'full')[j]`.

2. Mathematical specification

2.1 Grid representation and objective

- Decision vector: `h ∈ [0, 1]^n`.
