1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The supplied objective is a nonconvex finite-support crossover problem:

C(h) = max_m dx · [np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")]_m, dx = 2/n,

subject to 0 ≤ h_i ≤ 1 and sum_i h_i = n/2. The record construction is very close to the desired threshold; improving C by only 5×10⁻⁵ does not require discovering a global minimum, but it does require a feasible search mechanism that can move all coordinates coherently while never replacing the evaluator’s boundary-aware linear correlation by an autocorrelation, circular embedding, 1 − min autocorrelation, Fourier-only surrogate, or any incomplete objective.

The primary approach chosen here is an adaptive threshold-attainment method. Instead of minimizing C(h) directly with a log-sum-exp smoothing, LP epigraph, or fixed surrogate objective, this method treats the problem as a sequence of level-set feasibility problems. For a decreasing target τ, it tries to find any feasible h with all scaled correlation outputs close to or below τ by minimizing a smooth positive-part penalty
