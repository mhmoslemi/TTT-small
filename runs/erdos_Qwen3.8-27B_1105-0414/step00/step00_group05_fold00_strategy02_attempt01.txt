1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

The evaluator is a finite-support, bounded, nonconvex minimax problem:

C(h) = max_l dx * Cor_l(h, 1-h), dx = 2/n,

with Cor_l being the corresponding entry of
`np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")`. The supplied parent construction is already in the intended basin, so the desired improvement is extremely small. The bottleneck is therefore not a wholesale new construction, but a local mechanism capable of moving all coordinates coherently once while taking the exact active-overlap structure into account.

The proposed primary method is a trust-region linearized epigraph minimax method. At a current continuous iterate y, every raw overlap entry R_m(y, h-grid) is bilinear in h. Its first-order expansion around y is

R_m(h) ≈ R_m(y) + g_m(y)^T (h - y).

We solve a genuine linear program in an epigraph variable t:

minimize t  
subject to t ≥ R_m(y) + g_m(y)^T(h - y) for all correlation-output entries m,  
0 ≤ h_i ≤ 1, sum_i h_i = n/2,  
||h - y||∞ ≤ ρ.

This is not a log-sum-exp or Lp scalar penalty. It is a first-order approximation of the true min Gmax objective itself. At every step, the exact original discrete objective is evaluated for the generated LP iterate after joint feasibility restoration, and only exact evaluator scores are admitted as valid stored candidates. A trust-region schedule squeezes or widens ρ, changes the BP linearization point, and provides robustness to nonconvex curvature. The returned solution is always obtained from a canonical, single-shot feasibility construction, never from successive clipping and rescaling.

This approach is materially different from stochastic dyadic pair-transfer search and from continuous log-sum-exp temperature descent. It retains a true epigraph of the maximum-overlap constraint and generates a convex linearization of all active and nearly active finite-support overlap terms at once. It uses the exact metric for acceptance, not a surrogate, and treats the LP as a mineralized proposed trial, not an exact solution of the original nonconvex problem.

1.2 Why it addresses the task

The improvement from 0.38085 to 0.3808 is small, so the search needs:

1. feasible exact representations with enough resolution;
2. a search direction that uses the full vector gradient information from the actual correlation outputs;
3. corrections for the finite-support boundary terms;
4. exact scoring of some nonlinear traverse steps rather than assuming the linear model is exact.

The LP construct may move many coordinates simultaneously while obeying the unit-hyperplane and box exactly inside the subproblem. The trust radius ensures small steps stay in a reasonable linearization range; exact trials on rounded or projected points are accepted only if the exact C5 improves. The parent is always retained, while nearby and moderately different resolutions are sampled as alternative basins. A structured anti-symmetry construction is used as an additional starting variant and exact fallback, but the core optimizer remains the full-dimension LP-trust method.

This is a local nonconvex optimization mechanism. No convexity of the original overlap objective is claimed. Linearization cannot guarantee a global minimizer of the bilinear finite-support problem.
