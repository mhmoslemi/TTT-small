Approach and rationale:
The central idea of this approach is to use oscillatory patterns as base functions and optimize their amplitudes and frequencies to minimize the maximum overlap. We hypothesize that the optimal solution will be dominated by a few high-frequency oscillations with carefully chosen amplitudes and phases. This approach combines benefits of spectral optimization with structure-based exploration to find candidates that are not easily locatable by gradient-based methods alone.

The key limitations are:
1. We assume the optimal solution can be well-approximated by truncated Fourier series
2. The optimization space is high-dimensional but potentially structured
3. We trade off between exploration of different frequency bases and exploitation of known good patterns

The method should be better than previous approaches at finding strongly oscillatory candidates that may set new records.

Mathematical specification:
We represent the function h(x) as a linear combination of Fourier modes:
h(x) = a0/2 + Σ_{k=1}^K [ak*cos(2πk x/λ) + bk*sin(2πk x/λ)]

With constraints:
0 ≤ h(x) ≤ 1 for all x
∫_0^2 h(x)dx = 1

The continuous variables are the amplitudes (a_k, b_k) and parameters (λ). The objective is to minimize the maximum overlap defined by:
C_5 = max_{k} ∫ h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx

We enforce constraints via penalty terms in the objective function and use Gaussian Process regression to estimate gradient components.
