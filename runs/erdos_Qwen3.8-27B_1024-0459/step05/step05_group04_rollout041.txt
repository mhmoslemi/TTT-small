Let me also add one more idea: "correlation-targeted" moves. Instead of just looking at the top correlations and trying to reduce them, I can:
1. Compute the "excess" of each correlation above the average
2. Focus on the positions that contribute to the largest excess
3. Try moves that specifically reduce the excess at the worst correlation

This is a more targeted approach that might be more effective than the generic "top-k shifts" approach.

For a specific correlation at shift s (where s = n-1+d, d>0):
corr[s] = Σ_{i=0}^{n-1-d} h[i] * (1-h[i+d])

The "contributors" are the positions i where h[i]=1 and h[i+d]=0. To reduce corr[s], I can:
- Move a 1 from position i (where h[i]=1, h[i+d]=0) to position j (where h[j]=0)
  - This reduces corr[s] by 1 (removing the contribution at i)
  - But it might increase corr[s'] for other shifts s'

So the "best" move is one that reduces the max correlation by 1 without increasing any other correlation. This is a very strong condition that's hard to satisfy. A more relaxed condition is: reduces the max correlation by 1 and doesn't increase the second-max by more than 1.

In practice, the local search will find such moves if they exist. The key is to generate enough candidates.

def make_swap_delta(h, i, j, n):
    r = 2 * n - 1
    dr = np.zeros(r)
    lo = max(i, 0)
    hi = min(i + n - 1, r - 1)

        dr[lo:hi+1] += -(1.0 - h[i + n - 1 - ks])
