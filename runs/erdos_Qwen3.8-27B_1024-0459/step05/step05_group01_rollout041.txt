For each n in S, compute the delta contribution.

The delta contribution at n is:
h'[n+k]*(1-h'[n]) - h[n+k]*(1-h[n])

where h' differs from h only at i and j.

To compute this efficiently:
- h'[m] = h[m] for m ≠ i,j
- h'[i] = 0, h'[j] = 1

So h'[n+k] = h[n+k] unless n+k ∈ {i,j}
And h'[n] = h[n] unless n ∈ {i,j}

The delta at n:
= h'[n+k]*(1-h'[n]) - h[n+k]*(1-h[n])

Let a = h[n+k], b = h[n] (old values)
Let a' = h'[n+k], b' = h'[n] (new values)

a' = a unless n+k=i (then a'=0) or n+k=j (then a'=1)
b' = b unless n=i (then b'=0) or n=j (then b'=1)

Delta = a'*(1-b') - a*(1-b)

This is computable in O(1) given the old values and knowing which positions are i and j.
