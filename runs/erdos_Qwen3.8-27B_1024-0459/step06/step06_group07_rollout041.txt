Now let me think about the SLSQP polish. After finding a good binary solution, I can try to slightly adjust values away from 0/1 to reduce the maximum correlation.

The key idea: if the maximum correlation is at lag k*, then:
c_{k*} = sum_m h[m] * (1-h[m-lag*])

To reduce this, I want to decrease h[m] for m where h[m]=1 and h[m-lag*]=0, or increase h[m-lag*] for m-lag* where h[m-lag*]=0.

A simple approach: for each position m with h[m]=1 that contributes to the peak, slightly decrease h[m]. For positions m' with h[m']=0 that would reduce the peak if increased, slightly increase h[m'].

Let me add a 2-swap move to the local search:

A 2-swap: pick i1, i2 (both ones) and j1, j2 (both zeros), swap all four.
Change in c: A[i1] + A[i2] - A[j1] - A[j2]
New max: max(c + A[i1] + A[i2] - A[j1] - A[j2])

This is O(1) to compute the new correlation vector (element-wise), and O(nfull) to find the max. But trying all pairs of (i1,i2) and (j1,j2) is O(n⁴) which is way too much.

A compromise: fix one pair (i1, j1) and then try to find the best (i2, j2):
