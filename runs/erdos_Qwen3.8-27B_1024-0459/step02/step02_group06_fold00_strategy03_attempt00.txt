### Approach and Rationale

**Central Idea:**  
Leverage a hybrid optimization strategy combining **multi-resolution analysis** and **gradient-based refinement** to escape local optima discovered in the parent solution. Instead of relying solely on stochastic gradient descent or Bayesian optimization, this approach methodically explores function structures across varying scales and uses gradient information for targeted improvements. The bottleneck—local optimality in the parent solution—is addressed through precise feature localization and directional gradient updates.

**Key Differences:**
- **Variable Resolution Focus:** Systematically tests landscapes at 450, 900, and 1800 points, weighted toward higher resolutions last.
- **Gradient-guided Refinement:** Uses directional gradient components to identify edges and minimize peak overlap without stochastic drift.
- **Explicit Feature Tracking:** Monitors integral overlap across integer shifts to validate feature preservation in upscaled versions.

**Limtations and Assumptions:**  
- Scalable limits: Higher resolutions (n=1800) may slow convergence if feature detection fails. Budget-driven resolution priority minimizes this.
- Gradient effectiveness depends on smoothness: Sharp transitions in the optimal function may limit gradient signal. This is compensated for by explicit overlap tracking.

### Mathematics and Objectives
There.  
Representation:  
- A step function \( h: [0,2] \to [0,1] \) represented at \( n \) sample points as a vector \( \mathbf{h} \in \mathbb{R}^n \) with \( h[i] \in [0,1] \)
- Constraint: \( \frac{2}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n h[i] = 1 \) or equivalently \( \sum_{i} h[i] = \frac{n}{2} \)

**Objective:**  
- Compute  
  \[
  C_5 = \max_k \left( \frac{2}{n} \cdot (\text{maximum of } \|k\|\text{-lagged correlation between } h \text{ and } 1-h \text{ without clamping})
  \]

**Notation:**
- \( dx = \frac{2}{n} \)
- \( \text{score}(\mathbf{h}) = \max_k \left( dx \cdot \max_{i} |\text{cross-correlation}(h, 1-h)[i]| \right) \)

### Implementation Procedure

#### 2. Resolution-Aligned Initialization

#### 3. Directed Feature Refinement  
- **Track integral overlap:** For each shift \( k \), monitor  
  \[
  o(k) = \left( \sum_{i} h[i] \cdot (1 - h[i+k]) \right) \cdot dx
  \]
  Focuses optimization on minimizing peaks.  
- **Gradient pitching:**  
  For intervals with high overlap, use gradient descent in direction opposite peak correlation penalization.  
  \[
  g(i) = 
  \begin{cases} 
  1 - 2h[i] & \text{if } i \text{ is shift k - peak} \\
  0          & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  \]
