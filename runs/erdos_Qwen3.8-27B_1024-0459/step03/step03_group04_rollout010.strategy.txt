1. Approach and rationale
The central idea is to treat the search for the optimal step function h(x) as a structured optimization over function spaces. The Erdős minimum overlap constant C represents the supremum over all shifts of the overlap between h(x) and its shifted versions. 

We leverage a multi-resolution optimization framework combined with Frank-Wolfe style conditional gradient updates to navigate the complex solution space. Unlike the penalty-based method in the provided code, this approach transforms the optimization via moment conditions and uses an exponential family smoothing technique to approximate the non-convex overlap measures.

The bottleneck of standard gradient methods in such problems is their tendency to get trapped in approximations that are locally optimal but globally suboptimal. This approach addresses this by:

- Using multi-resolution coupling to balance exploitation (refining promising candidates) and exploration (scanning different resolutions)
- Performing structured updates using basis functions that correspond to the problem's symmetries
- Projecting solutions onto valid probability distributions with consistent integral normalization
- Applying exponential weights to penalize large overlaps while maintaining feasibility

This differs significantly from the original optimization path by reinterpreting the overlap constraint through entropy measures and using a dual formulation. It also requires building entirely new helper functions for correlation and basis transformations, avoiding reuse of existing code blocks from the parent implementation.

2. Mathematical specification
The objective is to minimize:
   C5 = max_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} dx * \sum_{i=0}^{n-1-k} h[i] * (1 - h[i+k])

Over the domain defined by:
   h: [0,2] → [0,1], 
   \int_{0}^{2} h(x) dx = 1

We use a discrete approximation with samples at (i+0.5)dx, dx=2/n. The optimization domain is parameterized via:

   h = \boldsymbol{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n [0,1]^n, with \sum x_i = n/2

To handle the non-convexity, we employ an exponential penalty transformation:
