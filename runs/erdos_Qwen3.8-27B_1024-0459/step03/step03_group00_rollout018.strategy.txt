1. Approach and rationale
The core idea is to use a **convolutional sparse coding** approach to learn a function h(x) with minimal maximum overlap. Unlike simple periodic sequences, we decompose h(x) into a dictionary of atoms that help reduce overlap through synergistic interference patterns. This connects to harmonic analysis by considering the Fourier transform of h(x) and its auto-correlation properties. The function is parameterized as a convolved sparse representation of elementary sequences that have good overlap properties with their shifts.

The key insight is that periodic artifacts, common in Thue-Morse or golden ratio sequences, can be avoided by ensuring that the autocorrelation function has a sharp peak at lag 0 and quickly decays elsewhere. This construction leverages mutual coherence minimization, where elementary components interfere constructively at lag 0 but destructively elsewhere.
