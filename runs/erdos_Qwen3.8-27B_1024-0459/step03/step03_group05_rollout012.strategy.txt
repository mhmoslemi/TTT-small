### Approach and Rationale

The central idea is to use a multi-resolution optimization strategy that combines deterministic search with stochastic perturbations. The goal is to escape local optima by exploring different resolutions and using a combination of global and local search methods.

#### Why?
1. The current best solution (C=0.38085) uses an initial parent with 160 samples (n=160). However, increasing the resolution might capture more details and lead to a better solution.
2. The previous optimization approach used high-dimensional spaces (n=640) and was computationally expensive. We need a balance between resolution and computational budget.

#### Key Rationale Points:
1. **Resolution Sweep**: We will consider several different resolutions (n) that are multiples of the parent's resolution. The parent has n=160. We will consider n=160*k for k=1,2,4,... up to a maximum of n=1024 (to stay within budget).
2. **Multi-Method Optimization**: We will use several types of optimization to cover different aspects of the search space:
   - **Deterministic Optimization**: We use a method like COBYLA for constrained optimization (handling integral constraint exactly) and also use a closed-form expression for the objective (avoiding correlation which is expensive to compute).
   - **Stochastic Search**: We use a stochastic local search with coordinated descent but with a different perturbation strategy (swaps and shifts) to avoid getting stuck in local minima.
   - **Surrogate Objective**: We smooth the max-correlation objective by a log-sum-exp (LSE) trick to make it easier to differentiate and integrate into gradient-based methods, but note that the problem is not convex.

#### Bottlenecks Addressed:
1. The original correlation computation is O(n^2) per evaluation (due to full correlation) and is expensive for large n. We will compute another objective (via LSE) that is proportional to the max-correlation but can be differentiated more easily. However, note that the original evaluator is fixed and must be used for the final score.
   - We will use the parent solution (n=160) as a starting point and then interpolate to higher resolutions (n=320, 640, 1024) to see if we can improve.

2. The integral constraint and box constraints must be maintained. We will use projection onto the L1-ball for the integral and box constraints, but note that the constraint sum(h)=n/2 is linear and the [0,1] is box. The projection must be done correctly.

3. The previous method used L-BFGS-B, stochastic coordinate descent, and SLSQP. We propose to replace some of these with:
   - A **gradient descent method** with line search for the LSE objective (which is differentiable).
   - A **random swap and shift** method at multiple resolutions.

#### Important Limitations:
1. We cannot use the entire budget (1300 seconds) because the budget is tight and we are calling the expensive objective several times. Therefore, we must set a limit on the number of function evaluations or use a faster surrogate for the interior of the search.

2. We must not deviate too much from the parent solution to avoid poor performance.
