One key idea: the optimal binary sequence for minimizing max cross-correlation with its complement is related to "minimum peak sidelobe" sequences. There's literature on this.

For a binary sequence of length n with n/2 ones, the "autocorrelation" (overlap with itself shifted) and "cross-correlation" (overlap with complement shifted) are related. Specifically:

Let a[i] = 2*h[i] - 1 ∈ {-1, 1} (mapping to ±1).
Then h[i] = (a[i]+1)/2.

The cross-correlation c_k = ∑ h[i+k](1-h[i]).
With a: h[i] = (a[i]+1)/2, 1-h[i] = (1-a[i])/2.
c_k = ∑ (a[i+k]+1)/2 * (1-a[i])/2 = (1/4) ∑ (a[i+k]+1)(1-a[i])
= (1/4) ∑ (a[i+k] - a[i+k]a[i] + 1 - a[i])
= (1/4) [∑a[i+k] - ∑a[i+k]a[i] + (n-k) - ∑a[i]]

For a balanced sequence (∑a = 0): c_k = (1/4)[∑a[i+k] - ∑a[i+k]a[i] + (n-k)]

The term ∑a[i+k]a[i] is the autocorrelation of a at lag k. So:
c_k = (1/4)[(n-k) + ∑a[i+k] - R_a(k)]

where R_a(k) = ∑ a[i+k]a[i] is the autocorrelation.

For a "sleeker" sequence with ∑a[i+k] ≈ 0 (which is true for balanced sequences away from boundaries), this simplifies to:
c_k ≈ (1/4)[(n-k) - R_a(k)]

To minimize max_k c_k, we want R_a(k) to be as close to (n-k) as possible for all k. But R_a(0) = n, and R_a(k) for k>0 can range from -n to n.
