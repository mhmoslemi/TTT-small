1. Approach and rationale
The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks to find a step function h: [0,2] → [0,1] with integral 1 that minimizes the maximum overlap with 1-h under any shift k. The central idea is to use a multi-resolution optimization strategy that combines interpolation from a known parent solution with local optimization. The primary limitations are computational feasibility (1 CPU, 1300 seconds) and the non-convex nature of the problem. We address these by increasing grid resolution dynamically and using efficient interpolation to transfer information between grids. Key hypotheses include:
   - Increasing resolution improves correlation evaluation accuracy.
   - Local optimization refines interpolated parent solutions.
   - Doubling grid size every step balances computational cost with solution quality improvement.

2. Mathematical specification
We represent h as samples over [0,2]. For grid size n, h \in \mathbb{R}^n satisfies:
- 0 \leq h[i] \leq 1 \forall i
- \sum h[i] = n/2 (assuming n even)
The objective is:
\[ C(n) = \max_k \left| \sum_i h[i] (1 - h[(i-k) \mod n]) \right| \times \frac{2}{n} \]
where the correlation is computed over all lags. For optimization, we use a penalty version:
\[ \Phi(h,\lambda) = C(n) + \lambda \cdot \left| \sum h[i] - \frac{n}{2} \right|^2 \]
where \lambda controls constraint satisfaction. Feasible solutions require projection if constraints are violated.
