Approach and rationale:
Central idea: This approach employs a multi-resolution optimization strategy combined with directional gradient descent to minimize the maximum overlap integral. Unlike traditional methods that use local search, it leverages directional gradients and resolution expansion to systematically escape local optima. The method treats the overlap integral as a piecewise maximum function that can be smoothed using exponential transformations to enable gradient-based optimization. Key improvements include gradient-based updates on directional rotations instead of point-updates, adaptive resolution control based on objective improvement, and a multi-start optimization strategy that evaluates candidates at multiple resolutions before final polishing.

Bottleneck addressed: Traditional stochastic local search methods often get trapped in local optima, particularly when the solution space contains multiple plateaus. This approach systematically explores higher resolutions while maintaining gradient guidance, whereas previous strategies depended too heavily on finely parameterized global optimizers without clear directionality.

Limitations: Requires careful selection of smoothness parameters; may still fail for extremely constrained problems; gradient calculations are approximate due to smoothing, potentially affecting convergence speed.

Rationale: By using directional gradients combined with resolution expansion, we can both maintain the global search ability of earlier coarse methods while bringing the refinement capability of local methods. This balance is critical for very challenging optimization problems like the minimum overlap problem where energy landscape contains deep basins.
