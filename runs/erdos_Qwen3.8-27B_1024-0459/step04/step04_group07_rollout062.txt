f(h) = dx * log(∑_k exp(β * corr_k)) / β  (smooth max approximation)

∂f/∂h[j] = dx * (1/β) * (∑_k β * exp(β*corr_k) * ∂corr_k/∂h[j]) / (∑_k exp(β*corr_k))
= dx * ∑_k p_k * ∂corr_k/∂h[j]

where p_k = exp(β*corr_k) / ∑_m exp(β*corr_m) (softmax weights)

And ∂corr_k/∂h[j] = 1 - h[j-lag_k] - h[j+lag_k] (approximately, as derived above)

The computation:
1. corr = np.correlate(h, 1-h, 'full')  # O(n²) but fast in C
2. p = softmax(β * corr)  # O(n)
3. For each j: grad[j] = ∑_k p_k * (1 - h[j-lag_k] - h[j+lag_k])
   This is: ∑_k p_k - ∑_k p_k*h[j-lag_k] - ∑_k p_k*h[j+lag_k]
   = ∑p_k - (p ⋆ h)[j] - (p ⋆ h)[j]  (if p is symmetric)
   
   But p is NOT symmetric in general. However:
   ∑_k p_k * h[j-lag_k] = (p * h)[j] where * is the cross-correlation
   ∑_k p_k * h[j+lag_k] = (p * h)[j] by a change of variable (if we define it properly)
   
   Actually: ∑_k p_k * h[j-lag_k] where k=0,...,2n-2 and lag_k = k-(n-1)
   = ∑_{lag=-n+1}^{n-1} p[lag+n-1] * h[j-lag]
   This is the cross-correlation (p ⋆ h)[j] = ∑_lag p[lag] * h[j-lag]
