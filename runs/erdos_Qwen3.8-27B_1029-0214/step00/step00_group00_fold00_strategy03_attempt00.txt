1. Approach and rationale

Central idea

The supplied parent construction is treated only as a high-quality incumbent, not as the optimization variable. The final returned bound must come from a vector that is independently validated under the exact discrete evaluator

    C(h) = max_k np.correlate(h[:, nothing], 1-h, mode="full") * (2/n)

and, independently, satisfies the box and mass constraints. To reduce the parent's nonsmooth max-over-correlations objective while automatically satisfying those constraints, I propose a constraint-free reparameterized continuous optimizer. The primal variable is not h itself. The algorithm optimizes raw unconstrained (or bound-regularized) variables whose smooth, index-preserving map into h closes the sum constraint exactly by a one-dimensional root solve for a common logistic shift:

    h_i = sigmoid(x_i + lambda(x)),
    sum_i h_i = n/2.

This map guarantees 0 < h_i < 1 and an exact mass constraint up to deliberate numerical Newton/bisection precision. Thus the box and sum constraints are never violated during the mathematical search, and there is no need for a heuristic projection after every proposed local perturbation.

The smooth surrogate to optimize is a log-sum-exp smoothing of exactly the full finite-support correlation terms (scaled by dx=2/n):

    F_beta(x) = (1/beta) log sum_l exp(beta * C_l(h(x)))

where C_l are the same 2n-1 finite-support overlap quantities whose maximum is the reported C_5. With beta large, F_beta approximates the exact upper objective from above in the sense that its minimizers should approach minimizers of the original max objective. The optimizer uses analytic gradients of this surrogate. The exact objective is nevertheless used for acceptance. Every new feasible primal h produced by the parameterized map is evaluated with the official np.correlate expression. Improvement is recorded only if the exact recorded bound decreases.

Why this should help the parent

- The supplied 45-point parent is likely close to a local minimax sink of the exact nonsmooth problem. Direct projection-based or box-sum perturbation schemes can get stuck because after a move the mass constraint is repaired and the inactive slack in clipped bounds hides local directions. The reparameterized primal map converts the feasible set into a smooth manifold independent of bound clipping.
- At parent points inside the feasible polytope, the exact objective has a well-defined subgradient obtained by activating only near-maximal correlation lags. After a weighted softmax law, the gradient through the reparameterization is a consistent Chebyshev-smoothed maximal-lag gradient and should reveal feasible steepest-descent mass transfers.
- Optimizing on duplicate or interpolated finer grids from the same parent can reduce artificial discretization error because larger n gives more pieces and more freedom without having to manually transfer there correctly; each new n has its own objective and its own parameter-to-h primal map.

What this method is, not

- The underlying exact problem is nonconvex due to the products h_i h_j in correlation lags. This is not a proven global optimizer. The strategy is a deterministic high-fidelity local nonconvex refinement with broad restarts, parent/generator-specific transformations, and a final one-dimensional direct exact objective polish.
- The output is certified only as a feasible construction with its exact edited C_5; there is no lower-bound optimality proof unless explicitly validated by another solver (not introduced here).
- The previous proposed active set epigraph solvers, random projected successive smootherpair direction work, and raw projection-box-sum local plans rely on direct step-by-step input feasibility checks and heuristics. This method's decision variable belongs to a primal debound proximal vector root; shift and box/sum constraints are embedded in the reconstruction function. The direct one-dimensional line polishing phase is only a secondary fallback. It tries to re-identify small largest-bound correspond reductions.
