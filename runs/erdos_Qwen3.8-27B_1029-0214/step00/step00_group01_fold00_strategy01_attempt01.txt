1. Approach and rationale

The central idea is a local, finite-support, exact-evaluation minimax solver built around an active-constraint linearized descent direction, combined with deterministic symmetry/reflection/roll variants and sequential resolution refinement. This is intentionally different from a smooth log-sum-exp trajectory or a logistic parametrization: the direction at every step is not chosen by internally minimizing a smoothed approximation of the continuous objective. Instead, at the accepted vector, the exact evaluator is used unchanged; the next step is chosen from a linear subproblem over the constraints that are exactly or nearly active in the discrete objective, and every raw step candidate is re-projected into feasibility and evaluated by the actual evaluator before acceptance.

The method addresses the bottleneck that the optimum lies extremely close to the supplied construction, so the required improvement is tiny. A global method is not asserted and no optimality is claimed. The expected gain comes from three complementary facts:

1. The supplied construction lies near a KKT-like local junction where a set of correlations is simultaneously close to maximum. A first-order quadratic term does not determine the descent direction, but selecting those near-active lags and solving a boxed linear program for a direction that reduces their linear slopes is a sharp nonsmooth local descent search.

2. The discrete construction can be invariantly transformed. Exact sample reversal and exact complementation preserve the exact evaluator objective up to reversal of the full-correlation vector and therefore preserve its maximum. The discrete density sum and box constraints are also preserved under reflection and complement averaging. Symmetry averaging can reduce a nonconvex maximum objective without claiming convexity; it is only accepted if the actual evaluator reports a reduction.
