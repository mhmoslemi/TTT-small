1. Approach and rationale

The supplied evaluation is the exact finite-support minimax quantity

- decision vector: `h ∈ R^n`, `dx = 2.0/n`,
- box bound: `0 <= h[i] <= 1`,
- integral/measure constraint: `dx * sum(h) = 1`, equivalently `sum(h) = n/2`,
- objective: `C5(h) = dx * max_l np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")[l]`.

This is the authoritative objective for acceptance and certification. Any periodic/circular objective, minimum-autocorrelation simplification, or other proxy must be used only internally and never to certify a candidate. The method below treats the exact evaluator as the only scoring and acceptance mechanism.

The previous proposed families were all deterministic local dense-vector minimax solvers or epigraph nonlinear-program routes. This plan is materially different: it is a memetic, globally diversified, feasible projected search with exact one-dimensional descent layers. The primary system is a projected differential-evolution (DE) exploration loop over feasible vectors, with hard joint feasibility restoration at every proposal. Local polish is not modeled optimization only; it is performed by:

1. exact tangent line search along active correlation gradients, with feasibility restored by a capped-box/simplex projection and the true evaluator for acceptance;
2. exact pair-coordinate line search on slices `h[p] += t`, `h[q] -= t`, which trivially preserves the sum constraint, with exact one-dimensional minimization of the true evaluator along that feasible segment.

This approach addresses the tight-improvement bottleneck in a different way:

- The supplied parent is likely very close to a local junction of many near-active lags. Deterministic local methods can become stuck exactly there because their models fail to explore alternative near-equivalent structures. Stochastic population diversity can reach nearby regions where an exact pair or gradient move_properties can reduce the maximum.
- Translation, averaging, flip, complemented-flip averaging, and roll transformations are cheap feasible seeds. They are not optimized by exact score, but they determine only seed diversity. Any accepted successor must decrease the evaluated true objective after exact scoring.
- Resolution refinement is performed independently: integer repeat of a vector is legal and the resulting vector must be compared with more sub-cell lags. If a refined vector attains a smaller exact score, it is retained; if not, discarded. The loop retains a parent/fallback candidate throughout.

This plan is still strongly local/heuristic. It does not claim global optimality or the existence of subrange positive improvements. It may return the repaired parent if no improvement is found, but its best candidate always requires exact verification and feasible certification.
