1. Approach and rationale
-------------------------

1.1 Central idea
The supplied metric is a max–min over linear finite-support cross-correlations:
for a vector `h` of length `n`,

`C5(h) = max_m np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode_full)[m] * (2/n)`.

The only nonconvex part is the bilinear product `h_p * (1-h_{p+s})`. The proposed method does not try to optimize every product simultaneously. It repeatedly solves a globally optimal linear programming subproblem in which the shifted complement factor is frozen. Concretely, given the current iterate `h0`, define the frozen positive surrogate vector

`q = 1 - h0`.

For a prospective vector `z`, the frozen constraint for lag `s` is

`U_s(z; q) = sum over valid p z[p] * q[p+s]`.

For fixed `q`, each `U_s(z; q)` is an affine function of `z`, so the problem

minimize `t` subject to `U_s(z; q) <= t` for all lags and the box/simplex constraints for `z`

is a convex linear program. This LP can be solved globally by SciPy’s `linprog` with HiGHS or another public SciPy LP method. It returns a candidate that exactly minimizes the max of the frozen linearized correlations over a chosen trust region.

The move toward full non-convex descent is made by repeatedly:
1. freezing the complement at the current iterate,
2. solving the resulting convex surrogate LP exactly,
3. floating the complement only after projecting the LP solution back to the device bounds and exact sum,
4. auditing the candidate by the original evaluator-shaped objective.
Candidates are kept only if they improve the original, non-linearized objective. This gives a legal nonconvex descent/filter method: the LP subproblem is convex and certified, but the last check is the authoritative full objective.

1.2 Why this may refine a near-plateau construction
A source or parent construction near `0.38085` is likely limited by the finite-resolution grid and by local simultaneous coupling of many lags. Pairwise or coordinate methods spend budget on local nudges. The LP surrogate treats all active lag constraints simultaneously, exploiting the true structure of the maximum. Because the constraint function is linear under the frozen surrogate, an LP can move every coordinate together in a complete max-tightening direction. The subsequent exact objective check filters out false directions caused by freezing `q`.

1.3 Resolution strategy
Once a reliable low-dimensional starting point exists, plainly `C5` can improve with finer discretization because more coordinates allow sharper boundaries and anti-correlation structure. The plan therefore transfers the current best feasible vector to larger grids, then applies the LP surrogate procedure with a conservative trust region. Multi-resolution together with exact evaluation avoids committing to alignment errors at the original grid. The final returned length is allowed to differ from the supplied parent length because the evaluator accepts any positive `n_points` satisfying feasibility.

1.4 Correct claims versus heuristics
This plan proves, for each LP iteration, only that the candidate is the global solver of the frozen linearized trust-region surrogate. It does not prove global optimality of the original bilinear problem. Acceptance is always against the exact array metric. Multi-resolution, random perturbations and small final neighbor scans are heuristics intended to escape local plateaus; they do not alter correctness.

1.5 Main limitations
The freeze-then-solve method may obtain the full objective exactly from an initial branch and accept only the LP-improving path. It is not suitable as an isolated solver unless blended as here. The method is constrained by available CPU time, LP immunity time, rounding error, and the fact that objective improvement on surrogate may not be full. These limitations are mitigated by strict filtering, shrinking trust radius, deterministic fallbacks and accepting-best-within time.
