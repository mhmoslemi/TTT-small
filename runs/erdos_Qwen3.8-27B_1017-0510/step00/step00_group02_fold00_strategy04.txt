{"plan":"1. Approach and rationale\n\nUse a sequential convex majorization-minimization (MM) search. At each feasible construction, compute every finite-support lag value and its analytic gradient, then solve a convex quadratic epigraph subproblem that minimizes a proven upper model of the maximum lag. Accept a proposed iterate only if the exact, full-correlation score improves. Start from the supplied construction when usable, retain it as an immutable fallback, and use a small deterministic set of symmetry-related and seeded feasible starts.\n\nThis is materially different from the earlier epigraph-SLSQP, differential-evolution, smoothed projected-gradient, and replica-exchange approaches: it does not optimize the original nonconvex epigraph with a nonlinear solver, use population mutation or Metropolis moves, or replace the maximum by log-sum-exp. Instead, every iteration solves a convex second-order-cone subproblem derived from a global Hessian bound for each lag. The bound and subproblem formulation are mathematical facts; multistart choices, trust radii, and finite iteration budgets are heuristics. The method is local and does not guarantee an improvement, the 0.3808 target, or a global optimum. Exact-score acceptance and retaining a feasible parent ensure that solver behavior cannot make the returned bound worse than the validated fallback.\n\n2. Mathematical specification\n\nFor n samples, let h=(h[0],…,h[n−1]), dx=2/n, and s=n/2. The feasible set is 0≤h[i]≤1 and sum_i h[i]=s. Include every finite-support lag. For d=0,…,n−1 define R_0(h)=sum_{i=0}^{n−1} h[i](1−h[i]). For d=1,…,n−1 define\nR_{+d}(h)=sum_{i=0}^{n−d−1} h[i+d](1−h[i]),\nR_{−d}(h)=sum_{i=0}^{n−d−1} h[i](1−h[i+d]).\nOrder these values by lag from −(n−1) through 0 to +(n−1). The authoritative score is C(h)=dx·max_l R_l(h), exactly `max(np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode='full')) * (2.0/n)`. These are finite overlap sums; retain both directions and their boundary ranges. Do not use circular correlation or replace them with an autocorrelation identity that omits boundary terms.\n\nThe gradients used in the subproblem are in raw correlation units. With indicator notation 1[condition], for d>0,\nG_{+d,j}=∂R_{+d}/∂h[j]=1[j≥d](1−h[j−d])−1[j<n−d]h[j+d],\nG_{−d,j}=∂R_{−d}/∂h[j]=1[j<n−d](1−h[j+d])−1[j≥d]h[j−d].\nFor d=0, G_{0,j}=1−2h[j]. These formulas include finite-endpoint terms. Store rows in increasing-lag order.\n\nFor any current feasible x and displacement u, each lag is quadratic and has the exact expansion R_l(x+u)=R_l(x)+G_l(x)·u+(1/2)uᵀH_lu. For zero lag H_0=−2I. For a nonzero lag, H_l has −1 in both symmetric entries for each pair (i,i+d), with no other nonzero entries. Those pairs form a graph of maximum degree two, so the absolute row sums of H_l are at most two and its largest eigenvalue is at most two. Thus, for every lag and every u,\nR_l(x+u) ≤ R_l(x)+G_l(x)·u+||u||₂².\nThis is a global upper bound, not a local approximation.\n\nAt each MM iteration solve the following convex subproblem in displacement u, scalar q, and scalar t:\nminimize t,\nsubject to ||u||₂²≤q, sum_j u[j]=0, −x[j]≤u[j]≤1−x[j], −rho≤u[j]≤rho, and\nq+R_l(x)+G_l(x)·u≤t for every one of the 2n−1 lags.\nHere rho is the current trust-box radius. The constraints are convex: the norm-square epigraph is a second-order-cone constraint and all remaining constraints are linear. Since q increases every lag upper model, at an optimum it equals ||u||₂². The zero displacement is feasible, so the model’s optimum is no greater than max_l R_l(x). The exact score of x+u is still used for all actual acceptance and return decisions; never report t as the score.\n\n3. Implementation procedure\n\n3.1. Program structure and grid.
