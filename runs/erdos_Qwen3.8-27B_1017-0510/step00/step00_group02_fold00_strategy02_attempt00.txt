At each positive temperature, a log-sum-exp over every finite-support lag gives gradients from all near-worst lags rather than relying on whichever single lag currently attains the maximum. Lowering the temperature in stages makes this surrogate approach the true maximum. Project every proposed iterate jointly onto the box-and-mass feasible set, and use the exact evaluator formula to decide which candidates may replace the saved incumbent.\n\nThis is materially different from an epigraph-constrained SLSQP search and from differential evolution: do not introduce an epigraph variable or solver constraints, and do not use population mutation, crossover, or pair-transfer polishing. The main bottleneck is the nonsmooth maximum, while each lag is a quadratic function of h. Log-sum-exp is differentiable for positive temperature, but it does not make the underlying problem convex. The smoothing error bound and feasibility of the projection are mathematical facts; the temperatures, starts, and iteration limits are heuristics. Mathematical specification\nFor n samples, let dx = 2/n, h ∈ Rⁿ, and s = n/2. Require 0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1 for every i and ∑ᵢ h[i] = s. Include all finite-support lags. Put these in increasing-lag order, from −(n−1) through 0 to +(n−1). The scaled lag value is qℓ(h) = dx Rℓ(h), and the exact objective is C(h) = maxℓ qℓ(h). These are the two finite overlap ranges of np.correlate(h, 1−h, mode='full'); do not use circular correlation, omit either lag direction, or replace the objective with an autocorrelation identity that loses boundary terms.\n\nFor derivatives, use row index n−1+d for +d and n−1−d for −d. For d=0, ∂R₀/∂h[j] = 1−2h[j]. For d>0,\n∂R₊d/∂h[j] = 1[j≥d](1−h[j−d]) − 1[j<n−d]h[j+d],\n∂R₋d/∂h[j] = 1[j<n−d](1−h[j+d]) − 1[j≥d]h[j−d].\nThese include the endpoint terms of the finite sums. Thus ∇qℓ = dx ∇Rℓ.\n\nFor τ>0, optimize the smooth surrogate Fτ(h) = τ log(∑ℓ exp(qℓ(h)/τ)). Evaluate it stably by setting qmax=maxℓ qℓ, wℓ=exp((qℓ−qmax)/τ), and Fτ=qmax+τ log(∑ℓwℓ). This is a surrogate only; the original exact C(h), not Fτ, determines the returned score and incumbent.\n\nThe feasible set is the capped simplex.
