**Core Idea – a convex continuous optimisation for circle packing**

The set of constraints defining a non‑overlapping packing of circles in a unit square is *convex* when the decision variables are the **centres** \((x_i,y_i)\) and the **radii** \(r_i\):

* Each circle must lie inside the unit square  
  \[
  \begin{cases}
  x_i-r_i \ge 0, & x_i+r_i \le 1,\\
  y_i-r_i \ge 0, & y_i+r_i \le 1.
  \end{cases}
  \]
* Two distinct circles must not overlap  
  \[
  \|[x_i-x_j,\;y_i-y_j]\|_2 \;\ge\; r_i+r_j \qquad \forall\, i<j .
  \]

All constraints are either linear (boundary constraints) or of the form  
“convex function \(\ge\) affine function” (the distance inequality).  
Thus the feasible region is a convex set.  
The objective we want to maximise is linear:
\[
\max \;\sum_{i=1}^{26} r_i .
\]
A linear program over a convex feasible set is a **convex optimisation problem** and has a **global optimum** that can be found in polynomial time with modern interior‑point solvers.

The strategy is therefore:
