**Conceptual Shift**  
The previous plan used a global simulated‑annealing walk over circle centres and a linear‑programme to compute radii every time.  
A markedly different design is an *order‑preserving block coordinate descent* that separates the problem into two alternating sub‑problems:

1. **Radius‑optimisation (LP step)** – given fixed centres, find the largest feasible radii that maximise the sum.  
2. **Centre‑adjustment (force‑step)** – given the current radii, move the centres in a deterministic direction that *relieves* the most binding constraints (i.e. the contacts that currently prevent the radii from growing).  

The two steps are iterated until convergence.  Because each sub‑problem is convex (LP for radii, a simple linear “force” step for centres), the method is numerically stable and fast, and the structure is entirely different from a stochastic search.

---

### 1.  Mathematical primitives

*Let*  
\(c_i=(x_i,y_i)\) – centre of circle *i* (fixed during an LP call).  
\(r_i\) – radius of circle *i*.  

**LP sub‑problem**

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max\quad &\sum_{i=1}^{26} r_i \\
\text{s.t.}\quad
 & r_i+r_j \;\le\; d_{ij} \qquad (1\le i<j\le 26)\\
 & r_i \;\le\; x_i,\; r_i\;\le\; 1-x_i,\;
   r_i\;\le\; y_i,\; r_i\;\le\; 1-y_i\\
 & r_i \;\ge\;0
\end{aligned}
\]

with \(d_{ij}=||c_i-c_j||_2\).  
All constraints are linear once the positions are fixed; the solver is `scipy.optimize.linprog` (`highs` method).  
The optimiser returns a *feasible* set of radii that satisfies every non‑overlap and boundary requirement.

**Force step (centre adjustment)**  

For each circle *i* compute a desirable displacement direction:

1. **Neighbour “push”** – for every *j* with a *tight* contact  
   \(d_{ij}\approx r_i+r_j\) (within a small tolerance), push *i* away from *j* along the unit vector  
   \(\frac{c_i-c_j}{d_{ij}}\).  
2. **Boundary push** – if \(x_i-r_i < \varepsilon\) push left, if \(1-x_i-r_i <\varepsilon\) push right, similarly for *y*.  
3. **Aggregate vector** \(\mathbf{v}_i = \sum \text{(push vectors)}\).  

If \(\|\mathbf{v}_i\|>0\), we move the centre:
\[
c_i \leftarrow \text{clip}\!\left(c_i+\alpha \frac{\mathbf{v}_i}{\|\mathbf{v}_i\|},\;0,\;1\right)
\]
with a small step size \(\alpha\) (e.g. 0.01 – 0.02).  
The clipping guarantees the centre stays in the unit square.

```
     a. Solve LP for radii given current centres
     b. Record sum of radii; update best if improved
     c. Compute push vectors for all circles
     d. Update all centres simultaneously
3. Return best centres, radii, sum
```

* **Zero distances** – If two centres accidentally coincide (unlikely because of the push step), the LP enforces `r_i+r_j <= 0`. This yields zero radii and the force step instantly pushes them apart.
