Let the 26 circles have centres \((x_i , y_i )\) and radii \(r_i > 0,\; i=1,\dots ,26\).  
The packing problem **maximises** the linear function

\[
\text{maximize}\qquad  \sum_{i=1}^{26} r_i
\]

subject to the following **convex** constraints:

1. **Boundary containment** (each circle lies inside the unit square)
   \[
   \begin{aligned}
   x_i - r_i &\ge 0, &\quad x_i + r_i &\le 1, \\
   y_i - r_i &\ge 0, &\quad y_i + r_i &\le 1 ,
   \end{aligned}\qquad \forall i .
   \]

2. **Non‑overlap**  
   The Euclidean distance between any two centres must be at least the sum of the radii:
   \[
   \|\, (x_i-x_j,\; y_i-y_j) \,\|_2 \;\ge\; r_i + r_j ,
   \qquad \forall\, i<j .
   \]
   This inequality is convex because the function
   \(f(x,y,r_1,r_2)=\|[x,y]\|_2-(r_1+r_2)\) is the difference of a convex function

   `norm([x_i-x_j, y_i-y_j]) >= r_i + r_j`.

3. **Non‑negativity of radii**
   \[
   r_i \;\ge\; 0 , \qquad \forall i .
   \]
