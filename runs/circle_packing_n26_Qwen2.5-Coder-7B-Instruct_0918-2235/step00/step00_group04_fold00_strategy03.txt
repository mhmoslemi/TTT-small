### 1. Core idea  

Replace the *explicit* non‑overlap and boundary inequalities by a **smooth penalty** that is only activated when a constraint is violated.  
We thus optimise **both** the 52 centre coordinates and the 26 radii simultaneously in one unconstrained continuous optimisation problem:
\[
\min_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{r}}\;
\underbrace{-\sum_{i} r_i}_{\text{core objective}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\lambda_B\,\Phi_B(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{r})
            +\lambda_O\,\Phi_O(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{r})}_{\text{soft penalties}}
\]
subject to the simple box bounds \(0\le x_i, y_i \le 1\) and \(r_i \ge 0\).  
The penalty terms are

* **Boundary penalty**  
  \[
  \Phi_B = 
  \sum_{i=1}^{26}\!\!\bigl\{
   \bigl[\max(0,\, r_i-x_i)\bigr]^2
  +\bigl[\max(0,\, r_i-(1-x_i))\bigr]^2
  +\bigl[\max(0,\, r_i-y_i)\bigr]^2
  +\bigl[\max(0,\, r_i-(1-y_i))\bigr]^2
  \bigr\}.
  \]

* **Overlap penalty**  
  \[
  \Phi_O =
  \sum_{1\le i<j\le 26}
  \bigl[\max(0,\; r_i+r_j-d_{ij})\bigr]^2,
  \qquad
  d_{ij}=\sqrt{(x_i-x_j)^2+(y_i-y_j)^2}.
  \]

The penalty coefficients  
\(\lambda_B = 10^6,\;\lambda_O = 10^6\)  
are chosen very large so that any feasible solution has essentially zero penalty and the optimiser is forced inside the admissible region, yet small enough that the optimiser step‑size is not overwhelmed by the penalty gradient.

Because all penalty terms are **piecewise‑quadratic**, their derivatives are trivial to compute:
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\max(0,\, r_i-x_i)^2
   = -2\,\max(0,\, r_i-x_i)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{r_i>x_i\}},
\]
and similarly for the other boundary terms.  
For the overlap part
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\max(0,\, r_i+r_j-d_{ij})^2
   = -2\,\max(0,\, r_i+r_j-d_{ij})
      \cdot \frac{x_i-x_j}{d_{ij}}\;\mathbf{1}_{\{r_i+r_j>d_{ij}\}}.
\]
Thus the full gradient can be assembled with a single pass over all pairs.

The penalty framework eliminates the need for an LP inside each iteration while still guaranteeing a feasible solution (the penalty drives any violation to zero).  The only explicit constraints are the box bounds on positions and radii, readily handled by standard optimisation libraries.

### 3. Mathematical justification

* The penalty functions are convex and vanish exactly on the feasible set of the original hard‑constraint problem.  
* For any *feasible* configuration, the penalty gradient is zero; hence the optimiser’s objective reduces to simply maximizing the sum of radii.  
* Infeasible points are penalised quadratically, thereby creating a strongly convex “barrier” that keeps the optimiser within a narrow margin of the feasible region.  
* Because the penalties are smooth where they are active, gradient‑based methods such as Trust‑Constr work efficiently and converge to a (local) optimum that automatically satisfies all constraints.

### 6. How this differs from previous strategies

* **No LP inside the main loop** – unlike the gradient‑aided LP method, we avoid re‑solving linear programmes every iteration, saving time and simplifying the gradient derivation.
* **Dual variables are not used** – instead we rely on smooth penalty gradients that directly incorporate the geometry of the constraints.
* **Global direction** – the optimiser is a *continuous* method that moves all circles and radii simultaneously, rather than mutating a single centre as in PSO or moving one circle at a time.
* **Parameter tuning** – the penalty coefficients replace the step‑size schedules of simulated annealing or PSO, making the algorithm more robust to initial configuration and less sensitive to internals of the update rule.
