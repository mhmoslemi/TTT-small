The goal is to find 26 circles whose centres \(c_i=(x_i,y_i)\) and radii \(r_i\) satisfy the unit‑square constraints

\[
\begin{aligned}
&0\le x_i,y_i\le 1,\\
&0\le r_i\le \min (x_i,\;1-x_i,\;y_i,\;1-y_i),\\
&r_i+r_j\le \|c_i-c_j\| \quad \forall\,i<j,
\end{aligned}
\]

and to maximise the linear objective \(S=\sum_{i=1}^{26} r_i\).

Instead of fixing the centres and solving an LP for the radii (as in the earlier strategy), we treat **both** the positions and the radii as decision variables and optimise **one continuous objective** that blends the primary goal (large sum of radii) with hard‑constraint violations via a penalty term.  
The optimisation is performed with a derivative‑free or quasi‑Newton method, and we employ multiple random restarts to escape poor local optima.

```
v = (x1, y1, r1, x2, y2, r2, …, x26, y26, r26).
```

Bounds on each component:

The explicit upper bound on \(r_i\) is harmless because the “inside‑square’’ constraints will be stricter for most centres.

### 2. Objective function (penalty formulation)

```
penality  = 0
penality += Σ   max(0, r_i + r_j - dij)^2  for all i<j   (overlap)
penality += Σ   max(0, r_i - xi)^2          for all i   (left boundary)
penality += Σ   max(0, r_i - (1-xi))^2      for all i   (right boundary)
penality += Σ   max(0, r_i - yi)^2          for all i   (bottom)
penality += Σ   max(0, r_i - (1-yi))^2      for all i   (top)
```
