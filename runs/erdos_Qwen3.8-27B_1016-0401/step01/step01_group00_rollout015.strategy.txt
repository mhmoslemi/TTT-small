Rationale:
The previous strategies are either local optimization with temperature annealing or global optimization with DE. The problem is non-convex and requires both global exploration and local refinement. However, the current approaches may not capture the optimal structure. We aim to use spectral methods (Fourier analysis) to represent the step function compactly and then optimize in the spectral domain, which often leads to faster convergence and better avoidance of local optima.

We will use the following multi-step strategy:

1. **Spectral Representation**: Represent the step function as a linear combination of Fourier basis functions of period 2 (since the domain is [0,2]). This gives us a unitary representation.

2. **Global Optimization in Spectral Space**: Use a global optimizer (like Differential Evolution) to search the spectral coefficients for a solution that satisfies the integral constraint (sum h_i * dx = 1). Since the Fourier basis can be constrained to have zero integral (or adjust one basis for the mean), we can enforce the integral by adjusting a constant (low-frequency) component.

3. **Constraint Handling**: Introduce a constraint that the integral of the step function is exactly 1. We can enforce this by using projection or penalty methods, but since we are in the spectral domain, we can adjust the constant term.

4. **Non-Negativity and Bound Constraints**: The step function must be between 0 and 1. We can use an exponential transformation of the Fourier coefficients to impose these constraints (similar to the log-sum-exp trick for positivity) but it's non-trivial. Alternatively, we can use bound constraints on the spectral coefficients, but that might lead to infeasible representations.

5. **Local Refinement**: Once a candidate is found in the spectral domain, we refine it by converting the function values to the grid and then optimizing over the grid in the original space to better approximate the integral.

6. **Progressive Resolution**: Start from a coarse grid and progressively refine it. At each stage, we transfer the solution from the previous stage and optimize further.
