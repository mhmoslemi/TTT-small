### 1. Approach and Rationale  

**Central Idea:**  
We use gradient-based optimization combined with grid doubling to systematically improve the solution. The optimization leverages the ergodic nature of the problem to explore sparse representation of step functions, transforming the discrete optimization into a continuous relax and enforce framework to escape local optima. Grid doubling enhances resolution, allowing refined optimization at higher grid sizes.  

**Problem Addressed:**  
The Erdős minimum overlap problem requires minimizing the maximum overlap of a step function with its shifts. The space is high-dimensional and non-convex. GRID DOUBLING allows exploring higher-resolution representations, while a FLIP SCORE OPTIMIZATION efficiently navigates the discrete solution space using continuous relaxations.  

**Bottleneck:**  
- Discrete representations are combinatorial, making exhaustive search infeasible.  
- Entanglement between neighborhood configurations (flips) hinders optimization convergence.  

### 2. Mathematical Specification  

**Representation:**  
- Step function \( h: \mathbb{R} \to [0, 1] \) discretized as \( n \times 1 \) vector of 0s and 1s (2 bits per unit interval).  
- Integral preserved via \( \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i \cdot \Delta x = 1 \), where \( \Delta x = 2/n \).  

**Variables:**  
- \( h = [h_0, h_1, \ldots, h_{n-1}] \): original binary solution (bit flips)  
- \( u = [u_0, u_1, \ldots, u_{n-1}] \): continuous optimization (score model)  

**Objective:**  
- Minimize \( \forall k: \left| \int_0^2 h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx \right| \)  
  - Transformed for optimization: \( -\text{mean}(\text{cross}_{u,k}) \)  
  - \( \text{cross}_{u,k} = u_i (1 - u_{i+k}) + u_i (1 - u_{i-k}) \)  

**Constraints:**  
- Binary: \( 0.2 \leq p_i \leq 0.8 \) at edges  
- Sum constraint: \( \sum_i h_i = n/2 \) (handled via projection)  

**Formulation:**  
Minimize continuous \( p = \min \left(-\max( \text{waveform}_{u,k} )\right) \) with:  
\[ \text{waveform}_{u,k} = \sum_{\text{shifts}} \exp\left(h_i (1-h_{i+k}) \cdot \sigma\right) \]  
where \( \sigma \) penalizes non-optimal overlaps.
