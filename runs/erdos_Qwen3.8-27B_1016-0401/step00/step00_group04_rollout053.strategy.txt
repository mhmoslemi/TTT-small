**Approach and Rationale**

The Erdős minimum overlap problem involves finding a step function h:[0, 2]→[0, 1] with integral 1 that minimizes the maximum integral overlap with its shifted versions. The current record is approximately 0.38085. 

Our approach combines three key components:
1. **Gradient-based Local Search**: Uses a stochastic gradient method combined with a corrected repair mechanism to maintain feasibility.
2. **Grid Optimization**: Iteratively optimizes the solution at different grid resolutions by first minimizing the unary potential (individual h[i] effects) and then refining through gradient descent.
3. **Problem Transformation**: Matches constraints through problem reduction to a Poisson equation, establishing a solver-friendly formulation.

The central idea is to (i) efficiently explore the large space of h vectors via stochastic gradient descent on an upper bound function and (ii) utilize grid refinement to maintain solution quality while dealing with discretization artifacts. This addresses the challenge of finding global minima in a highly non-convex optimization landscape.

**Mathematical Specification**

We optimize over h ∈ ℝⁿ restricting to [0, 1]ⁿ with sum(h)=n/2. The score function is defined as:
$$f(h) = \max_{k} \left[ \frac{1}{2n} \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-k} h_i (1 - h_{i+k}) + \sum_{i=1}^{k} h_i (1 - h_{i+k}) \right)_max \right]$$

Using problem reduction via ℓ₂ projection, we represent h(u) = u/(u σ) where u is the solution to:
$$-\nabla^2 u = C h - \frac{1}{2n} I(h)$$

This reduces the optimization to finding u ∈ ℝ⁺∞ satisfying the Poisson equation under positivity constraints.
