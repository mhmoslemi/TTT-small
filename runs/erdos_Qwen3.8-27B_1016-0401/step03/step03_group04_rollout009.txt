Actually, you know what, let me also try a completely different approach for some of the time: **greedy coordinate descent**.

For each coordinate h[i], find the optimal value of h[i] given all other h[j] fixed. This is a 1D optimization problem:

f(h[i]) = max_k ∑_x h(x)(1-h(x+k))

For a fixed h[i] = t, the contribution to shift k is:
- If i is in the valid range for shift k: the term h(i)(1-h(i+k)) or h(i-k)(1-h(i)) changes
  - Actually, h[i] appears in g_k as: (1-h[i+|k|])*h[i] + h[i-|k|]*(1-h[i]) (for the two terms where h[i] is involved)
  - = (1-h[i+|k|])*t + h[i-|k|]*(1-t)
  - = (1-h[i+|k|])*t + h[i-|k|] - h[i-|k|]*t
  - = h[i-|k|] + t*(1-h[i+|k|]-h[i-|k|])

So g_k as a function of t is linear! And max of linear functions is convex! So for each coordinate, the 1D problem is to minimize a convex piecewise-linear function over t ∈ [0,1], subject to the overall sum constraint.

This means coordinate descent will work! And at each step, it's a simple 1D convex minimization (find where the subgradient changes sign, or just check t=0, t=1, and the "knee" points).
