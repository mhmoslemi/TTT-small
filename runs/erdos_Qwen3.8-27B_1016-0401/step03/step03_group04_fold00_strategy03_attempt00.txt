### Approach and Rationale

The core challenge is to minimize a non-convex, expensive-to-evaluate objective function under box and equality constraints. Our approach leverages a novel combination of dimensionality reduction, randomized exploration, and adaptive resolution refinement to escape local optima. Unlike previous methods, we first project the high-degree-of-freedom h-values to a sparse representation, then optimize over reduced dimensions, and finally refine via adaptive oversampling. This workflow reduces the dual evaluation cost (expensive integrals) while preserving optimality guarantees through exact projection methods. The key innovation is independent parallel resolution paths: one pursues direct gradient descent paths (fast convergence) while another explores the optimization landscape through controlled disorder injection (escaping local minima). Our fallback mechanism ensures constraint satisfaction through linear programming projection, maintaining feasibility throughout.

**Bottleneck Addressed**: The curse of dimensionality and expensive integral evaluations prevent convergence to the global minimum in prior approaches. Our method reduces dimensionality, parallelizes evaluations, and uses accept/reject sampling to balance exploration vs. exploitation, leading to faster convergence to a better solution than brute-force optimization.

**Limitations**: The method relies on exact preservation of constraints via projection. Performance degradation occurs when exact projection fails (e.g., positive curvature). However, dimensionality reduction inherent to our sparse representation minimizes this risk.

   - Map original samples `h_original ∈ ℝⁿ` to sparse events `e ∈ ℝ^{n/g}` via Poisson sampling: each event marks a point where `h_original[i] > t` (t threshold)

2. **Sparse Optimization**:
   - Solve: minimize_m(`max( mean(e) * (1 - roll(e,k)), [r for k]`)
   - Subject to: ∑e[j] = n/g (constraint projection)
   - Variables: e[j] ∈ {0,1}

3. **Resolution Refinement**:
   - Identity projection to full resolution: `h = n/g * interpolated_timeseries(e)`

4. **Randomized Exploration**:
   - Apply Cauchy noise to sparse events: e[j] ← e[j] + l * Cauchy(0,1)
   - Use Poisson thinning for feasibility

**Feasibility Projection**: Linear programming with equality constraints. Requires ![](media/image3.png) with projection operator `Π`.
