1. Approach and rationale
We address the Erdős minimum overlap problem by formulating it as a constrained optimization problem and applying a multi-start optimization strategy combined with a local search. The core idea is to find a step function h(x) on [0,2] with integral 1 that minimizes the maximum overlap integral C5. The optimization is challenging due to the non-convex nature of the problem and the high-dimensional space (70 samples). Our approach uses a combination of global exploration (via random initial points) and local refinement (via gradient-based optimization) to escape local optima. The gradient information is used to guide the local search efficiently. The key limitation is that the gradient computation requires identifying the active lag that contributes most to the maximum overlap, which can be computationally intensive. However, this is manageable given the problem size.

2. Mathematical specification
- **Representation**: h is represented as a vector of 70 samples over [0,2] with dx=2.0/70.
- **Variables**: h ∈ [0,1]^{70} with ∑h_i = 35.
- **Objective**: Minimize C5 = max_k ∫ h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx, approximated by max_k (np.correlate(h,1-h,mode='full')*dx)
- **Constraints**: 0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1 for all i, and ∑h[i] = 35.
- **Gradient computation**: For a given h, compute the correlation for all lags. The gradient of the objective is derived from the active lag (the lag that achieves the maximum correlation). The gradient is:
  - For lag k ≠ 0: dx * (1 - h[i+k]) for each i where i+k is in bounds.
  - For lag k = 0: dx * (1 - 2*h[i]) for each i.
- **Optimization method**: SLSQP (Sequential Least Squares Programming) with bounds and equality constraints.
