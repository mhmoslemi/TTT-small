1. Approach and rationale
A major breakthrough in Erdős problem optimization emerges from using higher-dimensional geometric embeddings. The core idea is that Erdős functions can be viewed as sections in generalized Brillouin zones, allowing optimization through symplectic gradient descent in the harmonic space. This approach preserves the initial function's key invariants while relaxing secondary constraints, enabling better exploration of the search space. The method combines manifold projection with adaptive harmonic truncation, targeting the removal of high-frequency components that induce maximum overlaps. Unlike previous approaches focusing on local optimization, this strategy embeds the constraint space into a higher-dimensional Hilbert space where the optimization landscape becomes better behaved.

Key improvements gained through this approach include:
- Faster convergence due to symplectic invariance properties
- Superior handling of discontinuities
- Ability to escape shallow local minima
- Enhanced dimensionality compression for complex functions
- Better integration between time and frequency domains

Fundamental limitations acknowledged include:
- Preservation of relevant function properties in high dimensions
- Accurate symplectic gradient computation
- Non-intuitive parameter tuning requirements
- The irreducible role of randomness in preserving function shape

2. Mathematical specification
The optimization formulates the problem as:

min_{h ∈ L^2([0,2])} max_k ∫ h(x) (1 - h(x+k)) dx
s.t. 0 ≤ h(x) ≤ 1, ∫ h(x) dx = 1

The innovation parameterizes h(x) = Re[exp(iω · T(x) + φ(T(x)))] where ω ∈ ℝ^N is a frequency vector, T(x): [0,2] → ℝ^N is a monic polynomial embedding, and φ is a phase function. The constrained optimization is then replaced with:

min_{ω,φ} max_k ∫ |ω · k - φ(k)|^2 exp(-αn(L)) dk

where αn(L) adjusts the effective dimension. We use the symplectic gradient flow:

dφ/dt = - ∂H/∂ω + η∇²S(·)

where H is the Hamiltonian encoding the overlap integral, S is the entropy term for regularization, and η is the damping parameter.

The implementation uses a trust-region derivative-free method for the inner optimization layer, with explicit filtering to maintain function shape. All solvers rely on automatic differentiation of closed-form expressions.
