## 1. Approach and Rationale

The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks to minimize the maximum overlap of a periodic function \( h(x) \) with its shifted versions. Current approaches focus on discrete representations (piecewise constant) or Fourier expansions, but these face challenges in scaling and escaping local optima. Our approach represents \( h(x) \) as a piecewise exponential function, parameterized by widths, amplitudes, and positions of bumps. This provides a flexible representation with good aperiodic coverage properties. We use Bayesian optimization to minimize the overlap integral, combined with multi-grid exploration to handle different scale behaviors. The key improvements are:

- Parameterization with exactly solvable integral constraints
- Bayesian optimization with automatic feasibility handling
- Multi-layer optimization (coarse-to-fine resolution)
- Early termination strategy for time efficiency

The primary bottleneck is the global optimization of the non-convex overlap function. Bayesian optimization balances exploration versus exploitation while handling the constraints. The parameterization allows calculating the overlap integral without discrete sampling.

## 2. Mathematical Specification

We represent \( h(x) \) on [0,2] as a periodic combination of exponential basis functions. Define:
\[
h(x) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N} a_j e^{-\lambda_j |x - p_j|}
\]

Where:
- \( N \) number of bumps (default 128)
- \( a_j \in (0.5, 1.0) \) amplitudes (normalized)
- \( p_j \in (-1, 1) \) periodic positions
- \( \lambda_j > 0 \) width parameters

The integral constraint \( \int_0^2 h(x) \, dx = 1 \) is satisfied by construction through parameter normalization. The overlap integral is computed exactly using Fourier analysis for the periodic function. The objective function requires Bayesopt to minimize:
\[
C_5 = \max_k \int_0^2 h(x) (1 - h(x+k)) \, dx
\]
The constraints are:
1. \( 0 \leq h(x) \leq 1 \) for all \( x \)
2. \( \int_0^2 h(x) \, dx = 1 \)

Bayesopt handles the continuous search space and non-convex optimization. The prior distribution uses Gaussians with adaptive length scales triggered by large gradients.
