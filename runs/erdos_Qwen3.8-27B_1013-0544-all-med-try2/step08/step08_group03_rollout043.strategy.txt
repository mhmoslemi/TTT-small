#### 1. Approach and Rationale
Our approach centers on **active set optimization** and **gradient alignment**, combining insights from compressed sensing, structured optimization, and the underlying geometry of the Erds overlap problem. The key limitation addressed is the tendency of gradient-based methods to get stuck in shallow minima. By identifying and focusing on the fewest "active" components responsible for high overlap, we reduce computational cost and escape local optima. The method leverages:

- **Structured Learning**: Active sets identify sparse regions where most overlaps occur.
- **Gradient Projection**: Aligns descent directions with active components while maintaining feasibility.
- **Multi-scale Projection**: Transfers knowledge across resolutions to augment the local search.

This improves upon existing solutions by expediting convergence and refining critical components, targeting improved overlap bounds.

#### 2. Mathematical Specification
- **Representation**: 
  - Represent $h(x)$ as an $N$-point step function with $dx=2.0/N$.
  - Active set $A \subset \{1,\dots,N\}$: indices contributing to high-overlap regions.
- **Objective**: 
  $$\min \max_{k} \int h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx$$
  - Equivalent to $\max_{k} \left[\text{np.correlate}(h,1-h,\text{mode}="full")\right] \times dx$
- **Constraints**:
  - $0 \leq h[i] \leq 1$ for all $i$.
  - $\sum_i h[i] \cdot dx = 1$ (or $\sum_i h[i] = N/2$).
- **Active Set Estimation**:
  - For a candidate $h$, compute overlap contributions $\Delta_i = |\text{grad}(h)(1-h)[i]|$.
  - Active set $A$ = indices with $ \delta_i > \tau $ (threshold $\tau$), pruning least contributors.
- **Gradient Formulation**:
  - $\nabla C_5 \propto \circledast_{k} \{ (1-h^2) * \delta(k) \}$ (convolution kernel of $\circledast_{k}$).
  - Projection: $P_{constraints}(h) = \min_{h'} \|\nabla C_5\|_2 \text{ s.t. } \|h'_{A^c}\|_2 = 0$.
