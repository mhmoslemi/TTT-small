### 1. Approach and Rationale

The Erdős minimum overlap problem requires minimizing the maximum overlap integral between a step function and its shifted versions. The existing approach combines SLSQP and neighborhood searches, performing grid transfers. However, this is a local search method that can easily get stuck in suboptimal solutions. Simulated annealing (SA) offers a global optimization perspective by accepting worse solutions with varying probability during optimization, preventing local optima.

The core idea is to use simulated annealing to explore the search space globally. After finding a solution on a coarse grid, we refine it by interpolating to a finer grid and running SA again. This hierarchical approach balances exploration and exploitation: the coarse grid captures the solution structure, and the fine grid refines it while maintaining constraints.

A limitation is that the correlation-based objective function remains expensive for large grids. This is mitigated by careful budget allocation. SA's temporal IV (variable, depends on cooling schedule) nature requires tuning of temperature decay and perturbation strength, but these are fixed heuristically.

### 2. Mathematical Specification

**Variables:**
- `h`: A step function with `n_points` samples on `[0, 2]`, satisfying `∀x ∈ [0, 2]: 0 ≤ h(x) ≤ 1`, and `∫₀² h(x) dx = 1`.
- `dx = 2 / n_points`.

**Objective:** Minimize:
\[
C_5 = \max_k \int_0^{2-k} h(x)(1 - h(x+k))  dx
\]
Computed as:
\[
C_5 = \frac{\max}{dx} \left( \text{np.correlate}(h, 1-h, \text{mode}='full') \right)
\] 
with domain `[0, 2-k]$.

**Constraints:**
1. \( 0 \leq h[i] \leq 1 \)
2. \( \sum h[i] \approx n_points/2 \)

**SA Algorithm:**
- **Energy:** \( E(h) = C_5 \)
- **Move:** Perturbation of two points with transfer: \(\delta_i \leftarrow \delta_i \pm \Delta\), \(\delta_j \leftarrow \delta_j \mp \Delta\).
- **Acceptance:** Probabilistic: \( P(\text{accept}) = \exp\left(-|(\Delta E)|/T\right) \), with temperature \( T = T_0 \cdot \alpha^{-t} \).
