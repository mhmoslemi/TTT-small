So we want to maximize min_t R_ac(t).

Since R_ac(t) is a trigonometric polynomial with coefficients |ĥ_k|², making it as flat as possible (close to its average = |ĥ_1|² + |ĥ_2|² + ... for the first few terms... actually the average is the DC component of R_ac which is Σ|ĥ_k|²).

The average of R_ac over t is R_ac_avg = Σ_k |ĥ_k|² (by Parseval, this equals ∫|h_ac|²/2).

So we're trying to make R_ac(t) as close to its average as possible, i.e., minimize the variance of R_ac.

This is a well-studied problem! It's related to "flat polynomials" and "low autocorrelation sequences."

For a finite sum R_ac(t) = Σ_{k=1}^{K} |ĥ_k|² cos(πkt - φ_k) (after combining sine and cosine), the minimum possible maximum deviation from the mean is related to the "Riesz product" or "lacunary series" theory.

In practice, the optimal is to have |ĥ_k|² decrease in a specific pattern. For the "flat" spectrum, we'd want |ĥ_k|² = constant for all k, but that's not possible with the constraint h ∈ [0,1].
