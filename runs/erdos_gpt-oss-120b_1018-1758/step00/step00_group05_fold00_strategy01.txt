### Approach and Rationale
The goal is to find a step function h: [0,2] → [0,1] with ∫h dx = 1 that minimizes the maximum overlap integral over all shifts. The current record is approximately 0.3808, and our aim is to find a lower bound by improving this value. 

The approach combines convex optimization and non-convex relaxation to balance feasibility and optimality. Convex optimization efficiently handles local constraints but underestimates the maximum overlap due to the linear outer product. We use Nesterov's smoothing framework to transform the max-projection problem into a series of penalized problems, iteratively refining the solution.

Key advantages:
1. Uses multiple-objective (MO) component to handle the integral constraint.
2. Introduces time-evolving parameterization for adaptivity.
3. Employs saturation and low-dimensional proxies to reduce non-convexity.
4. Modularizes operations for safety (e.g., no I/O, no gpu).

This differs from previous strategies by:
1. Using smoothing instead of gradient descent

3. Incorporating parameter drift and adaptive proxies
