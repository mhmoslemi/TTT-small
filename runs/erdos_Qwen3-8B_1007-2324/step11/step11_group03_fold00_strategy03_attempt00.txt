### 1. Approach and Rationale
We combine stochastic optimization with repair mechanisms to enhance the search efficiency. The core idea is to use a self-adaptive genetic algorithm with heavy-tailed mutations to explore the solution space while a repair function ensures integral and box constraints are met. This approach differs from previous methods by:  
- Leveraging independent heavy-tailed mutations per dimension for exploration  
- Employing exponential cooling to precisely control mutation intensity  
- Using early improvement tracking to prioritize progress over pure exploration  
The primary bottleneck addressed is the lack of local refinement capabilities in previous optimization strategies, which often got trapped in shallow optima.

Key innovations:
- Repair function based on two-phase scaling and clipping
- Mutation profiles that dynamically shift from exploration to refinement
- Early termination triggered by sustained improvement decline

### 2. Mathematical Specification
**Representation:** Symmetric step function encoded by first half of samples (size n_half)  
**Constraints:**  
- Integral constraint: $\sum_{i=1}^{n_half} h[i] \cdot dx = 0.5$ ($dx=2/n_{points}$)  
- Box constraints: $0 \leq h[i] \leq 1$

**Variables:**  
- $n_{half} = n_{points}/2$  
- $\delta = \text{problem temperature parameter}$  
- $\eta = \text{mutation scaling factor}$  
- $\alpha = \text{initial parameter strength}$

**Objective:** Minimize $$
C_5 = \max_k \int h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx
$$  
**Objective Calculation:**  
\begin{align*}
h_{full} &= [h, h_{reversed}] \\
\text{Autocorrelation} &= \text{np.correlate}(h_{full}, 1-h_{full}, \text{mode}='full') \\
C_5 &= \max(\text{Autocorrelation}) \cdot dx
\end{align*}

**Repair Function:**  
Given a vector $x$, define:
\begin{align*}
\delta_x &= \max(0.1, \min(0.5, \delta_t \cdot c)) \\
x_{clip} &= \text{np.clip}(x, 0, 1) \\
s &= \frac{n_{points}/4}{\sum x_{clip}} \quad (\text{if denominator} \neq 0, \text{ else set to } 0.5) \\
x_{rep} &= x_{clip} \cdot \min\left(1, \frac{1}{\delta_x} \cdot s\right)
\end{align*}

**Mutation:**  
Independent heavy-tailed mutation with adaptive energy $\sigma$:
\begin{align*}
\sigma &= \alpha/(\delta_x^2 + \epsilon) \\
\Delta x_i &= v_i \cdot \exp(\sigma \cdot \mathcal{N}(0,1)) \quad \text{(Cauchy-distributed perturbation)} \\
x_{new,i} &= \min(\min(\Delta x_i \cdot \epsilon_t, 1), \max(-1, -\Delta x_i \cdot \epsilon_t))
\end{align*}

**Objective:** Minimize $C_5$

    improvement_threshold = best_known_boost

    return new_pop

### 4. Search Choices and Variations
**Initial Temperature:** Start at 0.6 if improvement_rate > 0.8, else 0.5  
**Mutation Strength (σ):** 
- Create Cauchy distribution with scale =  (0.1 * temperature if improvement < improvement_threshold else 0.05)
- Or maximum scale = 0.2 * temperature^{0.7} (temperature scaling from exponential cooling)

**Repair Strategy Variations:**
- Binary repair: Two phases: scale→clip→clip→scale
- Adaptive repair: Switch between multiplication and subtraction to improve feasibility

**Objective Function Variations:**
- Spatial operator crossover combined with uniform distribution over constraints
- Exploration vs. Exploitation parameter optimization using gradient descent with adaptive step sizes

### 5. Compute Budget and Robustness
**Time Estimation:**  
- Fitness evaluation: $O(n_{points}^2)$ operations (~84 million operations for n_points=280)
- Repair: $O(n_{half})$
- Mutation operator: $O(n_{half})$

**Optimization:**  
- Use local caching of values for fitness evaluation to minimize recomputation  
- Implement diagonal fitness evaluation to focus on most promising mutations  
- Use of vectorized operations significantly reduces computational overhead

**Fallback Mechanism:**  
- If optimization stalled with + early termination window,  
- Fall back to providing the initial mutation pattern from the student propagation framework  
- If constraints violated >20%, return double the scaled minimum solution
