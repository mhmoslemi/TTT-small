## Approach and rationale

**Central idea:** Use a multilevel optimization strategy combining a coarse-grained step function generation with fine-grained optimization to minimize the maximum overlap integral. The method leverages a fast heuristic, inspired by voting theory and minimal discrepancy, to generate an initial candidate that approximates an optimal step function. This candidate is then refined using gradient-based optimization. The rationale is that this combination balances exploratory power, stability, and exploitation, often leading to faster convergence to better solutions than direct global optimization. By focusing on a parametrized form that inherently reduces overlap (e.g., a piecewise linear or tent-like structure), we address the non-convexity and potential for local optima.

**Why it could improve:** The proposed strategy addresses key bottlenecks in the previous optimization:
- The parent solution used a simple symmetric triangular shape, which may not fully exploit potential structural improvements. By introducing a multi-peak profile (e.g., tent function), we can reduce peak overlaps and achieve a better constant.
- The method explicitly targets lower discrepancy, ensuring that the function avoids correlations with its shifted versions across multiple lags.
- Coarse-grained steps allow the search to escape narrow local optima accessed via global features, while fine-grained steps refine towards the analytical minimum.

**Bottleneck addressed:**
- Previous approaches struggled with the full non-convex optimization landscape of the maximum overlap integral. This strategy uses a parameterization and coarse-to-fine levels to focus the search, mitigating the risk of premature convergence.
- Computation time is controlled by leveraging fast approximations and efficient solvers, making it feasible within the budget.

**Important limitations:**
- The initial candidate generation is heuristic (it may not find the global optimum), and the gradient-based optimization relies on sensitivity analysis, so sub-optimal can occur if the initial shape is poor.
- The method requires specifying the number of pieces in the coarse level, but this is fixed based on standard constructions.
- Convergence is not guaranteed, but the method is designed to improve upon the parent solution.

  - Box constraints: `0 ≤ h_i ≤ 1` for all `i`
  - Sum constraint: ` ∫_0^2 h(x) dx ≈ (2/n_points) * sum(h_i) = 1`, so `sum(h_i) = n_points / 2 = 140` for `n_points = 280`

**Objective:** Minimize `C_5 = max_k ∫₀² h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx`  
In discrete terms (evaluated via correlation): Compute `h_full = concatenate(h, h[::-1] of length `2 * n_points`)`, then `corr = np.correlate(h_full, 1-h_full, mode="full")` (resulting in a vector), and `C_5 = max(corr) * dx`, with `dx = 2.0 / n_points`. `C_5` gives the peak maximum normalized overlap.

**Optimization formulation:**
- The objective `C_5` requires minimizing a function that is non-convex and NP-hard to compute analytically due to the max over all shifts.
- The optimization is performed in two stages:
  - **Stage 1 (coarse-grained):** Parametrize `h` using a piecewise constant or tribble function (e.g., a sum of tents) to quickly generate candidates. Candidates satisfy the area and box constraints by adjusting heights.
  - **Stage 2 (fine-grained):** Refine the candidate using gradient-based optimization (BFGS) to minimize `C_5`.

**Objective consistency check:** The candidate `h` must satisfy `0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1` and `sum(h) * dx ≈ 1 - tolerance`. After optimization, `C_5` is re-computed using `np.correlate` to ensure exact evaluation.

**Algorithm selection:**
- **Stage 1:** Use a heuristic construction (e.g., the sieve of Eratosthenes applied to shift designs) to generate an initial tangent at a desirable low overlap region.
- **Stage 2:** Use Scipy's `minimize` with method `'trust-constr'` for efficient constraint-handling and fast convergence. The objective function `compute_overlap` is implemented with analytical derivatives for gradients where possible, but if not available, finite differences are used.

**Solver details:**
- Initial constraints are enforced via projection (clipping and scaling).
- Stage 2 uses the equality `sum(h) - 140 == 0` as a constraint in `minimize`.
- Objective gradients are computed numerically when not defined, leverages bounds for acceleration.
