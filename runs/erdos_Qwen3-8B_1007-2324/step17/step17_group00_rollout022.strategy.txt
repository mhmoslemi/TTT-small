1. **Approach and Rationale**

The central idea is to represent the step function as a combination of localized "spike" patterns that balance the trade-off between minimizing the maximum overlap and maintaining the integral constraint. The spike representation ensures controlled, non-overlapping peaks with minimal values elsewhere, which naturally encourages solutions with concentrated energy and reduced autocorrelation. This differs significantly from previous methods that relied on alternating regions or Gaussian-like peaks, focusing instead on the "phase" relationship between spikes.

We address several key aspects:
- **Delayed Exploration**: By separating the search into distinct regions (early medium- to long-term studies) we avoid premature convergence
- **Structured Randomness**: Introduces controlled randomness to explore feature separation
- **Phase Sensitivity**: Recognizes that while functional form matters, the arrangement (phase) of features has equal importance in minimizing overlap
- **Spectrum Balance**: We may need to accept higher-eigenvalue features if their phase reduces overall power in overlaps

The proposed approach introduces a novel biphasic strategy: rapid evaluation of candidate solutions with extended feature studies followed by adaptive refinement phases.
