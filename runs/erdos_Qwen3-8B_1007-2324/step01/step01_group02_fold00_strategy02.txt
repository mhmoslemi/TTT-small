1. Approach and Rationale  
   The central idea is to represent the step function \( h(x) \) as a step function that is 1 on \( s \) disjoint intervals and 0 elsewhere. This reduces the dimensionality compared to high-dimensional vector optimizations, leveraging the geometric properties of interval arrangements to minimize the overlap integral \( C_5 \). By optimizing the positions of these intervals for various numbers \( s \), we explore a lower-dimensional space, enhancing optimization efficiency. The parent solution from `initial_h_values` (47 points) is used as a starting point when feasible. Key limitations include the discrete nature of interval selection, which might miss finer-grained optima, but the chosen parameters (e.g., range for \( s \), optimization steps) provide a balance. The strategy explicitly handles constraints by ensuring disjoint intervals and unit integral, and stops early if no improvement is found.

2. Mathematical Specification  
   - **Representation**: Fix \( s \) number of disjoint intervals \([a_i^* , b_i^* ]\) in [0,2] for \( i = 1, \ldots, s \). Define \( h(x) = 1 \) if \( x \in \bigcup_{i=1}^s [a_i^* , b_i^* ] \), else 0.  
   - **Constraints**:  
     - The intervals must be disjoint: \( b_i^* < a_{i+1}^* \) for all \( i=1,\ldots,s-1 \).  
     - The integral must satisfy \( \int_0^2 h(x) dx = 1 \), which is equivalent to \( \sum_{i=1}^s (b_i^* - a_i^* ) = 1 \).  
     - Parameters \( a_i^* , b_i^* \in [0,2] \) for all \( i \), and all \( a_i^* , b_i^* \) must satisfy the ordering after sorting.  
   - **Objective**: Minimize \( C_5 \), defined by discretizing \( h \) on an \( n \)-point grid (default: 1000 points) with \( dx = 2/n \), and computing:  
     \[
        C_5 = \max_{k} \int_0^2 h(x) (1 - h(x+k)) dx = \frac{\max_{k}}{2} \left( \sum_{i} h(x_i) (1 - h(x_i + k)) \cdot dx \right)
     \]
