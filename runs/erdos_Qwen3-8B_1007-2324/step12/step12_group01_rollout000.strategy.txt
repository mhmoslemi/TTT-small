1. Approach and Rationale
    The central idea is to employ a particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm combined with a repair mechanism tailored for minimizing the overlap integral, rather than relying on simulated annealing or Bayesian optimization. PSO mimics the collective behavior of particles searching for the global minimum, with each particle representing a candidate solution in the search space. This approach balances exploration and exploitation by leveraging velocity updates and social cognition, which often outperforms gradient-based methods in non-convex, high-dimensional spaces like this. The rationale is that PSO can escape local optima more effectively than simple local searches by considering multiple candidate solutions simultaneously, and it handles the constraints better through a repair mechanism that enforces `sum(h) * dx = 1` and `0 <= h[i] <= 1` in a robust way. The method specifically addresses the bottleneck of simulated annealing in terms of slow convergence and susceptibility to local minima by introducing diversity in the particle population and adaptive weighting schemes.

    The PSO algorithm will start with an initial population derived from the provided `initial_h_values`, scaled and repaired if necessary, to ensure a diverse starting point. We will minimize the overlap integral by iterating over a fixed budget, with a focus on reducing the maximum correlation value. Unlike previous methods, PSO does not rely on probabilistic improvement schedules or correlation with RBF models but instead uses the collective intelligence of particles to guide the search. A key advantage is that it only requires function evaluations, making it computationally light to implement and scale.

    Important limitations include the non-convexity of the objective function, which might still trap the swarm in suboptimal regions, and the discrete nature of the solution space, making gradient information unavailable. However, the repair mechanism ensures constraint satisfaction, and the algorithm is designed to explore the entire feasible space within the budget. Although PSO can be more challenging to fine-tune than annealing, its efficiency and adaptability to shifting landscapes during optimization offer a distinct improvement.
