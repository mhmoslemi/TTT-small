Let me think more carefully about what shape minimizes the score for large n.

The score is 2n * max(conv) / (sum x)^2.

Key constraint: conv[0] = x[0]^2 and conv[2n-2] = x[n-1]^2. These are forced by the boundary.
Also, conv[k] for small k involves only the first k+1 elements.

If we want max(conv) to be as small as possible relative to (sum x)^2, we want:
1. x[0]^2 and x[n-1]^2 to be small (so the boundary terms don't dominate)
2. The interior convolution values to be as flat as possible

But x[0]^2 being small means x[0] is small, which reduces the sum. There's a trade-off.

Actually, I realize the problem structure for large n. The convolution at the center (around lag n-1) involves all n terms and is approximately (for a "typical" sequence) on the order of (sum/n)^2 * n = sum^2/n. The boundary values are x[0]^2 and x[n-1]^2.

If the sequence is roughly constant at level 1/n * sum, then:
- Center conv ≈ sum^2/n
- Boundary: (sum/n)^2 = sum^2/n^2
