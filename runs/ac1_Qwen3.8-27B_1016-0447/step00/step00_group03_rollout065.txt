**Improved subgradient approach:**

The key issue with pure subgradient at the constant sequence is that the subgradient is uniform. To break this:
- Add a small random perturbation to the initial sequence
- Use a "smoothed" max: instead of max, use log-sum-exp: smooth_max = (1/τ) * log(sum(exp(τ*conv[k])))
  This gives a smooth objective whose gradient is well-defined and non-uniform.

The gradient of log-sum-exp(τ*conv) w.r.t. a[i] is:
(1/τ) * sum_k exp(τ*conv[k]) / sum_k exp(τ*conv[k]) * d(conv[k])/d(a[i])
= sum_k p_k * d(conv[k])/d(a[i])
where p_k = exp(τ*conv[k]) / sum_j exp(τ*conv[j]) (softmax weights)

This gives a weighted average of the subgradients at each lag, weighted by how "close" each lag is to the max. This is always well-defined and non-degenerate!

The score can be approximated as:
score ≈ 2n * (1/τ) * log(sum(exp(τ*conv[k]))) / sum_a^2

For large τ, this approaches the exact score.
