Specifically, if A = FFT(a), then the circular convolution is IFFT(A*A). For the linear convolution with sufficient padding, it's the same.

The max of the convolution is related to the "peak" of IFFT(A*A). To minimize this peak, we want A*A to have a flat magnitude in the frequency domain... but A is constrained by a ≥ 0 and sum(a) = 1.

Actually, this line of thinking leads to the "minimum peak-to-average power ratio" (PAPR) problem, which is well-studied. The standard RX (Ryan-Braker) algorithm for PAPR reduction works on the time-domain signal. Here, our "time-domain signal" is the convolution C, and our "constraint" is that C must be the self-convolution of a non-negative sequence.
