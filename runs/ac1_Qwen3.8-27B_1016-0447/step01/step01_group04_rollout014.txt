The problem is: minimize $\max_k c_k$ subject to $\sum a = 1$, $a \geq 0$.

By the minimax theorem (Sion's theorem), this equals:
$\min_a \max_k c_k = \max_w \min_a [\sum_k w_k c_k]$
where $w$ is a probability distribution over lags.

For a fixed $w$, $\min_a \sum_k w_k c_k$ subject to $\sum a = 1$, $a \geq 0$ is:
$\min_a a^T (T_w) a$ subject to $\sum a = 1$, $a \geq 0$
where $T_w = \sum_k w_k T_k$.

If $T_w$ is a Toeplitz matrix (which it is, since each $T_k$ is Toeplitz and we're taking a weighted sum), then:
$\min a^T T_w a$ subject to $\sum a = 1$, $a \geq 0$.

If the unconstrained minimum (allowing negative $a$) has all positive entries, then that's the answer. Otherwise, it's a QP.

The unconstrained minimum of $a^T T a$ subject to $\sum a = 1$:
Lagrangian: $L = a^T T a - \lambda(\mathbf{1}^T a - 1)$
$\nabla L = 2Ta - \lambda \mathbf{1} = 0$
$Ta = \frac{\lambda}{2} \mathbf{1}$
$a = \frac{\lambda}{2} T^{-1} \mathbf{1}$

If $T$ is a Toeplitz matrix, $T^{-1}\mathbf{1}$ might have a nice structure.

For the case where $w_k = 1/(2n-1)$ (uniform lag weights):
$T_w = \frac{1}{2n-1} \sum_k T_k$

Each $T_k$ has a 1 at position $(i, k-i)$ and position $(k-i, i)$ for valid indices. So $T_w$ has entries that count how many lags $k$ have a valid $(i, k-i)$ pair for given $(i,j)$.
