- For k ≈ n-1: all terms contribute, but p[i]*p[n-1-i] = small * large → moderate
- For k ≈ n: similar
- For k ≈ 2n-2: one term, p[n-1]*p[n-1] = large * large → could be large!

So for an increasing sequence, the max might be at k = 2n-2 (the right end), not at k = n-1!

For the linear ramp: conv[2n-2] = p[n-1]^2 = (2n/(n(n+1)))^2 = 4/(n+1)^2.
conv[n-1] = 2(n+2)/(3n(n+1)) ≈ 2/(3n).

For n=2535: 4/(2536)^2 ≈ 6.2e-7 vs 2/(3*2535) ≈ 2.63e-4.

So conv[n-1] >> conv[2n-2]. The max IS at n-1 for the linear ramp. ✓

For the quadratic (α=2): p[n-1] = 3n^2/n^3 = 3/n.
conv[2n-2] = 9/n^2.
conv[n-1] = 9*B(3,3)/n = 9*(1/30)/n = 3/(10n).

For n=2535: 9/n^2 ≈ 1.4e-6 vs 3/(10n) ≈ 1.18e-4.
conv[n-1] >> conv[2n-2]. Still at the middle.

But wait, what about conv[k] for k between n-1 and 2n-2? For the quadratic, is there a k where conv[k] > conv[n-1]?

For a monotonically increasing p, conv[k] for k > n-1:
conv[k] = sum_{i=k-n+1}^{n-1} p[i]*p[k-i]

As k increases from n-1 to 2n-2, the number of terms decreases from n to 1, but each term involves larger values of p. The maximum could be somewhere in between.

For the quadratic with n=2535:
conv[n-1] = 3/(10n) ≈ 1.18e-4
conv[2n-2] = 9/n^2 ≈ 1.4e-6

What about conv[1.5n]? (approximately)
The terms are p[i]*p[k-i] where i ranges from k-n+1 to n-1.
For k ≈ 1.5n: i ranges from 0.5n to n-1.
p[i] ≈ 3i^2/n^3, p[k-i] ≈ 3(k-i)^2/n^3.
conv[k] ≈ (9/n^6) * sum_{i=0.5n}^{0.99n} i^2(k-i)^2

This is a complex integral. But intuitively, since p is quadratic (growing as i^2), the product p[i]*p[k-i] is maximized when both i and k-i are large, i.e., near the endpoints of the sum. But the endpoints have only 1 term each. The bulk of the contribution comes from the middle of the sum.
