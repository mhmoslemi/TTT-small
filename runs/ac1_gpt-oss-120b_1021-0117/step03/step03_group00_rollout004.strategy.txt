# Approach and Rationale

The algorithm is designed to solve a non-convex optimization problem of minimizing the autocorrelation upper bound (AC1) of a sequence. The central idea is to use a gradient-based optimization method applied to the exponential smoothing of the convolution maximum. This approach improves upon previous methods by:

1. Using a more targeted gradient computation
2. Incorporating automatic smoothing decay
3. Maintaining a low-memory footprint
4. Using a self-improving mechanism

The key limitation is that we assume gradient descent will converge sufficiently close to the global optimum within the time budget, though convergence guarantees are not provided due to the problem's non-convex nature.

# Mathematical Specification

**Variables**:
- $a \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with $a_i \geq 0$, $\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i = \sqrt{2n}$

**Objective**:
Minimize $\frac{2n}{(\sum a_i)^2} \cdot \max_{k} |\{a \ast a\}|[k]|$

**Constraints**:
- $n = 2535$
- $0 \leq a_i \leq 1000$ for all $i$

**Gradient Estimation**:
- $\epsilon_{\text{step}} = \exp(-\text{iteration\_count}/50)$ (smoothness parameter)
- $\omega_i = \exp(\frac{(\{a \ast a\}[k] - \max_k(\{a \ast a\}[k])}{\epsilon_{\text{step}}}$ (exponential weights)

**Gradient Update**:
- $\nabla \approx \frac{2n}{\epsilon_{\text{step}}} \cdot \sum_{k} \omega_{k} \cdot \frac{\partial \{a \ast a\}[k]}{\partial a}$

**Component Derivatives**:
- For full convolution, $\frac{\partial \{a \ast a\}[m]}{\partial a_j} = 2 \cdot a_{m-j}$ for valid indices

**Binary Improvement Check**:
- Use hard comparison between candidate and current for selection
