## Approach and Rationale
The central idea is to construct and refine sequences composed of step-function pulses, which inherently limit the maximum autocorrelation by concentrating energy in discrete bursts. Step-function sequences allow precise control over the convolution peaks, addressing the primary bottleneck of gradient-based methods that rely on continuous optimization and may get trapped in local optima. 

This approach directly targets the AC1 upper bound by design—sequences with multiple, isolated peaks minimize the maximum autocorrelation relative to energy, as energy lingers less in shifted positions. Unlike the exponential-based method, step-function candidates enforce a structure that naturally suppresses detrimental autocorrelation peaks, and initial parents (if provided) can serve as blueprints. Importantly, the method starts with a step-function initialization and uses smart perturbations to preserve the beneficial structure while exploring the trade-offs between pulse amplitude, separation, and count.

Important limitations: Step-function sequences may not capture all global optima, but they form a well-defined subspace whose structure is computationally tractable and empirically supported for AC1 minimization. The boundaries of allowed structures are defined by pulse separations and amplitudes, enforced via algorithmic control.

## Mathematical Specification
### Representation
Sequences are represented as non-negative step-function sequences of length \(n\). Each sequence is defined by at most \(k\) consecutive intervals, where each interval contains a constant value (the height of the step). The sequence is formally defined by:
- Breakpoints: indices \( p_0, p_1, \ldots, p_{k-1} \) dividing the domain into intervals.
- Heights: values \( h_0, h_1, \ldots, h_{k-1} \) assigned to each interval.

The sequence can be flattened into a 1D array by repeating each interval's value for the sublength of the interval.

### Objective and Constraints
The objective to minimize is:
\[
\text{AC1} = \frac{2n \cdot \max(\text{convolve}(a, a))}{(\sum a)^2}
\]

Constraints:
1. \( a_i \geq 0 \) ∀ \( i \)
2. \( a_i \leq 1000 \) ∀ \( i \) (evaluator enforces by clipping)
3. \( \sum a \geq 0.01 \) (evaluator enforces by clipping)

### Candidate Generation
Candidates are generated as sequences with up to \(k\) steps, where \(k\) is a hyperparameter (e.g., \(k = 10\)). Breakpoints \(p_i\) are uniformly distributed, and heights \(h_i\) are normally distributed with mean proportional to \(n\) and standard deviation \(0.1 \cdot \text{mean}\).

### Evaluation Function
Use `evaluate_sequence` from the evaluator. A lower is better metric.
