1. Approach and Rationale  
We use a genetic optimization algorithm with exponential smoothing to minimize the AC1 upper bound. The algorithm first initializes a population of sequences, then evolves them through selection, crossover, and mutation. The key insight is to exploit the long-range structure of optimal sequences by using a smoothing technique based on the Fourier transform of the autocorrelation function. This approach avoids local optima by combining exploration through mutation with exploitation via crossover, and ensures sequences maintain structure even under aggressive mutations.

The AC1 bound relies on the maximum convolution value, which dominates the search space. By replacing the sequence with a smoothed version of its autocorrelation, we find sequences that minimize the maximum convolution without sacrificing length. This leverages the fact that optimal sequences are often periodic or recursive in structure.
Our method differs from previous gradient-based lazy propagation by focusing on structural changes and using sparse operations to handle the cancellation of high-convolution peaks.

- **Variables**: A sequence |\(a\)| of length |\(n\)|.

- **Smoothing**: Calculate the autocorrelation's FFT |\(b = |\text{FFT}(a \ast a)|\). The smoothed sequence |\(a'\)| is defined by the local maximum in the autocorrelation spectrum.
