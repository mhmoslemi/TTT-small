1. Approach and rationale
We address the problem of minimizing the AC1 upper bound for a nonnegative step function by employing a gradient-based optimization approach. The primary objective is to minimize the maximum value of the linear convolution of the sequence with itself divided by the square of the total sum. Our solution leverages the convolution theorem to compute gradients efficiently using Fast Fourier Transform (FFT), enabling large-scale optimization without dense matrix operations. We start from the provided parent sequence and use gradient descent with Barzilai-Borwein step sizes to find a locally optimal solution. If time permits and the solution surpasses the initial candidate, we perform a final random swap step to explore nearby regions.

2. Mathematical specification
We aim to minimize the objective function:
    score = (2 * n * max_conv) / (sum_a)^2
where `max_conv` is the maximum value of the linear convolution of the sequence with itself, and `sum_a` is the sequence's sum.

Given the large dimensionality (n ≈ 2500), we use FFT for efficient convolution computation. The gradient of the objective is approximated using the concept of active constraints (indices where the convolution is maximized). The Kelly function bounds search coefficients between 0 and 0 ⋯ 0 × the sum coefficient, but this simplification reduces the dimensionality by a factor of  50% through coefficient consolidation.
