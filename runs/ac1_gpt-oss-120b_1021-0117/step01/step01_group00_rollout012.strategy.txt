1. Approach and Rationale

This approach uses a genetic algorithm with a tournament selection mechanism to explore the space of coefficient sequences. The central idea is to quickly identify sequences with low autocorrelation by leveraging the scale-invariance property of the AC1 bound, which means we can normalize sequences to a fixed sum. The search space is reduced to sequences with values between 0 and 1000 and a fixed total sum of 1000 (for simplicity). The algorithm tracks the best candidate found during the search and relies on an FFT-based convolution to approximate the autocorrelation values efficiently during the search process.

The previous state-of-the-art used random swaps within a normalized sequence and occasional restarts, which could get stuck in local optima. This new approach enhances diversity through crossover and mutation operations, allowing the search to explore the space more thoroughly. The use of FFT-based convolution significantly speeds up the evaluation of candidates compared to direct convolution.

Key Limitations and Distinctions:
- The algorithm assumes normalized values between 0 and 1.
- The FFT-based convolution approximation may have small errors compared to the exact method used in the evaluator.
- The search may not find the absolute minimum but provides a strong lower bound within the given budget.

2. Mathematical Specification

- **Variables**: A sequence of n=6962 real numbers, x_i for i=0 to n-1, initially normalized to [0,1] with sum=1.
- **Objective**: Minimize f(x) = (2 * n * max_conv) / (sum(x))^2, where sum(x)=1 by normalization.

Convolution is computed using FFT for efficiency:
- FFT compute: Input sequence of length n padded to 2*n-1 for full convolution.
