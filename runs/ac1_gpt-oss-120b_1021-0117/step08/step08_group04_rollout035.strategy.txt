**Approach and Rationale**

The central idea is to minimize the AC1 upper bound using a sparse optimization method. Unlike previous approaches that relied on gradient-based methods with expensive full convolution computations, this strategy uses a combination of:

1. **Adaptive Step Size**: Uses line search and curve of steepest descent to find optimal step sizes.
2. **Sparse Updates**: Focuses computations on coordinates relevant to the current position in a Gaussian-convolution-based landscape.
3. **Potential Energy Mapping**: Models the optimization landscape as a potential energy field and takes steps proportional to gradient estimates with temperature decay.
4. **Early Termination**: Checks if the bound falls within the target before extensive computation.

The key advantage is computational efficiency - we avoid the expensive O(n²) full convolution operations during gradient calculation by using FFT-based convolution and adaptive sparse sampling. We also implement a restart mechanism if the solution quality plateaus.
