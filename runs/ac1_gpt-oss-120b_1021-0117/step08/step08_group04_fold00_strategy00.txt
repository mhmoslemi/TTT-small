**Approach and Rational**
The problem requires to find nonnegative, step-function coefficients that minimize the AC1 upper bound, defined as 2 × n × max(self-convolution)/sum(coefficients)^2. The AC1 bound measures the maximum autocorrelation of the sequence, which is minimized by concentrating energy and reducing cross-terms. The initial approach was a heuristic optimization that achieved 1.505808. The key limitation of this approach is its heavy reliance on full-gradient calculations via FFT, which are computationally expensive for large n. Additionally, the method updates are stochastic and can sometimes get stuck in local minima.

My rational approach balances:
1. **Efficient Gradient Calculation**: Use FFT-based autocorrelation to compute the gradient in O(L log L) time, where L is an FFT size estimate.
2. **Robust Optimization**: Switch between continuous and discrete updates depending on temperature decay.
3. **Early Termination**: Exit if the bound falls within target or time expires.
4. **Component-wise Repair**: When violating constraints, shift mass to keep coefficients within [0,1000].

**Mathematical Specification**
- **Variables**: `a` coefficients (initial simplex) of length `n`.
- **Objective**: Minimize `2×n×max(autoconvolution)`/`sum(a)^2`
- **Constraints**: `a_i ≥ 0`, `sum(a) = 1` (allows scale-invariance)
- **Gradient Smoothness**: Use a Gaussian kernel to approximate gradient of `max(autoconv)`
- **Stochastic Update**: Smooth updates at high `temp` (scramble), focused moves at low `temp` (systematic)
- **Solver Formulation**: Not directly applicable; use projected gradient descent
