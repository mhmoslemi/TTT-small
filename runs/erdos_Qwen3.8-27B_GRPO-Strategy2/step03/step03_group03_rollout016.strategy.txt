1. Approach and Rationale
The core idea of this strategy is to use a hierarchical optimization approach that combines coarse-grid and fine-grid optimizations to escape local optima. Unlike previous methods that relied on single-grid optimization, we partition the search space by exploiting varying resolutions. Our method starts by optimizing a coarse approximation of the function on a reduced grid, then progressively refines this approximation through interpolation and gradient descent. This addresses the issue of premature local convergence by providing diverse starting points and leverages interpolation to transfer optimal traits between resolutions. The main bottleneck addressed is the tendency of single-grid methods to get trapped in suboptimal regions of the search space, which this hierarchical approach mitigates by iterating through multiple scales with adaptive repair mechanisms.

2. Mathematical Specification
The function `h(x)` is represented with `n_points` samples over `[0,2]`, where `n_points` is dynamically determined as `10`, `50`, `100`, and finally `70` samples. The objective to minimize is:
    \[ \min_{h} \max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1-h(x+k))\,dx \]
