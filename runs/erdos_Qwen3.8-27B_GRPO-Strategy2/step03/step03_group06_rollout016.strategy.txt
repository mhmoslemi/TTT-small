Approach and rationale
The Erdős discrepancy problem concerns findings step functions that minimize the maximum overlap between the function and its shifted complement. The current record stands at approximately 0.3808. This approach improves upon previous methods by focusing on two-level step functions (using 0 and 1) with exactly n/2 units, which naturally satisfies the integral constraint for any n (as n/2 * (2/n) = 1). The rationale is that binary functions are often optimal for such discrepancy problems since they minimize interactions between shifted copies.

Unlike gradient-based optimization, this approach uses simulated annealing over combinatorial states. We generate neighbors by swapping one-bit perturbations between the positions of ones and zeros, ensuring the unit count remains constant. The objective function remains the cross-correlation at all lags (mode='full'). This combinatorial search avoids local optima by allowing uphill moves and gradually cools the acceptance criteria.

The primary limitation is the exponential combinatorial space (C(n, n/2)). However, the two-level assumption reduces computational burden compared to fully general continuous functions. The method finds high-quality solutions through random exploration and structured neighborhood sampling.
