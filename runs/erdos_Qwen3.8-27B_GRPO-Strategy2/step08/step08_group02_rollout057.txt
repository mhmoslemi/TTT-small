Δc[m] ≈ d * (g_i[m] - g_j[m]) where g_i[m] = ∂c[m]/∂h[i], g_j[m] = ∂c[m]/∂h[j]

Then the new max is approximately max(c[m] + d*(g_i[m] - g_j[m])).

This is O(n) to compute for all m (since each ∂c[m]/∂h[i] is nonzero for O(n) values of m).

Actually, for a fixed i, ∂c[m]/∂h[i] is nonzero for m in a range of size O(n). Specifically:
- h[i] appears in c[m] for shifts where i is either the "source" or "target" position
- This gives at most 2*(n-1) + 1 = O(n) nonzero entries

    n = _gs['n']
