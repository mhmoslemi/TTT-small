## 1. Approach and Rationale

The central idea is to represent the periodic step function in terms of a train of rectangular pulses distributed over the interval [0,2], enforcing both integral and boundary conditions. This approach leverages optimization in coefficient space to explore promising regions faster than direct sample optimization. The primary heuristic is that optimal solutions exhibit clustered features, and periodization naturally explains repetitive structures. Limitations include potential non-convergence if local optima dominate the landscape or if the basis representation is insufficiently flexible. This differs from the parent's interpolation methods by using analytic parameterization, though both rely on gradient-based optimization.

## 2. Mathematical Specification

### Representation and Constraints
Define h(x) = ∑_{j=1}^{N} (1/2) * rect((x - (2j-1)π/4) / w) * a_j  
where a_j ∈ [0,1] are amplitudes ensuring ∫h(x)dx ≤ 1, and w controls width. Points (2j-1)π/4 ensure even spacing over [0,8], later reduced to [0,2] by periodicity with fundamental period T=8/m for some m.  
Enforced via convexification: minimize ∑_{k} ∫h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx.s.t. ∫h(x)dx=1, 0≤h(x)≤1  
approximated by quadratic penalties rather than direct plane implementation.

### Objective and Variables
Variables: amplitudes a ∈ ℝ^{N} with 0≤aᵢ≤1  
Objective: max_{k∈[-M,M]} ∫_{0}^{2} h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx  
Penalized for norm: maximize - (sum_{i} aᵢ²)

### Repair
Adjust amplitudes to clamp h(x)∈[0,1], then enforce integral constraint via Lagrange multiplier technique.
