### 1. Approach and Rationale

The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks a step function h(x) on [0,2] with integral exactly 1 that minimizes the worst-case overlap integral C5 = max_k ∫ h(x)·(1-h(x+k)) dx over all integer shifts k. Solutions for this problem have been found with C5 values around 0.38. Our approach departs from typical gradient-based optimization by using a differential evolution algorithm for global exploration combined with a physics-inspired simulated annealing method for local refinement. This mixed strategy addresses several bottlenecks in previous methods:

1. **Global vs. Local Optimization Balance**: We reduce the risk of local minima by starting with broad search (differential evolution) before refining with a physical cooling process that models annealing in materials science.

2. **Adaptive Dimensionality**: We prioritize resolution levels based on expected payoff, dynamically balancing between computation time and solution quality.

3. **Representation Innovation**: Instead of fixed-position binary vectors, we use signed "block" moves that preserve integral constraints automatically through the optimization formulation.

4. **Multi-scale Lag Analysis**: The algorithm specifically targets lag positions that contribute most to C5 using gradient information from sparse evaluations.

5. **Predictive Restarting**: Remaining time is used to predict likely improvements and selectively retry promising configurations.

The potential benefits are hypothesized to be faster convergence to better solutions by combining directed search (physics-inspired moves) with undirected mutation (differential evolution). Implementation complexity is manageable with standard Python libraries.

**Objective**: Minimize C5(h) = max_{k=-n+1}^{n-1} [ (1/n) * Σ_{i=0}^{n-1} h[i]·(1 - h[i+k]) ] using circular convolution.
