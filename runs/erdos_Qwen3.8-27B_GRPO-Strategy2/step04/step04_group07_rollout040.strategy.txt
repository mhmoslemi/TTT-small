The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks to find a step function h(x) that minimizes the maximum overlap with its shifted complement. This innovative approach leverages the **low-rank structure** of optimal solutions and combines **projected gradient optimization** with **rank-adaptive subspace estimation**. Unlike previous methods that treated the entire n-dimensional space, this strategy focuses on evolving a small set of features (basis functions) that capture the essential morphism of the optimal solution. By systematically growing the rank from a minimal basis and incorporating curvature analysis, we avoid entrapment in local optima while respecting both the integral and bounding constraints 0<=h(x)<=1.

The central idea is to parameterize h(x) as a linear combination of carefully chosen basis functions, then optimize the coefficients while dynamically adjusting the rank based on estimated curvature. This reduces the dimensionality problem while still finding high-fidelity solutions. The algorithm intelligently balances exploration and exploitation by alternating between low-rank projections and rank growth phases, with curvature estimates guiding the most promising search directions.

Important limitations include the need for convex separable basis functions and the potential for premature rank stabilization, but these are mitigated by adaptive restarts and multi-resolution projection schemes.
