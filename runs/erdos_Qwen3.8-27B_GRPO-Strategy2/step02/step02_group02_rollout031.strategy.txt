**1. Approach and Rationale**  
The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks to minimize the maximum overlap between a function \(h: [0, 2] \to [0, 1]\) and its shifted versions, subject to \(\int_0^2 h(x)  dx = 1\). We unify **Fourier analysis** with **non-constrained optimization** using a two-phase strategy:  
1. **Fourier-Filtering Initial Guess**: Represent \(h\) as a weighted sum of Fourier components on frequency grid \(\{0, 2\pi, 4\pi, \ldots, 12\pi\}\) for global exploration.  
2. **Projected Gradient Optimization**: Refine the guess via gradient descent projected onto the feasible set \([0, 1]^n\) with integral constraint enforcement.  
This ensures strong boundaries for overlap reduction by designing the Fourier structure to avoid self-overlap under integer shifts. Key limitations:  
- Fourier representation ensures smooth functions, but optimization assumes continuous shifts (addressed by discretization).  
- Gradient projection may fail at boundaries; fallback uses singular value projection with restarts.  

**Bottlenecks Addressed**:  
- Initial parent stagnation: Fourier-filtered restarts escape local minima.  
- Structural overlap: Sinusoidal frequency control filters overlap-prone components.  
- Computational cost: Adaptive restarts free search from plateaus.  

**2. Mathematical Specification**  
**Variables**: \(h \in [0, 1]^n\), \(dx = 2.0/n\), \(n = 70\).  
**Objective**: \(\min \max_k \frac{1}{n} \left\lVert h \odot (1 - \shift_k h) \right\rVert_1\) (clipped to avoid grid dependencies).  
**Constraints**:  
\[
0 \leq h_i \leq 1 \quad \forall i, \quad \sum_i h_i = n/2.
\]  
**Representation**:  
\[
h_i = \sum_{m=0}^8 w_m \cdot f_m(i) \quad \text{where} \quad f_m(i) = \cos\left(2\pi m \cdot \frac{i}{n}\right).
\]  
**Projected Gradient Step**:  
Minimize \(f(h) = \max_k \overline{\text{overlap}}(k)\) subject to \(0 \leq h_i \leq 1\) and \(\sum_i h_i = n/2\), projected iteratively.
