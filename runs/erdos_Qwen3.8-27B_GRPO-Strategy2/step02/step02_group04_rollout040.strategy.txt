We are given a task to find an improved upper bound for the Erdős minimum overlap problem constant C.
 The objective is to minimize the overlap integral:
   C5 = max_k ∫ h(x) (1 - h(x+k)) dx
 Subject to:
   h(x) ∈ [0,1] for all x
   ∫ h(x) dx = 1

 We are to represent h as n_point samples over [0,2] with dx = 2.0 / n_points.

 We are given an initial solution (initial_h_values) and a budget of 1100 seconds.

 The current record is 0.38085 and our goal is to get lower (i.e., smaller) than 0.38085.

 We are encouraged to try a different approach than the previous one.

 The previous approach used a Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) with local refinement via SLSQP.

 We are going to propose a new approach that uses a combination of global optimization and local refinement, but with a different global strategy.

 Idea: We will use a genetic algorithm (GA) to explore the space of binary-like (or step function) solutions, and then use local refinement.

 Why GA? The problem is non-convex and has many local optima. GA can explore a wide range of solutions.

 1. Representation: We represent h as a vector of n_points. Each element is between 0 and 1, with sum n_points/2.

However, note that the correlation is computed with mode='full', so the length of the correlation array is 2*n_points - 1. We take the maximum value and multiply by dx to get the c5 value.

 3. However, note that the objective function is non-convex and expensive to evaluate (since it involves computing the correlation for every candidate).
