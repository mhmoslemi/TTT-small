**Key insight**: The problem is equivalent to finding a probability distribution $a$ on $\{0, \ldots, n-1\}$ that minimizes the maximum value of the "convolution" (which is related to the probability that two independent samples sum to a particular value).

So we want to find a distribution on $\{0, \ldots, n-1\}$ that makes $X+Y$ as "uniform" as possible over $\{0, \ldots, 2n-2\}$.

The uniform distribution on $\{0, \ldots, 2n-2\}$ has mass $1/(2n-1)$ at each point. So the ideal is $(a*a)_k = 1/(2n-1)$ for all $k$, giving score $2n/(2n-1)$.

This is achievable if and only if the "sum" of two independent samples from $a$ is uniform on $\{0, \ldots, 2n-2\}$. This is a well-studied problem in probability.

For the uniform distribution on $\{0, \ldots, n-1\}$, $X+Y$ has a triangular distribution (beta-like), with peak at $n-1$ and value $n/(n^2) = 1/n$ (times $n^2$ for the unnormalized case). The score is $2n \cdot (1/n) = 2$ (for normalized $a$).
