For continuous analogs, if f is the indicator of [0,1] (continuous uniform), then f*f is triangular on [0,2] with peak at 1. That's the discrete uniform case.

What if f is the indicator of a "fat" set with holes? In the continuous case, if f is the indicator of a set S with |S| = 1 (normalized), then f*f at point x is |S ∩ (x-S)|. To make this uniform, S should be a "Bohr set" or have some additive structure.

In the discrete case with n=256, we could try:
- f[i] = 1 for i in some subset S of {0,...,255}, 0 otherwise
- Then f*f[k] = |{i: i in S and k-i in S}|
- We want this to be as uniform as possible

This is related to "difference sets" in group theory! A (v, k, lambda) difference set in a group of order v is a subset of size k such that every non-identity element is represented exactly lambda times as a difference of two elements. This would make the autocorrelation (circular convolution) flat.
