The key insight is that the convolution `np.convolve(a, a)` is NOT the same as the autocorrelation in the standard sense. It's the "cross-correlation" of a with the reversed a, which is different.

Specifically, `np.convolve(a, a)[k] = sum_i a[i] * a[k-i]`. The maximum of this can be less than `sum a[i]^2` because the terms `a[i]*a[k-i]` at the center (k = n-1) involve `a[i]*a[n-1-i]` which is NOT `a[i]^2` unless the sequence is palindromic.

So for a palindromic sequence (a[i] = a[n-1-i]), the center of the convolution IS sum a[i]^2, and the score is always >= 2.

But for non-palindromic sequences, the center of the convolution can be less than sum a[i]^2, and the maximum might be at a different position.

This changes everything! The problem is now interesting.
