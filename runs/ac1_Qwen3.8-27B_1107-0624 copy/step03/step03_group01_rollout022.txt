def _next_pow2(n):

Let me also think about the Frank-Wolfe phase more carefully.

Frank-Wolfe (conditional gradient) for minimizing f(a) over the simplex:
- At each iteration, compute g = ∇f(a) (or a subgradient)
- Find s = argmin_{v in simplex} g · v = e_{argmin g} (a vertex of the simplex)
- Update: a ← (1-t) * a + t * s, where t = 2/(iter+2) or via line search

The subgradient of f(a) = 2n * max_k q_k(a) at point a is:
g_j = 4n * a_{k* - j} (for valid k*-j), where k* = argmax

This is a "shifted and reversed" version of a, scaled by 4n.

The Frank-Wolfe step: s = e_j where j = argmin g_j = argmin a_{k*-j} = argmin a (at the position that maps to the minimum of a).

So the FW step pushes weight from the current a towards the coordinate that has the smallest a_{k*-j}.

This makes intuitive sense: we want to reduce the peak of the convolution, which means spreading weight away from the "hot" positions.

The convergence of FW is O(1/t), so after 10000 iterations, we've reduced the gap by a factor of 10000. If the initial gap is 0.1 (from 1.6 to 1.5), after 10000 iterations it would be 0.00001. That's more than enough.
