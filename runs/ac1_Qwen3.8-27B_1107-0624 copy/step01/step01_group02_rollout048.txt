The full convolution `(x*x)[n] = sum_k x[k]*x[n-k]` is the convolution, while the autocorrelation is `(x * x_flipped)[n] = sum_k x[k]*x[k-n]` (correlating x with its flip).

So `np.convolve(a, a)` is the CONVOLUTION, not the autocorrelation!

For a convolution, there's no guarantee that the center is the maximum. In fact, the convolution of a non-negative sequence with itself can have its maximum anywhere.

Let me understand why. The convolution `c[n] = sum_k a[k]*a[n-k]` is NOT bounded below by `(sum a)^2 / (2n-1)` in the same way.

Actually, `sum_n c[n] = (sum a)^2`. So the average of c is `(sum a)^2 / (2n-1)`. For the score to be below 2, we need `max(c) < 2*(sum a)^2 / (2n)` = `(sum a)^2 / n`... wait, score = 2n*max(c)/(sum a)^2 < 2 means max(c) < (sum a)^2/n.

And the average of c is (sum a)^2/(2n-1). So we need max(c) < (sum a)^2/n, which is slightly above the average (2n-1)/n * avg = (2 - 1/n) * avg.

So we need the convolution to be "more flat than average" at its peak. This is possible!

The key insight: for the convolution (not autocorrelation), the values can be distributed more evenly. The uniform sequence gives a triangular convolution (peaked at center), but other sequences can give flatter convolutions.

In fact, the problem of minimizing the peak of the convolution of a non-negative sequence with itself is related to the "convex body" or "number theory" problems about the additive energy of sets.
