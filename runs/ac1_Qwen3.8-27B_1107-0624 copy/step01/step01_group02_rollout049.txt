Actually, I think the key insight is:
- In the continuous limit, the optimal g is a DECREASING, CONCAVE function.
- The more "mass" near the left end, the more the convolution peak at the center is reduced (because it pairs large values on the left with small values on the right).
- But too much concentration at the left makes g(0)^2 large, which increases the "edge" terms.
- In the continuous limit, the edge terms vanish (they're O(1/n)), so the optimal is determined by the center convolution.
