The gradient with respect to a_i is then:
  g_i = (4.0*n * (sum_{k} p_k * a_{k-i})) / (S^2) - (4.0*n * Tmax) / (S^3)
where p_k is the normalized p_k = (exp(c_k/tau)) / sum(exp(c/tau)), and S = sum(a).

We must ensure numerical stability by clipping the candidate to [0,1000] in the gradient computation.

Step 1: Preprocessing the parent sequence
- Given parent of length 6121, iterate to improve it and further explore other candidates.

Step 2: Define the optimization pipeline
- We will use Adam optimizer with a dynamically adjusted learning rate based on time budget.
- The gradients are approximated with finite differences for robustness but we have an exact analytic form. We use the analytic form due to the protection against large n.
- We avoid restarting the optimizer until the gradient becomes unstable.

Step 3: Hill climbing as a local search alternative
- Use the evaluator as a "black box" to explore neighbors without expensive gradient computations.

Step 4: Coordinate-wise updates

Step 5: Schedule based on time
- Start with parent as the main candidate.
- Use time budgeting: prioritize gradient-based updates when significant improvements are near.

Step 6: Save best candidate
- Compare each generated candidate with the best candidate using the evaluator.
- Pass only one copy of the best candidate to the evaluator for performance.

Step 7: Avoid normalization steps during optimization
- We will use the evaluator's scale directly to ensure we are minimizing the correct objective.
