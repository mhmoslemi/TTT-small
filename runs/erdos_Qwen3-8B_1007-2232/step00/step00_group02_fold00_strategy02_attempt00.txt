    <h1>Erdős Minimum Overlap Problem Refinement</h1>
    
    <h2>Approach and Rationale</h2>
    <p>This solution employs a hybrid optimization strategy that balances global exploration with local refinement. Unlike traditional approaches that rely on simple perturbations, this method combines three complementary phases:</p>
    <ol>
        <li><strong><em>Multi-scale decomposition:</em></strong> Instead of optimizing a single 45-point vector, we decompose the function into shorter, manageable segments that capture different frequency components.</li>
        <li><strong><em>Perturbation-based optimization:</em></strong> Each local segment is optimized separately, targeting specific conflict regions while maintaining global structure.</li>
        <li><strong><em>Projected gradient search:</em></strong> For the overall function, we use projected gradients constrained by the chosen segments, enabling continuous fine-tuning while maintaining segment consistency.</li>
    </ol>
    The key insight is that both short-range dependencies (narrow peaking) and long-range inconsistencies contribute to overlap, and addressing both requires complementary optimization techniques.

    <h2>Mathematical Specification</h2>
    <h3>Representation and Variables</h3>
    <p>We represent candidate solutions as a piecewise function composed of $m$ segments, with each segment defined by $b$ points. The entire function spans $n = b \cdot m$ points over the domain $[0, 2]$. Each segment comes from a uniform distribution defined at $b$ equally spaced points within its interval, ensuring overall integral $\sum h[t] \cdot dt = 1$ (with $dt = 2/n$).</p>
    <ol>
        <li>$n$: total number of points</li>
        <li>$m$: number of segments</li>
        <li>$b$: points per segment ($n = m \cdot b$)</li>
