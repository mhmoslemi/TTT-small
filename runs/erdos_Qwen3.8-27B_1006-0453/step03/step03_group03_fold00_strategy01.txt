Approach and rationale
The primary limitation of the current approach is its susceptibility to local optima due to overly aggressive hyperparameter tuning and a lack of systematic exploration-exploitation balance. My solution addresses this by implementing a Bayesian optimization framework that explicitly models the objective function's gradient and uses it for adaptive search. Unlike the previous method that relies on fixed sequential phases, this approach performs gradient-based optimization globally before polishing, ensuring sustained progress toward the optimum. The strategy leverages the exponential log-sum-exp smoothing to handle the non-analytic max operation, making gradients tractable. The use of dimensionally adaptive gradient estimation and a projection-based feasibility restoration ensures robust constraint handling. Finally, a Gaussian process surrogate model guides the search dynamically, exploiting known regions to jump to promising unexplored areas.

Mathematical specification
- Representation: h is a function on [0,2] discretized into n points, with integral sum(h)=n/2 and 0≤h[i]≤1.
- Objective: C5 = max_k ∫ h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx, approximated using np.correlate for discrete evaluation.
- Smoothed objective: Use smooth_erdos(h,T) defined with convex combination log-sum-exp: 
  f_T(h) = dx * [m + (1/T) * log(sum(exp(T*(corr_i - m)))]
  where m is the mode-peak of correlation, provides C5 smoothing.
- Constraints: Integral equality and box bounds enforced via projection function project_feasible.
