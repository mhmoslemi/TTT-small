### Approach and rationale
The current methods rely heavily on local search and gradient-based optimization, which may get stuck in local optima. We propose a hybrid method that combines:

1. **Structured Initialization**: We leverage the periodic nature of the problem by initializing the solution with a lattice that has a controlled density and phase shifts. This is inspired by the Moroz rank-1 lattice for high-dimensional sampling, which is known to spread points evenly over the domain.

2. **Geometric Optimization**: We formulate a continuous version of the problem and use a combination of deterministic annealing and geometric descent to minimize the maximum overlap. The continuous relaxation allows us to use gradient-based methods more effectively.

3. **Binary Refinement**: After obtaining a continuous solution, we refine it by converting it to a binary representation and performing a gradient-driven binary optimization. This is implemented via a thresholding procedure combined with a subgradient method.

The key difference from previous strategies is the use of a structured lattice-based initialization and a direct geometric optimization that exploits the periodicity of the objective function. This approach is expected to escape local optima more effectively.

### Mathematical specification
We represent h(x) as a piecewise constant function with samples at n_points locations in [0, 2]. The constraint is that the integral of h(x) is 1, which is enforced as the sum of h[i] over i equals n_points/2 (after discretization).

Objective to minimize:
C_5 = max_{k} \int_0^2 h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx

We discretize the convolution to be minimized, which is linear in h. The maximization over lags k can be done efficiently via the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) for the correlation.
