Let me also add: when the binary search is stuck (stall > 50), do a "pair swap" - find the two positions p1, p2 (both 1) and q1, q2 (both 0) such that the combined flip reduces the max correlation. This is a 2-opt move.

For the 2-opt move, the change in correlation at shift k is:
Δcorr[k] = D[p1,k] + D[p2,k] - D[q1,k] - D[q2,k]

I want to minimize max_k (corr[k] + Δcorr[k]). This is hard to solve exactly. A heuristic:
1. Find k* = argmax corr
2. Find p1, p2 (ones) that most reduce corr[k*]: top 2 by -D[p,k*]
3. Find q1, q2 (zeros) that least increase other shifts
4. Accept if max_k (corr[k] + Δcorr[k]) < P

This is O(N) per pair (to compute Δcorr) and O(N²) for trying all pairs. That's 380² = 144,400 pairs, each O(759). Total: ~109M per 2-opt attempt. Too slow.

Better heuristic: just try the "greedy" 2-swap:
1. Do the best single flip (p, q)
2. From the new state, do the best single flip (p', q')
3. If the combined state is better than the original, accept both
4. Otherwise, revert

This is just 2 sweeps' worth of computation, which is fast.
