One more thought: the `np.correlate` with `mode='full'` gives 2n-1 values. For the C₅ computation, we take the max of all 2n-1 values times dx. This includes negative shifts. The problem statement says the integral is ∫h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx, which for the full correlation includes all shifts.

But wait - should we only consider shifts where the integral makes sense? In the continuous problem on [0,2], the integral ∫h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx is over the domain where both h(x) and h(x+k) are defined. If h is defined on [0,2] and is 0 outside, then for k>0, the integral is over [0, 2-k], and for k<0, it's over [-k, 2].

In the discrete `np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode='full')`:
- Index 0 corresponds to shift -(n-1): sum h[i-(n-1)]*(1-h[i]) for valid i
- Index n-1 corresponds to shift 0: sum h[i]*(1-h[i])
- Index 2(n-1) corresponds to shift n-1

The "valid" shifts that correspond to the integral over [0,2] would be all shifts from -(n-1) to n-1, which is exactly what mode='full' gives. So we're correct.
