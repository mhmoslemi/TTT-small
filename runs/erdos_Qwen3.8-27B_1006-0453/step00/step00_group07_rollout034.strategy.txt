<strategy>
Approach and Rationale
The Erdős minimum overlap problem involves finding a step function h:[0,2]→[0,1] with integral 1 that minimizes the maximum overlap with its shifts. This non-convex, high-dimensional search space requires escaping local minima. My approach uses guided basin hopping with repair operations, where mutations drive solutions toward improving lags while dynamic repairs maintain constraints. This differs from local searches (Simulated Annealing/PSO) by combining targeted mutations with efficient repairs, ensuring integral constraints without penalty terms. The method leverages parent guidance for initialization and focuses on lag-based objectives to escape suboptimal regions faster.
</strategy>

<mathematical-specification>
Representation: A vector h[0..n_points-1] of real numbers [0,1] with ∑(h_i * dx) = 1 (dx=2/n_points).
Objective: C5(h) = max_k (dx * correlation_exp(h,k)) where correlation_exp(h,k) = sum_i h[i] * (1 - h[i+k mod n_points]) (discrete version approximating the continuous integral).
Constraints: 0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1, ∑(h_i * dx) = 1. The floating-point repair handles 0≤h[i]≤1, and periodic alignment ensures coverage (though in our discrete case we use circular cross-correlation simulation via: correlation_exp(h,k)[m] = cross_correlation(h, 1-h)[m] for m=[0,2*n_points)?] but we use open-ended computation with mode='full' and adjust ranges later)
Derivative-like guidance: Subgradient ±(1-h[i+k])±h[i] with clipping regions, used heuristically for mutation directions. Not fully validated as gradient but empirically effective.
</mathematical-specification>
