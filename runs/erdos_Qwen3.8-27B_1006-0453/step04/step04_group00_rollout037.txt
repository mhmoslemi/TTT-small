This is complex to differentiate. Let me just use SLSQP without gradient (it will use finite differences). Alternative: Compute the gradient analytically.

f(h) = max_k c_k * dx where c_k = sum_i h[i] * (1 - h[i + k - (n-1)])

The subgradient of max is: for each k achieving the max, ∂c_k/∂h[i] * dx.

For the smoothed version (log-sum-exp):
g(h) = (1/T) * log(sum_k exp(T * c_k)) * dx

where w_k = exp(T*c_k) / sum_m exp(T*c_m)

And ∂c_k/∂h[i]:
c_k = sum_j h[j] * (1 - h[j + lag_k]) where lag_k = k - (n-1)

∂c_k/∂h[i] = (1 - h[i + lag_k]) [if i+lag_k in range] - h[i - lag_k] [if i-lag_k in range]

Computing A is O(n²) and the matrix-vector product is O(n²). Total O(n²) per gradient evaluation, vs O(n³) for finite differences.
