Approach and Rationale
The Erdős minimum overlap problem requires finding a step function h on [0,2] with integral 1 that minimizes the maximum overlap with its shifts. Current methods relying on gradient descent or coordinate descent often get stuck in local optima. This approach combines global exploration via simulated annealing with noise-based perturbations and local refinement using a smart projection to maintain the integral constraint. The key innovation is a two-phase optimization: 
1. Simulated annealing explores the search space while favoring solutions with low overlap, using a smooth temperature-based acceptance criterion to escape local minima
2. A gradient-free coordinate-wise update then refines the solution while enforcing feasibility through an efficient projection method
The need arose from several limitations: immature use of symbolic optimization, separation of exploration from exploitation, and inefficient handling of the equality constraint (integral h=1). This architecture directly addresses these by embedding constraint satisfaction into the search and using the smooth objective's gradient information more effectively than previous methods.

Mathematical Specification
Variables:
h = [h0, h1, ..., h_{n-1}] ∈ [0,1]^n
Constraint: sum(h) = n/2 (since integral h = 1 requires sum h_i * dx = 1 with dx=2/n)

Objective:
Minimize C5 = max_k [dx * correlation(h, 1-h) at lag k]

We use a smooth approximation for binary-like solutions:
Smooth_C5 = (2/n) * [exp(T*(corr_max - θ)) / sum_{k} exp(T*(corr_k - θ))]

Where θ is a threshold and T is a temperature hyperparameter. This ensures C5 remains a well-defined quantity while being smooth for gradient-based methods.
