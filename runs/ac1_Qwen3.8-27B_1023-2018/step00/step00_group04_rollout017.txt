The optimal balance is when all peak positions are equalized.

Actually, you know what, let me just try a different approach entirely. Let me use scipy.optimize.minimize with a suitable method.

The problem: minimize max_k (f*f)(k) / (sum f)^2 subject to f >= 0.

Since it's scale-invariant, fix sum(f) = 1:
minimize max_k (f*f)(k) subject to sum(f) = 1, f >= 0.

I can rewrite this as:
minimize t
subject to (f*f)(k) <= t for all k
sum(f) = 1
f >= 0

But the constraints (f*f)(k) <= t are quadratic. However, since f >= 0 and (f*f)(k) = sum_j f[j]*f[k-j], each constraint is a product of two nonnegative linear forms. This is not a standard QP.

Alternative: use the "epigraph" formulation with the soft-max:
minimize (1/T) log sum_{k=0}^{2n-2} exp(T * (f*f)(k))
subject to sum(f) = 1, f >= 0

For large T, this approximates the max. And the objective is smooth (though potentially ill-conditioned for large T).

I can use scipy.optimize.minimize with method='SLSQP' or 'trust-constr' and analytical gradients.
