# Approach and Rationale

The Central idea is to optimize the autocorrelation upper bound by **evolving impulse sequences** through locally improving **worm-based perturbations**. The key insight is that for autocorrelation minimization, we should concentrate sequence energy away from its own shifts:

1. The objective is minimized when signals have minimal aligned energy across time shifts
2. Impulses (or step functions) can be arranged to limit cross-convolution peaks
3. Worm perturbations allow careful exploration of locally varying patterns

- Structured abnormalities rather than noise
- Adaptable worm shapes and extinction regions
- Exploration correlated with local objective gradients

**Bottleneck addressed:** Direct numerical minimization is infeasible here. This approach combines structured moves with randomization at the core.

**Scientific guidance:**
- The method uses first-order worm perturbations to bias exploration towards anticorrelation
- Local gradients are implicitly followed by the "worm wrapping" accept/reject logic
- The fallback is a uniform sequence, ensuring at least 0 convolutions exist

# Mathematical Specification

Variable Definitions:
- $a = (a_0, a₁, ..., a_{n-1}) ∈ ℝ^n$ sequence coefficients
- $M = \max_k (a * a)_k$ autocorrelation maximum
- $p = \|a\|^2_1$ sequence norm
- Objective: $f(a) = 2n M / p$

Representations:
- `worm` = contiguous segment of sequence (with shape label, base, width, deviation)
- `collar` = stretch of extinction surrounding worm to avoid artificial peaks

Constraints:
- $0 \leq a_i \leq 1000$ for all $i$
- $\sum_i a_i \geq 0.01$

$M \geq \max_{i,j} (a_i a_j \delta_{k_{ij}} (k))$

### Formulation Selection:
- Sparse impulse-based sequences are treated as experimental but high-yield
- Gradient calculations use numerical differencing
- The main program respects the objective as:

$f(z) = 2n \max_k (\text{convolve}(a,a)) / (\sum a)**2$
