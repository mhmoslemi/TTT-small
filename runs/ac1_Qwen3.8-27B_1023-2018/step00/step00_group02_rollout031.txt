Let me think about this problem from a different angle. The objective is:
$f(x) = \frac{2n \max_k \sum_i x_i x_{k-i}}{(\sum x_i)^2}$

By homogeneity: if we scale $x \to cx$, then $f(cx) = f(x)$. So we can fix $\sum x_i = 1$ (or any constant) and minimize $\max_k (x*x)[k]$.

With $\sum x_i = 1$: $f(x) = 2n \max_k (x*x)[k]$.

And $(x*x)[k] \geq 0$ for non-negative $x$.

The constraint $\sum_k (x*x)[k] = (\sum x_i)^2 = 1$ means the "area under the convolution" is fixed.

We want to minimize the peak height given a fixed area and non-negativity. The most "flat" distribution over $2n-1$ bins would give peak $1/(2n-1)$, but the shape is constrained by the fact that it's a self-convolution of a non-negative sequence.

For the all-ones (normalized) sequence: $x_i = 1/n$, $(x*x)[k]$ is triangular with peak $1/n$. $f = 2n/n = 2$.

Can we do better? The [1,1,1,0.5] example for n=4 suggests yes.
