**Now let me think about the overall algorithm more carefully:**

The issue with pure gradient descent on $\|c\|_2^2$ is that it minimizes the total "energy" of the autocorrelation, not the peak. A sequence could have low $\|c\|_2^2$ but still have a high peak (if the energy is spread thin but with one spike).

However, in practice, minimizing $\|c\|_2^2$ tends to flatten the autocorrelation, which also reduces the peak. So it should be a good proxy.

After the main optimization phase, I can add a "peak reduction" phase that specifically targets the maximum lag.

**Algorithm:**

Phase 1 (main optimization): Gradient descent on $\|c\|_2^2$
- Step size: adaptive (backtracking)
- Constraint: $a \geq 0$, $\|a\|_1 = S$
- This should run for ~80% of the budget

Phase 2 (peak reduction): Subgradient method on $\max c_k$
- Use the gradient of the single max (or top few) lags
- Run for ~20% of the budget

Phase 3 (polishing): Small random perturbations + keep best
- For remaining time
