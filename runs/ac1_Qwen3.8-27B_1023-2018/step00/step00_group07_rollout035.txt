Let me just use: $c[n-1] = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} x_i x_{n-1-i}$.

If I define $y_i = x_i x_{n-1-i}$, then $c[n-1] = \sum y_i$. And $\sum x_i = S$ but there's no direct constraint on $\sum y_i$ in terms of $S$ (other than $y_i \le \max(x_i, x_{n-1-i})^2$).

Actually, by AM-GM: $x_i x_{n-1-i} \le (x_i + x_{n-1-i})^2/4$.

And $\sum_i (x_i + x_{n-1-i}) = 2S$.

So $\sum x_i x_{n-1-i} \le \sum (x_i + x_{n-1-i})^2/4 \le (1/4) \cdot n \cdot (\text{max of }(x_i+x_{n-1-i}))^2$.

This doesn't give a clean lower bound. The point is: $c[n-1]$ can be as small as 0 (if $x$ and $x_{rev}$ have disjoint support), but then other $c[k]$ terms will be large.

I want all of these to be small. Let's try to equalize them.

With $b = c$ (palindromic), we're back to $f \ge 2$.
