Actually, let me step back and think about this more broadly. For large n (4498), I need a scalable approach.

Key insight: The score is scale-invariant, so I can fix sum(a) = 1 and minimize max(c).

The problem is: minimize max_k {sum_j a_j * a_{k-j}} subject to sum_j a_j = 1, a_j >= 0.

This is equivalent to: find a probability distribution a on {0,...,n-1} that minimizes the maximum value of its self-convolution.

This is a well-studied problem in probability and Fourier analysis! The self-convolution a*a is the distribution of the sum of two independent random variables with distribution a. We want to minimize the point mass (maximum) of this sum distribution.

The minimax problem: minimize max_k P(X+Y = k) where X,Y are iid with distribution a on {0,...,n-1}.

By the central limit theorem, if a is spread out, the sum distribution is approximately normal, and the maximum is about 1/sqrt(2*pi*var) where var is the variance of 2X.

For a uniform distribution on {0,...,n-1}, the sum distribution is triangular with maximum n (at the center). The "variance" approach gives max ≈ n/(2n-1) * 2n-1 = n... well, the actual max is n and it occurs at k=n-1.

Actually, the sum of two uniforms on {0,...,n-1} is triangular: P(X+Y=k) = (k+1)/n^2 for 0<=k<=n-1 and (2n-1-k)/n^2 for n<=k<=2n-2. Maximum is n/n^2 = 1/n at k=n-1.

So max(c) = 1/n when sum(a) = 1. Score = 2n * (1/n) / 1 = 2. This matches the constant sequence giving score 2.

To do better, we need a distribution whose sum is flatter than triangular. But the support of the sum is {0,...,2n-2} (length 2n-1), so the minimum possible max is at least 1/(2n-1). The question is how close to this we can get.

A distribution that makes the sum as uniform as possible on {0,...,2n-2} would be ideal. This is related to the concept of "perfect difference sets" and "fast counter" problems in additive combinatorics.

For the continuous analog, if we think of a as a density on [0,1], then a*a is the convolution, and we want to minimize its supremum. The uniform density gives a triangular convolution with max 2. A density that gives a flat convolution would be ideal.

Actually, in the continuous case, if f is a density on [0,1] with f*f = 1/2 everywhere on [0,2], then f would need to be... well, by Fourier transform, F(f*f) = F(f)^2, and F(1/2 on [0,2]) = (1/2)*2*sinc(f) = sinc(f) = sin(pi*f)/(pi*f). So F(f) = sqrt(sinc(f)), which is complex-valued. So no real non-negative density achieves this exactly.
