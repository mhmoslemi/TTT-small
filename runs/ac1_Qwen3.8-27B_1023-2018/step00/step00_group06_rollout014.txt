So the problem is more interesting than I initially thought. Let me think about what kind of sequences minimize this.

The goal is to make convolve(a,a) as flat as possible. The sum of all convolution values is S². There are 2n-1 values. So the minimum possible max is S²/(2n-1) (if all values were equal).

score_min_possible = 2n * S²/(2n-1) / S² = 2n/(2n-1) → 1 as n→∞.

For n=5: 10/9 ≈ 1.11
For n=5582: 11164/11163 ≈ 1.00009

So the theoretical minimum is close to 1, and the target of 1.5 is well within reach.

Now, what sequences achieve flat autocorrelation? This is related to the theory of "Golay sequences" or "complementary sequences" in signal processing.

A simpler approach: we want convolve(a,a) ≈ constant. In the frequency domain, this means |A(ω)|² ≈ constant * (something). Actually, the DFT of a constant sequence is a delta at zero frequency. The convolution in time domain is pointwise multiplication in frequency domain.

If convolve(a,a) = C (constant at all lags), then in frequency: |A(ω)|² = C * (2n-1) * δ(ω) ??? No, that's not right either.

The DFT of the linear convolution is the product of DFTs (with appropriate padding). If we zero-pad a to length 2n-1, then:
DFT(convolve(a,a)) = DFT(a_padded) * DFT(a_padded) = |DFT(a_padded)|²

If convolve(a,a) is constant = C, then DFT of that is C*(2n-1)*δ(0) (all energy at DC).
So |A(ω)|² = C*(2n-1)*δ(ω)

This means A(ω) is zero for all ω ≠ 0. But A(0) = sum a = S.
So we need a sequence whose DFT is zero at all non-DC frequencies. That means all a_i are equal! Which gives the uniform sequence.

But wait, we can't make the convolution EXACTLY constant (except for uniform, which has a triangular convolution, not constant). The uniform sequence gives a TRIANGULAR convolution, not a flat one!

convolve(uniform, uniform) = [c², 2c², ..., nc², ..., 2c², c²] — this is triangular, not flat.

So the uniform sequence does NOT have flat autocorrelation. It has a peaked (triangular) autocorrelation. The max is at the center.

To get a flatter autocorrelation, we need a sequence that "spreads out" the convolution energy more evenly.
