For the gradient method:

- `grad[i] = 2 * a[k*-i]` (if `0 <= k*-i < n`, else 0)
- This is the gradient of `c[k*]`
- The subgradient of the objective (with `sum(a)=1`) is `2n * grad - 2n*c[k*] * 2`... 

Wait, let me redo. `f(a) = 2n * max_k c[k]` (with `sum(a) = 1`).

At the optimum, `f(a) = 2n * c[k*]`. The subgradient w.r.t. `a` (before projection) is `2n * (grad c[k*])` where `(grad c[k*])[i] = 2*a[k*-i]` (if valid, else 0).

So `subgrad[i] = 4n * a[k*-i]` (if `0 <= k*-i < n`, else 0).

For the projected subgradient method on the simplex:
1. Compute `v = a - eta * subgrad`.
2. `a_new = v / max(0, v).sum()` (project onto simplex).

The step size `eta` can be `1 / (4n * max(a))` or adaptive.

Actually, a simpler and more effective approach: **exponential tilt / natural gradient**.

`a_new[i] = a[i] * exp(-eta * 2 * a[k*-i])` (if valid), normalized.

This is the multiplicative update. It's `O(n)` per step.
