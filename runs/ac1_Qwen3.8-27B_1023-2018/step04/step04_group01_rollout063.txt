Actually, let me reconsider the LP. The constraint is:
$(f*g)_k = \sum_{j: 0 \leq k-j < n} g_j \cdot f_{k-j} \leq M$

This is a linear constraint in $g$ for fixed $f$ and $M$. The LP is:
max $\sum g_j$ s.t. $A g \leq M \cdot \mathbf{1}$, $g \geq 0$

Where $A$ has shape $(2n-1) \times n$ and $A_{k,j} = f_{k-j}$ if $0 \leq k-j < n$ else 0.

The LP is always feasible (e.g., $g = 0$). The optimum has $g \neq 0$ if $M$ is positive (which it is as long as $f$ has nonzero entries).

After finding $g$, we form $f_{new} = (1-t)f + t \cdot g/\sum g$ (both normalized to sum 1).

The LP guarantees $(f*g)_k \leq M$ for all $k$. For the new autocorrelation:
$(f_{new}*f_{new})_k = (1-t)^2(f*f)_k + 2t(1-t)(f*g)_k + t^2(g_{norm}*g_{norm})_k$

where $g_{norm} = g/\sum g$.

We know the first two terms are $\leq (1-t)^2 M + 2t(1-t)M = (1-t)M$ each... wait, no:
$(1-t)^2(f*f)_k \leq (1-t)^2 M$
$2t(1-t)(f*g)_k \leq 2t(1-t)M$

Sum: $(1-t)^2M + 2t(1-t)M = (1-t)M$

Plus $t^2(g_{norm}*g_{norm})_k$.

So $(f_{new}*f_{new})_k \leq (1-t)M + t^2 \max(g_{norm}*g_{norm})$.

For the score (with sum 1): $2n[(1-t)M + t^2 M_g]$ where $M_g = \max(g_{norm}*g_{norm})$.

For this to be less than $2nM$: $(1-t)M + t^2 M_g < M$, i.e., $t^2 M_g < tM$, i.e., $t M_g < M$.

So $t < M / M_g$.

In practice, $M_g$ is related to the "concentration" of $g_{norm}$. If $g_{norm}$ is spread out, $M_g$ is small and we can take larger steps.

    for i in order:

        st = i
