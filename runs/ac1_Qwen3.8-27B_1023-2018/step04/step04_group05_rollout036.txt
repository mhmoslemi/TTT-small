For `k = n-1` (the center): `b_sequence[n-1] = Σ_{j=0}^{n-1} a[j] * a[n-1-j]`.

For `k = 0`: `b_sequence[0] = a[0]^2`.

For `k = n-1`: this is the "full overlap" term.

Actually, the maximum of the autocorrelation is NOT at `k=0` (which is just `a[0]^2`). It's typically at `k = n-1` (the center), where the overlap is maximum.

So the "energy" term `Σ a_j^2` does NOT appear directly in the full convolution. Instead, the full convolution at `k = n-1` is `Σ_j a[j] a[n-1-j]`, which is at most `Σ a_j^2` (by Cauchy-Schwarz) but could be smaller.

In fact, by Cauchy-Schwarz: `Σ_j a[j] a[n-1-j] ≤ sqrt(Σ a_j^2 * Σ a_{n-1-j}^2) = sqrt(Σ a_j^2)`... no wait, that's not right either.

`Σ_j a[j] a[n-1-j] ≤ sqrt(Σ a[j]^2 * Σ a[n-1-j]^2) = sqrt(Σ a[j]^2)^2 = Σ a[j]^2`.

So `b_sequence[n-1] ≤ Σ a[j]^2`, and by AM-GM, `Σ a[j]^2 ≥ s^2/n`.

But `b_sequence[n-1]` could be much less than `Σ a[j]^2` if the sequence is "antisymmetric" (but we require nonnegative, so this is limited).

For a nonnegative sequence, `b_sequence[n-1] = Σ_j a[j] a[n-1-j]`. If the sequence is symmetric (`a[j] = a[n-1-j]`), then `b_sequence[n-1] = Σ_j a[j]^2 ≥ s^2/n`.

If the sequence is NOT symmetric, `b_sequence[n-1]` can be less than `s^2/n`.

So the lower bound of 2 only applies to symmetric sequences. For asymmetric sequences, the score can be less than 2.

This is a key insight! The optimal sequence should be asymmetric to reduce the center autocorrelation peak.

The LP method should naturally find this, because it's minimizing the maximum of the full convolution, and the center peak is one of the terms to minimize.

OK so now I understand the problem better. Let me think about what the optimal structure looks like.

For a nonnegative sequence of length `n`, we want to minimize `max_k (a*a)_k` subject to `Σa = 1`.

The "flat" sequence `a_j = 1/n` gives `(a*a)_k = (n-|k|)/n^2` for `|k| < n`, so the max is `1/n` (at `k=0` in the centered sense, or `k=n-1` in the full convolution sense). Score = `2n * 1/n / 1 = 2`.

To do better, we need to make the sequence concentrated in certain regions so that the "overlap" at the center is small.

For example, if the sequence is concentrated at one end: `a_0 ≈ 1`, rest ≈ 0. Then `(a*a)_0 ≈ 1`, and the score is `2n * 1 / 1 = 2n`. Bad.

If the sequence has two peaks: `a_0 ≈ 1/2`, `a_{n-1} ≈ 1/2`, rest ≈ 0. Then the max autocorrelation is at `k=0` or `k=n-1`, and `(a*a)_{n-1} ≈ 1/4`. The score is `2n * 1/4 / 1 = n/2`. Also bad.

The optimal is somewhere in between: the sequence should be "spread out" enough to avoid large peaks, but "structured" enough to reduce the center autocorrelation.

I think the LP method is the right approach. Let me focus on making it work well.

One more thought: since the score is scale-invariant, I should normalize the sequence to have a fixed sum (e.g., `Σa = 1`) and minimize `2n * max_k (a*a)_k`. This simplifies the computations.
