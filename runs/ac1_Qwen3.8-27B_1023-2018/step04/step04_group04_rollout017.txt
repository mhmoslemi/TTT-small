Wait, I realize there's an issue with the subgradient escape. The subgradient of $\phi = 2nM/S^2$ should guide us to reduce $M$ while keeping $S$ roughly constant. But the subgradient I computed includes the $S$-dependence, which can point in a direction that changes $S$. Since the problem is scale-invariant, I should project the subgradient to be orthogonal to $\mathbf{1}$ (the direction of scaling).

Actually, since I'm normalizing after each step (dividing by sum), the $S$-dependence is handled automatically. The subgradient should be projected to be sum-zero:

$sg_{proj} = sg - \text{mean}(sg) \cdot \mathbf{1}$

This is what I do with `sg_full -= sg_full.mean()`. Good.

But actually, for the subgradient of $\max(a*a)$ (not the full $\phi$), the subgradient is just the average of $\nabla_k (a*a)$ over active $k$. Since I'm normalizing $a$ after each step, minimizing $\max(a*a)$ on the simplex $\{a \geq 0, \sum a = 1\}$ is equivalent to minimizing the original objective.

So I can just use the subgradient of $\max(a*a)$ and project it:

$sg = \frac{1}{|K|} \sum_{k \in K} \nabla (a*a)_k$
$sg_{proj} = sg - \text{mean}(sg)$

And step in the $-sg_{proj}$ direction.

This is simpler and should work well. Let me simplify.
