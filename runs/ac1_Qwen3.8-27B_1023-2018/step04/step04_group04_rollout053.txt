def _subgrad_step(f, n):

    active = np.flatnonzero(b >= M - eps)
    
    grad_max = np.zeros(n)
    for k in active:
        i_lo = max(0, k - n + 1)
        i_hi = min(k, n - 1)
        if i_lo <= i_hi:
            idx = k - np.arange(i_lo, i_hi + 1)
            grad_max[i_lo:i_hi + 1] += 2.0 * f[idx]
    grad_max /= len(active)

In the MPCA context, the solution quality is determined by:
1. The number of non-zero components (support size)
2. The "flatness" of the autocorrelation

A key observation: at the LP fixed point, the active constraints (where (f*g)(x) = M) determine the "shape" of the solution. The more active constraints, the more "constrained" the solution is, and the flatter the autocorrelation.

For n=414, there are 827 autocorrelation lags. If all 827 are active at the fixed point, the autocorrelation is perfectly flat (all equal to M), giving the optimal score. In practice, only a subset of lags are active.

The number of active lags at convergence is approximately n (the number of variables), since the LP solution is at a vertex with at most n non-zero variables, and the number of active constraints is at most n.

So at convergence, about n = 414 out of 827 lags are active (at the peak M), and the other 413 lags are below M. The "flatness" determines the score.

To improve the score, we need to either:
- Make more lags active (tighter to M)
- Reduce the peak M relative to the mean
