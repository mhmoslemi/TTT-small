**Approach: Direct minimization via subgradient**

The objective $f(u) = \max_k (u*u)_k$ subject to $u \geq 0, \sum u = 1$ is a convex optimization... wait, is it?

$(u*u)_k = \sum_j u_j u_{k-j}$ is a quadratic form in $u$. The Hessian of $(u*u)_k$ with respect to $u$ is the matrix $H_k$ where $(H_k)_{ij} = \delta_{i+j,k} + \delta_{j+i,k}$ (approximately). This is not PSD in general (e.g., for $k=0$, $H_0$ has 1s on the diagonal and 0s elsewhere, which is PSD. But for other $k$, the off-diagonal structure can make it indefinite).

Actually, $(u*u)_k = \sum_j u_j u_{k-j}$. Let me write this as $\frac{1}{2} u^T Q_k u$ where $(Q_k)_{ij} = \delta_{i+j,k}$ (symmetrized). The eigenvalues of $Q_k$ depend on $k$ and $n$. For $k$ near the middle, $Q_k$ has a checkerboard-like pattern and can have negative eigenvalues.

So the objective is not convex, and the LP relaxation is the right approach.

**Approach: Alternating projections**

1. Project onto $\{u: \sum u = 1, u \geq 0\}$ (simplex)
2. Project onto $\{u: \max_k (u*u)_k \leq M\}$ (harder)

The second projection is non-convex, so this won't work directly.

**Approach: Use the LP dual as a "potential"**
