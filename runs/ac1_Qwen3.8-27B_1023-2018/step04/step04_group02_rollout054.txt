This is like: given a probability distribution p on {0, ..., n-1}, minimize the peak of the "convolution" (which is NOT the standard convolution of the probability mass function - it's the sum of products of elements at positions that sum to k).

Wait, that's actually the distribution of the sum of two independent random variables X, Y where P(X=i) = P(Y=j) = p_i. The probability that X+Y = k is sum_i p_i * p_{k-i}. So we're looking at the peak of the sum distribution!

This is a well-studied problem in additive combinatorics. The peak of the convolution of a probability distribution with itself.

For a uniform distribution on {0,...,n-1}, the sum distribution is triangular with peak at n-1, value 1/n. Score = 2n/n = 2.

For a "spread" distribution, the sum distribution is flatter but the peak might still be significant.
