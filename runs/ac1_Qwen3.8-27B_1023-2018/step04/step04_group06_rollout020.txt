1. Compute $M = \max(a*a)$

The LP in step 2 gives the "best linear response." If $g/a$ is close to 1 (element-wise), we're at a fixed point. The blend in step 3 moves $a$ toward $g$.

The issue is that the LP is a linear relaxation. The actual constraint we want is $(a*a)_k \leq M$, which is quadratic. The LP uses $(a*g)_k \leq M$, which is linear in $g$. These are different!

At the optimum, we need $(a*a)_k \leq M$ for all $k$. The LP condition $(a*g)_k \leq M$ is a necessary condition for the blend to not increase $M$ (to first order), but it's not sufficient.

This is why the line search is important: even if the LP says $g$ is a good direction, the actual blend might increase $M$ at some $t$. The line search finds the best $t$ where $M$ actually decreases.
