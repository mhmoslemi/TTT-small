Approach and rationale
The central idea is to optimize a parameterized family of sequences using Bayesian optimization with Gaussian Process (GP) surrogate models. This differs fundamentally from the previous LP-based approach by:

1. Directly optimizing the sequence using a parameterized basis representation (instead of projecting arbitrary sequences)
2. Leveraging the non-convex structure of the problem through Bayesian optimization
3. Using domain-specific constraints and objectives without needing to solve multiple LP phases

This method is more efficient for expensive black-box optimization like this non-convex AC1 minimization problem. Unlike previous strategies that used sequences as fixed-length vectors, here we represent sequences with fewer parameters, allowing faster exploration while maintaining flexibility. The gradient information from the evaluators optimization method isn't needed because we directly learn the objective function.

Mathematical specification
1. Representation: 
   - Sequence of length n=32 (default, but lengths can vary)
   - Defined by 4 coefficients (w0, w1, w2, w3) for a weighted sum of basis functions
   - Basis functions: polynomial (degree 0-5), harmonic (frequencies 0-4), gaussian (widths 0.1-0.4)

2. Variables: 
   - 4 real parameters w for each basis element
   - Coefficient bounds: [0, 1000]
   - Element-wise clipping enforced

3. Exact objective:
   - Minimize score(a) = 2*n*max(convolve(a,a))/sum(a)**2
   - Constraints: sum(a)>=0.01 after clipping

4. Basis functions:
   - polynomials: (x-a)^k * e^{-k|3a-x|} to ensure positiveness near maxima
   - harmonics: sin(2πfreq*x)^2 + 0.1*cos(4πfreq*x) with clipping
   - gaussians: e^{-0.5( (x-center)/σ )^2} centered at points 0, n/5, n/2, 3n/5, n

   Each basis shape function β_k(x) is defined, and a_j = ∑_k w_k β_k(j/n)

5. Sampling strategy:
   - Sample coefficients uniformly in [min_co,max_co] with:
        min_co = [0.1,0.1,0.1,0.1]
        max_co = [3.0,3.0,3.0,3.0]
   - Use quasi-Monte Carlo for sampling new points

6. Acquisition function:
   - Expected improvement (EI)
   - Incorporates dimension-adaptive weighting for efficient exploration
