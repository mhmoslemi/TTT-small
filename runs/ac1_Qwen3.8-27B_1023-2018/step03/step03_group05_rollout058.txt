Actually, let me reconsider the problem structure. The score is:
score = 2 * n * max_k (a*a)[k] / (sum a)^2

This is scale-invariant. Let me normalize sum(a) = 1 and minimize 2n * max_k (a*a)[k].

For n=512, we want to minimize 1024 * max_k (a*a)[k] subject to a >= 0, sum(a) = 1.

The key observation: max_k (a*a)[k] is the peak of the auto-convolution. For a "nice" shape, this peak is at k = n-1 (center) and equals sum_i a[i]*a[n-1-i].

If the sequence is symmetric (a[i] = a[n-1-i]), then (a*a)[n-1] = sum_i a[i]^2, and by Cauchy-Schwarz, this >= 1/n. So score >= 2n*(1/n) = 2.

For non-symmetric sequences, (a*a)[n-1] = sum_i a[i]*a[n-1-i] can be less than sum_i a[i]^2. But the peak might shift to other k values.

The optimal shape is one where the auto-convolution is as flat as possible (minimizing the peak). This is analogous to designing a sequence with minimum "sidelobes" in its auto-correlation, but for convolution instead of correlation.

For the convolution case (as opposed to correlation), the "natural" shape that gives a flat convolution is one where the mass is distributed in a way that the overlap at every lag is approximately uniform.
