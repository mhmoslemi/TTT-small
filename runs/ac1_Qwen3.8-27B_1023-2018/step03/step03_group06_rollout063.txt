    if best_t < 0.002:
        return None, 0.0
    h = (1.0 - best_t) * f + best_t * g
    return _normalize(h), best_t

Let me also add a key improvement: instead of just one LP direction per iteration, I can do a "double LP":
1. Solve LP to get g (best response to f)
2. Solve LP to get h (best response to g)
3. Blend f with both g and h: minimize max over t1, t2 in a small neighborhood

This is more expensive but potentially more powerful. However, for n=256, the single LP should be sufficient. Let me keep it simple.
