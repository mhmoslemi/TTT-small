For the KKT with sum constraint: we need Σ_k λ_k * 2 * flip_k(a) = μ * 1 (constant vector), where flip_k(a)[m] = a[k-m] (valid) or 0.

This means the weighted sum of shifted-reversed a's must be constant. This is a strong condition.

For a symmetric sequence (a[m] = a[n-1-m]), the convolution is also symmetric around n-1, and the maximum is at k=n-1. Then ∇g = 2*a reversed = 2*a (since symmetric). KKT requires 2a = μ*1, i.e., a is constant. But that gives score 2, which is worse.

For asymmetric sequences, the situation is more complex. The LP approach should handle this.
