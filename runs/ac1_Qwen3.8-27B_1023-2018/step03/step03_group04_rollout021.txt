Wait, (a*a)_k = sum_{i: 0<=i<n, 0<=k-i<n} a_i * a_{k-i}

H[i,j] = (d^2 q_k / da_i da_j)

For q_k = sum_i a_i a_{k-i}:
d/d a_i: a_{k-i} + a_i * (if i appears with another index)
Actually, q_k = sum_{i in valid} a_i * a_{k-i}

If i != k-i (i.e., 2i != k): the term a_i * a_{k-i} contributes to the Hessian: H[i, k-i] += 1 and H[k-i, i] += 1.
If i = k-i (i.e., 2i = k): the term a_i^2 contributes: H[i, i] += 2.

So the Hessian H is symmetric with H[i,j] = 1 if j = k-i and 2i != k, H[i,i] = 2 if 2i = k, and 0 otherwise.

Is this PSD? The Hessian has non-zero entries only on the "anti-diagonal" (where i+j = k) plus possibly one diagonal entry. This is a permutation matrix (or 2x a permutation with one entry doubled). A permutation matrix is not PSD in general (it has eigenvalues that are roots of unity).

So q_k is NOT convex in general! The problem is NOT convex. This explains why the optimization is hard.

                      bounds=[(0, None)] * n,
