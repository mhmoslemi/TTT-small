But I need to be careful: the blend $(1-t)f + tg$ has sum 1 (since both $f$ and $g$ are normalized). So the score is indeed $2n \cdot \text{peak}$.

Let me also add a "subgradient polish" step:

After the LP converges, I'm at a fixed point. The subgradient of $M(a) = \max_k q_k(a)$ at the current point $a$ is a convex combination of the active gradients. If I take a small step in the negative subgradient direction and project onto the simplex, I might reduce $M$.

But this is tricky because the step size needs to be small (diminishing), and convergence is slow. Let me instead use a "proximal" approach:
- Solve $\min_b M(b) + \frac{\lambda}{2}\|b - a\|^2$ for a small step
- This is equivalent to: find $b$ that minimizes $\max_k q_k(b) + \frac{\lambda}{2}\|b-a\|^2$
- For small $\lambda$, this is a smoothed version of the original problem
