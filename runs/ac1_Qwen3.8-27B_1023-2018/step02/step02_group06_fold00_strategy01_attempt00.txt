# Approach and Rationale  
The central idea is to exploit the relationship between autocorrelation and optimization by modeling sequences with sinusoidal components, which have proven effective in similar minimization problems. This approach leverages Fourier analysis properties to represent sequences compactly but flips the optimization flow: instead of using sinusoidal bases to analyze sequences, we use sequences to estimate critical frequencies. This bidirectional approach allows us to avoid the scaling issue (the objective is invariant to positive scaling) and instead focus on adaptive frequency placement. By mixing two sinusoidal components and varying their frequencies and phases, we can search for configurations that minimize the maximum autocorrelation. The random initialization from uniform distributions ensures diverse exploration, while the optimization refines promising candidates. This method addresses local optima by introducing direction-specific random mutations and period checks.  

Limitations include reliance on parameters and the compact form's potential failure to capture complex autocorrelation patterns. However, the randomized exploration and adaptive optimization offer sufficient flexibility while being computationally feasible within the budget.  

# Mathematical Specification  
The sequence representation is:  
a[i] = A * sin(2π f1 * i + φ1) + B * sin(2π f2 * i + φ2)  
then exponentiated for positivity:  
a[i] = exp(a[i])  
clipped to [0, 1000], and scaled to "normalize" the sum. The optimization targets minimizing:  
score = 2 * n * max(convolution) / (sum(a))²  
The constraints are:  
- Non-negativity (via exponentiation)  
- Coefficient caps (via clipping)  
- Normalization of sum for score consistency  
The sinusoidal form provides a balance between computation cost and effective coverage of function space, with two independent sinusoids offering greater flexibility than weaker alternatives.  

# Implementation Procedure  
1. **Initialization**:  
   - Parameters: A ∈ [0.1, 15], B ∈ [0.1, 15], f1 ∈ [0.1, 0.8], f2 ∈ [0.1, 0.8], φ1 ∈ [0, 2π], φ2 ∈ [0, 2π]  
   - Represent sequence as: a[i] = exp(A*sin(f1*i+φ1) + B*sin(f2*i+φ2))  
   - Clip to [0, 1000] to enforce bounds  
   - Scale to ensure sum(a) ≈ sqrt(2*n) for direct score comparison  
2. **Optimization**:  
   - Use Bayesian optimization with acquisition function to iteratively select parameters  
   - Evaluate candidate via `evaluate_sequence` (computes convolution)  
   - Track best candidate and update state  
3. **Termination**:  
   - Maintain fallback candidate (parent sequence or random initialized)  
   - Return best candidate found within budget
