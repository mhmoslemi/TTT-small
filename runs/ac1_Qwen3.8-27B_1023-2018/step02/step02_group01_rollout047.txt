Wait, is max_k (a*a)_k convex in a? (a*a)_k = sum_j a_j * a_{k-j} is a quadratic form in a. Each (a*a)_k is not necessarily convex (it's a quadratic form, which can be indefinite). But the max of convex functions is convex. Are the individual (a*a)_k convex?

(a*a)_k = sum_j a_j * a_{k-j}. This is a bilinear/quadratic form. The Hessian of (a*a)_k with respect to a is 2*S_k where S_k is the "shift" matrix. The eigenvalues of S_k depend on k. For k=0, S_0 = I (all positive eigenvalues), so (a*a)_0 = sum a_j^2 is convex. For k > 0, S_k has both positive and negative eigenvalues in general, so (a*a)_k is NOT convex.

So the problem is NOT convex in general. The max of non-convex quadratics is non-convex. This means we can get stuck in local minima.

This explains why the LP approach (which is essentially a subgradient method for this non-convex problem) needs perturbation to escape local minima.
