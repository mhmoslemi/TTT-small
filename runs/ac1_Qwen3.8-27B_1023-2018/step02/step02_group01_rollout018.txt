The LP best-response iteration is essentially a multiplicative weights / mirror descent type algorithm on the space of probability vectors. The convergence rate depends on the "gap" between the current solution and the optimal.

Key insight: The LP finds the "best response" to the current a. The fixed point of the best-response map is where a is a Nash equilibrium of the zero-sum game where player 1 chooses a and player 2 chooses k (the lag). At equilibrium, max_k (a*a)[k] is minimized (by minimax theorem... but this isn't really a zero-sum game in the standard sense).

Actually, let me reconsider. The problem is:
min_a max_k (a*a)[k] / (sum a)^2

Given a, find g that maximizes sum(g) subject to (a*g)[k] <= M for all k.

This is different from finding a response in a game. It's more like: given the "landscape" a creates (through its convolution), find the shape g that fills in the low spots while respecting the height limit M.

The intuition is: if (a*a) has a peak at some k, then the constraint (a*g)[k] <= M limits g. The LP finds the g that maximizes total mass while respecting all constraints.

For the iteration to converge, we need the fixed point a* = g* (up to normalization) where the LP solution equals the current a.

Actually, I realize I should be more careful about the subgradient method. The issue is that the subgradient of max_k b_k is only the gradient of ONE of the b_k (the one at the argmax). When the argmax changes, the subgradient changes discontinuously. This can cause oscillation.

A better approach: use the "smooth max" approximation. Define:
F_τ(a) = (1/τ) * log(sum_k exp(τ * b_k))

As τ → ∞, F_τ → max_k b_k. The gradient of F_τ is:
∇F_τ = (1/τ) * sum_k exp(τ*b_k) * ∇b_k / sum_k exp(τ*b_k)
= sum_k w_k * ∇b_k (where w_k = softmax(τ*b_k))

This is a convex combination of the subgradients, weighted by the "importance" of each k. For large τ, it concentrates on the argmax. For moderate τ, it's smoother.
