The peak of the autocorrelation is related to the peak of the inverse DFT of |A(f)|^2. By the uncertainty principle, if A(f) is spread out in frequency, the autocorrelation is concentrated in time, and vice versa.

For the uniform sequence, A(f) has a sinc^2 shape, and the autocorrelation is a triangle. The peak of the triangle is 1/n.

To reduce the peak, we want A(f) to be more "peaked" in frequency (concentrated at f=0), which means a is more "spread out" in time. But a is already spread out (uniform). 

Alternatively, we want A(f) to be FLAT in frequency, which means a is more "peaked" in time. But a non-negative sequence that's peaked in time has a higher autocorrelation peak.

I think the key insight is: we can make the autocorrelation more "flat" by introducing oscillations in a. But a must be non-negative. So we can't oscillate freely.
