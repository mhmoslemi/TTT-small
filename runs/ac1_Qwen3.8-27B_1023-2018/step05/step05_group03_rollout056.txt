  Let me also think about a potentially important insight: the structure of the optimal sequence.
  
  For the convolution to be flat, we want a "wideband" sequence - one whose DFT has approximately constant magnitude. For non-negative sequences, the DFT is the Fourier transform of a non-negative function, which has specific properties (by the Fourier transform of a non-negative function being... well, it's just the integral of a non-negative function times a complex exponential).
  
  The key constraint is that a non-negative sequence has a DFT that is the integral of a non-negative function, which means the DFT at frequency 0 (the DC component) equals the sum, and the magnitude at other frequencies is bounded by the DC.
  
  For a flat autocorrelation (b[k] constant for all k), we need the DFT to have constant magnitude. This is the definition of a "perfect autocorrelation" sequence. For real non-negative sequences, this is very restrictive.
  
  The best we can do for non-negative sequences is probably a "nearly flat" autocorrelation, which is what the optimization is trying to achieve.
  
  One useful structure: a sequence that oscillates rapidly (alternating high and low). This puts energy at high frequencies in the DFT, which helps flatten the autocorrelation.
  
  Let me think about specific patterns:
  - Alternating: [H, L, H, L, ...] where H >> L. This gives a convolution that's high at even lags and low at odd lags.
