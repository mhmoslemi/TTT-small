Actually, let me think about this differently. The score is 2n*M/S². For a fixed S, minimizing M is the same as minimizing the score. The max M = max_k B[k]. 

The key insight from harmonic analysis: for a sequence with mean 1, the convolution at lag k is:
B[k] = (1/n) * sum_omega |A(omega)|² * e^{i*omega*k} ... (with appropriate normalization)

No wait, that's for the periodic convolution. For the linear convolution, the Fourier transform relationship is:
F(LinearConv(a,a)) = F(a) * F(a) (in Fourier domain)
where F(a) is the DFT of a (zero-padded to size 2n-1).

So B[k] = IDFT(|A|²)[k], where A = DFT(a, 2n-1).

To minimize max_k B[k], we want to minimize the peak of IDFT(|A|²). This is like designing a spectrum |A|² whose inverse DFT has low peak.

For a "flat" spectrum, the IDFT would be concentrated (like a delta), which is bad. For a "peaked" spectrum (like a Dirichlet kernel), the IDFT would be spread out, which is good but limited by the sampling.

This is related to the "minimum peak factor" problem in signal processing! The optimal sequences for minimizing the peak of the autocorrelation are related to Barker sequences, Zadoff-Chu sequences, and Golay sequences.

For nonnegative sequences, the problem is more constrained (a[j] >= 0). The optimal might be a "bump" shape: high in the middle, low at edges, or the reverse.

or a[j] = 1 + alpha * (2j/n - 1)² (parabolic, low in middle, high at edges).

The "low in middle" shape would reduce the central convolution peak (at k=n-1) because the "partner" positions in the middle have low values.
