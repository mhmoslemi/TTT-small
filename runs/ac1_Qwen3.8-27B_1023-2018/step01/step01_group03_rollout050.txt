Let me think about it from a different angle. The convolution c[m] = sum_k a[k]*a[m-k]. If we think of the sequence as a probability distribution (after normalization), then c[m]/(sum a)² is the probability distribution of the sum of two independent draws from that distribution.

So we're looking for a probability distribution on {0, 1, ..., n-1} that minimizes the maximum probability mass at any point of the convolution (sum of two iid draws).

The uniform distribution on {0,...,n-1} gives a triangular convolution with max n*(1/n)² = 1/n at the center. Score = 2n * 1/n * (sum)²/(sum)² = 2.

To beat 2, we need the convolution (as a probability distribution) to have max mass < 1/n.

The convolution of two copies of a distribution on {0,...,n-1} has support on {0,...,2n-2}, which has 2n-1 points. The average mass is 1/(2n-1) < 1/n.

So it's possible in principle to have max < 1/n if the mass is spread out enough. The question is whether the structure of the convolution (being a "self-convolution") constrains it.

For a distribution p on {0,...,n-1}, the self-convolution q[m] = sum_k p[k]*p[m-k].

The variance of q is related to the variance of p. If p is a delta (one point mass), q is two delta masses and the max is 1. If p is uniform, q is triangular with max 1/n.

Can we get q to be "flatter" than triangular? We'd need p to be "spikier" in some sense... but that makes q more concentrated.
