### Approach and Rationale

**Central Idea:**  
Minimize the AC1 bound (defined as \( \frac{2n \cdot \max(\text{full convolution})}{(\sum a_i)^2} \)) by iteratively reweighting candidate sequences based on their convolution strength, leveraging local optimization with analytic gradient information. This approach balances structural exploration with targeted refinement while avoiding prohibitively expensive finite-difference-based gradients. The key innovation lies in dynamically focusing optimization on constricting neighborhoods around promising candidates, reducing the d dimension gradually.

**Bottleneck Addressed:**  
Standard finite-difference gradient estimation is infeasible for high dimensions (\( n = 6962 \)) due to the quadratic convolution cost (\( O(n^2) \)). This strategy replaces numerical gradients with analytic gradient subgradients (handling the discontinuous max operator), enabling scalable optimization. It also improves upon the parent program by (1) avoiding particleswarm/brute-force methods and (2) integrating with the evaluator through explicit normalization.

**Limitations:**  
- Depends on exact gradient/tf math for the max operator.
- Requires careful handling of operational scaling constraints (normalization/clip boundaries).
- May diverge if inactive sets wrongly prioritize lower-convolution peaks.

def compute_gradient(a, max_k=None):
    conv, max_val = get_max_conv(a)
