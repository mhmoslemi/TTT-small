## Approach and Rationale

The central idea: we are to minimize the maximum magnitude of the linear convolution of the sequence with itself (the autocorrelation-like measure) normalized by the square of the sum. This is similar to minimizing the peak autocorrelation at lag 0 (but note: the full convolution has 2n-1 elements, so we are looking at the entire convolution).

We note that the problem is non-convex. However, we can exploit the fact that the objective function depends on the entire convolution. We can think of the sequence as a set of atoms (values) and the convolution as a weighted sum of these atoms.

We propose a two-step approach:

1. **Initialization**: Start with the provided parent sequence or a uniform distribution (or a log-uniform distribution) if the parent is not available.
2. **Optimization**: Use an iterative method (like gradient descent with a projection onto the feasible set) to minimize the objective. We will use the derivative of the score with respect to the sequence elements.

However, since the objective function is the maximum of a quadratic form (the convolution), which is non-differentiable, we cannot use standard gradient descent. Instead, we can use the method of "subgradient" or use random sampling (like in stochastic gradient) or other derivative-free methods.

The method improves upon a reference sequence by iteratively refining subregions of the search space and maintaining a repository of candidates, with a focus on handling the combinatorial nature of the optimization problem. The key insight is to balance exploration-exploitation trade-offs while respecting the nonnegative coefficient bounds and coefficient cap.

- **Constraints**: Enforces nonnegativity and coefficient bounds through projection

## 2. Mathematical Specification

### Optimization Problem
Minimize $f(x) = \frac{2n \max(\text{convolve}(x,x,\text{mode}=\text{'full'}))}{(\sum x_i)^2}$ over $x \in [0,1000]^n$
s.t. $\sum x_i \geq 0.01$

### Objective Transformation
Maintain invariable objective scaling by normalizing to $f(\lambda x + \mu) = f(x)$ for $\lambda,\mu$ projecting clips to $[0,1000]^n$ with sum normalization

### Representation Choices
1. **Standard Form**: Direct coefficients $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$
2. **Log Space**: $y = \log(x + \epsilon)$ for $\epsilon \approx 1e-6$
   - Maintains scale invariance: $y \to y+c$ preserves $f$
   - Facilitates Gaussian Process training
3. **Hybrid Form**: Switch between spaces using soft max transformation

### Gaussian Process Formulation
Let $g: \mathbb{R}^{n-1} \to \mathbb{R}$ where $g(x_{0:n-2}) = \frac{d(f(x))}{dx_{n-1}}$
Using Matérn kernel: $k(x_i,x_j) = \sigma^2 \left(1 + \sqrt{\nu} r + (\frac{3}{2\nu}) (1-r^2) \times \text{error modified Bessel function}\right)$
with $\nu=2.5$ and $r = \sqrt{\sum_{d} (x_i[d]-x_j[d])^2} / \theta_d$

### Decision Vector
$\theta = (l, \lambda, \tau, \text{mutationfactors}, \mu, \sigma)$
- l: subproblem dimension
- λ: crossover probability
- τ: temperature parameter for Laplace distribution
- mutationfactors: tunable magnitudes
- μ, σ: Gaussian Process mean and covariance parameters
