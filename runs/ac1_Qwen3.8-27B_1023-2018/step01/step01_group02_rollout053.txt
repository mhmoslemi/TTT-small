For a "bump" function that's concentrated in the middle: a[j] ∝ exp(-(j-c)²/σ²). The convolution will be a wider bump. The peak of the convolution relative to the mean...

Actually, let me think about it from the perspective of the "autocorrelation flatness". We want max(c[k]) / mean(c[k]) to be as small as possible. mean(c[k]) = (1/(2n-1)) * sum_k c[k] = (1/(2n-1)) * (sum a)² = S²/(2n-1).

So max(c) / mean(c) = (2n-1) * max(c) / S².

The score is 2n * max(c) / S² = 2n/(2n-1) * max(c)/mean(c) ≈ max(c)/mean(c) for large n.

So we want the autocorrelation to be as flat as possible. The "white noise" sequence gives a spike autocorrelation (delta). The "constant" sequence gives a triangular autocorrelation. We want something in between.

For the optimal solution, the autocorrelation should be as flat as possible while maintaining non-negativity of a.

This is related to the "minimum peak sidelobe" problem.
