Let me try a different approach: **iterative water-filling**.

The idea: at each step, identify the lags where conv is at or near the max. Then "push down" the contributions to those lags by adjusting f.

More concretely:
1. Compute b = conv(f, f), find the max M and the active set K*.
2. For each active lag k, the gradient of b_k w.r.t. f is 2 * f_{k-j} (as before).
3. We want to reduce b_k for k in K*. This means reducing f in the directions that contribute to b_k.
4. But we also need to maintain sum(f) = 1.

The projected gradient step is:
f_new = project(f - alpha * grad, {sum = 1, f >= 0})

where grad is the smoothed gradient (softmax-weighted).

This is straightforward and should work. Let me focus on this approach.

For the gradient:
grad_j = 2 * sum_{k in K*} p_k * f_{k-j}

where p_k are softmax weights (concentrated on the max).

The projection onto {sum = 1, f >= 0} is:
1. f_new = f - alpha * grad
2. f_new = max(f_new, 0)
3. f_new = f_new / sum(f_new)

Steps 2 and 3 don't exactly project onto the feasible set, but they're a reasonable approximation.
