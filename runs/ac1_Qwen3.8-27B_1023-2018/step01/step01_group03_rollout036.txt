  **Key observations:**
  1. The problem is to minimize `2n * max_k c[k] / (sum a)^2` where `c = conv(a, a)`.
  2. This is scale-invariant, so we can fix `sum(a) = 1` (or any convenient normalization).

  4. The problem is non-convex (max of linear functions of a quadratic, divided by a quadratic).

     - Also try various structured initializations (bilaterally symmetric, sine-based, etc.)

  2. **Optimization**:
     - **Gradient descent with adaptive steps**: Use the computed subgradient with backtracking line search.
     - **LP-based direction finding**: As in the parent, solve an LP to find a good direction.
     - **Combined approach**: Alternate between gradient steps and LP steps.

  3. **Multi-scale**: Start at small n, optimize, then upscale.

  - For n=512, the convolution takes O(n^2) = ~262K operations. Very fast.
  - Building the LP matrix takes O(n^2) as well.
  - The LP itself (with 2n-1 = 1023 constraints and n=512 variables) should be solvable quickly with HiGHS.

  3. Use the gradient for fine-tuning.

  Let me also think about what good sequences look like. Since we want `conv(a,a)` to be as flat as possible, and `conv(a,a)[k] = sum_i a[i]*a[k-i]`, we want the "energy" to be spread evenly across all convolution lags.

  For a sequence that is zero in the middle and concentrated at the ends, the convolution will have a peak at the center (where both ends contribute) and be small elsewhere. This is bad.

  For a sequence that is concentrated in the middle, the convolution will also have a peak at the center.

  The ideal is a "balanced" sequence where no pair of positions contributes too much to any single lag.

  One approach: think of it as a scheduling problem. We want to place "mass" at positions 0 to n-1 such that for every lag k, the sum of products of masses at positions i and k-i is minimized (in the max sense).
