Key insight: The parent's approach uses `grad_c = grad - grad.mean()` which is a projection that removes the "mean" component. This is related to the fact that the objective is scale-invariant - adding a constant to all a_j (before normalization) doesn't change the objective. The mean subtraction is an approximation of the constraint projection.

Actually, for the scale-invariant objective, the correct projection is:
projected_grad = grad - (dot(a, grad) / dot(a, a)) * a

This projects the gradient onto the tangent space of the "sphere" (constant L2 norm). But since we're on the simplex (L1 constraint), the projection is different.

However, since we re-normalize after each step anyway, the mean subtraction is a reasonable heuristic. Let me keep it.

Another idea: instead of pure gradient descent, I can use a "best-of" approach: try multiple random directions in addition to the gradient direction, and pick the best one. This is like a stochastic search combined with gradient.

Let me also consider: what if the max is always at lag 0? Then the problem reduces to minimizing sum(a_j^2) subject to sum(a_j) = 1, a_j >= 0. The minimum is 1/n (achieved by uniform). But we also need the other lags to be small. So it's a multi-objective problem.

Actually, for the parent sequence (which is a "step function"), the autocorrelation at lag 0 is probably larger than at other lags. Let me check: if a_j = 1/n for all j, then b_0 = 1/n and b_k = (n-k)/n^2 < 1/n. So the max is at lag 0.

For a non-uniform sequence, b_0 = sum(a_j^2) can be larger or smaller relative to other lags. The goal is to make ALL lags small, with the constraint that their sum is (sum a_j)^2 = 1.

So we want b_k ≈ 1/(2n-1) for all k. The optimal sequence would have a "flat" autocorrelation.

This is related to the "flat-top" autocorrelation problem. For real, nonnegative sequences, this is harder than for complex sequences (where Zadoff-Chu or CAZAC sequences work).

I think the gradient descent approach is the right one. Let me refine it:

1. Use Nesterov momentum for faster convergence
2. Use an adaptive learning rate based on the gradient norm
3. Periodically "anneal" the temperature
4. Use a "restart" strategy when stuck (go back to best_a and perturb differently)

Let me also add: when the max is at lag 0, add a small "flattening" pressure (push all a_j toward their mean). When the max is at a positive lag, the gradient naturally pushes to reduce that specific correlation.
