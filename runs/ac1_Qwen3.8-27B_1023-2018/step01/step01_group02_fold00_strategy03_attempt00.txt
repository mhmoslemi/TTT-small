1. Approach and Rationale  
   This approach addresses the problem of minimizing the AC1 upper bound for a nonnegative step-function sequence. The central idea is to leverage a representation based on approximate eigenfunctions of the autocorrelation operator in the frequency domain. Unlike fixed-kernel methods, this representation is based on smoothed eigenfunctions that capture the structure of optimal sequences for extremal eigenvalue problems. The representation is low-dimensional, reducing computational complexity while retaining elasticity to various autocorrelation objectives. Starting from an exponentially rising basis, we optimize the smoothed eigenfunctions through directional iteration, followed by projection onto the target sequence space.  

   A key bottleneck addressed is the limitation of fixed-kernel bases such as sinuses or sincs, which can constrain the sequence's ability to realize optimal smooth decay at critical frequencies. This method eliminates that by dynamically optimizing the basis functions themselves, ensuring alignment with the specific nonlinear objective. The approach is not derivative-based; instead, it combines iterative basis evolution with whole-spectrum optimization, making it applicable to any AC-like extremal problem.

   The method is suitable for improvement over the parent because it introduces adaptive basis elements tailored to the frequency structure of the current sequence, transitioning from narrowband to broadband optimization as the iteration proceeds.

2. Mathematical Specification  
   The sequence is represented as a linear combination:  
   \[
   x(t) \approx \sum_{k=1}^{K} c_k u_k(t), \quad t=1,\ldots,n
   \]
   where \(K\) is a fixed-dimension basis with \(c_k \in \mathbb{R}\), and \(u_k\) form approximate eigenfunctions of the autocorrelation integral in the Fourier domain. The basis functions are defined as smoothed sinc functions:  
   \[
   u_k(t) = \frac{\sin(\omega_k t)}{\pi t} \cdot g(t)
   \]
   with smoothing kernel \(g(t)\) chosen to ensure continuity and nonnegativity for the candidates.  

   The objective is to minimize:  
   \[
   \text{AC1} = \frac{2n \cdot \max_{\text{conv}} x \ast x}{\left( \sum x \right)^2}
   \]
   This formulation exposes the dual constraint between basis elements and strength in sustaining extremal behavior. Each iteration updates the basis kernels and coefficients simultaneously via a block-gradient method, projecting the evolving functions back into the discrete sequence space for exact evaluation.
