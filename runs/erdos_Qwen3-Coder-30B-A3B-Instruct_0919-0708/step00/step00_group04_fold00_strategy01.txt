**1.  Reformulation of the objective**  
For a discretised step‑function  
\(h\in[0,1]^{n}\)   (even length \(n\)),  \(\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i = n/2\) (so the integral equals 1).  
For a shift \(k\in\{-n+1,\dots ,n-1\}\) the overlap that the evaluator computes is  

\[
C_k(h)=\sum_{i:\,i+k\in[0,n)} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+k}\bigr) .
\]

The quantity that must be minimised is  

\[
F(h)=\max_{k} C_k(h)\qquad\text{and}\qquad C_5 = F(h)\cdot\frac{2}{n}.
\]

Because \(F\) is a non‑smooth max‑operator we replace it by a smooth upper bound that can be differentiated:

\[
\Phi_\alpha(h)=\frac1\alpha\log\!\Bigl(\sum_{k}e^{\alpha C_k(h)}\Bigr), \qquad \alpha>0 .
\]

When \(\alpha\) is small \(\Phi_\alpha\) is a gentle average, when \(\alpha\) grows it approaches the true maximum.  Minimising \(\Phi_\alpha\) therefore drives the maximum overlap down.

**2.  Gradient of the smooth surrogate**

* box constraints \(0\le h_i\le1\),  
* the exact integral \(\sum_i h_i=n/2\).

The Euclidean projection onto the intersection of a hyper‑plane and a box is obtained by “water‑filling’’:

3.  Set \(h_i=\operatorname{clip}(\tilde h_i-\lambda,0,1)\).
