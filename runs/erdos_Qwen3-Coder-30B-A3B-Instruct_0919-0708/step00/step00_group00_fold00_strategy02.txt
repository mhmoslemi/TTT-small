| **LP bound is too loose** (McCormick envelopes allow large product over‑estimates) | When many `h_i` lie near 0.5 the envelope is wide, so `t` may be far from the true minimal autocorrelation. | Apply the cutting‑plane tightening loop (Section 4) to add locally valid affine cuts; also consider increasing `n` modestly (e.g. 60) to give the LP more degrees of freedom. |
| **Solver returns a slightly infeasible `h`** (tiny violation of box or sum due to tolerance) | Numerical tolerance of interior‑point solvers. | After solving, **clamp** each `h_i` to `[0,1]` and re‑project onto the exact sum hyperplane using the capped‑simplex algorithm before any evaluation. |
| **Local refinement gets stuck** (no improvement after many moves) | The neighbourhood (pairwise transfer) may be too restricted once `h` is near a local optimum. | Occasionally perform a **global perturbation**: pick three indices and redistribute a small amount of mass among them; or restart the refinement from a randomly projected version of the LP solution. |
| **Time budget exceeded before refinement finishes** | Overly many LP re‑solves or too many annealing iterations. | Keep strict time checks before each LP solve (LP solves take < 0.1 s) and before each batch of refinement moves; fall back to returning the best LP solution if time runs out. |
| **Memory blow‑up for larger `n`** (quadratic number of `y` variables) | If the user tries `n > 150` the LP may have > 10 k variables. | Restrict `n` to ≤ 80 for the LP stage; if a finer discretisation is later required for refinement, generate the dense `h` by sampling the LP solution rather than solving a bigger LP. |
| **Dual information unavailable** (solver does not expose multipliers) | Some open‑source solvers hide duals. | Use the *primal* autocorrelation values (`S_k = Σ_i h_i·h_{i+k}`) computed directly from `h` to identify the worst shift; this requires only an extra correlation pass, negligible in cost. |

### 8.  Why this approach is materially different

* It formulates the problem as a **pure linear program** using **McCormick convex envelopes** for the bilinear products, guaranteeing a **provable bound** without any smoothing or gradient approximation.  
* The LP solution itself is a **feasible step‑function**, not a surrogate that needs to be projected later.  
* The method exploits **dual (or primal) information** to focus later local improvements on the most critical shifts, a strategy absent from the previous gradient‑based and CMA‑ES pipelines.  
* The overall workflow comprises **(i) a convex global bound**, **(ii) optional cutting‑plane tightening**, and **(iii) a lightweight, projection‑based annealing refinement** – a completely new algorithmic chain.
