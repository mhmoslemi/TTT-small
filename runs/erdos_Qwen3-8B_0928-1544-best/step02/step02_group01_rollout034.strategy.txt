### 1. Approach and rationale

We propose a Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) approach to tackle the Erdős minimum overlap problem. PSO is particularly suited for non-convex optimization problems and can efficiently explore the search space while avoiding local optima. The method relies on a population of candidate solutions ("particles") that move through the search space, guided by their own best experiences and the collective experience of the swarm.

Key advantages over previous approaches:
- Robust global exploration without relying on gradient information
- Natural handling of non-convex objectives
- Parallel evaluation of candidates
- Dynamic adaptation of search parameters

Limitations:
- Requires careful tuning of swarm dynamics
- May struggle with extreme discretization sizes
- Not guaranteed to find the global optimum

### 2. Mathematical specification

**Variables**:
- h ∈ [0,1]^n: step function values at n_points
- v ∈ ℝ^n: particle velocities
- pbest ∈ [0,1]^n: personal best position for each particle
- gbest ∈ [0,1]^n: global best position across swarm

**Objective**:
Minimize C₅ = max(np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full") * (2/n_points))

**Constraints**:
1. 0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1 for all i
2. sum(h) = n_points / 2

**PSO Dynamics**:
- Velocity update:
  v_i(t+1) = w*v_i(t) + c1*r1*(pbest_i - h_i) + c2*r2*(gbest_i - h_i)
- Position update:
  h_i(t+1) = h_i(t) + v_i(t+1)
- Feasibility projection:
  h_i = clip(h_i, 0, 1)
  h = h * (n_points/2 / h.sum())

Where:
- w = inertia weight (0.7)
- c1 = cognitive learning factor (2.0)
- c2 = social learning factor (2.0)
- r1, r2 = random numbers in [0,1]
