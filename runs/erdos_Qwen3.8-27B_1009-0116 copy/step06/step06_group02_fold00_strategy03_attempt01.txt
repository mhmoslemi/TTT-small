## Approach and Rationale
Our method to improve the Erdős minimal overlap constant uses an alternating optimization strategy on a novel spike representation. We represent the step function as a sum of symmetric, narrow triangular "spikes" placed at optimal positions. The key insight is that periodic solutions may minimize overlaps, and the triangular representation allows efficient computation of the correlation integral. Unlike gradient-based methods, we use coordinate descent to iteratively optimize spike positions and widths, then globally resample and compare solutions across different resolutions. This approach directly minimizes the max correlation without relying on surrogate objectives or initialization points.

This method is computationally intensive but necessary due to the problem's non-convex landscape and lack of known analytical solutions. We bottleneck our optimization to specific resolutions (50, 100, 200 spikes) for manageable computation. The performance will depend on the spike shape, width variation, and initial perturbations.

### Bottleneck Addressed:
- Prevents getting stuck in local minima
- Captures potential periodicity through optimizable spike placements
- Easily parallelizable with numpy operations

### Limitations:
- Fixed number of spikes: solutions may not globally optimal
- Limited physical interpretation compared to other approaches
- Computation may not scale to higher resolutions
