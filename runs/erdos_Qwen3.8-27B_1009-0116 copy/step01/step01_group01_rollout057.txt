The key insight for CD: for coordinate i, the objective max_k c_k(h) as a function of h[i] (with others fixed) is the max of linear functions of h[i], hence convex. The minimum of a convex function on [0,1] is at one of the "kink points" or boundaries.

For each lag k, c_k is linear in h[i]: c_k = a_k * h[i] + b_k where a_k and b_k depend on other h values.

The minimum of max_k (a_k x + b_k) on x ∈ [0,1] occurs at:
- x = 0
- x = 1
- Or at a point where the two "highest" lines (one with positive slope, one with negative slope) cross

To find the crossing: sort by slope. The upper envelope changes slope at the crossing points. The minimum of the upper envelope is where the envelope transitions from decreasing to increasing, i.e., where the slope goes from negative to positive.

In practice, for O(n) lags, I can:
1. Compute a_k and b_k for all k
2. Find the max at x=0 and x=1
3. Check if the minimum is interior by looking for sign change in the "effective slope"

Actually, a simpler approach that works well: ternary search on the convex function [0,1]. Since the function is convex (max of linear), ternary search converges.

Or even better: bisection on the subgradient. The subgradient at x is the set of slopes of active (maximizing) lines. We want 0 to be in the subgradient. Binary search for this.

- If i is in [p, n-1] (appears as h[j] with j=i): contributes -h[i-p]
