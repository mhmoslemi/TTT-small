Wait, I have a concern. The problem says the evaluation computes:
C₅ = max(np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full") * dx)

Note: `np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full") * dx` - this multiplies the ENTIRE correlation array by dx, then takes the max. So it's:
C₅ = dx * max_k corr[k]

Which is the same as what I'm computing. Good.

But wait, there's a subtlety. For 'full' mode, the correlation at different lags has different numbers of terms. At lag 0, there are n terms. At lag k (k>0), there are n-k terms. So the "average" per term is different.

The integral ∫₀² h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx is approximated by dx * ∑_{i} h[i](1-h[i+k]) where the sum is over the valid range. For the discretization to be consistent, we want:
dx * ∑_{i=0}^{n-1-k} h[i+k]*(1-h[i]) ≈ ∫_0^{2-k*dx} h(x+k*dx)*(1-h(x)) dx

This is correct - the number of terms adjusts automatically. So the formula is self-consistent.

One more thing: the lags go from -(n-1) to (n-1). For negative lags, np.correlate(h, 1-h, 'full')[n-1+k] for k<0 gives ∑ h[i](1-h[i+k]) over valid ranges. By symmetry, corr[-k] and corr[k] might differ (since h and 1-h are different).

Actually, let me think about this more carefully. np.correlate(a, v, 'full')[k] = ∑_i a[i+k] * v[i] where the sum is over all i where both a[i+k] and v[i] are valid.

With a = h, v = 1-h:
- At lag 0 (index n-1 in output): ∑ h[i](1-h[i]) = 0 for binary
