Based on analysis, the initial approach using uniform sequences normalized to `sqrt(2n)` yields a score of 2 due to the inherent autocorrelation properties. To achieve a lower score of approximately 1.5 for a 256-length sequence, the strategy must fundamentally change how the sequence is structured. The key insight is that sequences with high granularity—multiple distinct high values spaced in a way that minimizes overlap in autocorrelation—could reduce the maximum convolution value.

This strategy introduces a non-uniform structure with a block-like pattern. Each candidate sequence is constructed by selecting a random number of blocks (`k`), generating random high values (`dh`) for these blocks, ensuring non-negativity, and maintaining the total sum constraint (`sum_a >= 0.01`). The central idea is to spread out the large values (`dh`) such that their contributions to the convolution are incoherent, potentially lowering the score.

The algorithm uses three types of updates per iteration:
1. **Recentering**: Adjust the central positions to test for symmetry.
2. **Rescaling**: Change the overall levels to satisfy the sum constraint.
3. **Reflection**: Flip sections to explore different configurations.

These updates are applied in random order, with sequence representation and normalization handled explicitly. The defense layer in `evaluate_sequence` is acknowledged but not directly utilized in the initial candidate generation, to keep the search space bounded and the objective function consistent.
