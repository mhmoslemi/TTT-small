This is a high-dimensional, non-convex optimization problem with a nonlinear objective and both box and equality constraints. The initial solution provides a benchmark value of approximately 0.3808, and we aim to find a candidate with a lower upper bound.

Traditional gradient-based methods are prone to getting stuck in local minima due to the rugged objective landscape. A genetic algorithm (GA) is chosen for its population-based exploration while maintaining feasibility constraints through repair mechanisms. The key innovation is explicit crossover and mutation operators designed to preserve candidate feasibility and diversity. We also incorporate a fitness-based selection that favors solutions with lower overlap, using tournament selection and elitism to accelerate convergence.

The central idea is to decompose the parent solution into components and recombine them through crossover operations focused on high-impact regions. Mutation introduces exploratory noise to escape local optima. We repair infeasible candidates by first clamping values to bounds and then adjusting sums proportionally to meet the integral constraint. This approach leverages both local refinement and global exploration, which is essential for escaping plateaus common in such continuous-discrete problems.

The main limitations include computational cost and potential premature convergence. However, the GA's population dynamics are carefully tuned for balance between exploration and exploitation.
