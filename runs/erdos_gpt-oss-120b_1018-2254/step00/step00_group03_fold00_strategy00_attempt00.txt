1. **Approach and Rationale**
This approach involves using a combination of basis functions to represent the step function by its expansion coefficients. The idea is to minimize the representational dimension while maintaining a sufficient number of "pulses" to capture the optimization landscape's distinct features. The rationale is that reducing the dimensionality simplifies the optimization problem, addresses non-convexity, and leverages the smoothness and regularity of basis expansions for better convergence. The key limitation is that very low dimensions might miss crucial details, while higher dimensions increase computational complexity. We distinguish this iterative projection method from local optimization by preserving optimality certificates from higher dimensions, offering a balance between speed and theoretical guarantees.

**Iterations and Parameters:**
- We start with a predefined set of basis functions (e.g., orthogonal polynomials) and adaptively choose the optimal dimension (`d`) via a binary search on the error term, but for low-dimensional approximation, we use a logarithmic grid.
- We will use Legendre polynomials as a basis for their orthogonality properties, but the exact basis can vary to reflect the optimization landscape.
- The primary constraint is that the expansion coefficients must satisfy the integral constraint (feature scaling), and the resulting function must be clipped to `[0, 1]` with sum `d`.

2. **Mathematical Specification**
**Representation**: The candidate function `h(x)` is approximated as `h(x) = sum_{i=0}^{d-1} a_i * ψ_i(x)`, where `ψ_i(x)` are basis functions. For strict orthogonality and interpretability, we use Legendre polynomials for `[0, 1]` normalization, but note that the interval is `[0, 2]`, so we'll map accordingly.

**Variables**: Optimization variable `a` ∈ ℝᵈ, the coefficient vector. Other variables include `d` (dimension) and `ψ_i` (basis functions).
