The Erdős discrepancy problem requires minimizing the maximum overlap between a step function `h` and its shifts. The objective function is the maximum over shift `k` of the integral `∫ h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx`. This is a high-dimensional, non-convex optimization problem with nonlinear constraints. The initial solution (`n_points = 45`) provides a benchmark. The genetic algorithm (GA) is chosen for its ability to explore complex landscapes and escape local optima. It operates on populations of candidate vectors, applying crossover and mutation to recombine information and systematically search the space. The central idea is to decompose the parent solution into components and recombine them to form new candidates. Those with reduced overlap are retained.
