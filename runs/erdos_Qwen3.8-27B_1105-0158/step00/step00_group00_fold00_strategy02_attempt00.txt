1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea

This plan treats the evaluator exactly, without replacing its finite-support cross-correlation by circular autocorrelation, smoothing, a convex relaxation, or a global quadratic-program model that is not in fact convex. The core update is an **exact feasible two-coordinate exchange** embedded in a deterministic block-coordinate descent, augmented by random feasible restarts and an optional simultaneous-direction attempt. A move preserves the equality constraint automatically because it transfers a parameterized amount from one coordinate to another:

\[
h_p(t)=h_p+t,\qquad h_q(t)=h_q-t,
\]

with all other coordinates fixed and \(t\) selected in the interval where both coordinates remain in \([0,1]\). The objective at each trial value is evaluated by the official discrete metric. A one-dimensional model built from the exact row formulas—only for guiding and pruning line searches—is optional and always secondary; every acceptance is verified by the exact evaluator on a feasible vector as returned by the program.

This differs substantively from prior proposals that employ log-sum-exp smoothing plus projected quasi-Newton, or linearized epigraph LP subproblems. The update here is nonlinear, exact for the two-coordinate displacement, and always stores and returns fully feasible discrete step functions. It avoids the claimed convexity of SVX-solvable main objective, avoids epigraph linearizations that do not guarantee rejection of any candidate via the exact evaluator, and does not summarize using a large surrogate table.

1.2 Why it may reach or improve the supplied parent

Minimum-overlap constraints are composed of many finite-support quadratic pieces. A local optimum in the nonconvex problem may be nonimprovable for single-coordinate moves, which are generally impossible with the integral constraint, or for continuous averaged smoothed-gradient descent. Two-coordinate exchanges, however, are exactly the smallest tangent subspace preserving the integral. A full pair-exchange method explores this space directly. With restarts and randomization, it can move away from the supplied parent or any infeasible projection artifact and enter a basin where the block descent can refine it. Since the target requires only a conservative improvement relative to the current record, the article of the supplied parent as the starting point makes it a local objective graph rather than a global search puzzle.

1.3 Important bottlenecks addressed

1. Boundary-sensitive objective:
The full correlation has unnormalized left and right contributions whose size changes with lag. This plan uses the evaluator’s exact vector `complex correlate(h,1-h,mode="full")` directly. Zero lag and both lag directions are always included. There is no hidden transformation to a global autocorrelation or circular autocorrelation.

2. Nonconvexity and local minima:
The main search is a nonconvex min-max method; objective evaluations use the nonideal-object objective. Randomization of perturbed generated pair profiles escape local minimum from the parent.

3. Constraint feasibility:
All primary moves are two-coordinate transfers, so they preserve the discrete integral exactly at the algebraic level and preserve per-coordinate bounds by interval selection. A separate joint repair procedure handles floating drift and optional random or transferred candidates. The repair acts simultaneously on the box and the sum equality, avoiding incorrect independent clipping/rescaling.

4. Avoiding invalid solver warnings:
CVX and nonlinear programs are not used to claim global optimality. Any numerical or heuristic proposal is verified only after nondeterministic returned vectors are made feasible and scored by the provided objective vectorization.

1.4 Honest limitations

- The core block-coordinate method is not guaranteed to find a global minimizer. It should be regarded as a very exact local polish with randomized restarts.
