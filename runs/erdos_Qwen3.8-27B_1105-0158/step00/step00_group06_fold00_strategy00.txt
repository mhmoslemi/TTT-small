The current construction length is already small (`parent n`, normally 70). The task is therefore to make a careful local/stochastic refinement of that feasible step function, or of a small population nearby, while every returned vector is validated against the exact finite-support evaluator. The final returned sequence should normally remain the parent length or, only if it improves the exact score, one of a few controlled refinements such as repeating each parent sample twice.

The central mathematical object is the finite-support bilinear overlap array
`q=(np.correlate(h, 1-h, "full") * (2/n))`, whose largest component is the exact C5 score. This is not convex: each term is a bilinear difference of products of entries of `h`. The feasible set `S={h: 0<=h_i<=1, sum(h)=n/2}` is convex, and it admits a simple exact/near-exact Euclidean projection by clipping after a one-dimensional scalar search. The proposed optimization therefore combines:

- exact feasibility repair and projection;
- a smooth surrogacy `smooth_tau(h)` that concentrates penalty on the top overlaps (large tau penalizes all Lags, small tau penalizes only peak lags);
- a fully time-managed projected gradient classifier (like a smoothed projected gradient descent) that can be stopped at any point and has already stored the feasible exact best;
- a supporting decomposition routine and multi-start phase to avoid the initial point increasing as C5 increases;
- controlled use of `scipy.optimize.minimize` with narrow iteration bounds.
- and variance support to repeat metamorph of nearby good end sequences.
