1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea and name of the method

The method is called Exact Ray Minimax Descent with pool escaping, or ERMS. Its central primitive is an exact one-dimensional search along a feasible affine ray inside the feasible polytope. The ray is generated in a sum-zero direction, so every point on the ray has the required total mass. The exact objective along that ray is known to be the maximum of finitely many quadratic functions of the scalar ray parameter. Therefore the minimum of the objective on any finite ray interval can be found without smoothing, without replacing the max by an average, and without a convex relaxation: solve the individual quadratic extrema and pairwise intersections of the quadratic lag curves. Candidate points produced by this primitive are then always checked with the authoritative discrete evaluator. The exact objective never misuses a relaxation; it only chooses moves among feasible rays.

The algorithm is organized around three types of feasible ray moves.

First, coordinate-pair rays, where only two coordinates change oppositely: `h_i` is increased while `h_j` is decreased, and all others are fixed. These are the edges of the feasible transport face and are natural for enforcing the constraint `sum h = n/2` exactly. A selected set of promising coordinate-pair rays is scanned and each is optimized over its feasible interval.

Second, general sum-zero random rays, which permit simultaneous multi-coordinate perturbations while preserving the mass constraint. These are used after coordinate-pair scanning stalls or as steady metaheuristic diversifiers. Random directions use finite sum-zero Gaussian-like vectors and are confined to a local or moderate interval around the current feasible base. The feasibility interval and exact line minimization are identical to the coordinate-ray case.

Third, small feasible stochastic ray steps accepted by annealing-style local criteria.
