### Approach and rationale
The task is to minimize the evaluation function for a sequence of non-negative heights, which is given by \(2n \cdot \max_{\text{conv}}\) / \((\sum_{\text{seq}})^2\), where the convolution is performed in full mode. The previous program achieved an upper bound of 2.0 using a uniform sequence or block perturbations, but we need to achieve a lower value (1.5027) by finding a sequence that reduces the maximum convolution value relative to the sum.

I hypothesize that the optimal sequence should be concentrated on a few non-negative values (as large as possible, capped at 1000) to control the self-convolution peaks. This is because uniform sequences maximize the \(\max(\text{conv})\) for a given sum. By using a sparse sequence with carefully spaced large values, we can reduce the convolution maximum without proportionally increasing the sum, thus lowering the overall ratio.

The gradient information and the fact that the objective is invariant under scaling strongly suggest that the minimal ratio is 2 for any sequence. However, manual calculations for small sequences with large values showed lower ratios. This discrepancy arises because the gradient-based method can sometimes identify configurations where the ratio drops below 2, particularly in sequences with sparse, equidistant large values, but these might not scale to larger sequences due to increased overlap.

The key insight is to bisect the sequence length (4498) into two halves, place large values at critical points, and explore stair-step distributions. This leverages the step-like profile (delta functions) to manage overlapping autocorrelation peaks effectively.

### Mathematical specification
Define the sequence of length \(n = 4498\) as a step function. Let \(a\)' be a marker that indicates which positions are active. An active position \(i\) values to 1000, and others to a small \(\epsilon (0.001)\). The sum of the sequence is approximately \(1000 \cdot K + (4498 - K) \cdot \epsilon\), where \(K\) is the number of active positions.

The convolution \(\text{conv} = a * a\) (full mode) produces coefficients that are the dot products at each lag. The \(\text{max}(\text{conv})\) can be controlled by selecting \(K\), and it will be minimized when active values are spaced far apart, so no significant overlap occurs.

The objective is \(2n \cdot \max\text{(conv)}\) / \((\sum\text{seq})^2\).
