To achieve a lower objective than the previous upper bound of 2.0, the sequence should be designed to reduce the peak of the convolution while increasing the sum. This suggests sequences that are less focused or more uniformly distributed might be beneficial, as opposed to the symmetric normal distribution used in the parent program. By varying the sequence structure using a genetic algorithm, we can explore a broader range of configurations and increase the likelihood of finding an optimal or near-optimal solution within the time constraints (up to 2400 seconds). The genetic algorithm allows for elitism, crossover, and mutation operations to refine the search, and it can be run until the objective stabilizes or the time budget is exhausted. This approach is particularly suitable since the evaluation function calls can be time-consuming (O(n^2) for large n), and we need to control the search cost by focusing on efficient implementations.
