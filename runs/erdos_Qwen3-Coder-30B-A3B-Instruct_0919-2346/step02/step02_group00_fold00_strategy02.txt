### 1.  High‑level view  
The task is to minimise  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{k}\int_{0}^{2}h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx
      =1-\min_{k}\int_{0}^{2}h(x)h(x+k)\,dx ,
\]

subject to  

* \(0\le h(x)\le 1\) for every \(x\in[0,2]\),  
* \(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}h(x)\,dx = 1\).

We discretise the interval with **\(N\) points** (choose a power of two, e.g. \(N=256\)) and write \(h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\) with step size \(dx=2/N\).  
The core idea is to **replace the raw max‑over‑shifts by a smooth surrogate**, optimise a **low‑dimensional parametrisation** of \(h\) with a **gradient‑based constrained optimiser**, and finally polish the result with a cheap projected sub‑gradient method.  This differs fundamentally from the previous annealing / pair‑move schemes.

### 2.  Re‑parameterising \(h\) with a compact basis  

1. **Basis choice** – use the first **\(K\) discrete prolate spheroidal sequences (DPSS)** (`scipy.signal.slepian`).

2. **Linear combination** – collect the \(K\) vectors as columns of a matrix \(B\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times K}\).

3. **Coefficient vector** – denote the free parameters by \(\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{K+1}\).  
   The raw signal is  
   \[
   \tilde h(\theta)=B\theta .
   \]

4. **Enforcing the box \([0,1]\)** – apply a smooth, monotone squashing function that maps \(\mathbb{R}\) to \([0,1]\):  

   \[
   h(\theta)=\sigma\bigl(\tilde h(\theta)\bigr),\qquad

### 3.  Smooth approximation of the max  

Directly minimising \(\max_s f_s(h)\) yields a non‑differentiable objective.  Replace it by a **log‑sum‑exp (soft‑max) surrogate**:

\[
\Phi_\beta(h)=\frac{1}{\beta}\,
\log\Bigl(\sum_{s=-(N-1)}^{N-1} \exp\bigl(\beta\,c_s(h)\bigr)\Bigr),
\qquad
c_s(h)=\bigl(h\star (1-h)\bigr)_s\,dx .
\]

* For \(\beta\to\infty\) the surrogate converges to the true maximum.  
* For moderate \(\beta\) it is smooth and its gradient is a weighted average of the individual shift‑gradients with weights equal to a softmax over the correlation values.

We will use a **continuation strategy**:

| Stage | \(\beta\) | Purpose |
|------|-----------|----------|
| 1 – exploratory | \(\beta_0 = 1\) | Very smooth, the optimiser can move freely. |
| 2 – refinement   | increase geometrically (1 → 5 → 20) | The surrogate tightens around the true max. |
| 3 – final       | \(\beta_{\max}=50\) or larger | Approxates the real objective. |

### 4.  Analytic gradient of the surrogate  

Denote by \(w_s(\theta)\) the softmax weights:

\[
w_s = \frac{\exp(\beta c_s)}{\sum_{t}\exp(\beta c_t)} .
\]

The derivative of \(\Phi_\beta\) w.r.t. \(h\) is

\[
\frac{\partial \Phi_\beta}{\partial h}
     = \sum_s w_s \; \frac{\partial c_s}{\partial h}.
\]

The partial derivative of a single correlation entry \(c_s\) is exactly the **sub‑gradient** derived earlier:

*For a non‑negative shift \(s\ge 0\):*

\[
(\partial c_s / \partial h)_i
   = (1-h_{i+s})\,\mathbf{1}_{i+s<N}
     \;-\; h_{i-s}\,\mathbf{1}_{i-s\ge0}.
\]

*For a negative shift the expression is symmetric.*  

Because \(h=\sigma(\tilde h)\) we have to apply the chain rule:

\[
\frac{\partial \Phi_\beta}{\partial \theta}
   = \bigl(\frac{\partial \Phi_\beta}{\partial h}\bigr)^{\!\top}
     \operatorname{diag}\!\bigl(\sigma'(\tilde h)\bigr) B .
\]

All the terms are vectorised; the dominant cost is the **FFT‑based convolution** for computing the whole correlation vector \(c\) (cost \(O(N\log N)\)).  With \(N=256\) a single evaluation is far below a millisecond.
