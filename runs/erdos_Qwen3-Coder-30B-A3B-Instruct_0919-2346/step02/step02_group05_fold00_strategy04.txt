**1.  Core mathematical reformulation**

The quantity to be minimised is  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{k}\int_0^2 h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx .
\]

Because the total mass is fixed  
\(\displaystyle\int_0^2h(x)\,dx=1\), we can rewrite  

\[
C_5(h)=1-\min_{k}\int_0^2 h(x)h(x+k)\,dx .
\]

Hence the problem is **maximise the smallest autocorrelation** of \(h\) under the constraints  

* \(0\le h(x)\le 1\) for all \(x\),
* \(\displaystyle\int_0^2 h(x)dx=1\).

After discretisation with \(N\) equally‑spaced points (\(\Delta x=2/N\)) we obtain  

\[
g_k(\mathbf{h})=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h_i\,h_{i+k},\qquad k=-(N-1),\dots,N-1,
\]

and the objective becomes  

\[
\max_{\mathbf{h}} \; \min_k g_k(\mathbf{h})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
0\le h_i\le1,\;\sum_i h_i=\tfrac{N}{2}.
\]

The crucial insight is that the *worst* shift  

\[
k^\star=\arg\min_k g_k(\mathbf{h})
\]

pinpoints exactly where the autocorrelation is lacking.  Any modification that raises \(g_{k^\star}\) can only improve (or keep) the objective.  This observation drives the new algorithmic pipeline.

**3.  Stage A – Targeted binary hill‑climb**

*Representation* – an integer vector \(\mathbf{h}\in\{0,1\}^N\) with exactly \(N/2\) ones.

*Initial seeds* –  
1. Randomly shuffled binary vectors (uniform over all \(\binom{N}{N/2}\) choices).

*Iterative move* (repeat until a per‑run time limit or a patience counter expires):

1. **Compute the full autocorrelation**  
   \(g=\text{np.correlate}(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{h},\text{mode='full'})\).  

2. **Identify the worst shift**  
   \(k^\star = \arg\min g\) (the index offset is \(k^\star = \text{idx}-(N-1)\)).  

3. **Form two candidate index sets** (only positions that actually overlap under shift \(k^\star\)):  
   * \(A=\{i \mid h_i=1,\;h_{i+k^\star}=0\}\) – a 1 that does **not** meet a 1 after the shift.  
   * \(B=\{j \mid h_j=0,\;h_{j+k^\star}=1\}\) – a 0 that **does** meet a 1 after the shift.  

4. **Search for an improving swap**:  
   * Randomly sample up to 30 elements from each set (to keep the move cheap).  
   * For every sampled pair \((i,j)\) evaluate the effect of swapping the bits (the swap preserves the mass constraint).  
   * Pick the pair that yields the largest reduction in \(C_5\) (i.e. the largest increase of the minimal autocorrelation).  
   * If an improving swap exists, perform it, reset the “no‑improvement” counter and continue.  

5. **Exploratory move** (when no improving swap is found):  
   * Randomly select a 1‑index and a 0‑index and swap them anyway.  
   * Accept the move with probability \(\exp\bigl(-(C_{\text{new}}-C_{\text{old}})/T\bigr)\), where the temperature \(T\) follows a slow decay (e.g. \(T\gets T\cdot0.999\) each iteration).  This simulated‑annealing step lets the search cross plateaus.  

6. **Record the best binary vector** seen during the run (its objective value is stored even if later steps worsen the current state).

*Complexity*: a correlation costs \(O(N^2)\) (≈ 20 k elementary ops for \(N=140\)), a swap evaluation is also \(O(N^2)\) when recomputed, but the limited number of sampled swaps keeps each iteration under a few milliseconds. **4.  Stage B – Smooth‑max surrogate + projected gradient ascent**

The non‑smooth max is approximated by  

\[
\Phi_\beta(\mathbf{h})=\frac{1}{\beta}
\log\!\Bigl(\sum_k \exp\bigl(\beta\,c_k(\mathbf{h})\bigr)\Bigr),
\qquad
c_k(\mathbf{h})=\Delta x\sum_i h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+k}\bigr),
\]

with a smoothing parameter \(\beta\) (start around 10, increase gradually).  For moderate \(\beta\) the function is differentiable and its gradient can be expressed analytically:

\[
\frac{\partial\Phi_\beta}{\partial h_j}
=
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_k w_k\,(1-2h_{j+k})}
{\displaystyle\sum_k w_k},
\qquad
w_k = \exp\bigl(\beta\,c_k(\mathbf{h})\bigr).
\]

All required convolutions (\(\{c_k\},\{w_k\},\) and the weighted sum of shifted \((1-2h_{j+k})\)) are obtained with a handful of `np.correlate` calls (or FFT‑based convolution for speed, although the small \(N\) makes the direct method sufficient).

*Projected gradient step* (repeat while time remains):

2. **Unconstrained update** – \(\mathbf{h}'=\mathbf{h}+\eta\mathbf{g}\) with a step size \(\eta\) (initially 0.01, adapted by a simple back‑tracking line search).  
3. **Box projection** – clip \(\mathbf{h}'\) to the interval \([0,1]\).  
4. **Equality projection** – enforce \(\sum_i h_i=N/2\) by an affine correction:  

   \[
   \mathbf{h}'' = \mathbf{h}' - \frac{\bigl(\sum_i h'_i - N/2\bigr)}{N}\mathbf{1}.

6. **β‑schedule** – after a fixed wall‑clock interval (e.g. every 30 s) multiply \(\beta\) by 1.2 so that the surrogate increasingly concentrates on the true maximum.

**5.  Stage C – Global optimisation in a low‑dimensional block space**

Many high‑performing constructions are “banded”: the function stays nearly constant on long sub‑intervals and only a few jumps occur.  To explore this family efficiently:

1. **Choose a block count** \(B\) (e.g. 8 ≤ \(B\) ≤ 12).

   * Box constraints: \(0\le u_b\le 1\).

   This is a bounded simplex in \(\mathbb{R}^B\).

4. **Projection onto the bounded simplex** – given an arbitrary \(\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^B\) (the candidate produced by the optimiser), apply a water‑filling algorithm:

   * While \(|\delta|\) is larger than a tiny tolerance, distribute \(\delta\) uniformly over all *free* coordinates (those not already at a bound), re‑clamp, and recompute the remaining deficit.  Because \(B\) is small, a few iterations suffice.
