**1. Mathematical reformulation**

*Variables* – Discretise the interval \([0,2]\) into \(N\) equidistant points (step \(dx=2/N\)).  
Let \(h\in\mathbb R^{N}\) be the vector of function values, with the constraints  

\[
0\le h_i\le 1,\qquad \sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h_i = N/2 .
\]

*Overlap for a shift* – For an integer shift \(k\in\{-N+1,\dots ,N-1\}\) define  

\[
F_k(h)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+k}\bigr) ,
\]

where out‑of‑range indices are ignored (the same as the “full” correlation used by the evaluator).  
The quantity to be minimised is  

\[
C_5(h)=dx\cdot\max_{k}F_k(h) .
\]

Because \(\sum_i h_i = N/2\) is constant,  

\[
F_k(h)=\frac N2-\sum_{i}h_i h_{i+k}.
\]

Thus minimising \(\max_k F_k\) is equivalent to **maximising the minimal quadratic self‑correlation**
\(\displaystyle S_k(h)=\sum_i h_i h_{i+k}\).

The problem is non‑convex (bilinear terms), but we can attack it with a *smooth surrogate* and a
*projected subgradient* method, followed by a targeted deterministic refinement.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

**2. Smooth surrogate for the max**

Introduce a temperature‑like parameter \(\beta>0\) and define  

\[
\Phi_\beta(h)=\frac1\beta\log\Bigl(\sum_{k}e^{\beta F_k(h)}\Bigr) .
\]

\(\Phi_\beta\) is a differentiable upper bound on \(\max_k F_k\); as \(\beta\to\infty\) it converges to the true max.
We will minimise \(\Phi_\beta\) for a sequence of increasing \(\beta\).

---------------------------------------------------------------------

**3. Gradient of the surrogate**

For a fixed shift \(k\) the gradient (with respect to the unscaled sum) is

\[
\frac{\partial F_k}{\partial h_i}=1-
\bigl(h_{i+k}+h_{i-k}\bigr) ,
\]

where the terms are omitted when the index lies outside \([0,N-1]\).

Let  

\[
w_k=\frac{e^{\beta F_k(h)}}{\sum_{j}e^{\beta F_j(h)}}\qquad(\;w_k\ge0,\; \sum_k w_k=1\;).
\]

The gradient of \(\Phi_\beta\) is then a **soft‑max weighted combination** of the individual gradients:

\[
\nabla\Phi_\beta(h)=\sum_k w_k\,
\bigl[\,\mathbf 1 - \bigl(h^{(+k)}+h^{(-k)}\bigr)\bigr],
\]

where \(h^{(+k)}\) is the vector \(h\) shifted forward by \(k\) (zero‑padded),
and similarly for \(h^{(-k)}\).  In practice the two shifted copies are obtained with simple slice assignments; the whole operation is vectorised and costs \(O(N^2)\) (the same order as a correlation).

**4. Projection onto the feasible set**

After a gradient step we obtain a tentative vector \(v\).  
We must project \(v\) onto

\[
\mathcal{P}=\bigl\{x\in\mathbb R^{N} : 0\le x_i\le1,\; \sum_i x_i = N/2\bigr\}.
\]

The Euclidean projection onto \(\mathcal{P}\) has a closed‑form KKT characterisation:

\[
x_i = \operatorname{clip}\bigl(v_i-\lambda,0,1\bigr),
\]

where the scalar \(\lambda\) is chosen so that \(\sum_i x_i = N/2\).
Because the left‑hand side is a monotone decreasing function of \(\lambda\), a simple
bisection (≈ 50 iterations) finds \(\lambda\) to machine precision.  
The whole projection is \(O(N\log\text{(tolerance)})\) and negligible compared with the gradient computation.

**6. Deterministic refinement (targeted mass transfer)**  

After the smoothing loop we have a vector that is already close to optimal but may still have a few “spikes” that dominate the max overlap.  
We therefore perform a **greedy local optimisation** that directly attacks the worst shift:

1. Compute the full correlation `C = np.correlate(h,1‑h,'full')`.
2. Determine the offset `d` attaining the maximum (`d = argmax(C) – (N‑1)`).
3. Extract the overlapping slices:
   * if `d≥0`:  `hi = h[:N‑d]`, `hj = h[d:]`.
   * else:      `hi = h[‑d:]`, `hj = h[:N‑(‑d)]`.
4. The contribution of each paired index is `c = hi·(1‑hj)`.  
   Choose the pair `(i*,j*)` where `c` is maximal.
5. Compute the feasible transfer amount  

   \[
   \Delta = \min\bigl(\,\alpha\cdot c,\;
                     h_{i*},\;1-h_{j*}\bigr),
   \]

   with a tiny scaling factor \(\alpha\in(0,1]\) (initially 1).  
   Move \(\Delta\) from the larger entry to the smaller one:
   `h[i*] -= Δ;  h[j*] += Δ`.
6. Re‑evaluate `C5`.  If it decreased, keep the change; otherwise revert and halve \(\alpha\).
7. Repeat until no improvement is observed for, say, 200 consecutive tries or the time budget for this phase expires.

Because each trial reduces the contribution of the current worst pair, the maximal overlap can only stay the same or go down, guaranteeing monotonic progress.
