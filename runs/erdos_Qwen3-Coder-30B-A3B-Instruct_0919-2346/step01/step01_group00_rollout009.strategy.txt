and minimises  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{τ\in\{-n+1,…,n-1\}} C_τ(h),\qquad
C_τ(h)=\sum_{i} h_i\,dx-\sum_{i} h_i\,h_{i+τ}\,dx .
\]

The plan below replaces the random‑pair simulated‑annealing of the reference code by a **deterministic, gradient‑based minimisation of a smooth surrogate of the max‑operator**, together with an exact projection onto the feasible polytope and a lightweight stochastic “jitter” stage to escape shallow local minima.

### 1. Analytic reformulation

Write the autocorrelation of `h` (zero‑padded outside the interval) as  

\[
S_τ(h)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i\,h_{i+τ}\;,\qquad τ=-(n-1),…,n-1 .
\]

Then  

\[
C_τ(h)=1-dx\;S_τ(h),\qquad 
C_5(h)=\max_τ C_τ(h)=1-\min_τ S_τ(h).
\]

Thus decreasing the worst overlap is equivalent to **raising the smallest autocorrelation value**.

---

### 2. Smooth approximation of the max

For a smoothing parameter `α>0` define  

\[
F_α(h)=\frac{1}{α}\log\Bigl(\sum_{τ} e^{α C_τ(h)}\Bigr).
\]

* `F_α` is differentiable for any finite `α`.  
* As `α→∞`, `F_α(h) → C_5(h)`.  
* For moderate `α` the function is a soft‑max, providing a well‑behaved gradient that points toward the most offending shift(s).

The weights of the soft‑max are  

\[
w_τ(h)=\frac{e^{α C_τ(h)}}{\sum_{σ}e^{α C_σ(h)}}\;,\qquad \sum_τ w_τ=1 .
\]

---

### 3. Closed‑form gradient

From the definition of `C_τ` we obtain  

\[
\frac{\partial C_τ}{\partial h_i}= -dx\bigl(h_{i+τ}+h_{i-τ}\bigr),
\]

with the convention `h_j=0` whenever `j` lies outside `[0,n-1]`.  
Consequently

\[
∇F_α(h)=\sum_τ w_τ\,∇C_τ
       =-dx\sum_τ w_τ\bigl(h_{·+τ}+h_{·-τ}\bigr).
\]

The two sums are discrete convolutions:

* `c⁺ = conv(w , h)`   (shift “+τ”),
* `c⁻ = conv(w_rev , h)`  (`w_rev[τ]=w_{-τ}` for the “‑τ” part).

The gradient vector is therefore  

\[
g = -dx\,(c⁺ + c⁻).
\]

A crucial property: `∑_i g_i = 0`.  Hence a pure gradient step does not change the total mass, and we only have to enforce the box constraints.

Both convolutions can be carried out rapidly with FFTs (`O(n log n)`) or with NumPy’s `convolve` (`O(n)` for `n≈150`).

### 4. Projection onto the feasible set

After a tentative update `h' = h - η g` we must enforce  

* `0 ≤ h'_i ≤ 1`,
* `∑_i h'_i = n/2`.

The projection solves

\[
\min_x \|x-h'\|_2^2 \quad
\text{s.t. } 0\le x_i\le 1,\;
\sum_i x_i = n/2 .
\]

The solution has the **water‑filling** form  

\[
x_i = \operatorname{clip}(h'_i-λ,0,1)
\]

for a scalar Lagrange multiplier `λ`.  The function  

\[
φ(λ)=\sum_i\operatorname{clip}(h'_i-λ,0,1)-n/2
\]

is monotone decreasing; a simple bisection (≈30 iterations) yields `λ` to machine precision.  The whole projection costs `O(n log n)` (the log factor comes from the bisection loops).
