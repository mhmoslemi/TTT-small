**1. Mathematical reformulation**

- The objective is  
  \[
  C_5(h)=\max_{k\in[-2,2]}\int_{0}^{2}h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx .
  \]  
- Because \(\int_{0}^{2}h(x)\,dx=1\), write the integrand as  
  \[
  h(x)-h(x)h(x+k)\;,
  \]  
  so that  
  \[
  C_5(h)=1-\min_{k}\int_{0}^{2}h(x)h(x+k)\,dx .
  \]  
  The term \(\rho(k)=\int h(x)h(x+k)dx\) is the (non‑periodic) autocorrelation of \(h\).  
- **Goal:** maximize the *minimum* autocorrelation \(\min_k\rho(k)\) under the linear constraints  
  \[
  0\le h(x)\le1,\qquad \int_{0}^{2}h(x)dx=1 .
  \]  
  In a discrete setting with \(N\) samples (\(dx=2/N\)) the constraints become  
  \[
  0\le h_i\le 1,\qquad \sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h_i=\frac{N}{2}.
  \]

**2. Smooth max–min surrogate**

- The nonsmooth “minimum over lags’’ can be replaced by a differentiable surrogate using the Log‑Sum‑Exp (LSE) trick:
  \[
  \Phi_\beta(h)= -\frac1\beta\!\log\!\Bigl(\sum_{k}e^{-\beta\rho(k)}\Bigr) .
  \]  
  When \(\beta\to\infty\), \(\Phi_\beta\) converges to \(-\min_k\rho(k)\) and therefore
  \[
  C_5(h)=1+\Phi_{\infty}(h).
  \]  
- For a finite \(\beta\) the gradient is simple:
  \[
  \frac{\partial\Phi_\beta}{\partial h_i}= -\sum_{k}w_k\,\frac{\partial\rho(k)}{\partial h_i},
  \quad
  w_k=\frac{e^{-\beta\rho(k)}}{\sum_{j}e^{-\beta\rho(j)}} .
  \]  
  Since  
  \[
  \frac{\partial\rho(k)}{\partial h_i}=dx\bigl(h_{i+k}+h_{i-k}\bigr)
  \]  
  (indices outside \([0,N-1]\) are ignored), the gradient can be written as a *weighted* convolution of the current profile with the weight vector \(\{w_k\}\).

**3. Projection onto the feasible set**

- The Euclidean projection onto this set is obtained by a 1‑dimensional bisection on a Lagrange multiplier \(\lambda\):
  \[
  \Pi(v)=\operatorname{clip}(v-\lambda,0,1),\qquad 
  \sum_i\Pi(v)_i=\frac{N}{2}.

- The monotonicity of the sum w.r.t. \(\lambda\) guarantees fast convergence (<60 iterations). The projection is performed after every gradient or sub‑gradient step.
