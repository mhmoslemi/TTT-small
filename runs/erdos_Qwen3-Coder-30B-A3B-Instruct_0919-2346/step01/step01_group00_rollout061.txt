### Mathematical Insight and Reformulation
The problem is to minimize the maximum overlap integral:
$$
C_5 = \max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1 - h(x+k)) dx
$$
subject to:
- $h(x) \in [0, 1]$ for all $x$
- $\int_0^2 h(x) dx = 1$.

This is an optimization problem in function space, but it is discretized into a finite-dimensional vector optimization problem:
$$
\text{Minimize } C_5 = \max_k \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h[i](1 - h[i+k]) \cdot dx
$$
where the sum runs over all valid indices of convolution with a shifted version of $1-h$. 

### Structuring the Optimization Approach
To approach this optimally, we should:
1. **Model as a Convex Optimization Problem**: The objective is not convex over $h$, but its discrete version can utilize reformulations that improve convergence.
2. **Start with Good Initial Guesses**:
   - Use `initial_h_values` from the global scope.
   - Try variants such as step functions with multiple jumps.
3. **Use Better Optimization Techniques**:
   - Simulated Annealing can work, but it is highly sensitive to parameters and may miss global optima.
   - A more inspired approach is Gradient Descent with Projections or Quadratic Programming (QP)-inspired methods.

### Investigate Key Structural Properties
- Since the integral is overlapping, we want $h(x)$ to be high where $1-h(x+k)$ is also high — suggesting rising and falling edges need to be optimized carefully.
- A reasonable constant function like $h(x)=0.5$ yields $C_5=0.25$, which is too low, because the overlap is large due to uniformity.
- We want high values to be "grouped" around certain segments — avoiding the large overlap at larger slices. So, we can consider creating a piecewise constant h with abrupt changes.

- Each $h[i] \in [0, 1]$

During optimization, when adjusting $h[i]$, we’ll keep the total sum fixed through balancing adjustment between indices.
