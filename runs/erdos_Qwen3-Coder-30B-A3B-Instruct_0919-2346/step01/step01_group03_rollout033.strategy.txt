### 1.  Geometry of the objective

For a fixed shift *m*  

\[
f_m(h)=\sum_i h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+m}\bigr)
      =\underbrace{\sum_i h_i}_{\text{constant}=N/2}
       \;-\;\sum_i h_i\,h_{i+m}\ .
\]

Hence  

*minimising* `max_m f_m`  ⇔  *maximising* `min_m  ∑_i h_i h_{i+m}`.  

The only non‑linear part is the **bilinear overlap**  ∑ h_i h_{i+m}.  Because the linear term ∑ h_i is fixed, the gradient of a single shift *m* is

\[
\frac{\partial f_m}{\partial h_i}=1-\bigl(h_{i+m}+h_{i-m}\bigr) ,
\]
where out‑of‑range indices are omitted.  This simple closed form enables fast ( O(N) ) gradient computation for any shift.

The global objective is a **maximum** over shifts, which is non‑differentiable.  Two standard smooth surrogates are used:

* **Sub‑gradient of the true max** – pick the worst shift *m\*** and use its gradient.
* **Log‑Sum‑Exp (LSE) smoothing** –  
  \[
  L_\beta(h)=\frac{1}{\beta}\log\!\Bigl(\sum_m e^{\beta f_m(h)}\Bigr) ,
  \]
  whose gradient is a weighted average of the gradients of all shifts with weights  
  \(w_m=\frac{e^{\beta f_m}}{\sum_k e^{\beta f_k}}\).

Both surrogates give a descent direction that directly reduces the worst overlap.

### 2.  Projection onto the feasible set

Every update must keep the vector inside  

\[
\mathcal{F}=\{\,h\in[0,1]^N\mid\sum_i h_i=N/2\,\}.
\]

The Euclidean projection onto 𝔽 has a closed‑form KKT characterisation:

\[
h_i^\star=\operatorname{clip}\bigl(v_i-\lambda,0,1\bigr),
\qquad
\lambda\ \text{chosen so that}\ \sum_i h_i^\star=N/2 .
\]

Because the left‑hand side of the sum is monotone decreasing in λ, λ is found by a simple bisection (≈ 30 iterations) on the interval \([ \min(v)-1,\ \max(v)+1 ]\).  This projection is **exact**, cheap ( O(N) ), and guarantees feasibility after every step.

### 5.  Multi‑phase optimisation pipeline (per seed)

#### Phase A – **Projected Sub‑gradient Descent on the worst shift**
*Iterations*: ~ 2000 (or “as many as time allows”).

   1. Compute the full overlap vector `f` and locate the worst shift *m\***.

Because only the worst shift is used, this phase aggressively flattens the dominant peak, driving the max overlap down quickly.

#### Phase B – **Log‑Sum‑Exp (LSE) Smoothing**
After the sub‑gradient phase the objective surface becomes relatively flat, making pure max‑gradient noisy.  We switch to the smooth surrogate:

*Parameters*: start with β=2, increase multiplicatively (β←β·1.5) every 300 iterations until β≈30.  
*Iterations*: ~ 1500.  
*Step*: αₜ proportional to 1/√t (e.g. αₜ = α₀/√(t+1) with α₀≈0.4).  
*Iteration*:
   1. Compute the overlap vector `f` and the softmax weights `w_m`.

   3. Update, project, evaluate as in Phase A.  

Increasing β slowly guarantees that the iterates stay in a region where the surrogate closely mirrors the true max, while avoiding abrupt jumps.

#### Phase C – **Targeted Hill‑Climbing on the worst contribution**
When the smoothed descent stalls, we switch to a deterministic, *pair‑wise* improvement that directly attacks the dominant term of the worst shift:

*Loop* until time expires:
   1. Identify the worst shift *m\*** and the overlapping index pair \((i,j=i+m\*)\) that maximises the local contribution  
      \(\;c_{i}=h_i\bigl(1-h_j\bigr)\).

      \(\Delta = \min\bigl(h_i,\;1-h_j,\;\text{step\_size}\bigr)\).

   4. If \(C_5(h^{\text{new}}) < C_5(h)\) accept; otherwise try the *reverse* transfer (j → i) and, if still no improvement, halve `step_size`.

Because the move preserves the total sum, no extra projection is needed; only box limits are checked.

#### Phase D – **Final polishing**
With the remaining seconds:
   * Use a very small step (≤ 1e‑3) and repeat the pair‑wise move of Phase C, accepting any improvement.  
   * Occasionally perform a *global* random simplex re‑sample and keep it if it beats the current best.  
   * Keep the best vector encountered across *all* phases.
