### 2.  Low‑frequency Fourier representation  

Because the average value of `h` must be `½`, we write  

\[
h_i \;=\; \frac{1}{2}\;+\;\sum_{m=1}^{M} a_m\,
          \cos\!\Bigl(\frac{2\pi m i}{n}\Bigr)\;,
\qquad i=0,\dots,n-1 .
\]

*Only cosine terms are needed* – the sine coefficients are set to zero
so that the representation is automatically real and symmetric.  
*The constant term `½` guarantees the exact mean* (all cosine terms have zero mean).  

The vector of optimisation variables is  

\[
\mathbf{a}= (a_1,\dots ,a_M)\in\mathbb{R}^M .
\]

`M` will be increased progressively (see §6).  For `M≈20` the dimension is

### 3.  Enforcing the box constraints  

The cosine series can overshoot the interval `[0,1]`.  Two mechanisms keep the
iterates inside the admissible region:

1. **Soft penalisation** – add to the objective a large penalty term  

   \[
   P(\mathbf a)=\lambda\!\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}
        \bigl(\max\{0,h_i-1\}\bigr)^2
        +\lambda\!\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}
        \bigl(\max\{0,-h_i\}\bigr)^2 ,
   \]  

   with a constant `λ` (e.g. `λ = 10^4`).  This term is differentiable
   almost everywhere and strongly pushes any out‑of‑bounds entry back
   into `[0,1]`.

2. **Final projection** – after the spectral optimiser finishes, the
   obtained `h` is *exactly* projected onto the intersection of the box
   and the hyperplane `∑h_i = n/2`.  The projection is performed by a
   water‑filling (bisection on a scalar `τ`) algorithm:
   ```
   x_i = clip( h_i - τ , 0 , 1 )
   choose τ so that Σ x_i = n/2 .
   ```
   This step guarantees feasibility for the reported solution.

Because the penalty is smooth, the optimiser can use gradient information;
the projection is only applied once at the end.

### 4.  Smooth surrogate for the max‑overlap  

The exact objective `C_5(h)` contains a non‑smooth `max` over all integer
shifts `s∈{-(n‑1),…,n‑1}`.  To obtain a differentiable surrogate we use a
soft‑max with temperature `γ>0`:

\[
\Phi_\gamma(\mathbf a)=\frac{1}{\gamma}
\log\Bigl(\sum_{s} \exp\bigl(\gamma\,f_s(\mathbf a)\bigr)\Bigr)
\;+\;P(\mathbf a) .
\]

Here `f_s(·)` is the *overlap* for shift `s` computed from the current `h`
(see §5).  As `γ` grows, `Φ_γ` approaches the true `C_5`.  During optimisation
`γ` will be increased gradually (homotopy) so the early iterations see a
smooth landscape and later iterations focus on the worst shift.

### 5.  Computing the shift overlaps and their gradients  

**Overlap for a shift `s`** (zero‑based indices)  

\[
f_s(\mathbf a)=dx\!\sum_{i:\,0\le i,i+s<n}
               h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+s}\bigr) .
\]

*Computation*:  
* Build `h` from the current coefficients (`O(nM)`).  
* Use `numpy.correlate` (or a direct convolution) to obtain the vector  
  `c = correlate( h , 1‑h , mode='full' )`.  
* Multiply by `dx`; the entry with index `s+(n‑1)` is exactly `f_s`.

**Gradient w.r.t. a coefficient `a_m`**  

Because  

\[
\frac{\partial h_i}{\partial a_m}= \cos\!\Bigl(\frac{2\pi m i}{n}\Bigr),
\]

the chain rule gives  

\[
\frac{\partial f_s}{\partial a_m}
=dx\!\sum_{i\in O_s}
   \cos\!\Bigl(\frac{2\pi m i}{n}\Bigr)\bigl(1-h_{i+s}\bigr)
 \;-\;
dx\!\sum_{i\in O_s}
   h_i\;\cos\!\Bigl(\frac{2\pi m (i+s)}{n}\Bigr).
\]

Both sums are *convolutions* of the cosine vector with `h` or `1‑h`.  Using the
FFT (or a simple `np.dot` with pre‑computed cosine matrices) these
gradients are obtained in `O(n log n)` time.

The gradient of the soft‑max term follows the usual weighted‑average rule:

\[
\frac{\partial \Phi_\gamma}{\partial a_m}
    =\sum_{s} w_s \,\frac{\partial f_s}{\partial a_m},
\qquad
w_s = \frac{e^{\gamma f_s}}{\sum_k e^{\gamma f_k}} .
\]

The penalty gradient is trivial:
\[
\frac{\partial P}{\partial a_m}
   =2\lambda\!\sum_{i}\!
        \bigl(h_i-{\rm clip}(h_i,0,1)\bigr)\,
        \cos\!\Bigl(\frac{2\pi m i}{n}\Bigr) .
\]

Thus a **full gradient vector** of length `M` is assembled at each iteration.
