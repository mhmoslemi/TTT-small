### 2.  Smooth‑max approximation of the objective  

The max can be approximated by a log‑sum‑exp (soft‑max):

\[
L_\beta(h)=\frac{1}{\beta}\,\log\!\Bigl(\sum_{m}e^{\beta f_m(h)}\Bigr),
\qquad 
f_m(h)=\sum_{i}h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+m}\bigr)
\]

where the index `i+m` is taken only when the shift lies inside `[0,N‑1]`.  
For any `β>0`, `L_β` is smooth, convex‑like and satisfies  

\[
\max_m f_m \;\le\; L_\beta \;\le\; \max_m f_m +\frac{\log(2N-1)}{\beta}.
\]

By slowly increasing `β` we tighten the bound while keeping the gradient well‑behaved.

* **Gradient of `L_β`**  
  Let `w_m = exp(β f_m)/∑_k exp(β f_k)` (soft‑max weights).  
  The derivative of each `f_m` w.r.t. `h_i` is  
  \[
  \frac{\partial f_m}{\partial h_i}=1-\bigl(h_{i+m}+h_{i-m}\bigr)

  \[
