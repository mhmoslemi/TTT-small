\(h[0],\dots ,h[N-1]\in[0,1]\) with \(\sum_i h_i = N/2\) that minimises  

\[
C_5=\max_{k}\int_0^{2}h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k\,dx)\bigr)dx  
   = 1-\min_{k}\Gamma(k),
\qquad 
\Gamma(k)=\int_0^{2}h(x)h(x+k\,dx)dx .
\]

Hence we must **maximise the minimal auto‑correlation** \(\Gamma_{\min}=\min_k\Gamma(k)\).

The approach below does **not** rely on pairwise random moves or simulated annealing.  
It formulates the problem as a **convex semidefinite programme (SDP) relaxation**, extracts a feasible vector by randomised Gaussian rounding, and finally refines it by a deterministic projected‑gradient ascent on the original non‑convex objective.

### 2. Quadratic formulation with a rank‑1 matrix

Introduce the symmetric matrix  

\[
X = hh^{\top}\,,\qquad X_{ij}=h_i h_j .
\]

Because each entry of \(h\) lies in \([0,1]\), the diagonal of \(X\) satisfies  

\[
0\le X_{ii}\le 1,\qquad\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}X_{ii}= \frac{N}{2}.
\]

For a shift \(k\;(0\le k\le N-1)\) define the **shift matrix** \(S_k\in\{0,1\}^{N\times N}\) by  

\[
(S_k)_{ij}= 
\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if }j=i+k\text{ and }0\le i,i+k<N,\\[2pt]
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Because \(X\) is symmetric, \(\operatorname{tr}(S_kX)=\operatorname{tr}(S_k^{\top}X)=
\sum_{i} h_i h_{i+k} =\Gamma(k)\) (the discrete version of the auto‑correlation).

Thus the original max‑min problem can be written as  

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{h\in[0,1]^N}\;\;&\min_{k}\;\operatorname{tr}(S_k\,hh^{\top})\\[2pt]
\text{s.t.}\;&\sum_i h_i = \frac{N}{2}.
\end{aligned}

### 3. Convex SDP relaxation

Replace the rank‑1 matrix \(X=hh^{\top}\) by a general PSD matrix and drop the rank‑1 constraint:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{X\succeq 0,\;t}\;& t\\[2pt]
\text{s.t. }&\operatorname{tr}(S_k X)\ge t,\qquad k=0,\dots ,N-1,\\[2pt]
            &0\le X_{ii}\le 1,\qquad i=0,\dots ,N-1,\\[2pt]
            &\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}X_{ii}= \frac{N}{2},\\[2pt]
            &X_{ij}\ge 0\;\;(i\neq j) .
\end{aligned}
\]

All constraints are linear in the variable matrix \(X\) and the scalar \(t\); the only convex constraint is \(X\succeq0\).  
The objective is linear, so the whole programme is a **convex SDP**.

*Why this helps* – The SDP simultaneously enforces a lower bound \(t\) on every shift‑correlation while respecting the box constraints on the diagonal (the actual values of \(h\)).  Solving it yields the **largest possible** lower bound on \(\Gamma_{\min}\) that any feasible vector can achieve *under the relaxation*.

#### 5.1. Spectral rounding (deterministic)

After clipping, the sum \(\sum_i h_i\) may differ from the required target \(N/2\).  Perform a **projection onto the intersection of the box \([0,1]^N\) and the affine hyperplane**:

```

    if free is empty: break
    delta = excess / len(free)
```
