**1. Goal Reformulation**

The objective to be minimized is  

\[
C_5=\max_{k}\int_{0}^{2}h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx .
\]

Because \(\int_0^2 h(x)\,dx = 1\), the integral can be rewritten as  

\[
C_5 = 1-\min_{k}\int_{0}^{2}h(x)h(x+k)\,dx .
\]

Thus the problem is equivalent to **maximising the smallest circular auto‑correlation** of \(h\).  
Discretising with \(n\) equally spaced points (\(dx=2/n\)) and using periodic indexing gives  

\[
A_k=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i\,h_{(i+k)\bmod n},\qquad
C_5 = 1-\frac{2}{n}\,\min_{k}A_k .
\]

The constraints become  

* \(0\le h_i\le 1\) for all \(i\),
* \(\sum_i h_i = n/2\) (exactly one half of the total “mass”).

Hence we need a vector \(h\in[0,1]^n\) with fixed \(\ell_1\) norm that **maximises the worst‑case circular dot‑product** with its shifts.

**3. Fast Periodic Correlation**

Circular correlation is obtained with the FFT:

1. Pad \(h\) to length \(n\) (already the case).  
2. Compute \(\hat h = \text{FFT}(h)\).  
3. \(A = \text{IFFT}\bigl(\hat h\cdot\overline{\hat h}\bigr)\) – the result is a complex array whose real part contains the auto‑correlations for all shifts \(k=0,\dots ,n-1\).  
4. Keep the real part, round tiny imaginary leftovers, and obtain the vector `Acorr`.

The minimal correlation is `min_A = Acorr.min()`.  
The bound is then  
\[
C_5 = 1-\frac{2\,\text{min\_A}}{n}.
\]

All of the above is vectorised and runs in < 1 ms for \(n\le150\).
