**Goal**  
Find a step function `h` on `[0,2]` (discretized into `n` equally spaced points) with values in `[0,1]` and integral = 1 that minimizes  

\[
C_5=\max_{k}\int_0^2 h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)\,dx
      =\frac{2}{n}\;\max_{\ell\in[-(n-1),\;n-1]} 
        \sum_{i} h_i\bigl(1-h_{i+\ell}\bigr),
\]

where out‑of‑range indices are treated as zero (the same as `np.correlate(...,mode='full')`).  
A lower value of `C_5` yields a tighter upper bound on the Erdős constant.

**Key observations**

1. For binary vectors (`h_i∈{0,1}`) the objective simplifies to  
   \[
   s_\ell = \#\{i\mid h_i=1,\;h_{i+\ell}=0\},
   \qquad
   C_5 = \frac{2}{n}\max_\ell s_\ell .
   \]
   Minimising `max s_ℓ` is equivalent to *maximising the minimal number of overlapping
   1‑entries* for all shifts.  

2. The problem is non‑convex (quadratic terms `h_i h_{i+ℓ}`), so global optimisation is
   hard.  A pragmatic approach is a **two‑phase heuristic**:
   - **Phase A:** Search over the binary space, because the optimal solution is
     believed to lie on the vertices of the feasible hyper‑cube.
   - **Phase B:** Starting from the best binary pattern, perform a fine‑grained continuous
     optimisation allowing values in `(0,1)` to reduce the objective further.

3. The correlation `s = np.correlate(h,1-h,'full')` costs `O(n²)` but is extremely fast
   in C; for `n≈200` a single evaluation takes ∼ 10⁻⁴ s, so many thousands of
   iterations are affordable.
