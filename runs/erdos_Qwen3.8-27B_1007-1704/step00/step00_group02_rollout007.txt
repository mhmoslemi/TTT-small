Let me also add a "hill climbing" step that's more efficient: instead of checking all pairs, I can compute the gradient of the max and do gradient descent.

For the max: let k* be the argmax lag. Then the gradient of f_{k*}(h) = sum_i h[i]*(1-h[i+k*])*dx with respect to h[i] is:
- For h[i]: (1 - h[i+k*]) * dx (approximately, for the terms where i is valid)
- For h[i+k*]: -h[i] * dx

But the max is non-differentiable. With the log-sum-exp, the gradient is a weighted average of all lag gradients.

Actually, for the log-sum-exp objective:
f(h) = (1/alpha) * log(sum_k exp(alpha * c_k(h)))
where c_k(h) = sum_i h[i]*(1-h[i+k])*dx

df/dh[m] = (1/alpha) * sum_k exp(alpha*c_k) * (dc_k/dh[m]) / (sum_k exp(alpha*c_k))
         = weighted_average_of_k (dc_k/dh[m])

where dc_k/dh[m] involves:
- Terms where m appears in h[i]: (1-h[m+k])*dx
- Terms where m appears in h[i+k] (i.e., i = m-k): -h[m-k]*dx
