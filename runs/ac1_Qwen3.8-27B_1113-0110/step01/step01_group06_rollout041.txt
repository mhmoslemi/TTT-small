**Let me also consider a different angle: the problem is equivalent to minimizing the peak sidelobe level of the autocorrelation.**

In radar/communications, the PSL minimization problem is well-studied. The standard approaches are:
1. **Weinberger algorithm** (1971): Iteratively reduce the peak by adjusting the sequence
2. **Shao-Ghosh algorithm**: Uses a complex-valued gradient
3. **PSO (Particle Swarm Optimization)**
4. **Genetic algorithms**

But all of these typically work with complex sequences. For real non-negative sequences, the problem is different.

**Key insight for non-negative sequences:**

Since `a >= 0`, the autocorrelation `b_k = sum_i a_i a_{k-i}` is the coefficient of `x^k` in `(sum a_i x^i)^2 = P(x)^2` where `P(x) = sum a_i x^i`.

We want to minimize `max_k [x^k] P(x)^2`.

If `P(x)` is a "flat" polynomial (all coefficients close), then `P(x)^2` has a triangular shape (like the autocorrelation of a rectangle). The peak is at the center.

If `P(x)` is "spiky" (concentrated at a few points), then `P(x)^2` is also spiky.

The optimal trade-off is somewhere in between.

`b_k (linear) = r_k (circular) + r_{k-n} (circular)` (approximately, for the right range)

Actually, the exact relationship is more complex. But for `n` large and the sequence "well-spread", the circular and linear autocorrelations are similar.
