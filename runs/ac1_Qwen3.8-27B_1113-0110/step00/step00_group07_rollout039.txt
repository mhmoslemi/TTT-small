Let me think about the smooth max more carefully. We want to minimize `f(a) = 2n * max(b) / sum(a)^2`. Since the problem is scale-invariant, we can normalize `sum(a) = 1` and just minimize `max(b)`.

The smooth approximation: `L_beta(b) = (1/beta) * log(sum_k exp(beta * b_k))`.

As `beta -> infinity`, `L_beta(b) -> max(b)`. For finite beta, it's a smooth upper bound on max(b).

The gradient of `L_beta` with respect to `a[m]` is:
`dL_beta/da[m] = (1/beta) * sum_k (beta * exp(beta*b_k) / sum_j exp(beta*b_j)) * db_k/da[m]`
`= sum_k p_k * (a[k-m] + a[m-k])`

where `p_k = exp(beta*b_k) / sum_j exp(beta*b_j)` (softmax of beta*b).

So the gradient involves computing `p` (a probability distribution over the 2n-1 convolution outputs) and then computing the correlation/convolution with `a`.

Actually, for the linear convolution, the frequency domain approach is more complex. Let me think about it differently.

If we extend `a` to a periodic sequence of period P (where P is large enough to avoid wrap-around, e.g., P >= 2n-1), then the circular convolution of the periodic extension equals the linear convolution. And the circular convolution in the frequency domain is `|A_k|^2` (product of DFT with its conjugate).

So we want `|A_k|^2` to be "flat" for all k. But `A_k` is the DFT of a non-negative sequence, so `A_0 = sum(a) > 0` is fixed (real and positive). The other `A_k` can be complex.
