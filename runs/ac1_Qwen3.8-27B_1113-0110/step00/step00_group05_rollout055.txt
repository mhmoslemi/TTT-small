The question is: what probability distribution p minimizes the peak of its self-convolution?

This is related to the "minimum peak walk" or "minimum peak sidelobe" problem in radar/signal processing.

One approach: think of p as a "probability distribution" and we want its "autocorrelation" (which is the convolution, since p is not necessarily symmetric) to have a low peak.

For a symmetric distribution (p[i] = p[n-1-i]), the convolution is symmetric and the peak is at the center:
conv(p,p)[n-1] = sum_i p[i]^2

For non-symmetric, the peak might be elsewhere.

The minimum of sum p[i]^2 subject to sum p[i] = 1 and p[i] >= 0 is 1/n (achieved by uniform). So for symmetric distributions, the center peak is at least 1/n.

But for non-symmetric distributions, the peak might not be at the center. The peak at the center is sum p[i]*p[n-1-i], which can be less than sum p[i]^2.

For example, if p is "front-loaded" (large values at the start), then:
- conv[0] = p[0]^2 (could be large)
- conv[n-1] = sum p[i]*p[n-1-i] (could be small if p[0] is large and p[n-1] is small)

So the trade-off is: making the sequence asymmetric reduces the center peak but might increase edge peaks.

For the optimal solution, we want to balance all the peaks.

This is essentially a min-max problem. Let me formalize:
minimize M
subject to: conv(p,p)[k] <= M for all k = 0, ..., 2n-2
           sum p[i] = 1
           p[i] >= 0
