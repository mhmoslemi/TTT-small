The GA can escape local optima by maintaining a diverse population of candidate solutions and applying crossover and mutation operations.

### 1. Approach and rationale
The core idea is to use a genetic algorithm to explore the solution space, guided by fitness evaluations of the overlap integral. The algorithm:

- Maintains a population of candidate step functions
- Evolves solutions through crossover and mutation
- Retains good solutions via selection
- Assesses fitness based on overlap integral \( C_5 \)
- Operates within the problem constraints while exploring diverse solutions

This approach can overcome limitations of gradient-based methods like getting trapped in local optima. The genetic operations enable searching the solution space more thoroughly without assuming smoothness or convexity.

### 2. Mathematical specification
- **Step function**: \( h: [0, 2] \rightarrow [0, 1] \), discretized into \( n_{points} \) values
- **Objective**: Minimize \( C_5 = \max_k \sum_{i=0}^{n_{points}-1} h[i](1 - h[i + k \mod n_{points}]) \times dx \)
- **Constraints**:
  1. \( 0 \leq h[i] \leq 1 \) for all \( i \)
  2. \( \sum_{i=0}^{n_{points}-1} h[i] = \frac{n_{points}}{2} \)
- **Crossover operator**: Combines two parent solutions into offspring
- **Mutation operator**: Introduces small random variations
- **Selection operator**: Propagates fittest solutions to next generation

### 3. Implementation procedure
1. **Initialization**:
   - Set population size \( pop\_size = 50 \)
   - If `initial_h_values` exists, use it as one individual
   - Generate remaining individuals randomly, ensuring \( h[i] \in [0, 1] \) and \( \sum h = n_{points}/2 \)

2. **Fitness evaluation**:
   - For each individual, compute:
     - \( correlation = \text{np.correlate}(h, 1 - h, \text{mode='full'}) \)
     - \( C_5 = \max(correlation) * dx \)
   - Track best individual across evaluations

3. **Population evolution**:
   - For each generation until time budget exhausted:
     - Compute fitness for all individuals
     - Select top 40% fittest individuals
     - Generate new offspring via crossover (50% probability per parent pair)
         - Crossover:_blend nearby points from both parents
         - Ensure constraints maintained
     - Apply mutation to 20% of population:
         - Mutate 5% of points in each mutated individual
         - Clip to [0, 1] and rescale to maintain sum
     - Combine parents and offspring into new population

4. **Termination**:
   - Stop when time budget exhausted or no improvement in 100 generations
   - Return best individual found

### 4. Search choices and useful variations
- **Population size**: 50 individuals balances diversity and computational load
- **Crossover rate**: 50% balances exploration/exploitation
- **Mutation rate**: 20% maintains diversity while respecting constraints
- **Mutation intensity**: 5% of points mutated per individual
- **Selection pressure**: Top 40% ensures strong propagation of good solutions
- **Elitism**: Always retain best individual to prevent loss

### 5. Compute budget and robustness
- Each generation takes ~0.5 seconds for 50 individuals
- Can process ~200 generations in 100 seconds
- Mutation and crossover operations are O(n_points)
- Fitness evaluation is O(n_points^2) per individual
- Robustness:
  - Mutation ensures population diversity
  - Crossover preserves important features
  - Elitism maintains best solution
  - Constraint handling ensures feasibility

### 6. Validation and return contract

- Re-validate best individual before returning
