The key innovation is the use of **space-filling curves** to map the high-dimensional domain into a lower-dimensional space where optimization is more tractable. This approach promotes smoother convergence and reduces the risk of getting trapped in local minima.

Additionally, we incorporate a **multi-level strategy** to enforce constraints naturally:
1. **Log-sum constraints** for likelihood-based proximity checks
2. **Exponential weighting** to handle the integral constraint softly
3. **Phase transitions** between vague and crisp formulations

These elements combined create a balanced optimization landscape that naturally avoids premature convergence while maintaining feasibility. The expected result is a solution with a persistent optimum that generalizes across different resolutions.

### Mathematical Specification

#### Variables
Let `n` be an even integer defining the discretization of the interval `[0, 2]`. Our variables are the piecewise-constant function `h: [0, 2] → [0, 1]` represented as a vector `h ∈ ℝⁿ` with:

- `h[i] ∈ [0, 1]` for all `i`
- ∑`i` `h[i]` = `n/2`

The optimization problem is defined over the domain:

```
Ω = {h | 0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1 for all i, ∑h[i] = n/2}
```

#### Objective Function
The primary objective is to **minimize** the maximum correlation energy at any lag:

```
J(h) = (1/dx) * max_k ∫[0,2] h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx
```

But note: the continuous integral is approximated in the discrete setting by the full correlation followed by scaling by `dx = 2.0/n`.

#### Operations
The optimization proceeds in three intertwined phases:

1. **Successive Convexification**
   - Initial approximation via geometric programming
   - Progressive strengthening of the constraints (walls)

2. **Bayesian Optimization**
   - Model-based exploration of the design space

3. **Constraint-Immune Search**
   - Exponential softening of constraints

   where `gᵢ` are Lagrange multipliers
