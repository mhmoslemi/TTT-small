**Approach: Direct optimization of the smooth max**

The smooth max (log-sum-exp) approximation:
f(h) = (1/β) * log(sum_k exp(β * c_k(h)))

This is smooth and we can compute its gradient:
∇f(h) = (1/β) * sum_k exp(β * c_k(h)) * ∇c_k(h) / sum_k exp(β * c_k(h))
= sum_k p_k * ∇c_k(h) where p_k = exp(β * c_k(h)) / sum_k exp(β * c_k(h))

So the gradient is a weighted average of the gradients of the individual correlations, with weights proportional to their "temperature" values.

For large β, this concentrates on the worst lags, approximating the true max.

The gradient of c_k(h) = sum_i h[i+k]*(1-h[i]) w.r.t. h[j]:

where "valid" means the index is in [0, n-1] and the pair (i, i+k) is in the sum.
