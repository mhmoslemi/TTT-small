1. **Approach and Rationale**
   The central idea is to combine multinomial optimization with a genetic algorithm to explore the solution space more effectively than the gradient-based methods used previously. We will represent the function `h` as a sum of indicator functions, enabling the use of combinatorial search within continuous optimization. This approach addresses the high-dimensional, non-convex nature of the problem by leveraging genetic operations (crossover, mutation) on populations of candidate solutions. The key departure is the representation change: instead of treating `h` as a continuous function, we represent it as a stochastic selection process. This allows us to capture sharp transitions in `h` while maintaining control over the integral constraint.

   The algorithm introduces:
   - A novel population-based search where individuals are vectors of probabilities
   - A combinatorial crossover operation adapted for continuous spaces
   - Biased mutation towards regions of known lower correlations
   - Maintains rigorous bounds enforcement without clipping artifacts

   Limitations include increased computation due to population-based search, but the method's robustness to local optima is expected to yield significant improvements focusing on exploring the combinatorial structure of optimal solutions.

2. **Mathematical Specification**
