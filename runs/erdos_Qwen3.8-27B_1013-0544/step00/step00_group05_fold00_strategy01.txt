1. Approach and rationale
We use a hybrid optimization technique combining evolutionary search with local refinement to minimize the minimum overlap (Erds constant) for a step function on the interval [0,2]. The key idea is to represent the function with n_point pieces and evolve populations of candidate solutions, leveraging gradient information only in later stages to improve convergence. This approach balances exploration (finding potentially better solutions in the search space) and exploitation (refining around promising areas to escape local optima).

The central problem is non-convex and difficult due to:
- The max operation in the objective function creating non-smooth regions
- The linear equality constraint that complicates optimization
- High dimensionality increasing the risk of getting stuck in local optima
- The need for feasible solutions (integral=1, 0<=x<=1)

Our approach addresses these by:
- Using an evolutionary framework to explore the space efficiently
- Incorporating repair mechanisms to maintain feasibility
- Adapting the search strategy based on progress using time budgets
- Starting with coarse piece counts to handle the high-dimensional space gracefully

- Dynamic repair handling directly within evaluations
- Early termination support for time-constrained runs
- Coarse-to-fine resolution transfer capability
- Better separation of evaluation, repair, and search logic
