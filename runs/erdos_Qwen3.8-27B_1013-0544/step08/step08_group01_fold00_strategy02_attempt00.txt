<h1>Approach and Rationale</h1>

We will develop an algorithm rooted in **harmonic analysis** and **stochastic optimization**, specifically designed to find a periodic solution with suppressed maximum overlap. Our method systematically explores the space of periodic functions with period 1 (since the domain is [0,2], period 1 means two full periods) using the following intuition:

1. The Erdős discrepancy problem suggests that periodic functions might minimize overlap due to their repetitive structure.
2. We expect the optimal solution to have symmetry around x=1.
3. By constraining the function to be periodic, we reduce dimensionality while potentially improving the minimum overlap value.

Our algorithm will be:  
- **Non-parametric**: We work directly with discrete samples  
- **Hybrid global/local**: Combines global search with local refinement  
- **Adaptive dimensionality**: Starts with coarse representations then iterates over different resolutions  
- **Asymptotic properties-focused**: Minimizes maximum correlation in selected lags  

The key challenges this approach addresses:  
- Escapes local optima by maintaining multiple search trajectories  
- Balances exploration/exploitation with adaptive neighborhood selection  
- Handles the discrete space efficiently through stochastic comparison  
- Maintains feasibility through projection to the integral constraint space

Potential limitations:  
- Performance depends on solution quality/stability  
- May not find global optimum due to trade-offs between dimensions  
- Requires scalability checks for different resolutions  
- Convergence depends on neighborhood temperature coupled with data structure properties

<h1>Mathematical Specification</h1>

Our goal is to minimize:
$$ C_5 = \frac{2}{n} \max_{k} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h[i] \cdot (1 - h[i+k \mod n]) $$

Constraints:
