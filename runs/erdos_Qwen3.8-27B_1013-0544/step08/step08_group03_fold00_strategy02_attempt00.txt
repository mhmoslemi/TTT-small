By exploring multiple harmonic complexities (M), we effectively sample the solution space systematically. The key innovation is to optimize the harmonic spectra's outward and inward convergence rates, breaking symmetry and enabling a multi-resolution search that avoids getting trapped in the local optima that plague gradient-based methods. This differs significantly from earlier step-function or sine-based representations, which target either discontinuity points or fixed frequencies.

The primary bottleneck addressed is the rapid convergence of local search methods. By sampling harmonic frequencies up to high values (M), we ensure a broad global search before committing to detailed optimization. We then use targeted refinement at scale factors larger than 1 to improve local descriptors. Spectral separation learning progressively uncovers aspects of the solution hinted by discrepancies in different frequency regimes.

Mathematical specification:
We represent the function through its Fourier coefficients up to an adjustable frequency M:
  h(x) = σ_a + σ_b * [Σ_{k=1}^(M) (c_k * cos(ω_k * x) + d_k * sin(ω_k * x))]
with ω_k = k * π, and coefficients c_k, d_k in [-σ, σ]. The function is normalized to have integral 0.5 (equivalent to integral 1 over [0,2]). The objective is to minimize the max correlation coefficient C5, defined as:
  C5 = (2/n) * max_k [FFT{h}(k) * FFT{1-h}(k)] (slow)
But we use the precise full-mode correlation evaluation as in the task.

We use M as the key variable, with c_k and d_k parameters. Constraints: h(x) ∈ [0,1] and ∫h(x)dx=1.
