The score is 2n * max_k result[k] / S^2.

We want to minimize the maximum of the convolution. This is like a "minimax" problem: we want to spread the convolution as evenly as possible.

For a "nice" sequence (smooth, non-oscillatory), the convolution is also smooth, and its maximum is at the "center of mass" of the convolution.

Actually, I realize I've been overthinking this. Let me look at the problem from the perspective of the evaluation function again.

The score is 2n * max_b / S^2. We want to minimize this.

Key observations:
1. n appears linearly in the numerator. So longer sequences have a penalty.
2. max_b is the peak of the self-convolution.
3. S^2 is the square of the sum.

For a "nice" sequence where max_b ≈ c * S^2/n (for some constant c < 1), the score ≈ 2c.

We've seen that c = 1 is achieved by uniform sequences (score = 2), and c can be slightly less than 1 (score slightly less than 2).

To get score = 1.5, we need c = 0.75, i.e., max_b = 0.75 * S^2/n.
