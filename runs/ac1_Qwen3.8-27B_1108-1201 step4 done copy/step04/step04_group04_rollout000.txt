**Convergence rate of Frank-Wolfe**: For a convex problem with smoothness L, FW converges as O(1/t). With the "away steps" variant, it can be O(1/t^2). With "pairwise FW" or "sliding mode FW", it can be even faster.

**Idea 7: Use away-step FW or pairwise FW.**

In standard FW, we move from f toward the LP solution g: `f_new = (1-alpha)*f + alpha*g`.

In away-step FW, we can move AWAY from f: `f_new = (1-alpha-beta)*f + alpha*g + beta*h` where h is a vertex of the simplex to move away from. Simplified: `f_new = f + alpha*(g - e_i)` for some vertex e_i where f has significant mass.

Pairwise FW: `f_new = (1-alpha)*f + alpha*(g - e_i)` where e_i is chosen to minimize the objective.

Since the simplex vertices are the unit vectors e_0, ..., e_{n-1}, the away step means reducing the weight of some coordinate.

This can dramatically speed up convergence, especially when the optimal solution has many zero or near-zero coordinates.

**Idea 8: Use the "cutting plane" or "separation oracle" approach.**

Since the problem is convex, we can also think of it as: find the point on the simplex where the max convolution is minimized. The subdifferential of max_conv at a point a is the convex hull of the subgradients of the active constraints.

If only one convolution value is maximal (say at index k*), then the subgradient is `2*a[k*-::]` (the appropriate shifted version), and we can do projected subgradient descent.
