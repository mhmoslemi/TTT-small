### Approach and Rationale
The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks a step function h(x) on [0,2] with integral 0.5 and values in [0,1] that minimizes the maximum overlap integral when shifted by any k. We approach this by:
1. **Representation as Piecewise Constant**: Represent h(x) as a function with a few steps. This reduces the optimization to adjusting the positions and weights of these steps.
2. **Two-Scale Optimization**: Use a coarse grid for initial exploration and refine solutions on a finer grid to escape local optima.
3. **Periodicity Handling**: Ensure the periodicity condition h(x+2)=h(x) by representing the function on a periodic domain and using circular convolution for correlation.
4. **Gradient-Based Optimization**: Combine gradient descent with constraint projection to enforce the integral and box constraints.

The bottleneck is the non-convexity and high dimensionality. We overcome this by using multi-resolution optimization and adaptive step sizes.

### Mathematical Specification
We represent h(x) as a linear combination of basis functions that are periodic step functions. Let N be the number of steps (evenly spaced in the interval) and let w_i be the weight (height) of the i-th step. Then:
\[ h(x) = \sum_{i=1}^N w_i \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{x \geq x_i\}} \]
with constraints:
\[ \int_0^2 h(x) \, dx = 0.5, \quad 0 \leq w_i \leq 1 \]
and the periodicity is built into the basis by placing steps at positions defined on a circle.

The objective is to minimize:
\[ \max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1 - h(x+k)) \, dx \]

This integral is approximated by a discrete sum over samples.
