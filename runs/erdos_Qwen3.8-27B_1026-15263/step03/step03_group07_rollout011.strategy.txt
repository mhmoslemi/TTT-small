The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks to minimize the maximum overlap between a step function and its shifts. The given parent solution is a candidate, but we aim for a lower bound of 0.38085. The challenge is the non-convexity and high dimensionality of the problem.

We choose to use a combination of:

The primary bottleneck in gradient-based methods is the risk of getting trapped in local minima. Our hybrid approach will use gradient-based optimization for refinement but also employ a genetic algorithm for broader exploration.

### Mathematical specification
**Variables:** A function h(x) defined on [0,2] with values in [0,1] and integral 1.

**Objective:** Minimize the maximum overlap:
\[ C_5 = \max_k \int_0^2 h(x) (1 - h(x+k))  dx \]

Discretized with n points (dx = 2/n):
\[ C_5 = \max_k \left( \frac{2}{n} \right) \cdot \text{autocorrelation}(h, k) \]

**Constraints:**
1. \( 0 \leq h_i \leq 1 \) for all i
2. \( \sum h_i \cdot dx = 1 \) (i.e., \( \sum h_i = n/2 \) since dx = 2/n)
