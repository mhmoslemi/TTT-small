We will employ a multi-resolution optimization strategy combined with structured swap operations to systematically explore solutions at different scales. The core idea is that solutions with lower C5 values often emerge when we transition between discrete resolutions, leveraging the mathematical property that optimal solutions tend to be stable across scales. This method has been successfully applied to similar combinatorial optimization problems and addresses the primary bottleneck of gradient-based methods getting stuck in local optima.

The key innovations are:
1. Progressive resolution adaptation: We start at the given resolution (n=95) and gradually increase it to 150, evaluating solutions at each step
2. Structured swaps: Instead of random search, we perform targeted evaluations of swaps that maintain integral constraints while reducing overlap
3. Lagrange multiplier adjustment: We dynamically adapt penalty weights based on observed constraint violations

This approach balances exploration and exploitation by using gradient information for continuous improvement between resolution changes, while the discrete jumps help escape local minima. Lower is better, and we expect to find a solution with C5 < 0.3818 through these scale transitions.

The optimization is performed by:
