## 1. Approach and Rationale  

The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks to find a step function h defined on [0, 2] with integral 1 that minimizes the maximum overlap with its shift. The current record value is 0.3808, and we aim to find a better upper bound.  

Our approach leverages optimization of the function's autocorrelation properties. By treating h as a symmetric step function with a limited number of jumps, we reduce the optimization problem's dimensionality. We use gradient-based optimization (L-BFGS-B) with careful handling of constraints. The symmetry assumption helps reduce the search space significantly.  

We address the problem by:  
- Using a symmetric representation to reduce variables  
- Enforcing the integral constraint through Lagrange multipliers  
- Optimizing via gradient descent with line search  
- Starting from multiple initial configurations to escape local optima  
- Incorporating six-fold rotational symmetry where appropriate  

The primary limitation is that the approach assumes symmetry, which might constrain the optimal solution.

## 2. Mathematical Specification  

### Variables  
Let $h$ be a step function on $[0, 2]$ with possibly infinite discontinuities. We parameterize $h$ as:  
$$h(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} s_i \cdot \mathbf{1}_{[a_i, b_i)}(t)$$  
where $0 \leq s_i \leq 1$ and $\int_0^2 h(t) \, dt = 1$.  

### Symmetry Transformation  
Define a vector $x = (x_0, x_1, \dots, x_{k}) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with $d = M(k+1)$, where $k=5$ for six-fold symmetry, and $M$ is the number of scale points. For each $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ and scale $i$, let:  
$$t_{m,i} = \begin{cases} 
m \cdot s_i & \text{if symmetry is off}\\
\left( \frac{6}{6 \cdot M} \right) \sum_{j=1}^{M} \delta_{ij} \cdot m & \text{for six-fold symmetry}
\end{cases}$$  

### Objective  
Minimize $C_5 = \max_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} \int_0^2 h(t) \cdot [1 - h(t+k)] \, dt$.  

### Constraints  
1. $h(t) \in [0,1]$ for all $t$  
2. $\int_0^2 h(t) \, dt = 1$  

### Optimization Formulation  
We use constrained optimization with a barrier method for inequality constraints, and Lagrange multipliers for the equality constraint. The Lagrangian is:  
$$\mathcal{L}(x, \lambda) = \max_k \int_0^2 h(t) \cdot (1 - h(t+k)) \, dt - \lambda \left( \int_0^2 h(t) \, dt - 1 \right)$$  

Derivatives are computed analytically when possible.
