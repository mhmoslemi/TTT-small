Let me also think about whether I should use the "full" correlate or just the "forward" correlate (lags 0 to n-1). The "full" correlate includes both forward and backward lags. The max over all lags (positive and negative) is what we want.

For a step function on [0,2], the "backward" lags correspond to h(x) * (1-h(x-k)) for k > 0, which is different from h(x-k) * (1-h(x)) = h(x) * (1-h(x-k))... wait, these are the same! No:

Forward lag k: ∫₀^{2-k} h(x) * (1-h(x+k)) dx
Backward lag k: ∫_k² h(x) * (1-h(x-k)) dx = ∫₀^{2-k} h(x+k) * (1-h(x)) dx

These are different! The forward one counts "1→0" transitions at distance k, while the backward one counts "0→1" transitions.

For a binary function: forward = number of 1s followed by 0s at distance k, backward = number of 0s followed by 1s at distance k. The sum of both equals the total number of "non-equal" pairs at distance k.

The max could be at either forward or backward, so I need the full correlate.
