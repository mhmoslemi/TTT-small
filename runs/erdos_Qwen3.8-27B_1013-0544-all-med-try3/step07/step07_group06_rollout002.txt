For the continuous case, the objective is:
C(h) = max_k Σᵢ h[i]·(1-h[i+k])·dx

This is a non-convex problem (bilinear). One approach is to use the "max of linear" formulation:
minimize M
subject to: Σᵢ h[i]·(1-h[i+k])·dx ≤ M for all k
            0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1
            Σᵢ h[i]·dx = 1

This is a non-convex QCQP (quadratically constrained quadratic program). The bilinear terms make it hard.

For SLSQP, the smoothed max approach works:
f(h) = M + (1/α)·log(Σ exp(α(g_k - M)))
where M tracks max_k g_k and g_k = corr_k * dx.

Actually, I think a cleaner approach for the smooth objective is:
f(h, α) = (1/α)·log(Σ_k exp(α·g_k(h)))
This is the log-sum-exp, which is a smooth approximation to max_k g_k.

The gradient of f with respect to h involves the softmax weights exp(α·g_k)/Σ exp(α·g_j), which concentrate on the maximum as α→∞.

For very high α, this becomes very peaked and SLSQP struggles. A better approach might be to use α in the range 100-10000 and do many iterations, then switch to the binary case for final polishing.
