We are going to propose a fundamentally different approach from the previous strategies. The previous strategies were gradient-based optimization and Bayesian optimization, which are essentially local search methods or hybrid approaches. We will instead use a global optimization method that is based on the concept of "decomposition" and "relaxation" to handle the high dimensionality of the problem, combined with a multi-start and multi-resolution strategy.

 The key idea is to view the step function as a composition of impulses (binary values) but with the possibility of continuous values in between. We relax the problem by allowing h(x) to be any real number in [0,1] and then use a two-stage optimization: a coarser (binary) stage to find a configuration and then a finer stage to smooth and refine the solution.

 However, the problem is highly non-convex and requires maximizing the minimum separation between the function h and its shifted versions. We note that the overlap integral is defined as the maximum over shifts k of the integral of h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx. We can think of this as a measure of the worst-case overlap.

 We propose a method called "Two-Scale Optimization with Cross-Scale Constraints" (TSOCC). This method operates at two scales: a coarse scale and a fine scale. The coarse scale sets the "density" or "pattern" and the fine scale adjusts the phase or location of the impulses.
