**Binary pair-swap search**:
- After thresholding, h is 0/1 (approximately).
- We need sum(h) = n/2 exactly.
- Pair swap: pick i where h[i]=1 and j where h[j]=0, set h[i]=0, h[j]=1.
- This preserves the sum.
- Check if C5 decreases.
- Do multiple passes until no improvement.

The challenge is efficiency: checking all O(n²/4) pairs is too expensive for n=1000.

**Better: Greedy with gradient guidance**:
- Compute the gradient g = grad of smooth max.
- For positions with h[i] > 0.8: the "cost" of decreasing h[i] is approximately g[i] * delta.
- For positions with h[j] < 0.2: the "cost" of increasing h[j] is approximately g[j] * delta.
- The net benefit of a swap (i→j) is approximately -(g[i] - g[j]) * delta.
- So the best swap is the one that maximizes g[i] - g[j] for h[i]>0.8, h[j]<0.2.
