Wait, there's a problem with my local search: after accepting a swap, the `ones` and `zeros` arrays are stale. I should either:
1. Break and restart the sweep (safe but slower)
2. Update the arrays in place

For simplicity and correctness, let me restart the inner loop after each accepted swap. Or better, use a "first improvement" strategy: after finding and accepting one swap, go back to the beginning of the ones list.

Actually, the standard approach for swap-based local search is:
- For each (i, j) pair in some order:
  - If the swap improves, accept it and continue (don't restart)
  - But the "improvement" is relative to the current state, which has changed

This can lead to inconsistency. A safer approach:
- "Best improvement": try all swaps, find the best one, apply it, repeat
- "First improvement": try swaps in order, apply the first one that improves, then restart

For n=70, "best improvement" costs O(n² * n) = O(n³) per iteration (try all swaps, keep best, apply). That's 343K per iteration. Very fast.
