## 1. Approach and Rationale

The Erdős minimum overlap problem requires finding a step function on [0,2] with total integral 1 that minimizes the maximum overlap with its complement over all shifts. The core challenge is the non-convexity and combinatorial nature of the problem. While previous approaches used local searches and binary representations, our method adopts a novel framework called **Projected Stochastic Gradient Descent with Dynamic Budget Allocation**. 

Instead of hyperparameter-heavy annealing or SLSQP optimization, we:
- Pre-compute gradient approximations for random shifts
- Use confidence intervals to make informed updates
- Dynamically allocate computation budget based on landscape uncertainty
- Maintain exact constraints throughout optimization
- Sample multiple resolutions for escape from local optima

This differs fundamentally from previous approaches by using statistically principled exploration, adaptive budget management, and confidence-based search rather than arbitrary annealing schedules or fixed resolution phases.

The main user-surface interface is simplified: only `run(seed, budget)` needs implementation. The core optimization occurs via `projection`, `objective`, `finite_difference` helper functions, leaving complex strategies to configuration.
