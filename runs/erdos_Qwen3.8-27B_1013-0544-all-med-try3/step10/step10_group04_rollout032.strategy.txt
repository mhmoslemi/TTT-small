### 1. Approach and Rationale
**Central Idea:**  
The goal is to minimize the maximum correlation \( C_5 = \max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1-h(x+k))  dx \) for a step function \( h: [0,2] \rightarrow [0,1] \) with \( \int_0^2 h(x)  dx = 1 \). We leverage combinatorial optimization on a piecewise-constant representation of \( h(x) \) using breakpoints and optimization for piecewise-constant segments. This approach reduces the optimization space compared to continuous variables while addressing smoothness bottlenecks in existing methods.

**Bottleneck Addressed:**

By discretizing \( h(x) \) with piecewise-constant segments, we impose derivative discontinuities that better suit the minimization problem. This method explicitly enforces \( \sum h_i \Delta x = 1 \) via the breakpoints and segment values, ensuring no clipping penalties while handling circular shifts seamlessly.

### 2. Mathematical Specification
**Representation:**  
Break points at equally spaced intervals dividing \([0,2]\) into \( m \) fixed-length segments. Segment lengths \( \Delta x = 2/m \) ensuring integrals sum correctly.  

- Define breakpoints: \( x_i = i \cdot (2/m) \) for \( i \in \{0, 1, \ldots, m\} \).  
- Segment \( j \) from \( x_j \) to \( x_{j+1} \) has constant value \( v_j \in [0, 1] \).  

**Objective:**  
Minimize the maximum shift overlap:  
\[
C_5 = \max_k \sum_{i=0}^{m-1} v_i \min(1 - v_k, 1 - v_j)
\]  
where \( k \) is the segment index of overlap with neighboring segments. The integral is computed per segment, and the mode "full" correlation ensures valid lag analysis.

**Constraint:**  
Integral enforcement:  
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} v_j \Delta x = 1
\]  
with \( \Delta x = 2/m \), so:
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} v_j = m/2 \cdot \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta x} = \frac{m}{2} \cdot 1 => \sum v_j = m/2
\]  

**Optimization:**  
- Minimizes the maximum weighted overlap over all shifts via combinatorial search for small \( m \), then optimizes values for larger \( m \).
