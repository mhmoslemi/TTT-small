1. **Approach and Rationale**  
   **Central Idea**: This approach combines a lightweight evolutionary strategy with gradient-based refinement to explore a broader solution space than traditional gradient-based methods alone. By first finding promising candidates with a genetic algorithm (GA) at a coarse resolution, we can then refine them locally at high resolution, avoiding local minima that gradient methods often encounter.  
   **Key Differences**:  
   - Uses a mixed-integer repair mechanism with smoothing to enforce constraints.  
   - Switches between discrete shifts and continuous optimization based on the problem's structure.  
   - Exploits multi-resolution strategies, allowing adaptive upscaling of candidates to finer grids.  
   **Bottlenecks Addressed**:  
     - Narrow peaks exploit are resolved by cyclic shift sampling and priority-based feature repair.  
     - Grid artifacts are mitigated by spectral projection with Chebyshev nodes.  
     - Entropy-limited exploration is handled by adaptive dimensionality reduction.  

2. **Mathematical Specification**  
   **Representation**:  
     - The step function is represented as a grid vector \(h \in [0, 1]^n\) where \(n\) is the resolution.  
     - Integral constraint: \(\sum_{i=1}^n h_i \cdot dx = 1\) with \(dx = 2/n\).  
     - **Constraint Enforcement**: A repair mechanism minimizes the integral discrepancy via gradient projection:  
        \[
        \Delta h = \alpha \cdot (1 - \text{integral}(h)) \cdot \mathbf{1} / \| \mathbf{1} \|^2
        \]  
        followed by clipping:  
        \[
        h' = \text{clip}(h + \Delta h, 0, 1)
        \]  
        **Objective**:  
        \[
        \max_{k=- (n-1)}^{n-1} \int_0^{2 - |k|dx} h(x)(1 - h(x+k))  dx
        \]  
        Computed via:  
        \[
        C_5 = \max_k \left(\sum_{i} h_i (1 - h_{i+k}) \right) \cdot dx
        \]  
        **Smoothed Relaxation**: A log-sum-exp penalty:  
        \[
        \phi(k) = \exp\left(-\alpha \cdot (c(k) - \mu)\right)
        \]  
        where \(\mu = \max_k c(k)\), to handle non-convexity.
