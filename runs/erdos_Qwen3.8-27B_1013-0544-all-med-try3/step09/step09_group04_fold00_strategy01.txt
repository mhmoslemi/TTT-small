### 1. Approach and Rationale
We use a **heterogeneous particle swarm optimization (PSO)** with a local intensification phase to explore the solution space. This approach combines global exploration (PSO) with local optimization (gradient-based) to escape local optima. The key constraints and properties are:
- The function \( h(x) \) is represented as a discrete vector \( h \) of length \( n_{\text{points}} \) (to be determined), with values \( \in [0, 1] \).
- The integral constraint: \( \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\text{points}}} h_i \cdot dx = 1 \), where \( dx = 2 / n_{\text{points}} \) (equivalent to \( \sum h_i = n_{\text{points}} / 2 \)).
- The objective is to minimize the maximum overlap \( C_5 = \max_k \int h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx \).

The previous implementation used:
- Multiple phases of optimization with different methods (L-BFGS-B, SLSQP, active set).
- A fixed resolution approach (starting at 500 points, then up to 1500).

While this was effective, we hypothesized that:
- **Global exploration** is underutilized. PSO is better for exploring complex landscapes than local methods.
- **Resolution variation** (starting at single-digit points, then increasing) can reduce initial optimization costs.
- **Adaptive hyperparameters** based on PSO fitness (velocity, inertia) could better navigate the optimization space.

Key design choices:
- PSO for global search with dynamically adjusted exploration/exploitation.
- Local gradient-based refinement using exponential smoothing of the overlap objective.
- Progressive resolution increase: start with few points (e.g., 10) and double until a pseudolocal optimum is found.
- Smoothed gradient calculation to avoid Objective non-differentiability.

**Bottleneck Addressed**: Local optima arise from over-reliance on gradient-based methods that get stuck in narrow valleys. PSO allows wider exploration. The smoothing technique handles the non-differentiable max() operation.

### 2. Mathematical Specification 

#### Representation
The solution is a step function \( h(x) \), represented as a vector \( h \in \{0, 1, 0.5\} \) (initially) or \( \in [0, 1] \) (after refinement). The domain is \( [0, 2] \), and \( dx = 2 / n_{\text{points}} \).

#### Objective
Smoothed overlap calculation:
\[
C_{\text{smooth}}(h) = \alpha \left( \max_k \text{correlation}(h, 1-h) \right) + \frac{1}{\alpha} \log\left( \sum_{i} e^{\alpha (c_i^\text{raw} - \max(c^\text{raw})) \right)
\}
\]
where \( \alpha = 10^7 \) (for aggressive smoothing). Maximization is converted to minimization by negative weighting.

#### Constraints
1. \( 0 \leq h_i \leq 1 \) for all \( i \).
2. \( \sum h_i \cdot dx = 1 \) \( \iff \sum h_i = n_{\text{points}} / 2 \).

#### Optimality Formulation
\[
\min_{h} \left\{ - \alpha \cdot \max_k \text{correlation}(h, 1-h) + \frac{1}{\alpha} \log\left( \sum_{i} e^{\alpha (c_i^\text{raw} - \max(c^\text{raw})) \right) \mid \sum h_i = n_{\text{points}} / 2, \ 0 \leq h_i \leq 1\right\}
\]

#### Gradients
The gradient for the \( i \)-th particle is computed by:
1. Calculating \( \text{correlation}(h, 1-h) \) (mode="full").
2. Scaling by \( dx = 2 / n_{\text{points}} \).
3. Using a perturbation to compute directional derivatives.
