Approach and rationale
The central idea is to represent the step function h(x) using a compact basis expansion in a smooth space, then optimize over those coefficients. This differs from discrete optimization by allowing smoother transitions and leveraging mathematical analytic tools. The basis expansion captures global structure while maintaining integrability and continuity. The basis spans L2 rather than being discrete, enabling more effective optimization techniques. The key advantage is that gradients can be computed more readily and meaningful constraints on coefficients can be imposed. This approach should overcome local optima in discrete spaces by exploring continuous landscapes.
