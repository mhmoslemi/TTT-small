### 1. Approach and Rationale
The core issue with the current approach is likely **limited exploration of the search space** due to over-reliance on gradient-based local optimization methods. While the multi-phase optimization is thorough, it may become trapped in local optima specific to the given parent construction. To address this, we combine global exploration with high-resolution local refinement using a **dual-resolution strategy**: optimize on a sparse grid first, then refine promising solutions on a denser grid. This leverages the fact that meaningful solutions often exhibit block-like structures (e.g., piecewise constant), which can be approximated well even on coarse grids. The key novelty is optimizing **variable-length block representations** directly, allowing blocks to split and merge during optimization, which is unconstrained gradient descent in a Manhattan-metric space. Hyperparameter sensitivity is tackled by **progressive refinement**: starting with aggressive hyperparameter sampling and converging to a narrow range. This approach is grounded in the intuition that optimal solutions for minimal overlap require high-frequency peaks in the correlation function with predictable failure modes at certain shifts.

*Central Idea*: 
- Re-ezessentially represent candidate functions as ensembles of non-overlapping blocks with variable positions and widths
- Use multi-phase optimization where:
  - First phase: Block-level optimization over coarse grid with stochastic parameters
  - Second phase: Denser grid optimization with variable block splitting/merging
- Unlike previous continuous minimization, this preserves structural integrity found in optimal sparse solutions

*Limitations & Risks*:
