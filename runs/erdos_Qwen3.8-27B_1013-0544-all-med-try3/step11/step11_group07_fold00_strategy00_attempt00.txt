1. **Implementation Bottlenecks**: The L-BFGS-B optimization with fixed smoothing (\(\alpha\)) and multi-resolution sampling introduced computational costs growing with \(n^2\), limiting effective resolutions to under 2000 points.
2. **Exploitation of Symmetry**: The original code ignored periodic structure inherent to the problem domain (length 2), which could reduce the effective search space by \(\sim n \log n\) per function.
3. **Overreliance on Generic Solvers**: The problem’s logarithmic boundary conditions require specialized gradient projection to maintain feasibility, which was not fully incorporated in the smoothing-objective approach.

#### New Primary Methodology
We will use **gradient-based optimization on a periodic representation** of \(h\), enforcing symmetry and integral constraints via a projection operator. This combines:
- **Constraint Handling**: A convex solver (CVXPY) to project solutions into the feasible set \(\sum h_i = n/2\) and \(0 \leq h_i \leq 1\).
- **Gradient Guidance**: The objective's gradient is computed via **autocorrelation analysis**, but the auto-correlation's maximum shift is tracked to focus optimization effort where it matters.
- **Resolution Flexibility**: Our method explicitly supports multi-resolution exploration by periodically re-sampling the function between phases, requiring clear feasibility transformation functions (see section 4).

#### Assumptions and Objections
1. **Differentiable Penalty**: The smoothed max (\(\text{smooth\_max}_\alpha\)) approximates the non-differentiable \(\max_k\) but may diverge from the exact solution at high \(\alpha\).
2. **Feasibility Projection**: The simple clipping projection (\(\text{project\_feasible}\)) guarantees feasibility but may create pathological edges. We consider an interpolated projection (see section 4) as an improvement.

---

### Mathematical Specification
 Represent \(h\) as a periodic step function with period \(n\), so \(h(x) = h(x \bmod 2)\) with period \(2\). We define:
- **Objective**:
  \[
  C = \max_{k \in [-n+1, n-1]} \left\{\iiint_{x,\,\text{shifted}} h(x)\left(1-h(x+k)\right) dx\right\}
  \]
  where shift \(k\) ranges from \(-n+1\) to \(n-1\). The integral is over the domain \([0, 2]\), accounting for \(dx\) correctly as \(\frac{2}{n}\).

- **Constraints**:
  \[
  \int_0^2 h(x) dx = 1 \quad \& \quad 0 \leq h(x) \leq 1 \quad \forall x.
  \]

- **Periodicity Enforcement**:
  \[
  h(x+n) = h(x) \quad \forall x.
  \]

- **Duality Gap**: We cannot rely on a convex relaxation for the \(\max\) term but smooth it with \(\alpha\) (Eq. \(\text{smooth\_max}_\alpha\)).

### Search Choices and Suggested Variations

We recommend **multiple smoothing schedules** for gradient descent:

1. Start with large \(\alpha\) (to target specific peaks) then low \(\alpha\) (to allow exploration) as iterations increase.

2. **Warm-starting multiple resolution runs** with hierarchical contrast: at \(n=500\), then \(n=1000\) (optional improvement).

3. **Investigate period=2 symmetry** (checkerboard) or period=4 for \([0.25,0.5,0.5,0.25]\) cyclic patterns.

4. **Binary Particle Swarm Optimization (BPSO)** could be used as an alternative search method (but FPSO is still iterative).

5. **Eigenvector-based features** (Fast Multipole Method) for overlapping suppression is a further improvement.
