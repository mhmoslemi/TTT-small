If corr at lag d is the max, then:

To decrease this, we want to:
- Decrease h[m] for m where h[m] > 0 and h[m+d] < 1
- Increase h[m+d] for m where h[m] > 0 and h[m+d] < 1

- We want to reduce h[m] where h[m+d] is low
- Or increase h[m+d] where h[m] is high

But we need to maintain sum(h) = n/2, so any increase must be matched by a decrease elsewhere.

The gradient of corr[d] with respect to h[j]:
∂/∂h[j] Σ_m h[m]*(1-h[m+d]) = (1-h[j+d]) [if j+d valid] - h[j-d] [if j-d valid]

So the steepest descent direction (to reduce corr[d]) is:
For j where (1-h[j+d]) > h[j-d]: decrease h[j]
For j where h[j-d] > (1-h[j+d]): increase h[j]

But we also need to consider that changing h affects ALL lags, not just the worst one. The subgradient of max is the average over active lags.
