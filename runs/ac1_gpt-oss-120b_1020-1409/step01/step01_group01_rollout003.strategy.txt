### Proposed Method: Simulated Annealing

Simulated Annealing (SA) is a stochastic optimization method that does not require gradients. We define:

- **State**: sequence of n nonnegative numbers, each between 0 and 1000.
- **Energy**: the value returned by `evaluate_sequence(sequence)`, with lower being better.
- **Neighbor Move**: change one element of the sequence by a small random amount (say, add or subtract a single random value between [0, dmax]). Also, we must safeguard the bounds.

Steps:

1. Start with a valid initial sequence (if `height_sequence_1` is available, else generate a uniform sequence with sum=0.01).
2. Set an initial temperature T and a cooling rate.
3. For a given temperature T:
   - Iterate until a condition (max iterations or energy improvement).
   - Generate a neighbor by picking a random index and changing it by a random amount (while maintaining bounds [0,1000]).
   - Evaluate the neighbor with `evaluate_sequence`.
   - Accept the neighbor if it has a lower energy (good) or with a probability exp(-deltaE/T) (for downhill moves).
4. Decrease the temperature.
5. Stop when T is too low or after a fixed number of steps.

We must ensure that the neighbor remains in [0,1000] and the sum is at least 0.01.

However, note that the sequence length is fixed (n=6121). We cannot change the length.

One move: change one element by a small amount.

This method has the disadvantage of being slow for large n, but it is simple and does not require any knowledge of the gradient.

But how to choose the amount of change? We can use a fixed step size or a proportional step size.

We have to deal with the possibility of the sequence becoming negative or too large.
