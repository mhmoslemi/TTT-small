The current algorithm uses a combination of smooth gradient descent and subgradient methods to optimize the overlap integral. The bottleneck is the slow convergence and limited escape from local optima. We adopt a new hybrid approach using Bayesian optimization with a Gaussian Process Surrogate Model. This method is more robust to local optima and can explore the optimization landscape more efficiently. The core idea is to train a surrogate model that mimics the expensive objective function and use Bayesian optimization to find the minimizer with fewer evaluations.

2. Mathematical specification
The objective function is C_5 = max_k ∫ h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx, discretized as max_k (dx * corr_full[k]), where corr_full is the correlation of h and (1-h). The constraints are 0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1 for all i, and sum(h) = n/2 (exactly for integer counts).

The optimization problem is nonconvex and high-dimensional (n=70). We replace the expensive objective evaluation with a Gaussian Process Surrogate Model (GP) that can predict the objective at new points. Uncertainty in predictions guides the search: high uncertainty areas are preferred to promote exploration.
