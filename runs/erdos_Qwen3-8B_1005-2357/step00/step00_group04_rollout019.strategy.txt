### 1. Approach and Rationale
The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks to find a step function \( h: [0,2] \to [0,1] \) with \( \int_0^2 h(x)  dx = 1 \) that minimizes the maximum overlap integral:
\[
C_5 = \max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1 - h(x+k))  dx.
\]
The current record is approximately 0.38085, and the goal is to achieve a bound of 0.380876. The approach combines gradient-based optimization with a resolution doubling strategy to systematically refine the initial solution. The key insight is that minimizing the maximum overlap is equivalent to maximizing the minimum autocorrelation of \( h \). The optimization is challenging due to the non-convexity of the objective function, box constraints, and the linear equality constraint. The gradient is derived from the active lag (the shift \( k \) that maximizes the overlap), and the solution is projected back to the feasible set after each update.
