### 1. Approach and Rationale  
The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks to find a step function \( h: [0, 2] \to [0, 1] \) with integral 1 that minimizes the maximum overlap integral \( C_5 = \max_k \int h(x) (1 - h(x+k))  dx \). The current best-known bound is approximately 0.38085, and the goal is to find a construction that improves this bound to below 0.380876.  

The approach involves using a gradient-based optimization method with a line search to update the function \( h \) iteratively. The key idea is to:  
- **Localize the maximum overlap**: For each iteration, identify the lag \( k \) that maximizes the overlap integral.  
- **Gradient descent**: Update \( h \) in the direction of the gradient of the overlap integral for this worst lag, scaled by a step size determined via line search.  
- **Feasibility enforcement**: After each update, clip \( h \) to [0, 1] and adjust its integral to 1.  

This method leverages the structure of the overlap integral and uses efficient gradient computation to avoid expensive global optimization techniques. The rationale is that by iteratively reducing the worst overlap, we can systematically improve the bound.  

**Limitations**:  
- The method is gradient-based and may get stuck in local optima.  
- The gradient computation assumes a continuous relaxation, which may not perfectly capture the discrete nature of the problem.  
- The line search step sizes are fixed, which may not adapt optimally to the problem landscape.  

**Key Hypotheses**:  
- The gradient direction for the worst lag will consistently reduce the global maximum overlap.  
- Iterative updates with line search will converge to a better solution than the parent.

### 2. Mathematical Specification  
#### Variables and Representation  
- \( h \): A step function represented as a NumPy array of length \( n_{\text{points}} \), with values in [0, 1] and sum \( \frac{n_{\text{points}}}{2} \) (since \( \int h(x)  dx = 1 \) and \( dx = \frac{2}{n_{\text{points}}} \)).  
- \( n_{\text{points}} \): Number of grid points (default 80).  
- \( dx \): Grid spacing, \( dx = \frac{2}{n_{\text{points}}} \).  

#### Objective  
Minimize \( C_5 = \max_k \left( \text{np.correlate}(h, 1-h, \text{mode}="full") \times \frac{2}{n_{\text{points}}} \right) \).  

#### Constraints  
- \( 0 \leq h[i] \leq 1 \) for all \( i \).  
- \( \sum h[i] \times dx = 1 \), which simplifies to \( \sum h[i] = \frac{n_{\text{points}}}{2} \).  

#### Gradient and Update  
For a given lag \( k \), the overlap integral is \( I_k = \int h(x) (1 - h(x+k))  dx \). The gradient of \( I_k \) with respect to \( h(x) \) is:  
\[ \frac{\partial I_k}{\partial h(x)} = (1 - h(x+k)) - h(x-k) \]  
where terms are zero if indices are out of bounds.  

The gradient for \( 1 - I_k \) is \( -\frac{\partial I_k}{\partial h(x)} \), so the update direction is \( \frac{\partial I_k}{\partial h(x)} \) (to minimize \( 1 - I_k \)).  

#### Line Search  
Evaluate candidate step sizes \( \alpha \in \{0.01, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0\} \) and select the one that minimizes \( C_5 \).  

#### Feasibility Projection  
After updating \( h \):  
1. Clip values to [0, 1].  
2. Scale to ensure \( \sum h[i] = \frac{n_{\text{points}}}{2} \).  

#### Solver Selection  
- **Gradient-based optimization**: No CVXPY required, as the problem is non-convex but solved via gradient descent.  
- **Line search**: Manual implementation to avoid solver overhead.
