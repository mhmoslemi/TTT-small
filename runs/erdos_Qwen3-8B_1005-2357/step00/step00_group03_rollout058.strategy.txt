1. Approach and Rationale
We propose a hybrid optimization strategy combining global search with local refinement to tackle the Erdős minimum overlap problem. The core idea is to use a population-based approach to explore the solution space while leveraging gradient information to escape local optima. Unlike previous methods that relied solely on gradient-based optimization, our approach incorporates:

- **Differential Evolution (DE)** for global exploration, which maintains a population of candidate solutions and applies mutation, crossover, and selection
- **Gradient-based Local Search** using SLSQP to refine promising candidates
- **Adaptive Resolution Scaling** to progressively refine solutions at higher resolutions (n=70, 140, 280)

This multi-phase approach addresses the non-convex nature of the problem by:
- Using DE to explore diverse solutions and escape local minima
- Applying gradient-based refinement to exploit promising regions
- Scaling to higher resolutions to capture finer details
- Maintaining feasibility through repair mechanisms

The main limitations are:
- DE requires more function evaluations than gradient-based methods
- Higher resolutions increase computational cost
- The trade-off between exploration and exploitation needs careful tuning
