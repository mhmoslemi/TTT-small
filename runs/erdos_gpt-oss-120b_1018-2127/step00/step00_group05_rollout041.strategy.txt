Approach and Rationale

The central idea is to find a candidate ```h_values``` that minimizes the overlap integral ```C_5``` by placing the "mass" (integral 1) on  ```[0,2]``` in a way that reduces the interaction with shifted versions. This is achieved through a dynamic programming-like approach that gradually refines an initial guess. Unlike population-based optimization, this method starts from scratch and iteratively improves the solution by focusing on sparse or "measure" points, reducing discretization.

The rationale for this approach is that the minimum overlap constant is related to equidistributing the support to avoid high concentrations, but directly optimizing all points is high-dimensional and non-convex. Starting from a binary-like structure and refining it for larger support can lower the peak correlation. The method uses an adaptive greedy search and sparsity (user-defined) to escape local optima.
