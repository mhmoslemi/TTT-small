### Approach and Rationale
The goal is to minimize the evaluation function `2n * max(convolve(a,a)) / sum(a)^2` for a non-negative sequence `a` of length `n`. Previous attempts used a combination of local search (Fast Coordinate Descent) and global optimization (LP Fictitious Play, Mirror Descent). The primary bottleneck is escaping local minima quickly and improving convergence. This approach introduces two key optimizations:
1. **Enhanced Initialization**: Uses a fractal-inspired "Wolfram Rule" to generate candidate sequences, leveraging cellular automata patterns for better exploration of solution space.
2. **Directed Mutation Search (DM)**： Employs a stochastic local search with directed mutations around the highest peaks in the convolution to find optimal concentration. This avoids some of the computational costs of earlier optimization steps.

The strategy involves:
- Starting with two default initialization methods: random \( \sqrt{0.1 \times n} \) peaks and inverse cumulative distribution function (CDF) uniform distribution.
- Adding a Wolfram Rule 110-inspired initialization, which generates sequences with complex, hierarchical structures known to have low autocorrelation.
- Using DM search as a more efficient optimization step compared to traditional FCD/LP iterations.
- Switching to DM if the initial FCD runs exceed a time limit, or if improvement stalls.
- The process is designed to spend most time on high-dimensional initial candidates (starting from 1024-values) to accelerate critical path minimization.

- Let \( n \) be the sequence length (default 1024 if no parent provided).

**Objective:**

**Constraints:**

**Algorithm Specification:**

  - **Mutation Strategy**: Sample neighboring patches, scale by signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), and diversify periodically.
  - Steps:
    - **Detect High Peaks**: Select the K highest peaks in the convolution.
    - **Sample Neighbors**: Perturb surrounding indices.
    - **Scale by SNR**: Adjust candidate sequences based on local peak decay.
    - **Diversification**: Alternate between uniform and systematic mutation to avoid premature convergence.
