Actually, let me think about this from the perspective of the **sum of squares**. We know that:
- conv[n-1] = sum_j a[j]*a[n-1-j] (the "center" of the convolution)
- For a symmetric sequence (a[j] = a[n-1-j]), conv[n-1] = sum_j a[j]^2 = ||a||^2

And we know that ||a||^2 >= (sum a)^2/n = 1/n (by Cauchy-Schwarz, with equality when all a[j] are equal).

So for symmetric sequences, max(conv) >= ||a||^2 >= 1/n, giving score >= 2n/n = 2.

This means that for symmetric sequences, we can never beat the uniform! The uniform is optimal among symmetric sequences.

So we need **asymmetric** sequences to do better.

For asymmetric sequences, the center of the convolution is NOT the sum of squares. It's sum_j a[j]*a[n-1-j], which can be smaller than ||a||^2 if the sequence is "anti-correlated" with its reverse.

But wait, for non-negative sequences, a[j]*a[n-1-j] >= 0, so the center is always non-negative. And by the AM-QM inequality or just by the fact that for non-negative x,y: xy <= (x^2+y^2)/2, we get:
sum_j a[j]*a[n-1-j] <= sum_j (a[j]^2+a[n-1-j]^2)/2 = ||a||^2.

So the center is at most ||a||^2. The question is whether other lags can be the maximum.
