17. **Let me try a different approach: minimize the peak by making the convolution as flat as possible.**

The convolution of a with itself, in the frequency domain, is |A(f)|^2 where A is the DTFT of a. We want to minimize the peak of the inverse transform of |A(f)|^2.

By Parseval's theorem, the integral of |A(f)|^2 is proportional to sum(a^2). The peak of the inverse transform is related to the "concentration" of |A(f)|^2.

If |A(f)|^2 is as flat as possible (constant), then the inverse transform is a delta function, and the peak is as small as possible. But |A(f)|^2 = |A(-f)|^2 (real signal), and for a finite signal, A(f) is an entire function (trigonometric polynomial), so |A(f)|^2 can't be perfectly flat.

This is related to the "Golay sequence" problem and "low autocorrelation" sequences.
