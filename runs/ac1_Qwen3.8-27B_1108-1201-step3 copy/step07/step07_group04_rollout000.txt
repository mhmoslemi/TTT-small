The idea: we want (conv(a,a))[k] to be as equal as possible for all k. Define the "target" as the uniform distribution over the 2n-1 convolution positions: target[k] = (sum a)^2 / (2n-1).

For a non-negative sequence a of length n, with sum(a) = S:
- conv(a,a) has 2n-1 non-negative entries
- sum of all entries = S^2
- Therefore max(conv(a,a)) >= S^2/(2n-1)
- Score = 2*n*max/(S^2) >= 2*n/(2n-1)

For n=768: 2*768/1535 = 1536/1535 ≈ 1.00065.

So the theoretical lower bound is about 1.00065. The uniform sequence gives 2. The previous best is 1.504873.

To get a score of 1.5, we need max(conv(a,a)) = 1.5*S^2/(2n) = 0.75*S^2/n.
The average is S^2/(2n-1) ≈ S^2/(2n). So we need the max to be about 1.5 times the average.

This means the convolution should be fairly flat but with peaks about 1.5x the average. This is achievable!

The key question is: what structure of a gives a nearly flat auto-convolution?

For the uniform sequence, the auto-convolution is triangular: it rises linearly to the center, then falls linearly. The ratio of max to average is (2n-1)/(n) ≈ 2 for large n. This gives score = 2.

To reduce the peak-to-average ratio from 2 to 1.5, we need to "flatten" the triangular shape. This means:
- Reduce the center (where the sum is longest)
- Increase the edges (where the sum is shortest)

The way to do this: make a[j] larger at the edges and smaller in the middle.

Specifically, if a[j] ≈ 1/sqrt(j*(n-1-j)) (with appropriate modification at the edges), then:
conv(a,a)[k] ≈ sum_j a[j]*a[k-j]

For k near n-1 (center): the sum involves many terms but each term a[j]*a[k-j] is small (since j and k-j are both in the middle where a is small).

For k near 0 (edge): the sum involves few terms but a[0]*a[k] is large (since a[0] is large).

This could potentially flatten the convolution!
