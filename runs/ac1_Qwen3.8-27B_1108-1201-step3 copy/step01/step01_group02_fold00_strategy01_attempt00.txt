Approach and Rationale
I propose a fundamentally different optimization strategy called Constrained Fourier Annealing (CFA). Unlike the previous LP-based refinement, CFA leverages the fact that the step function's peak auto-convolution correlates strongly with its spectral properties. By decomposing the optimization into Fourier frequencies and gradually constraining them via projected gradients, we can avoid neighborhood traps and find global minima faster.

Key improvements over the existing approach:
- Uses analytic spectral decomposition to synthesize low-peak solutions
- Conservative dimensionality expansion and contraction
- Adaptive temperature scheduling based on convergence rate
- Piecewise least-angle selection for sparse solutions
- Protects against overrefinement through checkpoint evaluations

This approach theoretically finds sequences with lower scores than the Gaussian-initialized candidates by operating in the frequency domain where the optimization landscape is smoother.

Mathematical Specification
Let \( f(x) = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i \delta(x-i) \). Its full convolution yields:
\[ (f*f)(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{2n-1} c_k \delta(x-k) \]
where the coefficient \( c_k \) is the convolution value at position \( k \).

The objective to minimize is \( \frac{2n \cdot \max_k |c_k|}{(\sum_i a_i)^2} \), subject to \( a_i \ge 0 \) and \( \sum_i a_i \ge 0.01 \).
