Approach and Rationale:
The central idea is to optimize a sparse, comb-like sequence structure using multi-stage downhill simplex methods. Unlike traditional gradient-based approaches, we use a constructive paradigm where the sequence is built from scratch each iteration. This avoids gradient issues and allows deeper exploration of effective patterns. The rationale is that comb sequences can achieve high \( \|f*f\|_\infty \) to \( \sum_j a_j \) ratios through careful placement of positive peaks. The structure leverages positive-negative-pairs relationship, strategically boosting constructive interference at target lags while capping destructive effects.

The algorithm is divided into three primary phases:
1. Coarse Iterations: Use efficient simplex projection on low-resolution candidate sequences (small n).
2. Medium Iterations: Increase sequence resolution by doubling the effective length each phase while preserving structural properties.
3. Fine Iterations: Use a feedback mechanism inspired by the LP-based direction but with frequency-adaptive step scaling for precise optimization.

The key advantage is balancing rapid exploration (coarse phase) with precise refinement (fine phase). The primary limitation is the fixed expansion factor, but early simulations show this materializes deeper progress toward the target \( \|f*f\|_\infty / (\text{sum}(a))^2 \approx 1.503 \).

The searchable space is defined by:
