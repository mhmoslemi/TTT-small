Approach and Rationale
The core operation of the TriMul module is to compute a reduction over the sequence index k for each batch, sequence pair (i, j) and hidden dimension (d). This outer product reduction is typically O(N^3) per hidden dimension due to the nested loops over all sequence indices. We address the performance bottleneck by implementing a tightly fused kernel that leverages blocking strategies to minimize memory access overhead while maintaining accuracy.

Our approach involves:
1. Normalizing the input over the channel dimension
2. Projecting and gating the activations
3. Performing a batched reduction using element-wise multiplication
4. Finalizing with output normalization and gating

The reduction operation is fusing three key steps:
- Element-wise masks for hidden dimensions
- Intermediate output storage
- The O(N^3) reduction

Mathematical Specification
Given:
- Input: x ∈ ℝ^{B × N × N × C}
- Mask: m ∈ {0,1}^{B × N × N} (optional)

The forward pass applies LayerNorm to x over channels C, where:

Norm(x): Batched normalization over channels C for x

Then computes:
left_proj: W = x @ lw (where lw is the weight matrix for left projection)
 Similarly for right_proj, left_gate, right_gate.

Combined operation:
out_ijd[b,i,j,d] = 
  Σ_k (left_ijk[b,i,k,d] × right_ijk[b,j,k,d] × α_lg[i,k] × β_rg[j,k]) × γ_out[b,i,j,d]

where:
α_lg = element-wise apply sigmoid to left_gate
β_rg = element-wise apply sigmoid to right_gate
γ_out = sigmoid applied to out_gate

The reduction is then applied over k:

out_ij[b,i,j] = Σ_d (out_ijd[b,i,j,d])

At the output stage:

out_final = ((out_ij @ out_ij^T) @ to_out_weight) × out_gate
