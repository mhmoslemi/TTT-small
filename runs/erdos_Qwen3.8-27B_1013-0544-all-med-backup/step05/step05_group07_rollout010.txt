The Erdős minimum overlap problem: we want a set A of measure 1 in [0,2] such that max_k |A ∩ (A+k)| is minimized.

For discrete: n points, n/2 are in A. The overlap for shift k is the number of i with i in A and i+k not in A (for positive k), times dx.

The "ideal" would be all overlaps equal. The average overlap is:
avg = (1/(2n-1)) * sum_k overlap(k)

sum_k overlap(k) = sum_k sum_i [i in A AND i+k not in A]
= sum_i [i in A] * (number of k where i+k not in A and valid)

For a fixed i in A, the number of valid k where i+k not in A:
- k ranges from -(i) to (n-1-i) (so that both i and i+k are in [0,n-1])
- Total valid k: n (from -i to n-1-i)
- Of these, the ones where i+k is in A: |A ∩ {i+k : k valid}| = |A \ {i}| ∩ {shifted} ... this is complicated.

Actually, for a binary string of length n with n/2 ones:
sum_k overlap(k) = sum_i sum_k [h[i]=1 AND h[i+k]=0] * [valid]
= sum_i h[i] * (number of valid j where h[j]=0)
= sum_i h[i] * (n - n/2) = (n/2) * (n/2) = n²/4

So avg_overlap = n²/4 / (2n-1) ≈ n/8 for large n.
avg_C5 = (n/8) * (2/n) = 1/4 = 0.25.

But we can't achieve the average (we want to minimize the MAX). The minimum possible max is at least the average, so C5 >= 0.25. Our current best is 0.38, which is above 0.25.

The theoretical minimum is related to the "discrepancy" of the difference set. For a perfect difference set, all overlaps would be exactly equal to the average. But such sets may not exist for all n.
