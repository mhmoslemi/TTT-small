### 1. Reformulation as a max‑min autocorrelation problem

Since \(\int_0^2h(x)dx=1\),

\[
C_5(h)=\max_k\int h(1-h_k)
      =\;1\;-\;\min_k\int h(x)h(x+k)dx .
\]

Thus minimising \(C_5\) is equivalent to **maximising the smallest autocorrelation**
\(\displaystyle A_k(h)=\int_0^2 h(x)h(x+k)dx\) over all integer shifts \(k\).
When the domain is discretised with step \(dx=2/n\),

\[
A_k(h)=dx\;\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i\,h_{i+k},
\qquad
C_5(h)=1-\min_k A_k(h).
\]

For a binary vector \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\) with exactly \(n/2\) ones,
\(A_k(x)=dx\;\sum_i x_i x_{i+k}\) is simply the count of overlapping ones at shift \(k\).

Hence any feasible binary vector gives a valid upper bound for the continuous problem.  
If a binary optimum already attains a low bound, we are done; otherwise we will improve it continuously.

### 4. Continuous refinement with a smooth‑max surrogate

The binary pattern is a strong starting point but may be sub‑optimal in the continuous domain.

**4.1 Soft‑max objective**  
Define for the fine‑grid vector `h` (now allowed to be continuous in [0,1])  

\[
F_\beta(h)=\frac{1}{\beta}\log\Bigl(\sum_{k=-n+1}^{n-1}
   \exp\bigl(\beta\,dx\,\operatorname{conv}_k(h)\bigr)\Bigr),
\]
where `conv_k(h)=∑_i h_i(1-h_{i+k})` (zero‑padding outside [0,n)).

**4.2 Gradient computation**  
For a given shift `k`, the derivative w.r.t. `h_i` is  

\[
\frac{\partial\,\operatorname{conv}_k}{\partial h_i}
   = (1-h_{i+k})\mathbf{1}_{0\le i+k<n}
     - h_{i-k}\mathbf{1}_{0\le i-k<n}.
\]

We compute all shifted copies of `h` with `np.pad` and assemble the Jacobian implicitly.  
The soft‑max weights are  

\[
w_k = \frac{\exp(\beta dx\,\operatorname{conv}_k)}{\sum_t \exp(\beta dx\,\operatorname{conv}_t)}.
\]

Then  

\[
\nabla F_\beta = dx\;\sum_k w_k\;\nabla\operatorname{conv}_k .
\]

All operations are vectorised (O(n²) avoided); the two sums in the gradient are themselves convolutions and can be evaluated with FFTs.

**4.3 Constraints handling**  
*Box*: after each gradient step clip `h` to `[0,1]`.  
*Integral*: enforce `∑h = n/2` exactly by a **water‑filling projection**:
  - Compute excess `Δ = (∑h) - n/2`.
  - Subtract `Δ / m` from all interior entries (`0<h_i<1`) where `m` is their count.
  - Repeat until `|Δ| < 1e‑12`.  
The projection respects both bounds and the equality.

*Symmetry*: after projection enforce mirror symmetry `h = (h + h[::-1])/2`.  

**4.4 Continuation schedule**
