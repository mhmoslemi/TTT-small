**Mathematical reformulation**

For a discretisation with `n` equi‑spaced points (`dx = 2/n`) we have  

\[
C_5(h)=\max_{k}\int h(x)\bigl(1-h(x+k)\bigr)dx
      =dx\;\max_{k}\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i-
                         \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i\,h_{i+k}\Bigr).
\]

Because the integral constraint is \(\sum_i h_i\,dx = 1\) we obtain  

\[
\sum_i h_i = n/2,\qquad
C_5(h)=dx\;\Bigl(1-\min_{k\neq0}A_k(h)\Bigr),\qquad
A_k(h)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i\,h_{i+k}. 
\]

Thus **minimising** `C5` is equivalent to **maximising the minimum autocorrelation**  
\( \displaystyle t(h)=\min_{k\neq0}A_k(h)\).  
All \(A_k\) are bilinear forms; for a fixed shift `k` the gradient of the worst‑shift term is

\[
\frac{\partial A_k}{\partial h_i}=h_{i+k}+h_{i-k},
\qquad
\frac{\partial (dx(1-A_k))}{\partial h_i}=dx\bigl[(1-h_{i+k})-h_{i-k}\bigr].
\]

Only the shift attaining the current maximum of the overlap contributes to the gradient.

**Search methodology – a hybrid Particle‑Swarm‑Memetic algorithm**

   \[
