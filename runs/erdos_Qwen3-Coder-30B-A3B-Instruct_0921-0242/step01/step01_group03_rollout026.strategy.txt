\[

### 2. SDP relaxation

Introduce the lifted matrix variable \(X\succeq0\) and keep the diagonal equal to \(h\):

* **Variables** – \(h\in\mathbb R^N\), \(X\in\mathbb S^N\) (symmetric PSD), scalar \(t\).  
* **Constraints**  
  1. \(X_{ii}=h_i\)  for all i (ensures consistency with a rank‑1 matrix).  
  2. \(\sum_i h_i = N/2\).  
  3. \(0\le h_i\le1\).  
  4. \(\operatorname{tr}(A_k X)\ge t\) for every shift \(k\in\{0,\dots ,N-1\}\).  
  5. \(X\succeq0\).  

* **Objective** – maximise \(t\).  

Dropping the non‑convex rank‑1 requirement yields a convex SDP whose optimum \(t_{SDP}\) gives an **upper bound on the attainable minimum correlation** and therefore a **lower bound on C₅** via  

\[
C_5\le C_{SDP}=dx\Bigl(\tfrac N2 - t_{SDP}\Bigr)=1-\frac{2t_{SDP}}N .
\]

### 4. Rounding to a feasible step function

Two complementary rounding schemes are tried; the best outcome is kept.

#### 4.1 Principal‑eigenvector rounding
* Compute the leading eigenpair \((\lambda_1,v_1)\) of \(X^{SDP}\).

### 5. Deterministic local improvement (swap hill‑climbing)

The objective for a binary vector is  

\[
C_5(h)=dx\max_k\bigl(\tfrac N2-\sum_i h_i h_{i+k}\bigr).
\]

Changing a single 1 ↔ 0 “swap” alters only the terms involving the two positions and the shifts that pair them.  A **full‑correlation** of the current mask is computed once with a real FFT (cost \(O(N\log N)\)).  For a proposed swap \((p,q)\) we recompute the correlation **incrementally** in \(O(N)\) by subtracting the contributions of the old pair and adding those of the new pair; this is cheap for \(N≈150\).

Algorithm:
* Initialise `best_h` with the rounded candidate, `best_c5` its C₅.  
* Loop while time remains (≈ 200 s):
  * Randomly pick a 1‑index `p` and a 0‑index `q`.  
  * Compute the C₅ after swapping `p↔q` using the incremental update.  
  * If the new C₅ is lower, accept the swap and update the correlation array.  
  * Occasionally (every 500 accepted swaps) perform a *full* recomputation to avoid drift.  

The swap search converges quickly to a local optimum; because the objective is the maximum of linear forms, any local optimum for binary masks is often globally optimal for the given N.

The deterministic swap‑search then guarantees a locally optimal binary mask; for this class of problems the local optimum is often global because the feasible set is the set of all binary vectors with fixed weight.  
* **Scalability** – With \(N≈150\) the PSD variable has 11 250 entries; the SDP has ~150 linear constraints (one per shift) and is well within the capabilities of SCS on a single CPU.  If the solver hits the iteration limit, the algorithm automatically falls back to the coarser `N=100` SDP and proceeds with the same rounding pipeline.  
* **Numerical robustness** – All constraints are linear or PSD; floating‑point errors are mitigated by the final bounded‑simplex projection, which forces exact satisfaction of the mass constraint.  The log‑sum‑exp surrogate in the refinement stage uses a small temperature (τ≈1e‑4) to stay close to the true max while preserving differentiability.  
* **Potential pitfalls** – The SDP may return a matrix far from rank‑1, producing a weak rounding.  The eigen‑vector step mitigates this; if the resulting C₅ is still above 0.381 we rely on the swap‑search, which explores the combinatorial space exhaustively enough to reach the target for this N.  If after all phases the bound is still > 0.381, the fallback binary mask (random or block) is returned, guaranteeing a valid solution.
