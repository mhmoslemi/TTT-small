1. **Principal eigen‑decomposition** – compute the dominant eigenvalue `λ₁` and eigenvector `v`.  
2. Form a candidate `ĥ = √λ₁ * v`.  By construction `ĥ` may violate the box and sum constraints.  
3. **Projection onto the capped simplex** – apply the Euclidean projection onto  

   \[
   \{\,h∈[0,1]^n : \sum h_i = n/2 \,\}.
   \]

   The projection is performed by a simple bisection on a Lagrange multiplier θ:
   `h_i = min(1, max(0, ĥ_i - θ))` while adjusting θ until the sum equals `n/2`.  
   This yields a feasible `h_feas`.

4. **Evaluation** – compute `C5_feas = max_k np.convolve(h_feas, 1‑h_feas, mode='full')*dx`.  Store the best `h_feas` found.

The projection step introduces only a tiny loss of the SDP bound, but the resulting `h_feas` is guaranteed feasible.

---

### 6. Local polishing (optional but powerful)
The projected vector can be improved with a **fast projected gradient descent** on the true objective `C5(h)`:

* At each iteration compute the lag `k*` that realises the current maximum overlap (`k* = argmax_k ∫ h (1‑h_shift)`).
* The sub‑gradient of `C5` at a non‑degenerate `k*` is `g_i = (1‑h_{i+k*}) - h_{i‑k*}` (derived from differentiating the overlap expression).  
* Update `h ← h - α g` with a diminishing stepsize (`α = α₀ / √t`), then re‑project onto the capped simplex.
* Perform a few hundred iterations (≈ 500) – each iteration costs `O(n)` thanks to the explicit lag structure.

Because the start point comes from the SDP relaxation, the local phase usually converges quickly to a point with a strictly lower `C5` than the SDP bound.

---

### 7. Multi‑resolution scheme
To keep `n` modest while still exploring finer discretisations:

1. **Coarse pass** – run the SDP with `n=120`.  Keep the best feasible `h`.  
2. **Medium pass** – increase to `n=200` (or `n=256` if time permits) and repeat.  Initialise the SDP with the previous `h` (by linearly interpolating it onto the new grid) to warm‑start the solver.  
3. **Fine pass** – if remaining time > 30 s, run a final polishing on `n=300` using only the cheap projected‑gradient phase (starting from the interpolated `h`).

This hierarchy respects the 600 s limit while allowing a progressive refinement of the bound.

### 8. Time‑budget allocation
* **SDP phase** – ≤ 0.6 · budgetₛ (≈ 360 s).  Split evenly among the chosen grid sizes.  
* **Projection & evaluation** – negligible (< 5 s).  
* **Local polishing** – ≤ 0.3 · budgetₛ (≈ 180 s).  Run in parallel on the best candidate from each grid size.  
* **Safety margin** – keep 5 % of the budget unused to guarantee a return before the timeout.

The code monitors `time.time()` after each major step and aborts early if the remaining budget falls below 0.5 s, ensuring a valid return.
