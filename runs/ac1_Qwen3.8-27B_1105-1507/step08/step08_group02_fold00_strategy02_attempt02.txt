Our optimization strategy will focus on efficiently searching for the minimal value of this expression. I'll outline a detailed implementation plan including step-by-step execution, helper function definitions, and parameter choices.

## 1. Approach and rationale

The central idea of our strategy is to use a combination of:

- **Gradient-based optimization (Mirror Descent)**: For consecutive, localized improvements to the current best sequence.
- **Population-based methods (Genetic Algorithm with crossover)**: For escaping local minima by combining multiple promising solutions.
- **Length variation via Fourier transforms**: To systematically explore sequences of different lengths that might yield a lower objective value.

We believe that while gradient descent is effective at finding locally optimal sequences, it often gets stuck in individual optima. By combining these sequences through crossover, and also exploring different sequence lengths, we hope to achieve significant global optimization.

This approach is inspired by the principle of "divide and conquer" but applied in a continuous space. We maintain a population of sequences, each of which is then refined using mirror descent. The population size and other parameters are dynamically adapted based on progress and time remaining.

## 2. Mathematical specification

### Variables and constraints

- **Representation**: We represent sequences as vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n_+$ with $n$ being the sequence length.
- **Normalization**: Sequences are normalized such that $\sum a_i = 1$ while ensuring all $a_i \geq 0$ and $a_i \leq 1000$.
- **Objective**: The minimal value of $F(a) = 2n \cdot \max(\text{convolve}(a,a)) \cdot \frac{1}{(\sum a_i)^2}$.

Note: While $\frac{1}{(\sum a_i)^2}$ is part of the expression, the normalization by $\sum a_i = 1$ makes the objective dependent only on $n$ and the convolution result.

### The optimization procedure

1. **Initial Step**: 
   - If a parent sequence is available, validate and normalize it.
   - Otherwise, start from a predefined uniform distribution of length 1000.

2. **Gradient Estimation**: 
   - Let $w(t)$ be the sequence at time $t$.
   - Define $f(w(t), i) = \text{contrib}_{ij}(w(t)) = \text{weighted contribution to output } i \text{ from the convolution}$.
   - The gradient is computed as:
     $$g_k = 2 \sum_j w_j \cdot f(w(t), k_{\max})$$
   Here, $f(w(t), k_{\max})$ is computed using the coefficients $w_j$ that are closest to $k_{\max}$.

3. **Mirror Descent Update**:
   - The update rule is:
     $$w_{k+1} = \exp\left(\log w_k - \frac{1}{\beta} g_k\right)$$
   - The normalized vector is then obtained by dividing by its sum.

4. **Population Update via Crossover**:
   - We select a subset $S$ of the population and compute cross product pairs.
   - The crossover operation is defined over the normalized space with similarity scores.

5. **Length Variation**:
   - We control sequence length by padding or truncating based on the convolution's bounds.
