**Mirror Descent with Entropic Mirror:**

The update is:
`a_i^{new} = a_i * exp(-eta * grad_i) / Z`

where `Z = sum_j a_j * exp(-eta * grad_j)` and `grad` is the subgradient of `f(a) = max_k conv(a,a)[k]`.

The subgradient at `a` (when the max is achieved at a unique point `k*`) is:
`grad_i = 2 * a[k* - i]` (for valid `k* - i`).

When the max is achieved at multiple points, we can use any convex combination of the subgradients.

For the "entropic" mirror descent, the step size `eta` should decrease, e.g., `eta = 1/sqrt(t)`.

But in practice, a fixed or slowly decreasing `eta` often works better.

Actually, I realize that the mirror descent with entropic mirror on the simplex is equivalent to the Multiplicative Weights Update Algorithm (MWUA), which is also known as the Hedge algorithm in online learning. This has strong theoretical guarantees (regret bounds) but might converge slowly in practice.

The LP alternating is essentially a "best response" method, which is more aggressive but can oscillate.

A hybrid approach: use LP alternating for a few iterations, then switch to mirror descent for fine-tuning, then switch back.

**Let me also consider the "away-step" Frank-Wolfe method:**

At each iteration:
1. Compute the subgradient `g` at the current point `x`.
2. Find `v = argmin_{i} g_i` (the vertex with the lowest gradient component).
3. Optionally, find `w = argmin_{i in support(x)} g_i` (the "away" vertex).
4. Update: `x_new = (1-t)x + t*v` or `x_new = (1-t)x - t*w` (away step).

The away step can significantly speed up convergence by removing mass from coordinates that are no longer useful.

For our problem, the subgradient is not a simple vector (it's the gradient of the max of bilinear forms), so Frank-Wolfe needs to be adapted.

    n, m = len(a), len(b)

    while size < n + m - 1:
