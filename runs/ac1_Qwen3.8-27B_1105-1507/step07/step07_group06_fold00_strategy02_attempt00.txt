## Approach and Rationale

The central idea is to **target specific constraint supersets** that inherently bound the peak convolution value while **avoiding computational bottlenecks**. Unlike the previous L-BFGS-B and LP-based approaches, here we focus on sequences where coefficients are clustered within narrower windows, thus improving structure efficiency. The key insight is that when coefficients are forced to sum to at most 1.0 while maintaining non-negativity, the sequence's spatial autocorrelation increases but the normalized objective decreases due to the smoothing effect. This modification reduces the peak complexity relative to the squared coefficient binners.

## Mathematical Specification

We work with sequences \(a = (a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_{n-1})\) of length \(n\) (now capped at 1500). The objective is evaluated via a linear convolution:

\[
\text{objective}(a) = \frac{2n \max_{k \in [0, 2n-2]} ( (a * a)[k] ) }{(\sum_{i} a_i)^2 }
\]

However, we normalize \(a\) by ensuring \(\sum a_i = 0.5\) (instead of \(\sqrt{2n} \approx 1.414\)). This reduces the overall scale of coefficients while retaining the same shape, leading to:

\[
\text{objective}(a) = \frac{2n \max (\text{convolve}(a, a)} }{(0.5)^2} = \frac{8n \max (\text{convolve}(a, a)} }
\]

The sequence must satisfy:
1. \(a_i \geq 0\) for all \(i\)
2. \(\sum a_i \leq 0.5\)
3. \(a_i \leq 1000\) (default will enforce)

We can scale any feasible solution to match the sum constraint:
\[
\hat{a}_i = \frac{a_i}{ \sum a_j } \cdot S \quad \text{where} \quad S = 0.5 \cdot \frac{ \sum a_j }{ \sum a_j }
\]
Highly concentrated sequences (\( a_i = 0.5 \) for all \(i\)) yield:
\[
\text{objective}(\hat{a}) = \frac{8n \max(\text{convolve}(\hat{a}))}{0.5^2} = \frac{8n \max(\text{convolve}(a))}{0.25} \quad \text{but then normalized via}\quad a_i \cdot \frac{0.5}{1} = a_i / 2
\]

Therefore, we redefine the objective for normalized (sum=0.5) sequences.
