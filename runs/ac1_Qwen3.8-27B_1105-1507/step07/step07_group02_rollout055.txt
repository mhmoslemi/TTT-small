What if the elements have different weights? Let's say we want to minimize max_k conv(a,a)[k] subject to sum(a) = 1 and a >= 0.

This is related to the "flat spectrum" or "low peak-to-average power ratio" (PAPR) problem in signal processing!

The problem is: find a non-negative sequence a with sum(a) = 1 that minimizes max_k |sum_i a[i]*a[k-i]|.

This is equivalent to finding a non-negative sequence whose power spectrum |A(f)|^2 = |sum_i a[i] e^{-2πif*i}|^2 is as flat as possible, because the convolution is the inverse Fourier transform of |A(f)|^2.

For non-negative sequences, the spectrum is A(f) = sum_i a[i] e^{-2πif*i}, and the convolution is related to |A(f)|^2.

Actually, the convolution a*a has DFT |A(f)|^2 (up to normalization). So minimizing the max of the convolution is the same as minimizing the max of the inverse DFT of |A(f)|^2, which is the same as making |A(f)|^2 as flat as possible (since the max of the inverse DFT is related to the peak of the time-domain signal).

Wait, actually: if c = a*a, then C(f) = |A(f)|^2 (where A is the DFT of a). The max of c is related to how concentrated |A(f)|^2 is. If |A(f)|^2 is flat (constant), then c is a delta, and max(c) is large. If |A(f)|^2 has structure, c can be more spread out.

For a monotonically increasing sequence a[0] <= a[1] <= ... <= a[n-1]:
- The convolution at position k (near the right end) involves products a[i]*a[k-i] where i is small and k-i is large.
- At the rightmost position k=2(n-1): conv = a[n-1]^2.
- At k=2(n-1)-1: conv = 2*a[n-1]*a[n-2].
- At the center k=n-1: conv = sum a[i]*a[n-1-i].

The key question is: which position gives the max?

For a "steeply increasing" sequence (like [1,1,1,...,1,M]), the peak will be at the right end (a[n-1]^2) or near it. For a "gently increasing" sequence (like a constant), the peak is at the center.
