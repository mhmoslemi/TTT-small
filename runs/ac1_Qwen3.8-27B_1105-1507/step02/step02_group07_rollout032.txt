The key insight is: we want to concentrate the "energy" in a way that minimizes the peak autocorrelation. This is related to the theory of "low autocorrelation sequences" or "Golay sequences."

For the optimization, the key observation is:
- The objective is 2n * max(conv(f,f)) / sum(f)²
- For non-negative f, the peak is at some lag k, and we want to minimize it
- The LP approach (previous solution) is good because it directly addresses the constraint conv(f,g) ≤ M
- The gradient approach smooths out the peaks

Let me think about what improvements to make over the previous solution:

1. **Better initialization**: The [a,b,a,b,...] pattern (alternating) seems to be a good starting point. More generally, periodic patterns with low autocorrelation.

2. **Exploit the structure**: Since the objective is scale-invariant, we can fix sum(f) = 1 and minimize 2n*max(conv(f,f)).

3. **Direct optimization of the max**: Use a soft-max approximation that's tuned to the scale of the problem.

4. **Different optimization technique**: Instead of LP + gradient, try:
   - Subgradient method (the max is non-differentiable at ties)
   - Penalty method (add a large penalty for exceeding a threshold)
   - Bilevel optimization
   - Direct coordinate descent on the "active" lags

5. **Key improvement idea**: The LP direction finding is O(n³) per step (solving an LP with n variables and 2n-1 constraints). For n=512, this might be slow. We can replace it with a simpler approach:
   - Compute the gradient of the soft-max of the convolution
   - Use it with a better learning rate schedule
   - Or use the subgradient of the max (which is simply the indicator of where the max is achieved)

6. **Another idea**: Since the maximum is typically at a few lags, we can use the "active set" approach:
   - Identify the lags where conv ≈ max
   - Only optimize to reduce those specific lags
   - This is what the LP does, but we can approximate it
