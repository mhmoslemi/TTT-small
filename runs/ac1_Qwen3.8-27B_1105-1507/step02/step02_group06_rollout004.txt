Let me think about the problem from a different angle. If we think of the sequence as a probability distribution p (normalized so sum = 1), then:
score = 2n * max(p*p) (where * is convolution)

We want to minimize the peak of the self-convolution of a non-negative "distribution" of length n.

The self-convolution p*p represents the distribution of the sum of two independent random variables each distributed as p. We want this sum distribution to be as flat as possible.

This is a well-known problem in probability: "What is the most uniform sum of two iid discrete distributions?"

The answer is related to the Central Limit Theorem - as n grows, the sum distribution approaches a Gaussian, which has a peak. But for finite n, we can do better.

For a continuous uniform distribution on [0,1], the sum of two such variables has a triangular distribution on [0,2], with peak at 1. The peak-to-mean ratio is 2 (triangle peaks at 2x the mean).

For a discrete uniform on {0, 1, ..., n-1}, the sum has a triangular distribution on {0, 1, ..., 2n-2} with peak at n-1. Peak value = n, mean = (n-1)*n^2/(2n-1) ≈ n^2/2. Peak-to-mean ≈ 2.

So for uniform, the score is 2n * n / n^2 = 2 (consistent with earlier).

To beat 2, we need a distribution whose self-convolution has a lower peak. This means we want a distribution that, when convolved with itself, spreads out more.

One idea: use a "bimodal" distribution. If p is concentrated at the two ends, then p*p is concentrated at the edges and center, with a dip in the middle.
