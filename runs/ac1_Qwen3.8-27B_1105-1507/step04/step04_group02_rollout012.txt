Actually, (a ⋆ w)[i] = sum_{j=0}^{n-1} a[j] * w[j+i]

This is the same as the convolution of a with the reversed w, evaluated at a specific position.

Let w_rev[m] = w[2n-2-m] (reversed w, length 2n-1).
Then (a ⋆ w)[i] = sum_j a[j] * w[j+i] = sum_j a[j] * w_rev[2n-2-j-i] = (a * w_rev)[2n-2-i]

where * denotes linear convolution.

So: (a ⋆ w)[i] = linear_conv(a, w_rev)[2n-2-i]

The linear convolution of a (length n) and w_rev (length 2n-1) has length 3n-2.
We need positions 2n-2-i for i=0..n-1, which are positions n-1 to 2n-2.
