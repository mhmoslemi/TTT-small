Let me think about this differently. The problem is equivalent to:
minimize max_k b[k] subject to sum(a) = 1, a ≥ 0

where b = conv(a, a).

By the constraint sum_k b[k] = 1 (since sum(a) = 1), and there are 2n-1 values, the minimum possible max is 1/(2n-1). This is achieved iff all b[k] are equal, which would require a very special sequence.

For non-negative a, can we achieve b[k] = 1/(2n-1) for all k? This would mean the autocorrelation is flat, which is the "ideal" case. For real non-negative sequences of length n, this is generally impossible (it's possible for complex sequences of certain lengths, related to difference sets).

However, we can get close. The question is how close.

One approach: use the linear ramp as a starting point and then optimize to reduce the peak.

Let me think about what modifications to the ramp could help:

1. The peak of the ramp's convolution is at k = n (for 1-indexed) or k = n+1 (for 0-indexed). If we can reduce the value at that peak while not increasing other values, we improve.

2. The value at the peak comes from products a[j]*a[k-j] where both a[j] and a[k-j] are moderately large. To reduce this, we could make the distribution more "peaked" at one end.

3. Alternatively, we could try a non-monotone sequence that's large in the middle and small at both ends (like a semicircle). But this would increase b[n-1] (the center value).
