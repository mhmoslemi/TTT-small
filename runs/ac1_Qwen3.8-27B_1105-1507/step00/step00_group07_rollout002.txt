For the linear convolution, if we zero-pad p to length 2n-1 and take the FFT, then `(p*p)(k) = IFFT(|FFT_padded(p)|^2)(k)`.

The peak of `(p*p)` is related to the "concentration" of `|FFT(p)|^2` in the frequency domain. A flat `|FFT(p)|^2` would give a delta function in the time domain (peak = 1 at k=0). A concentrated `|FFT(p)|^2` (like a sinc) would give a spread-out time-domain signal.

For the uniform distribution, `FFT(p)` is a sinc-like function (concentrated at low frequencies), and `|FFT(p)|^2` is a sinc-squared, which gives the triangular convolution in the time domain.

To make the convolution flatter, we want `|FFT(p)|^2` to be more "spread out" in frequency. This means p should have more high-frequency content, i.e., p should be more "oscillatory" or "structured."

But p must be non-negative! This is the key constraint. A non-negative sequence with large high-frequency content is like a "combs" or "periodic" pattern.
