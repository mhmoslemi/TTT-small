A (v, k, λ) difference set in a group of order v is a subset D of size k such that every non-identity element can be written as a difference of two elements of D in exactly λ ways. This gives a flat autocorrelation.

But we need flat convolution, not autocorrelation. However, if we use a sequence that is "self-dual" in some sense (like a symmetric sequence), the convolution and autocorrelation are related.

Actually, for a sequence f, the convolution f*f at lag k is:
(f*f)[k] = sum_i f[i] * f[k-i]

The autocorrelation at lag m is:

These are different. The convolution reverses one of the sequences.

For a symmetric sequence (f[i] = f[n-1-i]), the convolution equals the autocorrelation (up to a shift).
