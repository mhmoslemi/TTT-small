Actually, I recall that the problem of minimizing the peak of the autocorrelation of a sequence is well-studied. For binary sequences (±1), Barker sequences have optimal properties. For non-negative sequences, the analog would be sequences with 0s and 1s (or other non-negative values).

The key insight is: for a {0,1} sequence of length n with k ones, the convolution at the center is the number of pairs (i, j) with i+j = n-1, a_i = 1, a_j = 1. This is essentially the "overlap" of the sequence with its reverse.

To minimize this overlap while keeping k large, we want the ones to be placed such that when the sequence is reversed, the ones don't align well.

This is similar to the problem of finding a subset S of {0, ..., n-1} that maximizes |S| while minimizing the number of pairs (s1, s2) in S with s1 + s2 = n-1.

For s1 + s2 = n-1, the pairs are (0, n-1), (1, n-2), ..., ((n-1)/2, (n-1)/2). There are ceil(n/2) such pairs. To minimize the number of "hits", we should avoid having both elements of any pair in S.
