- For all k = q + s: c[k] increases by 1
- Also, c[p+p] decreases by 1 (the self-pair) and c[q+q] increases by 1

The change in c[k]:

- c[k] increases by 1 for each s in (S \ {p} ∪ {q}) such that k = q+s

More carefully:
Before: c[k] = |{s ∈ S : k-s ∈ S}| = |{(s, k-s) ∈ S^2 : s + (k-s) = k}|
After: c'[k] = |{s ∈ S' : k-s ∈ S'}| where S' = S \ {p} ∪ {q}

The difference c'[k] - c[k]:
- Terms that were in S^2 but not in S'^2: pairs involving p that don't involve q
  - (p, k-p) if k-p ∈ S and k-p ≠ q: this pair is removed → c[k] decreases by 1
  - (k-p, p) if k-p ∈ S and k-p ≠ q: this pair is removed → c[k] decreases by 1

  - But we also need: the pairs (p, s) where s ∈ S \ {p} are removed, contributing to c[p+s]
