The key insight is: **the sum of all convolution values equals (sum a)^2**. So to minimize the max, we want to spread the "mass" as evenly as possible across the 2n-1 convolution values.

This is analogous to designing a sequence with a flat "energy distribution" across lags, which is related to the concept of "flat top" or "equi-ripple" designs.

Now, for the optimization:
- We want to minimize `max_k conv(a,a)[k]` subject to `sum(a) = S` and `a >= 0`.
- Equivalently (setting S=1): minimize `max_k conv(a,a)[k]` subject to `sum(a) = 1` and `a >= 0`.
- The optimal value is at least `1/(2n-1)` (the average).

This is a minimax problem. It can be formulated as:
minimize M
subject to: conv(a,a)[k] <= M for k=0,...,2n-2
            sum(a) = 1
            a >= 0

This is a quadratically constrained optimization (the constraints are quadratic in a). It's non-convex in general.

However, there's a useful observation: if we fix M and ask "is there a non-negative a with sum 1 and all conv values <= M?", this is the feasibility of a quadratic system.
