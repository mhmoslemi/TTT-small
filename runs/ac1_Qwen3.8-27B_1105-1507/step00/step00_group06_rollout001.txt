Actually, wait. Let me reconsider the mathematical lower bound.

We want to minimize `2n * max_k conv[k] / (sum a)^2` subject to `a[i] >= 0`.

The sum of all conv entries = (sum a)^2.
There are 2n-1 entries.
So max_k conv[k] >= (sum a)^2 / (2n-1).
This gives score >= 2n/(2n-1), which approaches 1 for large n.

But we have additional constraints. In particular:
- conv[0] = a[0]^2
- conv[1] = 2a[0]a[1]
- conv[n-1] = sum_i a[i]a[n-1-i]

The constraint conv[0] = a[0]^2 means if a[0] is large, conv[0] is large. Similarly for the other boundary terms.

Also, conv[n-1] = sum a[i]a[n-1-i]. By Cauchy-Schwarz, this is at most sum a[i]^2. And sum a[i]^2 >= (sum a)^2/n.

So the center term is at most sum a[i]^2, but it could be much smaller.

Actually, I realize the problem is non-trivial. Let me think about it from a different angle.

Consider the problem in terms of the "спекtral" approach. The linear convolution of a with itself can be computed via FFT (with zero-padding to avoid circular aliasing). If we pad a to length N >= 2n-1, then:

`conv = IFFT(FFT(a_padded)^2)`

where a_padded = [a, 0, 0, ..., 0] with N - n zeros.

We want to minimize max(conv) subject to sum(a) = 1 and a >= 0.

In the frequency domain, this is: minimize max_k IDFT_{N}(A^2)[k] subject to A[0] = 1 (since sum(a) = 1) and a >= 0.

This is related to the problem of designing "low peak" sequences, which is important in communications (OFDM PAPR reduction).

The key insight from the literature is that you can't make the peak arbitrarily small. The minimum peak-to-mean ratio for a length-n sequence (in the circular convolution sense) has been studied.

For our problem, the linear convolution peak is what matters, and we're minimizing `2n * peak / sum^2`.
