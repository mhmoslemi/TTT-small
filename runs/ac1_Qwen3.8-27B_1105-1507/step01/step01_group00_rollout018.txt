For a[i] = ((i+1)/n)^p with large p, the sequence is very concentrated near i = n-1. The convolution c[k] = sum_i a[i]*a[k-i] will be dominated by terms where both a[i] and a[k-i] are large, i.e., where both i and k-i are close to n-1.

For c[n-1]: i ranges from 0 to n-1, and we need a[i]*a[n-1-i]. The product is large when both i and n-1-i are close to n-1, which can't happen simultaneously (unless i ≈ n-1 and n-1-i ≈ n-1, i.e., i ≈ n-1 and i ≈ 0, contradiction). So the products are actually moderate.

For c[n-2]: i ranges from 1 to n-1, and we need a[i]*a[n-2-i]. Similarly, the products are moderate.

The key difference is that c[n-1] has one more term than c[n-2]. For a rapidly increasing sequence, the additional term (i=0 or i=n-1) involves a[0] (which is tiny) times a[n-1] (which is 1), so it contributes almost nothing.

So for rapidly increasing sequences, c[n-1] ≈ c[n-2] ≈ ... and the maximum might be at a different lag!
