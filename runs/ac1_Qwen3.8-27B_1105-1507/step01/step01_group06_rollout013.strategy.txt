Our approach leverages a combination of gradient-based optimization and two-dimensional Fourier transforms to navigate the complex step function landscapes. We systematically explore different sequence representations and optimization pathways while respecting the mathematical constraints. The core idea is to generate candidate step sequences through Fourier-based synthesis, then refine these candidates using gradient descent in a massive parameter space but restricted by frequency-domain information.

1. Approach and rationale
We adopt a frequency-domain optimization strategy that:
- Isolates the mathematically critical features (spikes) in the convolution
- Uses analytic gradients rather than numerical differentiation
- Incorporates structured optimization targets based on second derivatives
- Maintains multiple active sequences across different length scales and representations
- Explicitly preserves complementarity relationships during optimization

The rationale is that convolution extrema in step functions are inherently tied to Fourier coefficient interactions. By targeting these interactions directly through optimization, we can achieve faster and more stable convergence without sacrificing mathematical correctness.

2. Mathematical specification
The optimization targets are formally defined as:

   J(a) = 2 * n * max_{0 ≤ k ≤ 2(n-1)} { (a * a)[k] } / (sum_{i} a[i])^2

Where:
- a is a sequence of non-negative real numbers
- n is the sequence length (parameter to be optimized)
- The convolution is performed in full mode (mode="full")
- The numerator must be minimized subject to the denominator being maintained (no scaling)

The optimization uses:
- Second-order gradient projection with finite-difference estimates
- Fourier-filtered updates to preserve mathematical stability
- Weighted norms to control the spread of values
- Hypnagogic resampling to escape local optima
