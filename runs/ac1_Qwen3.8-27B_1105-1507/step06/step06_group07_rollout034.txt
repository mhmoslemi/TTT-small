For continuous functions, the optimal is a uniform distribution (giving a triangular convolution with peak at center). For non-negative discrete sequences, the linear ramp seems to be a good choice because it "spreads out" the mass in a way that the self-convolution is more uniform.

For a general monotonically increasing sequence a[i], the convolution at lag k (for k between n-1 and n) is:
conv[n] = sum_{i=1}^{n-1} a[i]*a[n-i]
conv[n-1] = sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a[i]*a[n-1-i]

If a is monotonically increasing, then a[i]*a[n-i] is a "hill" peaked at i=n/2. The sum is maximized when the individual terms are as large as possible while keeping the sum fixed.

By the rearrangement inequality, for fixed sum, the sum of products a[i]*a[n-i] is maximized when a is as "concentrated" as possible (extreme values) and minimized when a is as "spread" as possible (uniform). But we're looking at the maximum over all lags, not just this one.
