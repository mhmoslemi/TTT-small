After careful consideration, I will now propose a completely new approach that departs from the previous template-based optimizations. The core idea is to model the step function generation as a signal processing problem, specifically optimizing a pulse shape to minimize a certain energy-sidelobe ratio.

### Approach and Rationale
The evaluation function can be interpreted as minimizing the product of sequence length and maximum autocorrelation value relative to the sum of squares. This resembles optimizing a pulse shaping filter in electronic engineering:

1. The sequence acts as filter coefficients
2. The convolution computes the autocorrelation
3. The goal is to minimize the peak autocorrelation for a given energy

The optimal solution likely lies with sequences that are maximally separated in both time and frequency domains. My approach will:

- Formulate the optimization as finding filter coefficients that minimize energy concentration while keeping coefficients nonnegative
- Use iterative inversion of the filter constraint to enforce the length-objective tradeoff
- Focus on creating sequences with minimal time/frequency coupling

### Mathematical Specification
We represent the sequence as filter coefficients {aᵢ} with:
- aᵢ ≥ 0
- ∑aᵢ ≥ 0.01 after endpoint clipping
- The constraint is to minimize: (2 * n * max( (a*a)[k] )) / ( (sum(a))² )

But note: The constraint is equivalent to minimizing the maximum of the normalized autocorrelation. In signal processing terms, we want:

**minimizeₐ f(a) = max(|(a * a)[k]|)**
subject to:
**g(a) = ∑aᵢ = 1**
In the optimal transformation, the autocorrelation peaks are minimized while maintaining normalized energy. This optimization is strongly non-convex.

### Implementation Procedure
Here is the detailed, self-contained plan for generating sequences:

1. **Initialization** (matlab-like approach):
   We'll start from scratch using a randomized initializer with smooth transitions.

2. **Iterative Optimization**:
   Each iteration forces the convolution autocorrelation under specified bounds while maximizing the sum of coefficients (to 'fill' the energy).

3. **Constructive Approach**:
   - We decompose the sequence into orthogonal components to spread the energy more evenly
   - Projection onto a cyclic space reduces autocorrelation peaks dramatically
   - Spectrum shaping via inverse FFT adjustment

4. **Gradient Step**:
   - Use a soft-max gradient descent that automatically sets the temperature based on current energy
   - The damping coefficient depends on the steaminess score of the autocorrelation

5. **Termination**:
   - After each update, validate against the original objective function
   - Update best solution whenever improvement is detected
   - Automatically adapt the search space based on convergence
