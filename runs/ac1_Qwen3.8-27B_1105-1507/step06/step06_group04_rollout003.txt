    n = len(a)

The pattern seems to be: make the sequence "front-loaded" (larger at the beginning). This reduces the peak because:
- conv[0] = a[0]^2 is the peak
- conv[1] = 2*a[0]*a[1] is close
- conv[2] = 2*a[0]*a[2] + a[1]^2 is even lower (a[2] is small)

The key is to make a decreasing sequence where a[0]^2 ≈ 2*a[0]*a[1] ≈ 2*a[0]*a[2] + a[1]^2 ≈ ...

For a geometric sequence a[i] = r^i * (1-r)/(1-r^n):
conv[0] = a[0]^2 = ((1-r)/(1-r^n))^2
conv[1] = 2*a[0]*a[1] = 2*r*((1-r)/(1-r^n))^2
conv[2] = 2*a[0]*a[2] + a[1]^2 = (2*r^2 + r^2)*((1-r)/(1-r^n))^2 = 3*r^2*((1-r)/(1-r^n))^2

For the peak to be at conv[0]: need 1 > 2r > 3r^2 > ...
For r = 0.5: 1 > 1 > 0.75 > ... So conv[0] = conv[1] > conv[2]. Peak at the edge.

For r = 0.4: 1 > 0.8 > 0.48. Peak at conv[0].

So a geometric sequence with r < 0.5 has its peak at conv[0] = a[0]^2.

The objective is 2*n*a[0]^2 (with sum normalized to 1).
a[0] = (1-r)/(1-r^n)
For large n and r < 1: 1-r^n ≈ 1, so a[0] ≈ 1-r.
Objective ≈ 2*n*(1-r)^2.

To minimize: maximize r (subject to r < 0.5 for the peak to be at the edge).
At r = 0.5: objective ≈ 2*n*0.25 = n/2.
