**Approach and Rationale**
The central problem is to minimize the evaluation function \( J(a) = 2n \max(\text{convolve}(a, a)) / \sum a^2 \), where \( n \) is the sequence length. This resembles finding a sequence of coefficients that minimizes autocorrelation at its maximum, which is critical for signal processing applications. I recognized that while the previous program approached the goal well, it was implicitly constrained by fixed initializations and limited hyperparameter tuning. To overcome this, I propose a **domain-specific gradient method** tailored for sparse but informative searches in the autocorrelation space. The idea is to:  
1. **Focus on the maximum autocorrelation value** more directly, since it's the dominant term in \( J(a) \).  
2. **Use Lagrange multiplier techniques** to bound the autocorrelation growth, mapping the optimization to a constrained minimization problem solvable via gradient descent with analytical constraints.  
3. **Truncate and lower the dimensionality** of the problem not just at fixed lengths but proportionally to the improvement, balancing real-time feedback against computational cost, taking advantage of the piecewise linearity of \( \text{convolve}(a, a) \).  

This differentiation from the LP mirrordescent approach stems from its reliance on linear programming iterations, which are inefficient for minimizing highly nonlinear functions under integer and non-negativity constraints. My gradient method, however, respects the API by evaluating sequences directly via \( \text{evaluate\_sequence} \) while leveraging the structure of the problem—specifically, that autocorrelation maxima are often found at symmetric positions relative to a candidate sequence's "active" components.

**Mathematical Specification**  

We define the sequence as a non-negative vector \( a \in \mathbf{R}^n \) (normalized to \( \sum_a = 1 \) for consistency with the provided function). The constraints are \( a_i \geq 0 \) for all \( i \), and \( \sum a = 1 \). The objective is to minimize:  
\[
J(a) = 2n \max(\text{convolve}(a, a)) / (sum_a)^2
\]  
corresponding to the provided evaluation function.

The optimization procedure uses:  
\[
\min_{a} J(a) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad a_i \geq 0, \quad \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i = 1
\]  

We express the constraints separately, allowing the gradient descent to update only the variables that influence the objective, ideally stopping at lengths where the search is no longer beneficial.
