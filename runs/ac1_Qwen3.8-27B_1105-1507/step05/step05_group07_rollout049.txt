**Key observation**: The LP mirror descent step is solving:
max sum(g) subject to A_g * g <= M * 1, g >= 0

where A_g is the matrix with A_g[k,j] = a[k-j] (the convolution matrix). This is a max-flow-like problem or a packing LP.

The dual is:
min M * sum(y) subject to A_g^T * y >= 1, y >= 0

The complementary slackness conditions tell us that at the optimum:
- y[k] > 0 implies (a*g)[k] = M (the constraint is tight)
- g[j] > 0 implies sum_k a[k-j]*y[k] = 1 (the dual constraint is tight)

This gives us a characterization of the optimal g: it's supported on indices j where the "dual price" equals 1.

For the outer loop, the idea is that by moving towards g, we're reducing M (the max convolution value). This is similar to the "multiplicative weights" algorithm or "exponential weights" in online learning.

**Alternative: Multiplicative Weights / Exponential Weights approach**

Instead of the LP mirror descent, we can use:
a_new[i] ∝ a[i] * exp(-eta * (a*a)[i-related terms])

This is the "mirror descent" with the KL divergence as the Bregman divergence. The update is:
a_new[i] = a[i] * exp(-eta * g[i]) / sum_j a[j] * exp(-eta * g[j])

where g is the subgradient of max(conv(a,a)) at a.

The subgradient of max_k (a*a)[k] with respect to a[i] is:
- If k* is the argmax: g[i] = a[k*-i] + a[i-(k*-n+1)] (the terms in (a*a)[k*] that involve a[i])
- More precisely, if the max is achieved at a set S of indices, the subgradient is the average over S.

This is simpler than the LP and might be faster. Let me try this approach.
