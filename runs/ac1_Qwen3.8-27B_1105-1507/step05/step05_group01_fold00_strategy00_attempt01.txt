# Approach and rationale
The central idea is to use a combination of frequency-domain shaping and gradient-based optimization to minimize the normalized peak autocorrelation ratio. Unlike the previous LP-based approach, this algorithm directly manipulates the frequency components of the sequence while using backtracking and validation checks. Here's the breakdown of the approach:

1. **Frequency-Domain Shaping**: 
   We convert the sequence to frequency space and spectroscopically shape its power spectrum to minimize the peak autocorrelation in the time domain. This involves targeting high-frequency components that cause oscillations in the autocorrelation.

2. **Adaptive Initialization**:
   Starting sequences are initialized with various shaped patterns (asymmetric, symmetric, random) but ensuring low initial peaks through early validation.

3. **Iterative Refinement**:
   After initializing with frequency shaping, we perform multiple gradient steps to locally refine the sequence, then repeat the frequency shaping to expand the search space.

4. **Backtracking and Patience**:
   To prevent local minima, we periodically adjust sequence lengths and parameters through explicit length changes, ensuring the search continues even if some optimizations stall.

5. **Objective Validation**:
   Critical candidates are validated with the original `evaluate_sequence` function (not approximated), ensuring that the objective remains competitive.

This approach minimizes the risk of premature convergence while maintaining focus on reducing the peak autocorrelation ratio.

            candidates_with_objectives = [(cand, evaluate_sequence(cand.tolist())) for cand in candidate_set_filtered]
            candidate_sorted = sorted(candidates_with_objectives, key=lambda x: x[1])
            optimal = candidate_sorted[0][0]
            best_obj = candidate_sorted[0][1]
    else:
        optimal = seed_seq
        best_obj = obj_initial
        
    #Returnthebestcandidatefoundwiththegivenobjective
    return optimal, best_obj
