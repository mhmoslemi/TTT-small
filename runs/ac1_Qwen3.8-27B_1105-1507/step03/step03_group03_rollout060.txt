Let me think about what's special about the optimal solution. The LP mirror descent approach from the previous solution is essentially solving:
min max_k conv(a,a)[k] subject to sum(a) = 1, a >= 0

This is a convex optimization problem! (The max of linear functions is convex, and the constraint is linear.) Wait, is conv(a,a)[k] linear in a? No! conv(a,a)[k] = sum_j a[j]*a[k-j] is quadratic in a!

So the problem is: min max_k (sum_j a[j]*a[k-j]) subject to sum(a) = 1, a >= 0. This is NOT convex because the objective is the max of quadratic forms.

However, the LP mirror descent approach from the SOTA paper treats it differently. It fixes a and solves the LP: max sum(g) subject to (a*g)[k] <= M for all k, g >= 0. Here (a*g)[k] = sum_j a[j]*g[k-j] IS linear in g (since a is fixed). So the LP is valid.

Let me also think about the LP mirror descent approach more carefully. The key insight from the SOTA paper is:

At each step:
1. Compute M = max_k conv(a,a)[k]
2. Solve LP: max sum(g) s.t. (a*g)[k] <= M for all k, g >= 0
3. Update: a_new = (1-t)*a + t*g (where g is normalized)

This is essentially a "mirror descent" step in the game-theoretic formulation. The LP gives the best response for the "column player", and the update mixes the current strategy with the best response.

The advantage of this approach is that it naturally handles the non-convexity of the original problem (by alternating between two convex problems).
