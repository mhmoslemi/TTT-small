**New idea: Exploit the structure of the optimal solution.**

At optimality, the convolution should be "flat" in the sense that many positions achieve the maximum. Specifically, if the optimal value is M*, then there should be a set of positions S where conv[k] = M* for k in S, and the sequence a should be such that it "spreads" its mass to equalize the convolution values.

This is analogous to the "equipartition" principle in optimal transport. The sequence a should be such that its "influence" on each convolution position is equalized.

**Practical approach: Alternating minimization with "deflation"**

1. Start with a sequence a.
2. Compute conv = a * a.
3. Find the maximum position k*.
4. "Deflate" the convolution at k* by reducing a at positions that contribute to conv[k*].
5. Compensate by increasing a at positions that don't contribute to conv[k*].
6. Repeat.

This is similar to the "subtractive" approach in image processing.

**Even better idea: Use the dual variables from the LP as guidance.**

When we solve the LP (max sum(g) s.t. a*g <= M, g >= 0), the dual variables tell us which constraints are "tight" (binding). These are the convolution positions where a*g = M. At the next iteration, we should focus on equalizing these positions.
