### Approach and Rationale
**Frequency-Domain to Time-Domain Translation:**
The evaluation function's denominator resembles $\chi^2$ distributions, which can be linked to assuming sequences represent observations with degrees-of-freedom $\nu = 2n$. In statistics, a sequence with minimal $\max(\text{convolve})$ relative to sum can be achieved by a proper Gaussian approximation (which minimizes high-frequency components). 

**Choosing Sequences with Statistical Properties:**
We bridge this by generating sequences with (1) smooth transitions between regions and (2) ordered distribution Almost uniform distribution of energy over indices can prevent accumulation in the convolution's maximum. Such sequences are naturally minimal in spectral impact.

**Bottleneck:** Not all increases might be computed at once, but spectral properties can be leveraged to provide starting points for gradient-based optimization. This approach allows for rapid substantiation that subsequent regions require less computational effort.

### Mathematical Formulation
Given a sequence $a$ (non-negative floats, any length $n$), the objective minimizes:
\[
\text{obj}(a) = \frac{2n \cdot \max(\text{convolve\_full}(a, a))}{(\sum a)^2}
\]

where $\text{convolve\_full}(a, a)$ is the linear convolution mode="full" of $a$ with itself.

> **Equivalence:** Assume $a$ has valid sum (after normalization or clipping), then $\text{obj}(a)$ is defined without modifications. Ignoring normalization steps for optimization derivation, but noting the final sequence must meet the evaluator's criteria.

**Convolution Definition:** Let $b_k = \sum_{i+j=k} a_i a_j$ for $k = 0,1,\dots,2n-2$. Compute $\max(b_k)$.

**Target Strategy:**
Minimize $\text{obj}(a)$ by ensuring $a$ has bounded spectral content and carefully balanced high-degree contributions.
