(p*p)(n-1) = sum_j p(j) p(n-1-j) = sum_j p(j) p(j) = ||p||_2^2

Yes, they're the same for symmetric sequences! So the "central peak" and "edge peak" are the same.

Then what about other k values? For symmetric p, (p*p)(k) = (p*p)(2n-2-k) by symmetry. And (p*p)(k) for k near 0 or 2n-2 is small (only a few terms contribute).

So for symmetric p, the max of (p*p)(k) is achieved at k=0 (= k=n-1 = k=2n-2) and equals ||p||_2^2.

Wait, that means for any symmetric non-negative sequence with sum 1, the score is 2n * ||p||_2^2 >= 2n * (1/n) = 2 (by Cauchy-Schwarz).

So symmetric sequences can NEVER beat 2! The minimum for symmetric sequences is exactly 2, achieved by uniform.

This means the optimal sequence for score < 2 must be ASYMMETRIC!
