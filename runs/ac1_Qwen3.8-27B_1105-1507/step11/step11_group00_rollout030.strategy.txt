# Approach and Rationale
The goal is to minimize the evaluated objective function \(\frac{2n \max(\mathbf{a} * \mathbf{a})}{(\sum \mathbf{a})^2}\), which corresponds to the maximum autocorrelation of the sequence normalized by its total sum. The key observation is that sequences with higher entropy (such as those with non-trivial coefficients) tend to have lower autocorrelations. However, the previous approach suffered from slow convergence near the optimal solution, which may be due to being trapped in local minima that appear arbitrarily favorable but are not globally minimal.

To address this, our strategy will:
1. Use log-density transitions instead of linear ones to better model the underlying distributions.
2. Employ maximum likelihood estimation as the core optimization criterion, interpreting the objective as a negative log-likelihood.
3. Utilize advanced sampling techniques from Bayesian inference to explore better directions.
4. Schedule temperature-dependent updates to phase out suboptimal directions.
5. Allow parameter space to be independent of time, ensuring hyperparameter flexibility.

This approach aims to escape spurious convergence by directly optimizing the negative log-likelihood, rather than relying on fixed schedules of local updates.

# Mathematical Specification
**Variables**: Let the sequence represent a distribution \( \mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^n_{+} \) such that \( \| \mathbf{a} \|_1 = 1 \).

**Objective Function**: The evaluated objective \( f(\mathbf{a}) = \frac{2n \max(\mathbf{a} * \mathbf{a})}{(\sum \mathbf{a})^2} \), but note that after normalization, \( \sum \mathbf{a} = 1 \) so this simplifies to \( 2n \max(\mathbf{a} * \mathbf{a}) \). We model this as analogous to squared error in Bayesian settings.

**Optimization**: We interpret \( \mathbf{a} \) as a density estimate and use Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) for sampling. The negative log-likelihood becomes the minimization criterion.

**Constraints**: The sequence must satisfy \( \mathbf{a} * \mathbf{a} \le \text{bound} \) in expectation if normalized, which we enforce via uniform bound constraints.
