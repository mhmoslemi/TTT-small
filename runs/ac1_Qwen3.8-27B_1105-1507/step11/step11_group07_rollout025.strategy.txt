## 1. Approach and rationale

We propose a fundamentally different optimization approach inspired by spectral methods. While prior methods relied on iterative refinement through gradient descent or linear programming, our algorithm leverages the connection between step function properties and polynomial multipliers. 

For a given sequence `a` of nonnegative numbers, the evaluation metric is equivalent to `2n / (sum(a))^2 * max_k sum_{i=0}^{n-1} sum_{j=0}^{n-1-1} a_i * a_j`. By the Cauchy-Schwarz theorem, we can bound this value using the sequence's spectral properties.

Our method involves:
1. Representing sequences in the Fourier domain (0 to π)
2. Using randomized orthogonal projection to satisfy constraints
3. Employing convex optimization via the method of multipliers
4. Maintaining a sequence representable by product of two functions
5. Using an extremely accelerated gradient descent

This approach is more globally aware than local refinement techniques, targeting the optimal spectral structure directly. We represent the sequence as `g(x)` in the Fourier space and minimize the max-convolution value through constrained spectral optimization.

Our algorithm cleverly balances these constraints by approximating the sequence Fourier domain and then turning the optimization into polynomial coefficient adjustment.

The key innovation is that we think of the sequence as a product of two functions and optimize accordingly, maintaining the norm while reducing the max-convolution. We provide a robust convergence fallback and leverage iterative refinement.
