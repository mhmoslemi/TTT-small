1. Approach and rationale:
   The central idea is to address the Erdős minimum overlap problem by systematically improving upon the initial solution through multi-resolution optimization and curve reconstruction. The bottleneck in the current approach is its heavy reliance on local search methods, which often get trapped in suboptimal regions. To overcome this, we introduce a hierarchical framework that:
   - Uses grid refinement to better capture correlation behavior at different scales
   - Employs analytic curve reconstruction from binary templates to generate smooth candidates
   - Implements simulated tempering to break out of local minima across resolutions
   - Combines gradient-based and combinatorial optimization phases

   The selection rationale includes:
   - The overlap objective is non-convex and highly multimodal
   - The function shows better performance at grid points near multiples of π
   - Multi-scale analysis can identify correlation features while minimizing computational cost
   - Smooth templates tend to reduce discontinuous correlation spikes

   Limitations include:
   - Numerical stability constraints require explicit maximum bounds
   - Phase transitions in the search space remain challenging
   - Statistically significant improvement requires hundreds of function evaluations

2. Mathematical specification:

   Let h(x): [0,2] → [0,1] be a step function with weighted integral 1. We minimize:
     C₅ = max_{k ∈ Z} ∫₀² h(x)(1−h(x+k)) dx

   Constraints:
     ∫₀² h(x) dx = 1
     0 ≤ h(x) ≤ 1

   We use a three-stage framework:
     A. Template generation: Exponential basis functions ℓ_j(x) = exp(−x²/(σ_j²)) shifted to [0,2]
     B. Binary representation: h(x) = F(∑ₙ βₙ ℓₙ(x)) clipped to [0,1]
