1. Approach and rationale

The current approach relies on multi-stage optimization with greedy methods and simulated annealing, which often gets stuck in local optima. The new approach uses a population-based genetic algorithm (GA) to explore the binary solution space more thoroughly. The central idea is to initialize a population of binary vectors with sum constraint and evolve them through crossover and mutation while enforcing the fixed sum. The GA helps escape local optima and discover better solutions than the parent by exploring a wider search space. The algorithm is simple yet effective for this non-convex optimization problem.

2. Mathematical specification

- Representation: Binary vectors of length n points, with exactly n/2 ones (since `sum(h) * dx = 1` and dx=2/n, so `sum(h)=n/2`).
- Objective: Minimize `max_k ∫ h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx` (computed via FFT correlation).
- Constraints: `0 <= h[i] <= 1` for all i.
- Repair: Maintain fixed sum by swapping one and zero when sum is violated.
- Crossover: Single-point with probability p_c.
- Mutation: Bit flip with probability p_m, followed by repair.
- Fitness: Returns the maximum overlap value from FFT correlation.
