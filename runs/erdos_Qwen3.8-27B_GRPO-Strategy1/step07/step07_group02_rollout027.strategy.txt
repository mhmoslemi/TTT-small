1. Approach and rationale
The central idea is to address the Erdős minimum overlap problem by solving a non-convex optimization problem through a multi-resolution hybrid framework. The bottleneck is escaping local minima in the high-dimensional space of step functions. The proposed approach combines three phases:

- Coarse-to-fine optimization: Starts with a low-resolution version (n=80) generated by a Fourier-Granger neural network to escape poor local optima
- Continuous refinement: Uses exponential basis functions solved with projected dampened conjugate gradient to explore continuous space
- Binary cleanup: Properly thresholds and applies graph partition operations to enforce binary representation

This differs from the provided parent by using endogenous and dimension-independent templates (Fourier-Granger network vs. interpolation), separating optimization/representation roles, and formalizing the repair constraints. The Fourier-Granger model allows operations independent of resolution, enabling multi-resolution optimization without complex interpolation.

2. Mathematical specification
The optimization minimizes:
$$C_5 = \max_k \iint_{|x-y|=k} h(x)(1-h(y))  dx dy$$
subject to:
$$\int_0^2 h(x) dx = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad h(x) \in [0,1]$$

Representation:
- Phase1: Fourier-Granger template embedding h(x) = exp(-x*σ) * (1-exp(-(2-x)*σ))
- Phase2: Exponential basis functions φ_j(x) = exp(-(x-τ_j)/ζ) / (1+exp(-(x-τ_j)/η))
- Phase3: Binary enforcement via graph-based partition

Constraint handling:
- During continuous optimization: Projection to [0,1] with normalized moments
- After optimization: Sort-based thresholding preserving normalized cumulative sum
- Integral enforcement: Binary template repair with quantization-based sorting

    cdf = compute_cdf_to_integral(h)
    n_pos = int(np.sum(h))

def enforce_binary(h):

    indices = np.argsort(h)
    h_binary[indices[:n_zero]] = 0.0
    h_binary[indices[n_zero:]] = 1.0
