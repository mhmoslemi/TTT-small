### 1. Approach and rationale  
**Central idea**: We address the Erdos minimum overlap problem by using a coordinate ascent method combined with probabilistic thresholding. The key insight is that high minimizers often exhibit sparse structures with bimodal amplitude distributions (peaks at 0 and 1). This approach balances the trade-off between local refinement and global exploration by iteratively optimizing coordinates that maximize the minimal correlation peak while dynamically adjusting the target distribution of active coefficients.  

**Why this could improve**:  
- Current methods rely heavily on gradient-based local optimization, which gets stuck in shallow local optima.  
- Our row-wise coordinate ascent avoids overwhelming gradient violations and better navigates the non-convex loss landscape.  
- The harmonic thresholding mechanism targets the fundamental frequencies of the correlation structure, promoting cleaner oscillatory patterns needed for minimal overlap.  
- Breaking the optimization into discrete steps ensures monotonic progress toward the global minimum.  

**Bottleneck addressed**:  
The previous implementation treated all coordinates uniformly during gradient descent, leading to suboptimal local solutions. We introduce a structured optimization path that adaptively focuses on regions contributing most to the maximum correlation penalty. Additionally, the probabilistic element guards against premature convergence while still allowing rigorous exploitation steps.  

### 2. Mathematical specification  
**Representation**:  
- Discretize [0,2] into uniform grid with step size dx = 2/N  
- Represent h as real-valued vector → projected to binary space via probabilistic thresholding

**Optimization formulation**:

**Decomposition schedule**:

- Threshold selection: dynamically compute optimal sigmoid threshold based on local gradient magnitudes
