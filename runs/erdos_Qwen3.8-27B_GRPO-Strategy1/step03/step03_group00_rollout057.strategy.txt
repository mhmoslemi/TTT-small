1. Approach and rationale
We introduce a hierarchical optimization strategy that leverages non-convex optimization on multiple resolutions to escape local optima. The approach combines three phases:

1) Low-resolution global exploration with differential evolution
2) Medium-resolution local refinement using coordinate-wise optimizati
3) High-resolution polishing with geometric interpolation

The central idea is to first explore the solution space broadly at lower resolutions to identify promising regions, then refine these regions at medium resolutions to escape local optima, and finally translate the best candidate to high resolution with minimal perturbation. This multi-resolution strategy mitigates the curse of dimensionality while maintaining strong convergence properties. The method features innovative use of hierarchical interpolation (from coarse to fine) and adaptive penalty weighting in the objective function to better handle integral constraints and correlation terms.

2. Mathematical specification
- Domain: h(x) defined on [0,2] with x discretized to 2^N grid points
- Constraints: ∫₀² h(x)dx = 1, 0 ≤ h(x) ≤ 1
- Objective: C5 = max_k ∫₀² h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx
- At resolution level K:
    - FEASIBLE: h ∈ [0,1]^M where M=2^K
    - PROBLEM: Function mapping (h, K) → C5(h, K)
    - SEARCH SPACE:  M-dimensional arrays with values in [0,1] and sum= M/2 (from integral constraint)

The optimization proceeds in three hierarchical stages:
1. Low resolution (K=6): Population-based exploration with DE
2. Medium resolution (K=8): Cayley-stationary optimization
3. High resolution (K=9): Geometric refinement

The projection operation maintains constraints:
- Unit sum projection: φ(h) = h / (1 + ε) * min(1, max(0, (M/2 - sum(h)) / (M - sum(h)))) for h not summing to M/2 within ε tolerance
