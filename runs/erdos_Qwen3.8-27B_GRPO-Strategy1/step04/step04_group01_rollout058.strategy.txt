### 1. Approach and Rationale

The current solution uses coordinate descent and local optimization, which often gets stuck in local minima, leading to plateaued performance near the existing record. An improved approach is needed to explore the space more efficiently and escape these local optima. Simulated annealing is chosen for its ability to balance exploration and exploitation: it finds good local solutions by fine-tuning around promising regions but also accepts worse solutions with a probability that decreases over time, allowing escapes from local minima. This is combined with a binary sequence assumption, as binary functions (h_i = 0 or 1) are known to achieve lower overlap values in practice and simplify the computation due to the discrete nature of the problem. The rationale is that binary sequences have been successfully used in combinatorial optimization for similar autocorrelation minimization problems, and the constraints (sum h_i = n/2, h_i ∈ {0,1}) align perfectly. The key bottleneck to address is generalization to the continuous case without harming performance, but binary is expected to suffice.

Limitations: 
- Binary sequences assume only two states, potentially missing the optimal continuous solution. However, since C5 is scale-invariant and the problem is discretized, binary is likely optimal or near-optimal.
- The objective involves a max function, which is non-differentiable, making gradient methods challenging. The smooth approximation handles this, but binary simplifies computations.
- Initialization quality matters; we rely on a heuristic starting point from the initial_h_values, which may not cover all regions.

Differences from previous strategies:
- Instead of multiple local optimizations, simulated annealing with global exploration replaces the phase of coordinate descent and SLSQP.
