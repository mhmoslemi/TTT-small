Approach and rationale

Our approach replaces the previous optimization sequence with a multi-resolution method based on periodic sequences. We generate periodic candidates optimized for shifts by leveraging binary sequences with minimal autocorrelation and then refine them using continuous optimization. This strategy addresses the high dimensionality issue in the original Adam-based approach by combining structure-based initialization with targeted perturbations. The key bottleneck was the limited exploration of shift-invariant structures, which formed the basis of our improvement.

The Erdős minimum overlap problem requires minimizing the maximum overlap between h(x) and its shifted versions across all lags. By exploiting the periodicity and autocorrelation properties, we can construct patterns that naturally minimize cross-correlations. We start by generating binary sequences with minimal autocorrelation (like Gold codes), then iteratively perturb them using gradient information from continuous optimization to lower the objective without prematurely converging to local minima.

Mathematical specification

We represent h on [0,2] with N=sample points. The objective is:
$$ \max_l \left| \int_0^2 h(x) \cdot (1-h(x+l)) dx \right| $$
Discretized for computation:
$$ C_5 = \frac{2}{N} \max_{k=0}^{2N-2} [ \text{np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode='full')[k]} ] $$

The optimization sequence:
1. Start with a periodic pattern of length L dividing N
