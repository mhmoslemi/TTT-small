If h[i] changes from old to new:
- Term j=i: if i+q in [0,n): change = (new-old)*(1-h[i+q])
- Term j=i-q: if i in [0,n) (always) and i-q in [0,n): change = h[i-q]*(0-(new-old)*(1-1))... 

No wait. The term j=i-q gives h[i-q]*(1-h[i]). Change: h[i-q]*((1-old)-(1-new)) = h[i-q]*(new-old).

So:
Δcorr[q] = (new-old)*[(1-h[i+q])*[i+q∈[0,n)) + h[i-q]*[i-q∈[0,n)]] (for q≠0)
For q=0: the term is h[i]*(1-h[i]), change = new(1-new) - old(1-old)

This means I can update the correlation in O(number of affected shifts) = O(n) per coordinate change!

This is a HUGE speedup. Instead of O(n²) per evaluate, it's O(n).
