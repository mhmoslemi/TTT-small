Actually, I realize there's a subtlety with np.correlate. Let me verify the indexing:
- For arrays a, v of length n:
  - np.correlate(a, v, mode='full') has length 2n-1
  - The element at index k (0-based) corresponds to:
    sum_{m} a[m+k-(n-1)] * v[m] for valid m
  
  Actually, the NumPy docs say:
  c[k] = sum_n a[n+k] * conj(v[n])
  
  For mode='full', k ranges from -(len(v)-1) to len(a)-1.
  The output array is indexed 0 to 2n-2, where:
  - Output index k corresponds to lag k - (n-1)
  
  So:
  - Index 0: lag = -(n-1), which is a[n-1+k] * v[k]... 
  
  Actually let me just think of it as: the full correlation output has 2n-1 elements. The middle element (index n-1) is the zero-lag correlation: sum h[i]*(1-h[i]). Elements to the right are positive lags, elements to the left are negative lags.

  For our purposes, the exact lag interpretation matters for the targeted CD, but not for computing C5 (which just takes the max).

Let me simplify and just use the index directly. The key insight for targeted CD: if the max is at correlation index k_idx, then the "lag" is k_idx - (n-1). For a positive lag L (meaning h is shifted right by L), the correlation involves pairs (i, i+L). For negative lag -L, it involves pairs (i+L, i).
