The central idea is to combine global exploration with local refinement to escape local optima and find a lower Erdős constant C5. Unlike previous methods relying on projected gradient descent, our approach uses simulated annealing (SA) for global optimization and polynomial chaos expansion (PCE) as a surrogate model to approximate the expensive-to-evaluate C5 function. The PCE surrogate reduces the cost of function evaluations, enabling more global exploration. We initialize our search from multiple diverse points, including the provided parent solution, to enhance robustness against local minima. The key bottleneck addressed is the high computational cost of evaluating the max-overlap integral accurate, which can be approximated reliably by PCE based on Chebyshev expansions.

    - **Objective**: C5 is defined as the maximum cross-overlap integral:
