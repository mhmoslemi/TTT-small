When $j = j_{\text{start}}$: $g_{j_{\text{start}}} = 2 a[k^* - j_{\text{start}}] = 2 a[k^* - \max(0, k^*-n+1)]$

So $g = [2a[k^*], 2a[k^*-1], \ldots, 2a[0], 0, \ldots, 0]$

$\min_a \max_k q_k(a)$ s.t. $a \geq 0, \sum a = 1$

And $q_k(a)$ is a quadratic form, this is a non-convex optimization problem. But we can use the following iterative scheme:

1. At each iteration, identify the "active" lag $k^*$ where the max occurs.
2. Minimize $q_{k^*}(a) = a^T H_{k^*} a$ subject to $a \geq 0, \sum a = 1$ (ignoring the other constraints temporarily).
3. This is a quadratic program (QP) which can be solved efficiently.

Step 2 is a trust-region-like approach. The QP is:
$\min_a a^T H_{k^*} a$ s.t. $a \geq 0, \sum a = 1$

The key constraint is $a_i \geq 0$. If we were allowed negative values, we could use sequences with negative sidelobes (like Barker sequences) to get much lower peak autocorrelation. But with $a_i \geq 0$, we're limited.

For nonnegative sequences, I believe the optimal shape is a monotone sequence (either increasing or decreasing). The reason is that the "anti-alignment" of the sequence with its reverse (which minimizes the center autocorrelation) is achieved by monotone ordering.
