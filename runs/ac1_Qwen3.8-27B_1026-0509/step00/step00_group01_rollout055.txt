Let me think about it differently. The convolution c[k] = sum_j a[j]*a[k-j] is the "autocorrelation" in a sense. For nonnegative a, all c[k] >= 0.

The sum of all c[k] is S². There are 2n-1 values. If we could make them all equal to S²/(2n-1), the max would be S²/(2n-1) and the score would be 2n/(2n-1) ≈ 1. But this is not achievable because the convolution of a nonnegative sequence has structure.

The key constraint is: c[k] >= 0 for all k, and c is the self-convolution of a nonnegative sequence.

For a "reasonably spread" sequence, c[k] is largest near k=n-1 (the "center" of the convolution). The maximum is at least c[n-1] = sum_j a[j]*a[n-1-j].

By Cauchy-Schwarz: c[n-1] <= (sum a[j]²)^{1/2} * (sum a[n-1-j]²)^{1/2} = sum a[j]².

And sum a[j]² >= S²/n. So c[n-1] <= sum a[j]², but this doesn't give a lower bound on c[n-1].

Actually, c[n-1] can be much smaller than sum a[j]² if a[j] and a[n-1-j] are "anti-correlated". But since both are nonnegative, they can't be truly anti-correlated. The minimum of c[n-1] = sum_j a[j]*a[n-1-j] given sum a[j] = S and a[j] >= 0 is... 0 (if a[j] and a[n-1-j] have disjoint support, e.g., a = [1,1,0,...,0] for even n). But then other c[k] values are large.

I think the optimization is non-trivial but the uniform sequence is likely near-optimal for large n. The previous program achieved 2.0, which is the uniform sequence score.
