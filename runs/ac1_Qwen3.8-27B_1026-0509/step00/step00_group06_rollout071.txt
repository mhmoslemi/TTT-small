7. Repeat until convergence or time runs out.

The key question: does this reduce M?

If the LP gives b* with sum(b*) > S (the sum of a), then normalizing b* to sum S gives a sequence with conv(a, b_norm) <= M * S/sum(b*) < M. So the "cross-convolution" is reduced.

And the update a_new = (1-t)a + t*b_norm gives:
conv(a_new, a_new) = (1-t)^2 conv(a,a) + 2t(1-t) conv(a, b_norm) + t^2 conv(b_norm, b_norm)
<= (1-t)^2 M + 2t(1-t) M * S/sum(b*) + t^2 M_b

where M_b = max(conv(b_norm, b_norm)).

If t is small, this is approximately M * ((1-t)^2 + 2t(1-t)*S/sum(b*)) which is < M if S/sum(b*) < 1 (which it is, since sum(b*) > S).

So the LP approach should reduce M (and hence the score).
