Key insight: the objective $\phi(a) = \frac{1}{\beta} \log \sum_k e^{\beta (a*a)[k]}$ is 2-homogeneous in $a$ (scaling $a$ by $c$ scales $\phi$ by $c^2$). The gradient is also 2-homogeneous: $\nabla \phi(ca) = c^2 \nabla \phi(a)$.

For a step $a_{new} = a - \eta \nabla \phi(a)$, the change in the objective is approximately:
$\phi(a - \eta \nabla \phi) \approx \phi(a) - \eta \|\nabla \phi\|^2 + \frac{\eta^2}{2} \nabla^2 \phi[\nabla \phi, \nabla \phi]$

For the step to decrease the objective, we need $\eta < 2/\lambda_{max}$ where $\lambda_{max}$ is the largest eigenvalue of the Hessian.

The Hessian of $\phi$ at a point where the max is achieved at a single lag $k^*$ is:
$\nabla^2 \phi \approx \beta \cdot \nabla^2 (a*a)[k^*] + (1-\beta \cdot \text{var}) \cdot \nabla^2 (a*a)[k^*]$... this is getting complicated.
