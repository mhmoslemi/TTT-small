Actually, this is related to the problem of finding a sequence whose auto-convolution is flat. This is equivalent to finding a sequence a such that a*a (convolution) = constant. In the z-domain, this means A(z)^2 = C * (1 + z + z^2 + ... + z^{2n-2}), which means A(z) = √C * √(1 + z + z^2 + ... + z^{2n-2}).

The square root of a polynomial is generally not a polynomial, so an exact solution doesn't exist for finite n. But we can get approximately flat.

The sequence b[i] I'm computing is the Taylor coefficients of (1+z+z^2+...+z^{2n-2})^{1/2} = ((1-z^{2n-1})/(1-z))^{1/2}.

For large n, this approaches (1-z)^{-1/2} = sum C(2k,k)/4^k * z^k.

And the sum of b[k] from k=0 to n-1 ≈ sum_{k=0}^{∞} C(2k,k)/4^k = (1-1)^{-1/2} = ∞.
