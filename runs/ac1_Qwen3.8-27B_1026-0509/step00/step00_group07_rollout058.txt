Let me try a different approach. What if I use a sequence that's 1 on a carefully chosen subset of positions?

For a binary sequence (0s and 1s) of length n with k ones, the convolution peak is at most k (when all ones are at the same position, but they can't be since it's a set). The peak of the convolution of a 0-1 sequence is the maximum number of "pairs at distance d" over all distances d. This is related to the "addition table" of the set.

If the set S of ones forms a "Sidon set" or has the property that all pairwise differences are distinct, then the autocorrelation is very flat. But we want the CONVOLUTION, not correlation.

For the convolution: (1_S * 1_S)[k] = |{a,b in S : a+b = k}|. This is the number of representations of k as a sum of two elements of S. We want this to be as uniform as possible.

If S = {0, 1, 2, ..., k-1} (a contiguous block), then (1_S * 1_S)[m] = m+1 for m < k, and 2k-1-m for m >= k. This is the classic triangle. Peak = k, and score = 2n*k/k² = 2n/k. To minimize, maximize k, giving k=n and score=2.

If S is "spread out" (like a Golomb ruler), the sums a+b are more spread out, making the convolution flatter. But with n positions and k ones, the sums range from 0 to 2(n-1), giving 2n-1 possible values. We want to distribute the ~k²/2 sums (each pair (a,b) with a≤b contributes) across these 2n-1 values as evenly as possible.

For k ones in n positions, total sum of convolution = k². Spread over 2n-1 values, mean = k²/(2n-1). Peak ≥ k²/(2n-1). Score ≥ 2n*k²/(2n-1)/k² = 2n/(2n-1) ≈ 1.

But achieving this lower bound requires the convolution to be perfectly flat, which is generally impossible with nonnegative (or 0-1) sequences.
