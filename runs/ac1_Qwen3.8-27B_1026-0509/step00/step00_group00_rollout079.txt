Actually, let me reconsider the problem from the perspective of the LP.

The LP is: maximize sum(g) subject to (f*g)_k <= M, g >= 0.

If f is uniform with value c, then M = n*c^2, and the constraint is c * window_sum(g, k) <= n*c^2, i.e., window_sum(g, k) <= n*c for all k. The tightest constraint is k=n-1 (full window): sum(g) <= n*c = S. So the LP gives sum(g) = S, and g = uniform (by symmetry).

If f is non-uniform, the LP is more interesting. The constraint (f*g)_k <= M means that the weighted sum of g (with weights f) at each lag k is bounded. The LP will find g that "exploits" the low-weight regions of f.

For example, if f has a dip at index i, then g can be large at index i (because f_i is small, so f_i * g_i contributes little to every (f*g)_k).

So the LP solution g will be "spiky" in the valleys of f. The update f = (1-t)f + tg will then create a more "uniform" f (since the spikes in g fill in the dips in f).

This suggests that the LP iteration should converge to a sequence where the "effective weights" are as uniform as possible, which would flatten the convolution.
