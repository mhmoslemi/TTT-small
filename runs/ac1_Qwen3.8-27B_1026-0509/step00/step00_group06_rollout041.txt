Alternative approach: **Alternating minimization / active set method**

The idea from the "state of the art" described in the problem: solve a LP at each step to find the best direction. But the LP has quadratic constraints, making it non-convex.

Another idea: **Mirror descent / exponential weights**

Since we're on the simplex ($a \geq 0, \sum a = 1$), mirror descent with the negative entropy potential gives:
$a_i^{t+1} \propto a_i^t \cdot \exp(-\eta_t \cdot g_i^t)$

This is multiplicative weight updates! Each iteration:
1. Compute subgradient $g^t$
2. Update: $a_i \leftarrow a_i \cdot \exp(-\eta_t \cdot g_i / \max(g))$
3. Renormalize

This tends to be more stable than additive updates and naturally maintains non-negativity.

Let me also consider: what if I use a **penalty method** instead of a hard max?

Define: $f(a) = 2n \cdot \frac{1}{L}\log\sum_k e^{L \cdot c_k} / (\sum a)^2$ where $c_k = (a*a)[k]$ and $L$ is a large lagrange multiplier. As $L \to \infty$, this approaches the max.

The gradient of this smooth function is well-defined and can be computed efficiently. The challenge is computing the softmax over $2n-1$ values, but that's O(n).

Actually, for the smooth approximation:
$\frac{\partial}{\partial a_i} \left[\frac{1}{L}\log\sum_k e^{L c_k}\right] = \frac{\sum_k e^{L c_k} \cdot \frac{\partial c_k}{\partial a_i}}{\sum_k e^{L c_k}} = \sum_k p_k \cdot \frac{\partial c_k}{\partial a_i}$

where $p_k = e^{L c_k} / \sum_j e^{L c_j}$ is the softmax distribution over convolution positions.

And $\frac{\partial c_k}{\partial a_i} = 2a[k-i]$ (as derived).

So the gradient is: $\nabla_i = 2\sum_k p_k \cdot a[k-i]$

This is a weighted sum of shifted copies of $a$, with weights given by the softmax of the convolution values. For large $L$, this concentrates on the argmax.

This is computable in O(n^2) naively, but for large $L$ it's O(n) (only one shift matters). For moderate $L$, we can use FFT.

Actually, $\sum_k p_k \cdot a[k-i]$ is a convolution of $p$ and $a$! So we can compute it with FFT in O(n log n).

This gives us a smooth optimization problem with:
- O(n log n) per gradient evaluation
- Well-defined gradient (no subgradient issues)
- Amenable to L-BFGS or Adam

Let me use this approach with a moderately large $L$ (say $L = 50$ or $100$), and use a simple optimization method.
