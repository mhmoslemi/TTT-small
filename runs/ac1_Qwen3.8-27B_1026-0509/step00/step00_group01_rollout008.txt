    """Return sparse matrix A such that A[j, i] = f[lags[j] - i] if valid."""

The key insight: the LP is linear in g because A is built from the current f. The constraint A@g <= M means conv(f, g)[k] <= M at the active lags. This is the "first-order" condition: if we increase f in the direction of g, the linear part of the autocorrelation peak doesn't increase.

For a small step, the new autocorrelation is:
conv((1-t)f + tg, (1-t)f + tg)[k] = (1-t)^2 conv(f,f)[k] + 2t(1-t) conv(f,g)[k] + t^2 conv(g,g)[k]

The LP ensures the middle term doesn't exceed M (at the active lags). So for small t:
new_peak ≈ (1-t)^2 M + 2t(1-t) M + t^2 * conv(g,g)_peak
= (1-t+t^2*M/M... 

Let me just compute: at the peak lag k*:
new = (1-t)^2 * M + 2t(1-t) * M + t^2 * conv(g,g)[k*]
= M(1-t)^2 + 2t(1-t)M + t^2 * conv(g,g)[k*]
= M[(1-t)^2 + 2t(1-t)] + t^2 * conv(g,g)[k*]
= M(1-t^2) + t^2 * conv(g,g)[k*]
= M - t^2(M - conv(g,g)[k*])
