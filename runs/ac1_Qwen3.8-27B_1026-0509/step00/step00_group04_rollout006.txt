Let me also consider: what if I just use a very specific analytical construction?

For the "ideal" sequence that makes the convolution perfectly flat, we'd need:
sum_j a[j]*a[k-j] = C for all k.

This is a system of 2n-1 quadratic equations in n unknowns. For large n, 2n-1 > n, so it's overdetermined and generally has no solution. But we can find a least-squares solution.

Alternatively, in the frequency domain: DFT(a*a) = DFT(a) * DFT(a) = |DFT(a)|^2 (for real a). A perfectly flat convolution means a flat power spectrum (white noise). So we want |DFT(a)|^2 to be as flat as possible, which means |DFT(a)| is constant. This is the "constant modulus" condition, related to Zadoff-Chu sequences and Barker codes.

But for LINEAR (not circular) convolution, the DFT relationship doesn't hold exactly. The linear convolution is related to the circular convolution via zero-padding.

For large n, if we take a to be approximately "white" in the sense that its DFT is flat, the linear convolution will be approximately flat in the middle (the "bulk" region) but will have triangular edge effects.

One practical approach: use a window function that's approximately flat in frequency. A Gaussian window has a Gaussian frequency response. A rectangular window has a sinc frequency response. A Hamming/Hanning window has a more concentrated main lobe.

Actually, for our purpose, we want the convolution to be flat, which means we want the autocorrelation to be a delta (for circular convolution) or as close to it as possible.
