Actually, let me think about this problem from a different angle. What sequences achieve low peak-to-average ratio in autocorrelation?

In the signal processing literature, this is related to "Cauchy" or "radar" sequences. The key result is:
- For a sequence of length n with values in [0,1], the minimum peak autocorrelation is related to the Welch bound.
- The Welch bound says: max|b[m]| (for m≠0) ≥ (T-K)/T · |b[0]|/K for a K-sequence of length T.

But our problem is different: we want to minimize the PEAK (including the zero-lag) relative to the sum.

Actually, for our problem, the "zero lag" in the full convolution is at m = n-1, and b[n-1] = Σ a[i]² ≥ (Σa[i])²/n = S²/n by Cauchy-Schwarz. So b[n-1] ≥ S²/n, and score ≥ 2n·S²/n/S² = 2.

The Welch bound and related results give:
For a sequence of length n, max|m|≠0 |b[m]| ≥ S²/(2n-1)·(something).

But we're looking at max over ALL m, including m=0 and m=2(n-1).

b[0] = a[0]², b[2(n-1)] = a[n-1]². These are single elements squared.

If a is roughly uniform, b[0] = a[0]² ≈ (S/n)² = S²/n², which is much smaller than S²/(2n-1) ≈ S²/(2n) for large n.

The main peak is at m = n-1 (the "zero lag" in autocorrelation terms), where b[n-1] = Σ a[i]·a[n-1-i].

For a uniform sequence, b[n-1] = n·(S/n)² = S²/n. The "average" b[m] is S²/(2n-1). So the peak is about (2n-1)/n ≈ 2 times the average.

To reduce the score from 2 to 1.5, we need b[n-1] to be about 0.75·S²/n instead of S²/n.

This means we need to reduce the "zero-lag" autocorrelation by 25%. This is a significant but achievable reduction.

One way: make a[i]·a[n-1-i] small for many i. For example, if a[i] is large for i < n/2 and small for i > n/2, then the products a[i]·a[n-1-i] for i near n/2 would involve one large and one small value.

But this creates large b[m] for other m values (like b[m] for m near n, which involves a[i]·a[m-i] where both are in the "large" region).
