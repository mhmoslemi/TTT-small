    cmax = conv.max()

Actually, let me reconsider the problem from scratch. The key question is: what's the minimum of 2n*max(conv(a,a))/sum(a)^2 over non-negative a?

This is a known problem in the theory of sequences with low autocorrelation, but with non-negative reals (not complex or 0-1).

For the continuous analogue: minimize n * ||f*f||_L2 / (||f||_L2^2)^2... wait that's different.

Actually, let me think about it in terms of the Fourier transform. For a finite sequence a of length n:
- conv(a,a)[k] = sum_i a[i]*a[k-i]
- The Fourier transform of conv(a,a) is |A(freq)|^2 where A is the DTFT of a.
- M = max_k conv(a,a)[k] >= (1/(2n-1)) * sum_k conv(a,a)[k] = S^2/(2n-1)

So score >= 2n/(2n-1) → 1 as n→∞. The uniform sequence achieves score = 2, which is 2x the lower bound.

Can we get closer to the lower bound? The lower bound is achieved when conv(a,a) is constant, i.e., a is a "flat spectrum" sequence. For non-negative reals of length n, this is generally not achievable (it would require a to be an Zadoff-Chu or similar sequence, which for reals is limited).

However, we can get somewhat close by using sequences that approximate a flat spectrum. For non-negative sequences, one approach is to use a "Kaiser window" or similar taper.

But actually, I realize that for the specific problem of non-negative real sequences, the achievable minimum might be close to 2 for large n, because the constraint of non-negativity is very restrictive. The "flat spectrum" sequences (like Barker) require negative values.

Let me think about what the gradient approach will find. Starting from uniform (score 2), the gradient at the unique max (center) will try to reduce a[m] where a[n-1-m] is large (i.e., reduce the product at the center). This will create a "dip" in the middle of a, reducing the center of the convolution. But it might increase the convolution at other lags.

The process is:
1. Start uniform: conv is a triangle peaking at center.
2. Gradient says: reduce a[m] where the partner at center is large. Since all partners are equal (uniform), the gradient is uniform, and the step is zero (after subtracting mean). Stuck!

Ah, this is the problem! For a perfectly uniform sequence, the subgradient is uniform (2a/S for all m, with a single active lag), and after subtracting the mean, the gradient is zero. We're at a (sub)stationary point!

The subgradient of the max function at a point where it's achieved by a unique element is the gradient of that element. For uniform a, the center convolution value is the unique max, and its gradient w.r.t. a is 2*c for all positions (since each a[m] contributes 2*a[n-1-m] = 2c to the center). After subtracting the mean (2c), the projected gradient is 0.

So we're at a local (sub)minimum! The score is 2, and the subgradient method can't move from a symmetric stationary point.
