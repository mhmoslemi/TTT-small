Key question: what does a good sequence look like? It should have a "flat" autocorrelation. For nonnegative sequences, this means the sequence should be as "spread out" as possible without being uniform (since uniform gives a triangular autocorrelation).

Actually wait - uniform gives a TRIANGULAR autocorrelation, not a flat one. The max of the triangle is at the center. So uniform is actually the worst in terms of peak-to-average ratio!

Let me verify: uniform a (length n), autocorrelation r_k = (n-|k|) * c^2 for k = -(n-1), ..., n-1 (circular) or for linear: r_k = (min(k+1, n, 2n-1-k)) * c^2.

The max is n*c^2 (at k=0 for circular, or k=n-1 for linear... wait I need to be careful).

For linear convolution of uniform length-n sequence:
r_k = (number of overlapping terms) * c^2
= min(k+1, 2n-1-k, n) * c^2 for k = 0, ..., 2n-2

The max is n*c^2 at k = n-1.
The average is S^2 / (2n-1) = n^2 c^2 / (2n-1).
Peak-to-average = n / (n^2/(2n-1)) = (2n-1)/n.

For a "flat" autocorrelation, the peak-to-average would be 1 (perfectly flat). The score would be 2n/(2n-1).

So the idea is to find a sequence that makes the autocorrelation as flat as possible. This is a well-studied problem in sequence design.

For complex sequences, there are constructions (Chirp, Zadoff-Chu) that give very flat autocorrelation. For real nonnegative sequences, it's harder but still possible to get close.

One approach: use a "windowed" version of a flat-spectrum sequence. For example, take a complex chirp sequence and use its magnitude (or real part, shifted to be nonnegative).
