The log-sum-exp: `LSE_t(b) = t * log(sum_k exp(b_k/t))`

For t small (e.g., t = 0.01 * max(b)), this is close to max(b) but smooth.

The gradient of LSE_t:
`∂LSE_t/∂a_m = (1/sum_k exp(b_k/t)) * sum_k exp(b_k/t) * ∂b_k/∂a_m`
`= sum_k p_k * 2*a[k-m]`

where `p_k = exp(b_k/t) / sum_j exp(b_j/t)`.

This is a softmax-weighted average of the individual gradients. For small t, it's close to the subgradient at the peak.

The advantage is that it's smooth, so we can use better optimization methods (e.g., L-BFGS, or simple gradient descent with line search).
