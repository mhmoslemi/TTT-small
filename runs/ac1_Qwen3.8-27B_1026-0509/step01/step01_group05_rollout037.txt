We want to minimize `2n * max(conv(a,a)) / sum(a)^2` over `a ≥ 0`.

Since it's scale invariant, set `sum(a) = 1`. Then minimize `2n * max(conv(a,a))`.

The sum of all elements of `conv(a,a)` is `(sum(a))^2 = 1`. The convolution has `2n-1` elements. So the mean of the convolution is `1/(2n-1)`. If the convolution were perfectly flat, the max would be `1/(2n-1)` and the score would be `2n/(2n-1) ≈ 1`.

The question is: how close to flat can we make the convolution?

For a sequence of length n, the convolution has `2n-1` elements. The first and last elements are `a[0]^2` and `a[n-1]^2` respectively (single products). These can't be made as large as the middle elements unless all a_i are equal. So there's an inherent triangle shape to the convolution.

Wait, that's the key insight! The convolution of any length-n sequence with itself MUST taper at the edges. The first element is `a[0]^2` and the last is `a[n-1]^2`, while the middle elements involve sums of n products. So the convolution always has a peak in the middle and tapers to the edges. We can't make it flat.

Given this, the question becomes: what shape of a minimizes the ratio `peak / mean` of the convolution, where mean = 1/(2n-1)?

Equivalently, minimize `max(conv(a,a)) * (2n-1)` subject to `sum(a) = 1, a ≥ 0`.
