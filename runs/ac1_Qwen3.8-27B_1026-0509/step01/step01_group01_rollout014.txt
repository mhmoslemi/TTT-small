**Idea: Use the structure of the problem more directly.**

The score is 2n * max(c) / (sum a)^2. Since we normalize sum(a)=1, we want to minimize max(c).

Key observation: c_0 = a_0^2 and c_{2n-2} = a_{n-1}^2. So max(c) >= max(a_0^2, a_{n-1}^2). Also, c_{n-1} = sum_i a_i a_{n-1-i}.

If we think of it as: we want all c_k to be approximately equal (= 1/(2n-1) for uniform). The "excess" above 1/(2n-1) is what we're trying to minimize in the max sense.

**Idea: Use a "flattening" approach.**

At each step:
1. Compute c = fconv(a)
2. Compute the target: t = average of c (which is 1/(2n-1) for sum(a)=1)
   Actually, sum of all c_k over k=0..2n-2 = (sum a)^2 = 1. So average = 1/(2n-1).
3. The "excess" is c_k - t for each k. We want to reduce the positive excess.
4. The gradient of the "excess energy" would push a in a direction that reduces the excess.

This is essentially what the subgradient approach does, but we could also use a "flattening" heuristic:
- For each k where c_k > t, "spread out" the energy that contributes to c_k.
- The energy at c_k comes from pairs (i, k-i). To reduce c_k, we could decrease a_i or a_{k-i} for the pairs that contribute most.

This is equivalent to the subgradient step.

**Idea: Use a different norm than max.**

Instead of minimizing max(c), we could minimize a smooth approximation like log-sum-exp(c / tau) for small tau. This gives a differentiable objective and we can use gradient descent.

The gradient of log-sum-exp(c_k / tau) is: (1/tau) * softmax(c/tau) * (dc_k/da)
= (1/tau) * sum_k softmax(c/tau)_k * (dc_k/da)

This is equivalent to the subgradient with h = tau * softmax(c/tau) (normalized). So it's the same as the softmax weighting I mentioned earlier!

For small tau, softmax(c/tau) concentrates on the max, recovering the subgradient. For larger tau, it's smoother.
