So the full convolution is NOT the same as the autocorrelation! The full convolution includes cross terms that the autocorrelation doesn't.

The autocorrelation at lag k: R[k] = Σ_{i=0}^{n-1-k} f[i]*f[i+k]
The full convolution: c[t] = Σ_{k} f[k]*f[t-k] for valid k

For t = n-1+k: c[t] = R[k] + R[-k]... no, that's also not right.

The diagonal entries (c[0], c[6]) are the single-element squares. The "energy" Σ f[i]^2 = f0^2 + f1^2 + f2^2 + f3^2 is NOT a single entry of c.

Now, the question is: what is the theoretical minimum of the score?

For a non-negative sequence f with sum S:
score = 2n * max(c) / S^2

We want to minimize max(c). The sum of all c[t] is S^2 (since it's (Σf_i)(Σf_j) = S^2). There are 2n-1 terms. So average c is S^2/(2n-1). If all c values were equal, max(c) = S^2/(2n-1), and score = 2n/(2n-1) ≈ 1.

But we can't make them all equal with a non-negative sequence. The minimum of max(c) for non-negative sequences is a well-studied problem (related to "Barker sequences" and "Golay complementary sequences" in the general case, but for non-negative it's different).
