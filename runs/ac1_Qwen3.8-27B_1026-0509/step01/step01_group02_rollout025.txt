$\nabla_p [(p*p)(k)] = 2 \cdot (p[k-j])_{j=\max(0,k-n+1)}^{\min(k,n-1)}$

This is just 2 times a "window" of p (reversed and shifted). For the smooth max with top_k values, it's a weighted sum of such windows.

Let me think about this more efficiently. If the top values are at indices $k_1, ..., k_m$, then:
$g = \sum_{i=1}^m w_i \cdot \nabla_k_i$

where $\nabla_{k_i}$ is the gradient at lag $k_i$. Each gradient is a sparse-ish vector (nonzero only in a window of size $\min(k_i+1, n, 2n-1-k_i)$).

For n=4806 and top_k=5, this is very manageable.

Now, the key question is: what step size to use? The gradient of $\max_k (p*p)(k)$ has a specific scale. If p is in the simplex (sum=1), then each p_i is about 1/n, and the gradient components are about 2/n. The Lipschitz constant of the convolution operator is related to the norm of p.

For the projected gradient method on the simplex, a reasonable step size is $\alpha = 1/L$ where L is the Lipschitz constant. The convolution operator $p \mapsto p*p$ has Lipschitz constant $\|p\|_2 \cdot ... $ in the appropriate norm. Actually, the bilinear form $(p, q) \mapsto (p*q)(k)$ has operator norm $\|p\|_2$ (for the Euclidean norm). So the gradient of $\max_k (p*p)(k)$ has norm at most $2\|p\|_2 \cdot \|p\|_2 = 2\|p\|_2^2$.

For p in the simplex, $\|p\|_2 \leq 1$ and $\|p\|_2 \geq 1/\sqrt{n}$. So the gradient norm is at most 2. A step size of $\alpha \approx 0.1$ to $0.5$ should work.
