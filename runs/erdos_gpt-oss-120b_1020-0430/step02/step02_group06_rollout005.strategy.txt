<h3 class="strategy-title">Genetic Algorithm Approach for Optimization</h3>
<p>The Erdős minimum overlap problem requires finding a step function that minimizes the maximum overlap between the function and its shifted versions. While gradient-based methods like SLSQP are effective for local optimization, they are prone to getting stuck in local minima. This strategy employs a genetic algorithm (GA), which is a population-based optimization technique that explores a broader search space by mimicking natural selection processes. GA's crossover and mutation operations allow for more effective exploration of the solution space, increasing the chances of finding the global optimum for this challenging problem.</p>

<h3 class="section-subheading" id="approach">1. Approach and Rationale</h3>
<div class="implementation-box">
  <p><strong>A. Core Rationale:</strong> GA creates a population of candidate solutions and iteratively improves them through selection, crossover (recombination), and mutation (random alteration). This approach is particularly powerful for non-convex optimization problems like this one, as it doesn't rely on gradient information and can escape local optima.</p>
  
  <p><strong>B. Benefits:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li>Better exploration of the search space compared to gradient-based methods</strong></li>
    <li>Increased chance of finding the global optimum</li>
    <li>Handles both equality (integral constraint) and inequality (bounded values) constraints effectively</li>
    <li>Parallelizable nature reduces wall-clock time</li>
  </ul>

<p>The algorithm balances exploration and exploitation through controlled mutation rates and crossover operations. By using tournament selection and elitism, it preserves good solutions while introducing diversity through crossover and random mutations. The crossover mechanism exchanges genetic material between parents to combine beneficial traits, while mutation introduces new, unexplored regions of the solution space.</p>
