### Approach and rationale  

**Central idea**: The Erdős discrepancy problem involves finding a function h(x) that minimizes the maximum overlap with its shifted versions. The key challenge is the non-convex, high-dimensional search space. To improve upon the initial solution, I'll combine (1) a statistical learning-based initial guess generation to explore the domain's topologies and (2) multi-start local optimization using limited gradient approximation. This leverages basin jumping and surrogate modeling to effectively explore the search space.  

**Strengths**:  
- Statistical surrogate initializes conditioned on constraints (*Statistical Initializer*).  
- Local optimization handles the nonlinearity; repair mechanisms ensure constraints are never violated (*Constraint Repair*).  
- Early stopping and adaptive step sizing control computational effort (*Optimization Loop*).  
- Budget-aware implementations ensure timely solutions.  

**Limitations**:  
- Global optimality not guaranteed, but expected to beat the initial solution.  
- Real dependencies between shifts inhibit exact convexification.  

### Mathematical specification  

**Variables**:  
- `h` (n_points,): Discrete vector representing `h(x)` on [0,2].  
- `dx` (Scalar): Grid spacing, `dx=2.0/n_points`.  
- `target_integral` (Scalar): Target sum, `target_sum = n_points / 2`.  

**Objective**:
