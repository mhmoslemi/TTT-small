1. **Approach and Rationale**: This strategy employs a multi-scale evolution and refinement algorithm for the Erdős minimum overlap problem. Unlike previous gradient-based approaches, it combines global exploration with local optimization using a hierarchical strategy that: (1) starts students and is characterized by in the spatial frequency domain, providing a natural landscape for symmetry analysis; (2) adapts scales by frequency upscaling to refine candidates; (3) uses sparse repairments to maintain sum constraints without excessive projections. The key innovation is separating frequency space optimization from real space optimization, which avoids local minima common in gradient descent. The algorithm is inspired by the theory that high-frequency components can be minimized while low frequencies provide structure. This approach allows better exploration of the solution space and reduction of the maximum overlap by forcing long-range cancellations.

2. **Mathematical Specification**: We work with step function $h: [0,2] \to \mathbb{R}$ represented as $n-$point samples. The objective is:
   $$ \min \max_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} \left( \int h(x)(1 - h(x+k)) dx \right) $$
subject to:
   - $0 \leq h_i \leq 1$ for all $i$
   - $\sum h_i = n/2$

We use a harmonic decomposition in the Fourier space where $H(\omega) = \sum_{i} h_i e^{i2\pi i\omega/n}$. The objective transforms into a max-norm constraint on the correlation between $H$ and $\hat{H}$ (the Fourier transform of $1-h$). The algorithm works by:
   - **Phase 1**: Frequency space construction - creating candidate functions with desired symmetry properties in the Fourier domain
   - **Phase 2**: Real space refinement - converting frequency-restricted functions to valid real functions
   - **Phase 3**: Scale adaptation - doubling resolution for finer details
