### Approach and Rationale  
The core challenge is to minimize the worst-case overlap maximum value \(C_5\) for a step function \(h: [0,2] \to [0,1]\) with integral 1. The previous approach relied on hybrid local search methods (hill climbing and gradient-based optimization) starting from a binary representation. The new approach combines:  
1. **Parent Repair Metaheuristics**: A two-stage process that balances exploration and exploitation by starting from a parent solution and refining it with repair mechanisms.  
2. **Goal-Oriented Mutation**: Introduces mutations targeted specifically at regions contributing to high overlap scores.  
3. **Directed Local Search**: Uses gradient information to guide movements toward lower overlap values.  
4. **Timeout Robustness**: Handles computational limits by prioritizing timely evaluations and recovery of valid candidates.  

The method aims to overcome the previous solution's limitations by combining exploration (binary search) with directed intensification (gradient-guided mutations) and maintaining feasibility through repair steps. The primary bottleneck—escaping local optima—is addressed by dynamically adjusting mutation targets based on the worst-case overlap gradient.

### Mathematical Specification  
#### Representation  
- **Input**: A piecewise-constant step function \(h\) with time steps over \([0,2]\), represented as a vector of \(n\) samples. The function must satisfy \(0 \leq h_i \leq 1\) for all \(i\) and \(\sum h_i = n/2\).  
- **Objective**: Minimize \(C_5 = \max_k \int h(x)(1-h(x+k))  dx\).  

#### Operations  
1. **Repair Consistency**: Ensure \(0 \leq h_i \leq 1\) and \(\sum h_i = n/2\) via:

   \[
   \Delta = \frac{n}{2} - \sum h_i, \quad \text{correct}\; h_i \text{ via local adjustments}.
   \]  
2. **Gradient Calculation**: For the worst-case shift, compute the gradient using finite differences, which indicates how changes to \(h_i\) impact the overlap.  
3. **Mutation**: Vectors are updated with Gaussian perturbations in regions with high gradient magnitudes, scaled by a factor governing the perturbation's magnitude.  
4. **Feasibility Projection**: After mutations, enforce constraints by redistributing excess/subdeficit while minimizing large adjustments.
