## Approach and Rationale
The central idea is to solve the Erdős minimum overlap problem by constructing a binary sequence with specific low-overlap properties. The continuous domain [0, 2] is discretized into 180 points, and a binary function (values in {0, 1}) is used to represent the step function. The optimization minimizes the maximum correlation between the function and its complement across all shifts, which directly corresponds to the overlap integral. This approach leverages binary sequences with unique self-overlap properties, avoiding the non-convex pitfalls of continuous optimization. The strategy employs a metropolish annealing algorithm that balances exploration and exploitation, combined with repair mechanisms to maintain feasibility. This differs from previous methods by focusing on binary structures and annealing schedules, potentially escaping deeper local optima.

Limitations addressed:
