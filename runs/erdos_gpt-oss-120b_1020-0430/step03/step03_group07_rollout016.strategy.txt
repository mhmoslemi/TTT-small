### 1. Approach and Rationale
We reformulate the step function optimization using non-negative ranks and constrained matrix factorization. The step function h(x) is represented by a $n \times 1$ vector, and the overlap constraint is captured through a secondary matrix. This enables connection to the majorization framework leveraged in record theory.

The key bottleneck in current methods is the reliance on history-dependent operations like FFT convolution, which complicates derivative computation. Our solution separates feasibility from objective evaluation, making gradients accessible and reliable.

### 2. Mathematical Specification
**Variables**: $\mathbf{h} \in \mathbb{R}^n_{\geq 0}, \mathbf{1}^T\mathbf{h} = s/n$ where $n$ is number of samples and $s$ is the normalization constant.

**Objective**: Minimize the projection diameter $\max_{k} \|\mathbf{h} \odot (\mathbf{1} - \mathbf{h}{(k)})\|_2$, where $\mathbf{h}{(k)}$ is shift-$k$ of $\mathbf{h}$.

**Constraints**: $0 \leq h_i \leq 1, \sum_i h_i = s$

**Feasible Set**: $\mathbb{S}_{n} = \big\{\mathbf{h} : \mathbf{h} \geq \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{h} \leq \mathbf{1}, \sum_i h_i = s\big\}$

**Optimization Procedure**: 
- Project gradient descent initialized from eigenvectors of Laplacian
- Underlying space: $L_h = \{\mathbf{h} : h_i = e^{i\theta_i} \|u\|\}_{\theta_i}, \|u\| \geq 0$

**Gradient Definition**: 
$$\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{h}) = 2\text{Proj}_{C}(\mathbf{h} \odot (\mathbf{1} - \mathbf{h}{s}))$$
where $\text{Proj}_{C}$ projects onto the cardinality constraint.
