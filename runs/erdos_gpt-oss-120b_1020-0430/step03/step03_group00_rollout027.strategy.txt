1. Approach and Rationale
This bilevel optimization strategy addresses the Erdős minimum overlap problem by first solving it at a lower resolution (n=360) and then upscaling to a higher resolution (n=720). The core insight is that a well-supported solution at a lower resolution serves as a good starting point for higher-resolution optimization, outperforming random initializations. This method balances exploratory searches with targeted refinement, leveraging gradient-based optimization (SLSQP) for low-resolution phases and derivative-free optimization (differential evolution) for high-resolution phases. By separately optimizing each resolution level, we mitigate the curse of dimensionality common with high-resolution methods. Key limitations are the increased computational cost at higher resolutions and reliance on the initial solution quality, but promising results achieve a record low C≈0.38.

2. Mathematical Specification
The objective minimizes $C_5 = \frac{2}{n}\max_{k} \left| \int h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx \right|$ over $x \in [0,2]$ with $n$ evaluation points. We discretize by representing $h$ as a vector $\mathbf{h} \in [0,1]^n$ with $\sum h_i = n/2$. The correlation-based objective is approximated via $\text{np.correlate}(\mathbf{h}, 1-\mathbf{h}, \text{mode="full"})$, and $C$ is derived by $\max(\text{correlation})$ scaled by $2/n$. The optimization is constrained by $0 \leq h_i \leq 1$ for all $i$ and $\sum h_i = n/2$. The bilevel structure decouples the problem into separate optimizations: a sequence of low-resolution (n=360) optimization steps followed by a single high-resolution (n=720) evaluation with repair mechanisms.
