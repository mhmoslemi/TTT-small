1. Approach and rationale

Central idea

The problem is an explicitly nonconvex finite-dimensional min-max problem

minimize max_p q_p(h),  where  q_p(h) = dx * sum_valid h_i (1 - h_j),

with h in [0,1]^n, subset_sum(h) = n/2, dx=2/n. The exact evaluator uses ordinary linear correlation

C = max(np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode='full')) * dx,

over all 2n-1 lags. This means every algorithmic step must be judged by the original np.correlate objective, not by a circular autocorrelation, truncated approximation, or by replacing products h_i h_j with some unproven surrogate.

The supplied parent array is at n=44 and is the best practical starting point. The previous strategy was already a combination of local smoothing, cutting planes, LSE descent, and SLSQP epigraph refinement. The new primary strategy is not a rerun of that large family. Primary method: an exact-objective, feasible-line-search, active multi-gradient method.

It keeps only the most active correlation peaks, constructs a feasible direction in the zero-sum subspace that simultaneously decreases their first-order model, line-searches along that direction with exact feasible projection and exact correlation evaluation, and uses mild randomized variations to escape plateaus. Weight multi-resolution resampling from the parent plus compact multistart seeds is added to improve the optimization budget.

Why it may improve the parent

Smooth LSE methods average across many lags; active multi-gradient methods explicitly try to reduce the binding constraints.
