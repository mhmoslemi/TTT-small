1. Approach and rationale

Primary approach: active-set smoothed Frank-Wolfe local optimization.

The evaluator uses a zero-padded finite-support linear correlation

C(h) = dx * max_r corr_r(h),   corr_r(h) = np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")[r],

where dx=2/N and the sum of h samples is N/2. The objective is a max over about 2N-1 bilinear terms. It is not convex, and replacing it by a smooth global surrogate does not guarantee exact-objective improvement. The plan therefore uses only the exact evaluator for acceptance and recorded bounds.

The key observation is that, once we choose active correlations close to the current maximum, the direction that most directly decreases the active maximum can be obtained from the gradient of a weighted average of those correlations. For this weighted average, the first-order direction has a simple linear subproblem after weighting: choose a binary vertex z in the feasible polytope

P = { z in [0,1]^N : sum(z)=N/2 }

minimizing <g,z>, where g is the gradient of the active smoothed objective. The Frank-Wolfe move is h -> (1-α)h + α z. This move is exactly feasible for any α in [0,1] because it is a convex combination of two points in P. This avoids repeated projection after every update and keeps strict feasibility of box and mass constraints.

This is materially different from a plain projected subgradient method because:

- The active objective is restricted to a smoothed set of current-largest correlations, not all correlations.
- The descent candidate is generated by an exact linear-Frank-Wolfe vertex solve rather than by projected coordinate descent.
- The method strictly maintains mass and box constraints through convex combinations and only accepts optional local increases under bounded acceptance rules.
- Multiple randomly seeded deterministic starts at one moderate resolution, with validation by the exact evaluator, are used to avoid getting trapped in the parent basin.

Limitations and hypotheses:

- The active smoothing and Frank-Wolfe step are local heuristic descent directions. This is not a global optimizer and does not prove optimality.
- The estimate may decrease only after several iterations because decreased active correlations can increase others near the max. Therefore we keep a global best candidate from exact evaluator evaluations, independent of the local accepted state.
- The analytic gradient formula must match the indexing convention of np.correlate. We include validation rules. If mismatch persists in a specific environment, the runtime excludes that initialization update method and relies on valid fallbacks; otherwise the default variant is the one matching the evaluator.

Primary algorithm: active-set smoothed Frank-Wolfe descent from algorithmically initialized feasible step functions, with all accepted moves and the reported bound validated by the exact evaluator.

The objective is

C(h) = max_r dx · corr_r(h),   dx = 2 / N,
corr_r(h) = np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode="full")[r],

under 0 <= h_i <= 1 and sum(h) = N/2. This is a nonconvex bilinear max. The natural active information at a current function is the set of correlations near the current maximum. We therefore do not optimize an indirect global mean of all correlations; we form a relaxed weighted maximum over active correlations, compute its first-order gradient, and use that gradient for a Frank-Wolfe move against the same feasible polytope. Because the move is a convex combination of two feasible points, feasibility is preserved exactly without repeated projection after each local update.

Why this can help:

- A Frank-Wolfe linear programming subproblem over the simplex-box P_N = {h: 0<=h_i<=1, sum(h)=N/2} is trivial and global: fill mass at indices with smallest gradient coefficients. This is much more stable than naive projected subgradient because all moves are in the feasible hull.
- Active correlations with weights moved near the maximum provide descent pressure for the exact maximum while not letting many distant correlations dilute the gradient.
- Exact evaluation is used at acceptance and for best tracking, so local surrogate errors cannot hide in the final C5_bound.
- Multiple deterministic threaded starts, including a reconstructed parent, a mirror, smoothed random Fourier perturbations, and seeded Gaussian perturbations, help avoid one local basin from the supplied parent.

Limitations and hypotheses:

- This heuristic is not a proof of global optimality.
- Active-value smoothing and Frank-Wolfe linearization are analytical and convex in the surrogate subproblem but do not be valid guarantee with objective; exact surrogate line checking is used to avoid local escape upward.
