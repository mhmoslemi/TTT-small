1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central objective and mode of solution
The scientific target is to produce, within the supplied discrete model, a feasible vector h of samples on [0,2] whose exact evaluator score
C5(h) = max_l np.correlate(h,1-h,mode="full")[l] * (2/n)
is as small as possible, with acceptance goal C5(h) <= 0.3808 if possible and certainly never worse than the validated parent. It is safe and useful to work purely with the discrete variables h[0:n], because the authoritative score is finite sums of products; no hidden continuous integral enters after discretization. The variable sum is fixed by sum_i h[i] = n/2 exactly, which is already equivalent to the supplied continuous constraint ∫_0^2 h dx = 1 up to equality in floating point.

The centered core difficulty is nonconvexity: minimizing max of correlations contains bilinear terms h[a]*(1-h[b]), and the Hessian is not convex. Therefore a claim of global optimality should not be made. The robust strategy is to build a candidate funnel: validate the parent, optimize transformed smooth approximations of the exact max, polish candidates with an exact epigraph local solver in primal variables, and create diverse restarts including different resolutions. This attacks the practical bottleneck: a local optimum such as a parent vector may satisfy many constraints tightly and have tiny one-dimensional improvements only under exact evaluation, not under an approximate LSE objective and not under a single coordinate swap. Since the required improvement from 0.38085 to <=0.3808 can often be small, multiple independent basins and an exact finite-line polish are essential.

1.2 Coherent chosen strategy
The primary algorithm is:
Stage A: Exact validation and safe storage of the parent.
Stage B: Warm-start or membrane transfer to selected resolution n, optionally with lower and higher n.
Stage C: Reparameterize h on the strictly interior feasible set n/2 = Σ h_i with a volatility-transformed logistic projection. Optimize a smoother maximum function, decreasing smoothing μ. This robustly explores continuous landscapes while keeping the exact constraint and bounds automatic.
Stage D: Convert the best transformed solution into feasible natural h values and run exact local line-search improvements over feasible coordinate pair moves that preserve the sum.
Stage E: Run exact nonconvex epigraph optimizations from the top candidates, with bounds and a sum equality, to try to push several tight correlation constraints down simultaneously.

min t
subject to dx * Σ_b h[b+l]*(1-h[b]) <= t for every l in {-(n-1),...,n-1},

1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central objective
The task is to return a feasible discrete vector h over n sampled points that minimizes, according to the authoritative evaluator, C5(h)=max_l np.correlate(h,1-h,"full")*dx, dx=2/n, subject to 0≤h_i≤1 and Σ_i h_i=n/2 exactly. Improvement over the supplied parent can usually come from one or more very small changes after the current parent satisfies many of the 2n-1 lag constraints tightly. A single deterministic local solve is unlikely to be more reliable than a family of methods, because the model is nonsmooth and nonconvex: products h_a(1-h_b) produce bilinear terms and minima are local.

1.2 Primary algorithmic idea
Use a three-layer candidate generation and acceptance procedure.

Layer 1: Validate and preserve the parent. This provides a reliable fallback and a warm start.

Layer 2: Smooth unconstrained-reparameterized search. Search the exact feasible set without inequalities or equality constraints by using a logistic one-parameter scalarization, then optimize a stabilized smooth maximum corresponding to all evaluator constraints. This produces continuous, numerically robust motions in an unconstrained parameter space while exact mass is maintained by the logistic scalar shift.

Layer 3: Exact local and epigraph polish in natural h coordinates. Apply exact pair/vector line polishing and an epigraph nonlinear constrained optimization. Constraints reuse the exact evaluator relationships, but the final objective is only the true evaluator score. Because the constraint formulation is nonconvex, these solvers are treated as local searches; all reported values are exact.

1.3 Multiple starts and resolution
The search includes the parent resolution. If parent length is available, additionally include a dynamically selected set of nearby lower/higher resolutions. Lower resolutions are faster and satisfy the “less than 2k samples” preference. Higher resolutions can model fractional behavior more finely and may produce lower scores, although each exact evaluation uses more work. Candidates are always scored on their own resolution with the supplied metric. The smallest feasible resolution is never automatically preferred unless it gives a sufficiently small score, to avoid choosing an artificially worse size.

1.4 Why it should reach the target area
The previous construction is close enough that only a small reduction is required. The combination of: smooth multidirectional descent over the full nonsmooth envelope; exact locally steepest-directed line or pair modifications; epigraph joint optimization of all tight constraints; and multiple warm initializations maximizes the probability of finding exact feasible reductions of 1e-5 to 1e-4 in the nonconvex landscape. No method gives a guaranteed global optimum; correctness is restored by exact evaluation and strict Feasibility checks.

1.5 Justified limits
- Local solving of nonconvex products has no global guarantee.
- Resolution changes are not a nested convergence operation; therefore transfer is problem.

1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central mathematical target
For an integer resolution n, with step size dx = 2.0/n and target mass tau = n/2, the task is to find
h in [0,1]^n, sum_i h_i = tau
minimizing
C5(h)=max(normalize(np_correlation(h,1-h,mode="full"))[q] dx, q=0,...,2n-2).
Every returned vector is jointly feasible for both constraints and evaluated by the same finite linear correlation expression used by the authoritative evaluator. The improvement should be demonstrated by that score, not by a surrogate objective.

1.2 Nature of the difficulty
The objective is a nonsmooth maximisation of exactly 2n-1 finite-sum constraints. It contains terms h_a(1-h_b), hence is bilinear and generally nonconvex. Therefore the procedure cannot legitimately assert a global optimum. Its design purpose is to overcome the supplied parent’s local plateau by creating many feasible motions that can reduce subtly active constraints simultaneously.

1.3 Primary method: validate–smooth-smooth–exact refine
The main search is a cascade:

Stage A: Validate the parent exactly. Keep its exact vector and score as the mandatory baseline fallback.

Stage B: Logistic smooth surrogate search. Represent a strictly interior feasible vector with an internal parameter vector z under an exact mass constraint, using an invertible logistic parametrization and λ-affine shift. Minimize a stabilized log-sum-exp surrogate for the maximum of correlation values while decreasing smoothing. This avoids evaluating nonsmooth kinks directly and retains exact constraints automatically.

Stage C: Exact feasible local refinement in natural h coordinates. Use randomly selected exact objective vector/pair line searches and weighted-gradient directions to move from interior, smoothed solutions to slightly better exact values while maintaining sum exactly by elementary feasible moves. These local motions can retrieve improvements lost by surrogate objectives.

Stage D: Epigraph exact nonconvex local optimization in natural h coordinates. Optimize variables t,h subject to all exact-dx correlation constraints being less than t, with sum exactly and h between 0 and 1. This is solved as a local NLP because it can adjust all active constraints jointly. Nonconvexity means this is local polish, not a global certificate.

Stage E: Diversified restarts over relevant resolutions. Re-outline to nearby lower or higher resolutions; warm those with interpolated best vectors. Candidate resolutions use exact scoring and the best feasible globally valid result. This prevents one neighborhood from absorbing all budget and gives a chance to discover cheaper or more flexible sample grids.

1.4 Why this can improve from 0.38085 toward 0.3808
The parent apparently satisfies multiple constraints tightly. A single smoother surrogate optimum can leave some tight constraints at their exact maximum kink and may bounce. The epigraph NLP directly modifies all near-tight constraints subject to the exact mass and bounds. The exact line search catches any one-dimensional feasible improvement that the smooth solutions do not identify. Searching several resolutions increases diversity. Multiple accepted solutions can be further converted or improved after successful basins. The combination is necessary because target change is tiny relative to flat plateau size.

1.5 Limitations
- The NLP formulation is nonconvex because products h_a(1-h_b) have indefinite Hessian. Solvers may stop at saddle regions or boundary separators.
- Resolution changes are not an order-preserving projection of the exact finite correlation problem. Every resolution result must be independently judged by the actual metric.
- Smooth surrogate scores are used only for search, never as proof. A candidate is accepted only after exact correlation evaluation.
