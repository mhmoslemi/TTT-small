1. Approach and rationale

Central new idea: use an exact-lag linearized bottleneck search, not a smoothed maximum surrogate, as the main local engine. The previous approach primarily used a log-sum-exp smoothing of the maximum and projected gradient steps in the full `h` space. The current goal is only about `5e-5` to `7e-5` tighter than the parent, so small oscillations between many active lags and boundary-boundary constraints matter more than stable smoothed descent. I therefore propose a trust-region-style active-maximization algorithm: at a feasible iterate, compute the full finite-support correlation vector, linearize every finite-support lag against a move vector plus a scalar bottleneck variable, solve a small linear program that minimizes the linearized maximum with sum preservation, bounds, and a small regularization on movement, then accept only by re-evaluating the exact evaluator on the repaired full step. This differs substantially from the previous LSE projection because:

- it treats the bottleneck via an epigraph linear variable trained against both active and non-active lags instead of smoothing the maximum and hoping to inherit the exact value;
- it uses feasible-preservation and bound preservation in a linear program instead of an LSE surrogate followed by bisection;
- it accepts and stores only valid repaired feasible vectors by exact evaluation; the LP value is only a search signal, never a reported bound;
- it adds candidates by complement/reflection/anticorrelation variational constructions and by exact feasible binary-half/quantized starts, with exact objective acceptance throughout.

This is a scalar maximin method for a nonconvex bilinear problem, so no claim is made that it is globally optimal or that a single convex LP makes the original problem convex. The only claimed upper bound is always the exact `np.correlate` result for the returned vector. The strategy is motivated specifically by the tiny gap target, where active-lag precision matters: the leading monotone max may indeed be very close to a finite-dimensional cone intersection problem, and active-lag conic tangent steps can create small accepted margin gains better than LSE would.

Bottleneck addressed: previous projected LSE stagnated around `0.3814` from an initial construction even with multi-start noise and re-resolution, suggesting the smooth average of peak correlations hid which lags were binding and how the infeasible convex-regions bottleneck can be crossed by exploiting the bilinear geometry rather than by replacing it with a smoke substituent. This retains enough nonconvex continuity to obtain small derivatives, resolves cases where local smoothing moves the wrong set of lags, and handles the finite interval boundary terms through every exact lag.

Plausible improvement source, not proof: the optimization landscape may be locally shaped by a small set of active lags; the LP approximates all constraints by the current tangent cone and selects one direction that decreases all correlated constraints; line search checks the actual spike, so the exact topology is omitted but practical refinement may happen.
