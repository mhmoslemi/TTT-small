1. Approach and rationale

Central idea

We construct a deliberately diversified upper-bound search for the exact task objective. We do not attempt to prove optimality or to solve a convex relaxation as if it were globally exact. We generate many feasible candidate vectors via algorithmic transformations and random generation, improve them using a combination of smoothed auxiliary projected-gradient descent, exact nonsmooth local line-descent in feasible coordinate exchanges, and binary/local combinatorial search, then retain only candidates whose exact task score improves the incumbent valid best solution. This guarantees that every stored upper bound is a monotone exact improvement.

Why this can improve the supplied parent or the previous algorithm

The previous mostly projected-gradient method is effective locally but was mostly anchored to one smoothed surrogate and one scale/path schedule. The main nonconvexity and local maxima come from the exact maximum over all finite support lags. Boundary effects, edge lags, and the asymmetry between nonzero lags and the zero lag can make local minima insensitive or misleading under a log-sum-exp surrogate. The new strategy targets these bottlenecks:

- It directly seeds transformations of the parent (reverse, complement, cyclic shifts, resolution deformations) because the exact score is inexpensive on small n. These are not assumed symmetric; each is always re-evaluated under the official exact evaluator. This is beneficial because boundary/finite support structure prevents clean symmetrization.
- Instead of relying only on continuous log-sum-exp projected gradients, it adds pair-preservation line searches using the exact task score. Pair moves maintain the sum and bounds exactly or nearly exactly and can make bold discrete-like score reductions. This is the “nonsmooth” complement to the smooth solver and matters especially when many lags are nearly binding.
- It searches discretized binary constructions (even lengths) with fixed ones count. Binary search directly redistributes where the 1/0 crossing situation contributes to the correlation. The parent might already sit near such a pattern, and optimizing from a good binary start may jump out of a soft-surrogate local optimum.
- It optimizes at several resolutions, transferring vectors at vector-resolution scales with feasibility correction, because different finite discretization lengths may have materially different exact scores and neighbor sharing between resolutions helps escape one-dimensional resolution penalty. The program still returns small vectors (<2k).
- Since parent and best candidates fall back at all times, the returned solution is always a valid candidate under the evaluator. We do not claim global optimality. The upper bound’s validity is guaranteed by direct re-evaluation of the returned h. The improvement is a search hypothesis supported by the fact that exact pair-line and binary paths can change the exact maximum in a way smooth gradients may not.

Main limitation and honest framing

The method is local/multiplicative and stochastic. It can fail to reach the desired target and it may rely on random luck. We sharpen this by using numerous diversified points of variation and by separating surrogate improvement steps from exact acceptance: every candidate that proposes low surrogate score is re-evaluated if its exact score contradicts the incumbent, and only the valid exact improvement is committed. That prevents accepted states, within a slight numerical tolerance, from pretending different interpretation of the objective as surrogate information. The binary phase’s failure if there is no valid global exact is not harmful; it may return amendments enabling only neighbors when required.
