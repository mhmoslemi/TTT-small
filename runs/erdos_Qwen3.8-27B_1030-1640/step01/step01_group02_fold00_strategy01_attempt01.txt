1. Approach and rationale

1.1 Central idea: exact objective with a linearized minimax oracle

The previous approach primarily improved a smoothed maximum of the overlap
constraint set. That helps locally, but the true evaluator is a bilinear,
finite-support, piecewise-quadratic maximization. There are two major
bottlenecks:

1. Smoothing the maximum may generate directions which only nominally
   reduce the surrogate maximum, while the exact maximal lags do not move
   jointly in a useful way.
2. When many lags are nearly active, the surrogate has nearly cancelling
   sensitivities. A one-dimensional projected-gradient path can therefore
   stall even though complete, coordinated feasible changes can lower the
   exact maximum significantly.

The new strategy is not to optimize the exact max directly as an interior
NLP. Instead it locates good feasible directions with a sequence of linear
minimax subproblems, then performs exact line searches and keeps only moves
that improve the stated evaluator.

At a current feasible iterate h, the exact overlap constraint for every
nonzero and zero lag is linearized using exact coordinate sensitivities.
Then it solves a small linear program:
            minimize            α
   variables x and α
   subject to linearized overlap values of the selected lags ≤ α,
              0 ≤ x ≤ 1,
              sum(x) = n/2.
The resulting LP direction x − h is a coordinated direction that tries to
lower many current worst-or-near-worst lags simultaneously, subject to bounds
and mass conservation. The nonconvexity of the true quadratic objective terms
remains in the exact line search and global acceptance step. The direction is
therefore used as an oracle, not as proof of optimality.

This is materially different from the earlier LSE-projected-gradient
construction because the first step is a boxed, mass-preserving linear
minimax direction selection over multiple active constraints, rather than a
weighted log-sum-exp gradient. The complete search trajectory is still judged
by:
np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode='full') * (2.0/n),
and improved candidates are accepted only when this exact desired evaluator is
strictly lower than the incumbent best.

1.2 Why this can improve the supplied parent

- Multiple peaks can be moved simultaneously: an LP can infer which positions
  to increase/decrease when many radial overlap directions are tied.
- Exact acceptance guarantees monotone validity: a bad linearization never
  becomes a stored bound because the bitwise exact score is recomputed.
- Directional exact minimization over a line incorporates the quadratic terms
  omitted by the LP model.
- Repeated trust-region adaptation naturally selects safe steps even when
  nonconvex curvature makes the linear model misleading.
- It can coordinate redistribution across a broad support region, rather than
  slowly transferring mass through many one-dimensional gradient paths.
- By seeding many algorithmically constructed starts—smooth, thresholded,
  cyclically transferred, or locally noised—it gains diversity without hard
  coding a particular dataset.

1.3 Component roles and limitation

The principal search is the exact-objective linearized minimax loop command.
Secondary polishing uses mass-preserving pair transfers and line searches with
the exact objective. It is a globalized local/bi-level search, so no global
optimality claim is made. The returned bound is a witness: a feasible h and
its re-evaluated exact objective. Since the objective is indefinite under the
box constraint, no linearized subproblem should be interpreted as a proof that
a score below a given value is impossible.

The proposed main direction oracle is the one essential novelty. It avoids the
destination local minimum of previous trajectory curves. But still if a high-
confidence stage is bounded under. Robust by fallback. Evaluation function.
