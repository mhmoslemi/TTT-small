For a given h, the objective C(h) = max_k f_k(h). A small change h[s] -= tau, h[t] += tau changes f_k by approximately:
delta_f_k ≈ -tau * d(f_k)/d(h[s]) + tau * d(f_k)/d(h[t])

The change in the objective is approximately:
delta_C ≈ max_k (f_k + delta_f_k) - max_k f_k

If the maximum is achieved at lag k*, then:
delta_C ≈ delta_f_{k*} = tau * (d(f_{k*})/d(h[t]) - d(f_{k*})/d(h[s]))

So to decrease C, we want d(f_{k*})/d(h[t]) < d(f_{k*})/d(h[s]), i.e., the gradient at t should be smaller than at s for the active lag.

This gives a clear direction: for the active lag k*, find s where d(f_{k*})/d(h[s]) is large and t where d(f_{k*})/d(h[t]) is small.

But we also need to consider other lags that might become active. This is where the "guard" set comes in.
