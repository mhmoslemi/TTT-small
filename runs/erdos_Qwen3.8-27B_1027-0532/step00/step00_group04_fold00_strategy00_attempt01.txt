### 1. Approach and Rationale  
The Erdős finite maximum discrepancy problem seeks to minimize the maximum overlap integral \( C_5 \) for a step function \( h \) with integral 1 over [0, 2]. The optimization is non-convex and has many local minima. The provided initial_h_values (n=70) serves as a starting point. The approach leverages a combination of local search and stochastic perturbations to escape local optima. It uses the COBYLA method for constrained optimization, which handles bounds and equality constraints efficiently. For out-of-bound function values, a repair mechanism is applied. The global search is accelerated by introducing a small stochastic gradient component to explore nearby regions.  

### 2. Mathematical Specification
- **Variables**: h is a vector of length n_points (even) in [0,1] with sum = n_points / 2.  
- **Objective**: \( C_5 = \max_k \text{dx} \times \text{np.correlate}(h, 1-h, \text{mode}=\text{"full"}) \).  
- **Constraints**:  
  - Box: \( 0 \leq h[i] \leq 1 \) for all i.  
  - Sum: \( \sum h[i] = n_points / 2 \).  
- **Repair Mechanism**: Clamps out-of-bound values and adjusts the sum via L2 projection onto the equality constraint (derived via Lagrange multipliers).  
- **Methods**:

  - **Global Perturbation**: Add small random noise, repair to bounds, and normalize the sum.  
  - **Optimizer**: COBYLA handles constraints, used with or without stochastic components based on phase.
