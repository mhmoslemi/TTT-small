**1. Approach and Rationale**

The central idea is to model the optimization problem as a mathematical program with complementarity constraints (MPCC) that naturally discretizes the continuous problem. This involves formulating the problem as a mix of binary and continuous variables at the grid points, ensuring the step function property and exact integral. The MPCC formulation allows us to use a sequence of nonlinear programming (NLP) relaxations with ever finer grids, enabling convergence to the global optimum as the grid resolution increases. This approach leverages the structure of the overlap integral to decompose the problem into smaller trust regions around the initial parent solution, making it computationally tractable while still providing a rigorous upper bound.

The bottleneck in the previous strategies was the high computational cost of optimization algorithms in the continuous space and the difficulty of handling the non-convexity of the overlap integral. By discretizing the function as a step function defined on a grid, we can efficiently handle the switching points (value changes) and use a directed search based on their probabilities of occurrence across different lags. This reduces the dimensionality and convexity issues, while the initial parent solution provides a strong starting point.

**2. Mathematical Specification**

*   **Representation and Variables**:
    Let `n_points` be the number of grid points (initially 97). Let `h_values` be a vector of length `n_points`, representing the step function evaluated at the grid points. We assume `h_values_i` takes value `0` or `1` for a step function. However, during optimization, we relax this to `0 <= h[i] <= 1` to maintain potential overlap regions.

*   **Objective Function**:
    The goal is to minimize the maximum overlap integral over all integer lags `k`:
    C_5 = max_{k} ∫ h(x) . (1 - h(x+k)) dx
    This is evaluated discretely using a Riemann sum approximation:
    C_5 ≈ (dx) . max_k ∑_{i} h[i] . (1 - h[i+k])

    where `dx = 2 / n_points`.

*   **Constraints**:

       ∑ (h[i] * dx) = 1   →   ∑ h[i] = n_points / 2 = 48.5 (for `n_points` = 97)
    2. Each grid point must satisfy 0 <= h[i] <= 1.

*   **Optimization Formulation**:
