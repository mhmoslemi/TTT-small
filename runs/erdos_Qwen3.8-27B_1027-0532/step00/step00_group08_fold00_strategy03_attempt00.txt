1. Approach and rationale
This approach uses Bayesian optimization with a Gaussian process model to find the step function that minimizes the maximum overlap integral. Unlike local search methods that can get trapped in suboptimal regions, Bayesian optimization balances exploration and exploitation by maintaining a probabilistic model of the objective function. We model the problem as minimizing C5, the maximum overlap integral, using a Bayesian framework that can efficiently explore the high-dimensional space of possible step functions while incorporating the integral constraint. The key innovations are:
   - A probabilistic surrogate model that learns the correlation between candidate solutions and their scores
   - An acquisition function that balances exploration of uncertain regions with exploitation of known good regions
   - A repair mechanism specific to the problem constraints
   - A scoring function that exactly matches the Erdős evaluation method

The approach differs from previous strategies by using a preference-based Bayesian optimization rather than pure evolutionary operations. This allows us to maintain domain knowledge about the function space and focus the search more effectively, while still providing a method to escape local optima.
