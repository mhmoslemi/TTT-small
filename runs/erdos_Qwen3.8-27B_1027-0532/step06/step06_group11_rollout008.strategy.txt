1. **Approach and Rationale**
   This plan addresses the Erdős minimum overlap problem by exploiting the non-convex nature of the objective function through adaptive resolution optimization. Unlike grid searches or gradient-based local methods, it first operates on extremely coarse grids (2 to 64 points) to find globally promising patterns, which are then refined in higher dimensions. This leverages the fact that coarse-grained solutions often reveal topological structures that are lost in dense optimizations. The core innovation is using sinusoidal patterns adapted from the solution's autocorrelation properties. The solution space mapping ensures these are physically realizable, and the hierarchical refinement avoids being trapped in local minima common in grid-based searches.

2. **Mathematical Specification**
   - **Representation**: The function h(x) is discretized on [0, 2] into n uniform grid points. Coarse grids use 2^k points.
   - **Objective**: C₅(h) = (max_k corr_k) * (2/n), where corr_k is the normalized correlation.
   - **Constraints**: 0 ≤ h[i] ≤ 1 ∀i, sum(h) = n/2.
   - **Differential Structure**: Define a Hamiltonian-like quantity H = ln(sum_{k} e^{-β·Cₕ(k)}), where β is an inverse temperature parameter. Here, Cₕ(k) represents the cross-correlation component. This turns the optimization into a probabilistic exploration matching the Manhattan eigenvalues.
   - **Adaptive Projection**: Solutions transition between grids via interpolation preserving both the sum of integrals and box constraints. For grid transition from n to m, we solve an optimal transportation problem to maintain satisfaction of sum(h) = n/2.
