1. Approach and Rationale
A novel method combining spectral analysis with probabilistic search is proposed to improve upon the current upper bound for the Erdős minimum overlap constant. The approach leverages discrete Fourier transforms to convert the slide correlation problem into a frequency domain optimization, which is then solved via a population-based Monte Carlo method with adaptive walk steps. This differs substantially from local optimization techniques by addressing the correlation structure through spectral decomposition. The method is designed to escape shallow local minima while maintaining the integral constraint via a multi-stage repair mechanism.

2. Mathematical Specification
Variables: h ∈ [0,1]^(n) with ∫h dx = 1
Objective: minimize C₅ = max_k ∫h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx over [0,2]
Fourier formulation: The cross-correlation operator can be represented in frequency space using the Fourier transform. Let F(k) = ∫e^{2πi k t}h(t)dt. Then the maximum correlation occurs at frequencies where |F(ω)²| is maximized.

Projective constraint satisfaction: For any proposed h, the adjustment is done via iterative projection: h ← h + (target_sum - ∑h) / n with subsequent clipping to [0,1] until integral constraint is met.
