1. Approach and Rationale  
The Erdős minimum overlap problem seeks to minimize the maximum overlap between a step function \( h \) and its shifted versions. 
Our approach leverages combinatorial optimization and binary search through a **genetic algorithm with repair operations**. 
Unlike continuous optimization, this method directly searches the binary space of valid step functions, ensuring feasibility (sum constraints met, bounded values) while escaping local optima via crossover and mutation. 
The binary representation avoids smooth relaxations and focuses on discrete structure, potentially finding lower bounds better than existing methods. 

Key rationales:  
- Binary representation limits the search space to valid feasible candidates  
- Repair mechanisms maintain integral constraints during evolution  
- Population diversity is preserved via tournament selection  
- Early termination tracks best solution per generation  
Bottleneck: Continuous optimization may explore interpolated solutions; here we force discrete jumps.
