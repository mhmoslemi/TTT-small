### 1. Approach and Rationale

The core idea is to combine **Fourier basis templates** with **constrained optimization** to capture the periodic behavior often found in optimal solutions for minimization problems involving shifts. This approach leverages the observation that optimal solutions tend to possess minimal correlation at various shifts, a property aligned with Fourier features. By initializing the optimization with Fourier-derived templates, we avoid local optima common in gradient-free methods while still enabling gradient-based optimization. The problem's objective involves finding a step function minimizing the maximum overlap with shifts, which is inherently non-convex. Our method treats this as a constrained optimization over a high-dimensional space, using gradient-based methods with careful projection for constraints.

### 2. Mathematical Specification

The goal is to minimize:
$$C_5 \triangleq \max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx$$

Subject to:
- $h(x) \in [0, 1]$ for all $x \in [0, 2]$ 
- $\int_0^2 h(x) dx = 1$

In discretized form (with $n_{\text{points}}$ samples and $dx = 2/n_{\text{points}}$), we represent $h$ as a step function:
$$ h_i \in [0, 1] \quad \forall i, \quad \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\text{points}}} h_i \cdot dx = 1 $$

The objective becomes:
$$ \text{objective}(h) = \max_{|k| \leq n_{\text{points}}-1} \left( dx \cdot \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\text{points}}} h_i (1 - h_i + k) \right) $$

Note: The integer shifts $k$ can be up to $n_{\text{points}} - 1$, though for minimization, we consider the discrete correlation equivalent to all practical shifts.
