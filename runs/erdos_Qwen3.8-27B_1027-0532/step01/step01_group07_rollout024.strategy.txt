### Approach and Rationale
The central idea is to use Bayesian optimization to search for the optimal step function that minimizes the maximum overlap of h(x) and (1-h(x+k)) for all k. Bayesian optimization is a sequential design strategy for global optimization of black-box functions that do not provide derivatives. It is especially effective for expensive-to-evaluate functions because it uses a probabilistic model (Gaussian process) to balance between exploration and exploitation.

The previous methods (gradient descent and genetic algorithms) are either gradient-based and can get stuck in local optima or are population-based but lack a strong global optimization strategy. Bayesian optimization, however, builds a surrogate model that estimates the objective function and its uncertainty, then selects the next point to evaluate based on an acquisition function. This balance allows it to find the global minimum more reliably.

We will use a Gaussian process (GP) to model the objective function. The GP will be updated with function evaluations, and the acquisition function (e.g., Expected Improvement) will guide the selection of the next point to evaluate. The step function will be parameterized by its values at grid points, and we will enforce the constraints 0 <= h[i] <= 1 and sum(h) = n/2.
