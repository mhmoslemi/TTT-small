### Approach and Rationale

The Erdős minimum overlap problem requires minimizing the maximum overlap of a step function with its translations. This nonconvex optimization problem is challenging due to its high dimensions and complex objective landscape. Existing methods like SLSQP and grid search often get stuck in local minima. Our approach leverages gradient-free optimization (Differential Evolution) prioritizing:

1. **Reliability**: Avoids gradient issues and guarantees black-box search.
2. **Exploration**: Uses population-based global search for robustness.
3. **Escaping Local Minima**: Incorporates restart strategies and parameter adjustments.
4. **Efficiency**: Trades off higher computation time for better global optima.

The key innovation is refining grid representations iteratively while maintaining feasibility. We transfer solutions between grid sizes using interpolation/adjustment, enabling gradual refinement. This contrasts with previous aggressive local optimization, which often missed critical areas.

### Mathematical Specification

**Variables**:  
- \( h \in \mathbb{R}^n \): Vector representing function samples over [0,2]

**Constraints**:  
1. \( 0 \leq h_i \leq 1 \) for all \( i \)
2. \( \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i = n/2 \)

**Objective**:  
Minimize \( C = \max_{k=-(n-1)}^{n-1} \left( \frac{2}{n} \sum_i h_i (1 - h_{i+k}) \right) \)  
where \( k \) ranges over physically meaningful shifts (noting edge effects).

**Representation Handling**:  
For grid size changes, represent interpolation with:
\[
h_{\text{new}}[j] = \text{interp}\left(x_j, h_{\text{old}}(\cdot)\right)
\]
followed by sum and clipping projection.
