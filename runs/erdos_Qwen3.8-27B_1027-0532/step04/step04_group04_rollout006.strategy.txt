### Approach and rationale
**Central idea:**  
The Erdős discrepancy problem requires minimizing the maximum overlap of a step function \(h(x)\) with its complement at all possible shifts. The proposed method uses a **Fourier-difference hybrid approach** to explicitly target frequencies contributing to high correlations. By penalizing specific frequency components while optimizing the time domain, we address the non-convexity and structure of the problem more effectively than local search alone.

**Addressing bottlenecks:**  
The primary bottleneck in previous approaches was local optima trapping greedy or gradient-based methods. This hybrid method leverages Fourier analysis to decouple symmetry-breaking frequencies and scales non-trivially across grid sizes.

### Mathematical specification
**Variables:**  
- Discrete step function \(h\) defined on a grid of size \(n\) (even), with \(\sum h_i \cdot \Delta x = 1\) (where \(\Delta x = 2.0/n\)).

**Objective:**  
Minimize  
\[
C_5 = \max_{k} \int_{0}^{2-\Delta x \cdot |k|} h^k(x) \cdot (1-h(x))  dx
\]  
where \(h^k(x) = h(x+k)\) for valid shifts \(k\). Discretized as:  
\[
C_5 = \max_k \text{smooth\_correlation}(h, k) \cdot \Delta x
\]

**Constraints:**  
1. **Box constraints:** \(0 \leq h_i \leq 1\) for all \(i\)  
2. **Integral constraint:** \(\sum_{i=1}^n h_i = n/2\)

**Fourier insights:**  
The correlation operator corresponds to a convolution, with penalizable components in the Fourier domain. We project onto the space orthogonal to high-correlation frequencies and use a modified \(L_1\) penalty to guide optimization toward symmetry-breaking solutions.

\[
