Approach and rationale:
We will use a multi-resolution optimization strategy based on sparse grid representations combined with local search. The key idea is to represent the function as a linear combination of few-scale basis functions (Fourier modes) at different levels, optimizing coefficients at each resolution and transferring the best solution to the next. This approach leverages the separation of scales in the optimization problem and reduces the dimensionality for local searches.

We start from a coarse resolution (n=4) and progressively refine the discretization using basis functions that are piecewise constant or sparse. The coarse-to-fine transfer ensures global exploration, while local optimization with SLSQP ensures local refinement. This strategy escapes local optima by maintaining diverse solutions across resolutions.

Mathematical specification:
1. Representation: 
   - Base: Piecewise constant steps with m breaks
   - Progressive: Add boundary conditions and basis functions at each resolution
   - Optimization: Minimize $\max_k \int h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx$
   - Constraints: $\sum h_i \cdot dx = 1$, $0\leq h_i \leq1$

2. Objective function formulation:
   - Original problem: $\min \max_k \hat{C}(k)$ where $\hat{C}(k)=\int h(x)(1-h(x+k))dx$
   - Stabilized objective: $f_c(x) = \max_{k} \hat{C}(k) + \frac{1}{\alpha} \log(\exp(\alpha \cdot (\hat{C}_{ref} - \max_k \hat{C}(k)))$
   - Used $\alpha=1000$ for stability and to penalize solutions falling below previous bounds

3. Optimization formulation:
   - Variables: Coefficients of sparse representations at each resolution level
   - Objective: Minimize the stabilized objective function
   - Constraint: Integral constraint maintained through projection

   def scale_previous_solution(prev_h, new_n):
       """Extrapolate solution to new resolution"""
       x_old = np.linspace(0, 2, len(prev_h))
       x_new = np.linspace(0, 2, new_n)
       interp_vals = np.interp(x_new, x_old, prev_h)
       # Preconditioning to maintain integral
       target_integral = new_n / 2
       total_integral = np.sum(interp_vals) * (2.0 / new_n)
       interp_vals *= target_integral / total_integral
       return interp_vals
