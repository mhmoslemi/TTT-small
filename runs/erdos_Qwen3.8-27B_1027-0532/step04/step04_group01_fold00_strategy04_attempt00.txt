Approach and rationale:

We recognize that the step function optimization is highly non-convex and best suited for global optimization methods. Our strategy involves the following steps:

1. **Initialization and candidate generation**:
   - Generate several candidates using different methods including the provided initial_h_values, QR sequence, and M-sequence.
   - For diversity, use random interpolation and permutation between these initial forms.

2. **Local search refinement**:
   - For each candidate, apply a simulated annealing algorithm that makes binary moves (0->1 and 1->0) to escape local minima.
   - Balance exploration and exploitation by adaptive temperature control.

3. **Continuous optimization**:
   - If time permits, use continuous relaxation followed by greedy rounding to converge to high-quality solutions.

4. **Validation and comparison**:
   - Evaluate candidates using the provided overlap integrals and keep the best found.

We choose this approach because it combines two essential elements in non-convex optimization: global exploration via multiple restarts and local refinement. The known transformations from the provided code (like QR and M sequences) provide promising starting points, while randomization helps avoid early convergence.

Mathematical specification:

- **Objective**: Minimize C5 = max_{k} (1/n) * | <h, 1 - h_{k}> | * (2.0 / n) where the discrete dot product <a,b> is replaced by appropriate correlation.

- **Constraints**: 
   0 <= h[i] <= 1 for all i, 
   sum_i h[i] = n/2.

- **Representation**: h is a vector of length n=600, with dx=2/600. The overlap integral is approximated by:

      C5 = max_k (max_corr) * (2.0/600)

   where max_corr = np.max(np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode='full')).

- **Continuity**: We permit continuous h for the optimization phase but clip to [0,1] upon evaluation to mimic the discrete problem.

1. **Multiple initialization**: Using periodic pattern generation (like QR, M-sequences) and random perturbations.  
2. **Adaptive temperature control**: Based on move acceptance rate.  
3. **Smart move generation**: With focus on structural changes (swaps, fills) adapted to known optimal patterns (QR-like).  
4. **Time budget allocation**: Allocates 90% of time to diversity exploration and 10% to best solution refinement.  

This approach handles the high dimension (~600) and non-convexity by focusing on reduced-move exploration and leveraging prior knowledge from known good candidates.
