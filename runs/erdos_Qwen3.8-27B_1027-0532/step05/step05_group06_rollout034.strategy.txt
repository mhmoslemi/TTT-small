## Approach and Rationale

The Erds minimum overlap problem involves minimizing the maximum overlap between a function h(x) and its shifted versions by k. The non-convex nature of the problem often leads local optimization methods to get stuck in suboptimal solutions. This approach uses a genetic algorithm to explore the solution space more thoroughly, combined with resolution doubling (i.e., increasing the number of grid points) to refine the solution.

### Core Idea
1. **Multi-start Genetic Algorithm (GA)**: Start with an initial population of candidate solutions, including the given `initial_h_values` and some random or structured initializations. The GA explores the solution space by crossover and mutation.
2. **Adaptive Resolution**: If time allows and the budget is sufficient, double the resolution (number of grid points) of the candidate solutions. This allows for a finer representation and potentially better overlap reduction.
3. **Local Refinement**: For candidates that show promise, apply a local optimization (like L-BFGS-B) or coordinate descent to refine the solution.

### Why Different?
- Genetic algorithms are good at exploring the global solution space, which is crucial for escaping local optima.
- Adaptive resolution allows the method to balance between exploration (low resolution) and exploitation (high resolution).
- The combination of GA and local refinement leverages the strengths of both stochastic and gradient-based methods.

## Mathematical Specification

### Objective
Minimize:
\[
C_5 = \max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1 - h(x+k))  dx
\]

### Constraints
\[
0 \leq h(x) \leq 1, \quad \int_0^2 h(x)  dx = 1
\]

### Discrete Representation
Let h be represented on n points in [0, 2] with step size \(\Delta x = 2/n\). Then, the integral constraint is \(\sum_{i=1}^n h_i \cdot \Delta x = 1\).

### Objective Computation
The integral minimum overlap is computed as:
\[
c_5 = \max_{k} \left[ \text{np.correlate}(h, 1-h, \text{mode}='full') \right] \times \Delta x
\]

### Resolution Handling
When doubling the resolution from n to 2n, interpolate the data from the parent array. Maintain the integral constraint by re-normalizing or clipping if necessary.

### Helper Functions
- `project_feasible`: Adjusts h to meet the integral constraint and clip values to [0,1].
- `compute_c5`: Computes the objective value for a given h.

### Genetic Algorithm Setup
- **Individual Representation**: Each candidate in the GA is an array of n numbers in [0,1] satisfying the integral constraint.
- **Initialization**: Start with the initial_h_values and several random initializations. The random initializations are:
  \[
  h_i = \text{clip}( \text{random}(n) , 0, 1)
  \]
  then projected to meet the integral constraint.
- **Crossover**: We use a multi-point crossover or simulated binary crossover (SBX) for two parents.
- **Mutation**: Use Gaussian mutation or random mutation.
- **Fitness**: The negative of the objective value, so that minimizing the original problem becomes maximizing the negative.
