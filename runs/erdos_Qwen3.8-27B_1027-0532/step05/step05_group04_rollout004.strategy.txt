1. Approach and rationale
The current approach relies on a long sequence of optimization phases with multiple interpolation and projection steps. This is a valid method for a challenging nonconvex optimization problem, but the computation time is dominated by the gradient evaluations (auto-differentiation) and repeated constraint enforcement. The key bottlenecks are:
- The `compute_erdos` function fires an unoptimized gradient (Central Difference) for nearly every step
- The Nelder-Mead and DE solvers don't scale well to large problems
- The correlation computation is inherently O(n^3) through auto-diff with jitter

A fundamentally faster approach: use direct gradient computation in precompiled kernels (via just-in-time compilation) and reformulate the problem with separable components. We'll keep the core "sparse grid" idea but use convolution to compute the gradient without autodiff, and parallelize gradient computations. This parallel gradient descent using fixed-points.

2. Mathematical specification
a) Objective: min max_k ∫[0,2] h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx
b) Constraints:
   • 0 ≤ h(x) ≤ 1 for all x
   • ∫[0,2] h(x) dx = 1
c) Discretization: h(x_i) for i=1..n (n point samples over [0,2])
d) Optimization variables: h ∈ R^n with bounds [0,1]
