Appraoch and rationale
We propose a multidirectional search method that explicitly explores both high-concentration (peak) and uniform (plateau) regions of the solution space. The key bottleneck in previous methods was their tendency to get stuck in local optima at the edges or midpoints of the domain, missing globally better solutions. Our approach enriches the search by:

1. Exploring peak-based solutions where h(x) sharply concentrates around certain points
2. Testing plateau-like solutions with more uniform distributions
3. Maintaining multi-level representation (low-resolution blueprints for high-resolution implementation)
4. Employing specialized local optimizers for each structural type

The mathematical rationale draws from harmonic analysis by balancing between:
- Frequency content (higher frequencies help distribute mass to reduce maximum overlap)
- Energy concentration (sharp peaks locally reduce but increase the risk of forming hotspots)
- Barycentric invariance (the integral remains unchanged regardless of mass distribution)

The method deliberately trades computational time for breadth of search, using multiple hyperplane projections to escape local minima.
