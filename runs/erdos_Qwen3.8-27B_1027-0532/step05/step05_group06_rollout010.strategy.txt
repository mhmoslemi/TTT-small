### Approach and Rationale  

This approach aims to improve the solution to Erdős's minimum overlap problem by combining multiple optimization techniques with adaptive resolution. The core idea is to **increase the resolution** of the optimization steps to better capture the essential features of the optimal function $h:[0,2]\to[0,1]$ that minimizes the maximum overlap integral $C_5$. The current solution, while effective, may be trapped in local optima due to its fixed resolution. By **doubling the number of points** during optimization, we can exploit a smoother representation of $h$, leading to tighter bounds. This is supported by the observation that fine-grained representations help escape shallow local minima in non-convex optimization problems.  

The primary limitation is computational cost: doubling a $n$-point solution quadruples the number of variables, slowing down L-BFGS-B optimizations. To mitigate this, we use **multi-resolution optimization** only when the time budget allows, and we prioritize efficient restarts and coordinate descent instead of full-scale resolution adjustments. The key bottleneck addressed is the original algorithm's reliance on a single fixed resolution, which may miss globally optimal configurations.  

### Mathematical Specification  

**Optimization goal**: Minimize $C_5 = \max_k \int h(x) \cdot (1-h(x+k))  dx$ subject to $\int h(x)  dx = 1$ and $0 \leq h(x) \leq 1$.  

**Discrete representation**: Represent $h$ at $n$ points in $[0, 2]$ with step size $\Delta x = 2/n$, ensuring $\sum h_i = n/2$ via clipping and projection.  

**Objective computation**: Discrete convolution $\text{np.correlate}(h, 1-h, \text{mode}="full")$ gives a vector of length $2n-1$, then scale by $\Delta x$ to compute $C_5$.  

**Resolution handling**: For a given $n$, define the grid points $x_i = i \cdot (2/n)$ for $i=0,\dots,n-1$. When doubling $n$ to $n/(n\rightarrow 2n)$, use linear interpolation. Maintain the integral constraint by applying the $\text{project}_\text{feasible}$ function.  

**Algorithm structure**:  
1. **Initial expansion**: Start with the given $n=400$ points, introduce random restarts with $\text{random\_initialization}$.  
2. **Resolution doubling**: For remaining time, double $n$ via interpolation and optimize.  
3. **Optimization sequence**: Apply coordinate descent (5 sweeps), then L-BFGS-B with 5 alpha values, then top-lag equalization, and finally coordinate descent with SLSQP polishing.  

**Variable bounds**: All $h_i \in [0,1]$ are enforced via projection.  

**Constraint handling**: The integral constraint $\sum h_i \Delta x = 1$ is projected using iterative clipping and adjustment.  

**Solver selection**: Use L-BFGS-B for smooth optimization, then SLSQP to enforce equality constraints. The requirement of 0/1 outputs ensures discrete observations.
