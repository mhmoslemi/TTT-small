One more thought: the smoothing approach. The log-sum-exp with alpha gives:
f_alpha(h) = (1/alpha) * log(sum(exp(alpha * corr[k])))

The gradient is:
∇f_alpha = (1/sum(exp(alpha*corr))) * alpha * sum(exp(alpha*corr[k]) * B_k)

where B_k is the sensitivity vector for lag k (which is exactly B[:, k] from our precomputed matrix).

So ∇f_alpha = alpha * sum_k w_k * B[:, k] where w_k = exp(alpha*corr[k]) / sum(exp(alpha*corr))

This is a weighted average of the B vectors, weighted by the softmax of the correlations. For large alpha, this concentrates on the maximum lag.

The projected subgradient step would be:
h_new = project_feasible(h - step * ∇f_alpha, target)

With Nesterov momentum:
h_+ = h + momentum * (h - h_prev)
h_new = project_feasible(h_+ - step * ∇f_alpha(h_+), target)

This should be efficient since computing the gradient is O(n * 2n) = O(n^2) (just a matrix-vector product with B and a softmax).
