The objective is C₅(h) = max_k corr[k] * dx

where corr[k] = ∑_{i} h[i] * (1 - h[i+k]) (with appropriate ranges)

The gradient of corr[k] with respect to h[j] is:

- For j such that k ≤ j < n: ∂corr[k]/∂h[j] = -h[j-k]

Wait, this is the gradient of the k-th cross-correlation. The gradient of the max is the gradient of the active (maximum) cross-correlation.

But at the optimum, multiple lags are active (they all achieve the same maximum). So the gradient of the max is undefined (it's a subgradient). The subgradient is any convex combination of the gradients of the active functions.

The subgradient method uses this: pick the worst lag (or a few worst lags), compute the gradient, and take a step in the negative direction.

The issue with subgradient methods is that the step size is hard to tune. Too large → oscillation. Too small → slow convergence.

The cross-correlation at lag k (k ≥ 0) is:
corr[n-1+k] = ∑_{i=0}^{n-1-k} h[i] * (1-h[i+k])

The gradient with respect to h[j]:

∂corr[n-1+k]/∂h[j] = (1-h[j+k]) - h[j-k] for j in [k, n-k-1]

def compute_corr(h):

def compute_c5(h):
