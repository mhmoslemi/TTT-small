 The core idea is to build a statistical model (response surface) of the objective function and use it to drive the search. This allows us to escape local minima by moving in directions that are predicted to yield improvements, even if the gradient is not available.

1. **Approach and Rationale**

   We address the Erdős minimum overlap problem by implementing an Evolutionary Algorithm (EA) that balances exploration and exploitation through a population-based method. The key issue with gradient-based approaches is their susceptibility to local optima, especially in nonconvex problems. EA, with its population diversity and crossover operations, can escape local traps and explore the solution space more broadly.

   Our EA uses a combination of crossover operators (arithmetic and simulated binary) and two mutation strategies (Poisson and random walk) to generate new candidate solutions. Feasibility is maintained by projecting all individuals to the constraint set (integral=1, box constraints [0,1]). We initialize the population with the provided parent and perturbed variants. The fitness is determined by the C5 value (lower is better), and selection favors individuals with the lowest C5.

   The primary limitation is the high-dimensional space (400 dimensions) which may lead to slower convergence, but we manage this by using a moderate population size and focusing on diversity. We start with moderate mutation (exploration) and shift to more exploration as the optimization progresses.

   The name of the algorithm is Progressive Evolutionary Optimization (PEO).
