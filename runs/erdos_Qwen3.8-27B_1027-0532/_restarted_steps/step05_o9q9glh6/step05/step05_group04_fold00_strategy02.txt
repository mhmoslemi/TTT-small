## 1. Approach and rationale  
The primary optimization technique is **simulated annealing with adaptive neighborhood sampling**. This method is chosen to prevent premature convergence to suboptimal local minima in the high-dimensional search space. Unlike gradient-based approaches, simulated annealing explores diverse candidate functions by strategically perturbing the current solution and accepting worse solutions with a temperature-dependent probability. This global exploration ensures we escape local optima typical of neighborhill methods. The key improvement over standard implementations is the adaptive selection of perturbation size and temperature schedule based on search progress. Performance is enhanced by focusing optimization on regions where large overlaps occur, using importance-based perturbation targets. This differs fundamentally from the provided record holder program's method of multi-optimizer sequence. The main bottleneck for Erdős-like constants is the ruggedness of the optimization landscape, which simulated annealing addresses by allowing temporary increases in overlap during the search.
