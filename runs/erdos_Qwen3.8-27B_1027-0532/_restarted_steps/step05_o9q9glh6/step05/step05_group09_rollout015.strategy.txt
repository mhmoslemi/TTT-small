### Approach and Rationale
The Erds minimum overlap problem seeks to find a function h(x) that minimizes the maximum overlap of h(x) and its shifted versions. Current solutions rely on local optimization techniques like FISTA and coordinate descent, which often get stuck in suboptimal regions. This approach combines global search with local refinement using a hybrid evolutionary strategy. The algorithm begins by generating a population of candidate solutions through random mutations and crossover operations, enforced by a feasibility projection. Good candidates are further optimized using a fast iterative shrinkage-thresholding method (FISTA) enhanced with continuation in penalty parameters. This hybrid strategy balances the exploration of the solution space with efficient exploitation, leveraging global movement via crossover and targeted local descent.

The key innovation is the systematic coupling of global exploration (crossover) with local refinement (FISTA), ensuring both broad searching capability and the ability to escape local optima. The algorithm adapts its search intensity based on a budget-aware annealing schedule, guaranteeing progress even under tight time constraints.

Limitations include the O(n²) computational complexity for the objective function and the binary search used in projection, which may occasionally lead to suboptimal projections. The approach is primarily derivative-free but requires careful handling of the integral constraint through binary search projection.

### Mathematical Specification

The algorithm maintains candidates in the form of n-dimensional floating-point vectors that satisfy the integral constraint through post-processing projection.
