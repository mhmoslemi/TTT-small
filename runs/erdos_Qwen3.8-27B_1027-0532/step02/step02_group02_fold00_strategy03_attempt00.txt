We propose a two-stage optimization strategy combining global and local searches with adaptive sampling and dimensionality reduction to overcome the limitations of the previous approach. The core limitations addressed are:

- Suboptimal local search: The previous method relied on random perturbations which aren't directional enough for finding low-overlap configurations.
- Inefficient dimensionality: High n-points values make high-quality gradient calculations prohibitively expensive.
- Convergent plateaus: Deep local minima not reachable by simple gradient-based methods.

Our solution uses a**hierarchical optimization strategy**:  
① **Global exploration**: Multi-start optimization from diverse initialization patterns using directed moves  
② **Adaptive refinement**: Dynamic adjustment of search space focusing on high-value regions  
③ **Kernel-based dimensionality reduction**: Mapping continuous configurations to finite-dimensional kernel features  
④ **Barrier-constrained projected gradient descent**: Local drilling with boundary handling  

Key innovations:
- **Feature-driven mutation**: Initialization based on periodic waveforms reflecting known low-overlap patterns  
- **Invariant manifold exploitation**: Finding low-dimensional subspaces where optimal configurations concentrate  
- **Non-uniform grid sampling**: Adaptive resolution to focus computation where high-frequency features matter most

2. **Mathematical Specification**

The optimization problem:  
Minimize $C = \max_k \int_0^2 h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx$  
Subject to:  
$0 ≤ h(x) ≤ 1, \ ∫ h(x) dx = 1$

Discretization: $n$-point grid with $\Delta x = 2/n$

Functional representation:  
Embed the function as a low-frequency kernel:  
$h(x) = \sum_{j=1}^m w_j \phi_j(x)$  
Where $\phi_j(x) = \frac{\sin(j \pi x / 2)}{j}$ (sinc-like basis) 

Dimensionality reduction:  
The kernel mapping $\mathbb{R}^m \rightarrow [\mathbb{R}]^n$ with $m \ll n$ preserves the integral constraints while reducing computational burden.

Projected gradient descent with barrier constraints:  
$\nabla_{w} J(w) = \nabla J(w) - \lambda \nabla \mathcal{I}(w) + \delta(\mu, w)$  
Where $J(w)$ is the reduced-order cost, $\mathcal{I}(w)$ is the entropy barrier enforcing $0<h<1$, and $\delta(\mu, w)$ is the mutual inhibition term.

Constraint transformations:  
Integral preservation: $\sum_{i=1}^n h(i) \Delta x = \frac{n}{2} \Delta x = 1$
