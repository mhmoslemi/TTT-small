1. Approach and rationale
The current approach uses a combination of subgradient descent and refinement, but it may be trapped in local optima. The proposed approach uses **differential evolution (DE)**, a population-based optimization algorithm that explores the search space more thoroughly. DE is less likely than gradient-based methods to get stuck in local minima due to its population-based nature. The key improvements are:
   - Using a DE algorithm to optimize the function values directly
   - Maintaining multiple candidate solutions simultaneously
   - Periodically updating the population to balance exploration and exploitation
   - Automatically handling constraints through a repair mechanism
   - Allowing explicit comparisons between different grid resolutions

2. Mathematical specification
- **Variables**: A probability distribution h(x) over [0,2] represented as samples at specific points, with ∑h(x_i) = N/2 (ensuring integral=1)
- **Objective**: Minimize max_lag (∬h(x)h(y)dydx for |x-y| in each lag window) weighted by dx²
- **Constraints**: 0 ≤ h(x) ≤ 1 for all x, ∑h(x) = N/2
- **Representation**: Use a grid of N points with spacing Δx=2/N
- **DE algorithm**:
  - Initialize a population of P vectors of length N
  - For each generation:
    - Repair infeasible chromosomes (where h(x) or sum are not constraint-compliant)
    - For each target chromosome, mutate and crossover with neighbors
    - Evaluate fitness as negative C5 to minimize
    - Replace worst solutions with mutated variants if improvement is detected
