But also, h[i] appears in the (1-h[m]) factor when m=i, and h[j] appears when m=j.

When m=i: the term is h[i+k-n+1] * (1-h[i]). Changing h[i] from 1 to 0 changes this term from 0 to h[i+k-n+1].
When m=j: the term is h[j+k-n+1] * (1-h[j]). Changing h[j] from 0 to 1 changes this term from h[j+k-n+1] to 0.

So total change:
Δc[k] = -(1-h[i-k+n-1]) * 1_{valid(i-k+n-1)}  [h[i] as first factor, 1→0]
       + h[i+k-n+1] * 1_{valid(i+k-n+1)}  [h[i] as second factor, (1-h) goes 0→h[i+k-n+1]]
       + (1-h[j-k+n-1]) * 1_{valid(j-k+n-1)}  [h[j] as first factor, 0→1]
       - h[j+k-n+1] * 1_{valid(j+k-n+1)}  [h[j] as second factor, (1-h) goes 1→0]

This is O(1) per k to compute the change (just 4 lookups and additions), and O(n) total (for all k). But we need to evaluate this for many candidate swaps.
