  We address the problem of finding a step function h that minimizes the maximum overlap integral (Erds constant) by tackling its non-convex nature and high dimensionality (n=180). The current state-of-the-art is 0.3808, and we aim for a lower value. Our approach leverages distributed computing to explore the search space more effectively than sequential methods, using tailored local optimization around distributed points. We partition the domain into chips and optimize locally, then combine them heuristically. This balances exploration and exploitation, leveraging the high dimensionality where parallel evaluations are feasible.

  Mathematical specification
  \subsection{Variables}
  - $h$: A step function with $\sum h_i \Delta x = 1$ and $0 \leq h_i \leq 1$ for all $i$.
  - $\Delta x = \frac{2}{n}$, $n=180$.
  - Objective: $\min \max_l \sum_i h_i (1-h_{i+l}) \Delta x$.

  \subsection{Method}
  Use distributed parallel optimization via interval partitioning (chipping) and combine with linear interpolation for cross-chip correlations. For each chip:
  - Express constraints via quadratic penalties.
  - Optimize using CVXOPT or ECOS solvers.
  - Combine via eigenvector-like interpolation under linear correlation constraints.

  Represent $h$ as a vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$ with $\sum h_i = \frac{n}{2}$ and $0 \leq h_i \leq 1$.
