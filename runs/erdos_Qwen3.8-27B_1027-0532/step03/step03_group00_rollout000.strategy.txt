2. Approach and Rationale
Central Idea: Instead of heuristic local search optimizations, we employ a structured two-level optimization approach that leverages continuous relaxation and Bayesian optimization for global exploration. This method replaces the discrete n-dimensional optimization space with a continuous surrogate using a Gaussian Process, which allows for adaptive global search and covetance-aware local refinement. By minimizing a penalized objective that includes derivative information, we systematically explore the reduction space, focusing on convex and concave regions to find global minima rather than relying on iterative local improvements.

Bottleneck Addressed: The parent method's heavy reliance on local searches increases the risk of early convergence to suboptimal local minima, leading to higher C values. Our approach reduces this risk by incorporating gradients into the decision process and using a phased optimization that starts globally and refines locally, ensuring better coverage of the search space and a lower final C.

3. Mathematical Specification
Variables: h = [h1, h2, ..., h_{n_points}] ∈ ℝ^{n_points} with 0 ≤ h_x ≤ 1 for all x, and ∑_{i=1}^{n_points} h_i * dx = 1 (where dx = 2.0 / n_points) is enforced as a linear constraint to maintain the integral.

Objective: Minimize C_5(h) = max_{k} [ ∫_0^2 h(x) (1 - h(x+k)) dx ] evaluated numerically as (max_k ∑_{i=1}^{n_points-|k|} h_i * (1 - h_{i+k})) * dx for appropriate k indices.

Constraints:
- Bound constraints: 0 ≤ h_x ≤ 1 for all x
- Linear constraint: ∑ h_x dx = 1 (equivalent to ∑ h_x = n_points / 2 for discrete equality)

Representation: h is a continuous vector in ℝ^{n_points} to allow gradient-based optimization. The values are constrained to the interval [0,1].

Algorithmic Formulation:
- **Outer Level (Bayesian Optimization):** Minimize C_5(h) with a Gaussian Process (GP) surrogate. The GP is used for expected improvement acquisition function to balance exploration (for uncertainty-rich regions) and exploitation (for low-C_5 regions). Penalize derivative information to ensure smoothness and concentration in promising areas.
- **Inner Level (Local Descent):** Once BayesOpt identifies a candidate h, use projected gradient descent with an L2 penalty on the gradient to enforce bounds. Steps: compute gradient of C_5 with respect to h, project gradients to maintain bounds, update variables with Armijo line search, and check feasibility.
- **Derivative Calculation:** For C_5(h), the derivative dC_5/dh_x depends on the correlations at various lags where h_x affects the overlap. Specifically, for a small change δh_x, dC_5 ≈ ∑_k δh_x * |correlation derivatives| * dx, but this is non-trivial. To simplify, use finite differences with a central-difference step size δ = 10^{-5} for gradient estimation during descent.
