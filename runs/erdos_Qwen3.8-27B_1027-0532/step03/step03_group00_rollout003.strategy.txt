#### 1. Approach and rationale
We propose a solution based on **two-interval decomposition** with **convex optimization** to find the best placement of two disjoint intervals. This approach reduces the dimension of the problem significantly compared to optimizing over the entire 400-dimensional space. The key idea is to decompose the function `h` into two rectangular steps, each with total mass 1 (integral = 1). This structure is motivated by known near-optimal solutions for smaller cases (e.g., [a,b] = [0,0.5] and [c,d] = [0.5+2(p-1)/n, 1+2(p-1)/n] for small `n`). 

The method uses a hierarchical optimization scheme:
- **Global optimization**: Minimize the maximum correlation by sampling interval boundaries across the domain `[0,2]`.
- **Local refinement**: For promising interval sets, use convex optimization to fine-tune the function values as weights (instead of strict 0/1), which often yields slightly better results than binary solutions.
- **Gradient interpolation**: Smoothly connect interval boundaries with linear segments for continuity, a common step in relaxation methods.

This approach inherently satisfies the constraints by construction: intervals are disjoint, and the total integral is enforced by scaling the area. The mathematical guarantees include:
- `0 <= h[i] <= 1` by the linear interpolation frequently between intervals.
