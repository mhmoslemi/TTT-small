1. Approach and Rationale
We propose to use a hierarchical optimization strategy that combines spectral methods and non-convex optimization to find a step function h that minimizes the maximum overlap integral. The core idea is to represent the step function in both frequency and time domains to escape local minima. This approach addresses the main bottleneck in previous methods—local optima—by:
- Leveraging the spectral decomposition to balance frequency components
- Maintaining a population of candidate solutions to explore diverse areas
- Converting the continuous optimization space into a discrete representation for better control

2. Mathematical Specification
   We optimize over a piecewise constant function h: [0,2] → [0,1] with exactly n samples (n must be even). The objective is:
   minimize C_5 = (1/dx) * max_k ∫h(x)(1-h(x+k)) dx
   subject to:
   ∑h_i = n/2, and 0 ≤ h_i ≤ 1 for all i
   where dx = 2/n

   We approximate the integrals with Riemann sums. The spectral decomposition is used to:
   • Represent h in the frequency domain as ℱ(h)
   • Project the overlap scores onto the dominant frequency components
   • Apply Tikhonov regularization to prevent overfitting
