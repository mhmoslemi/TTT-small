**New idea – a hybrid genetic optimisation of *positions* coupled with an exact linear‑programming solver for the radii**

The key insight is that for any fixed set of centres the radii that maximise the sum are obtained by a *linear program*:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{\,r\ge 0}\;&\sum_{i=1}^{26} r_i \\
\text{s.t.}\;& r_i \le x_i,\; r_i \le 1-x_i,\; r_i \le y_i,\; r_i \le 1-y_i \quad (i=1,\dots,26) \\
             & r_i+r_j \le d_{ij}\quad (1\le i<j\le 26),\\
             & d_{ij}=\sqrt{(x_i-x_j)^2+(y_i-y_j)^2}\, .
\end{aligned}
\]

Because all constraints are linear in the radii, the LP can be solved to machine
precision by the `highs` backend of `scipy.optimize.linprog` in a few microseconds
even when repeated thousands of times.  
Thus we can treat **only the 26 coordinates** as optimisation variables and let the LP supply the best possible radii for each candidate layout.

The remaining challenge is to explore the 52‑dimensional position space.
We do this with a *stand‑alone genetic algorithm (GA)* that

* maintains a population of random centre configurations,
* repeatedly evaluates each candidate using the LP above,
* applies crossover and mutation to generate new individuals,
* retains the fittest individuals (elitism) and refines them with a final
  local SLSQP step to correct any tiny numerical drift.

This GA‑LP combination is qualitatively different from the previous
SLSQP + DE approach:  
– the optimisation of radii is *exact* (no penalty functions),  
– the GA operates on the continuous search space and relies on stochastic
  recombination rather than deterministic gradient steps,  
– the final refinement uses the same LP as the fitness evaluation,
  guaranteeing that the returned packing is optimal for its own centres.

### 3. GA workflow

1. **Initial population**  
   - Generate 48 random individuals: coordinates uniformly sampled in `[0,1]`.
   - Optionally slightly perturb a systematic 5×5 grid plus an extra point to give a decent starting baseline.

2. **Evaluation**  
   - For each individual compute fitness via `evaluate_fitness`.
   - If the LP fails (rare), re‑sample that individual.

3. **Selection**  
   - Tourney selection with size 5: repeatedly pick the best of 5 random picks.

4. **Reproduction**  
   - For each new offspring pair:  
     *With probability `crossover_prob` perform crossover, otherwise copy one parent.*  
     Afterwards apply mutation to each gene with prob `mutation_rate` and noise std `sigma`.  
     Clamp to square and re‑evaluate fitness.  
   - Repeat until a new population of 48 is built.

5. **Elitism**  
   - Replace the worst individuals in the new population with the top 5
     from the previous generation (if not already present).

6. **Cooling**  
   - Multiply `sigma` by `0.99`.

7. **Loop**  
   - Repeat steps 2–6 for 180 generations.

---

### 4. Final refinement

After GA convergence:

1. Take the best individual found (highest `sum_radii`).
2. **Local SLSQP fine‑tuning**  
   - Treat all 52 variables as continuous again, impose the same SOC
     constraints (`x_i - r_i ≥ 0`, etc.) and pairwise non‑overlap
     (`norm([x_i-x_j, y_i-y_j]) ≥ r_i + r_j`).  
   - Use `SLSQP` with `maxiter=2000` to further push radii up to the exact
     boundary.  
   - The initial point for SLSQP is the GA‑derived centre set and the
     radii from the LP evaluation.  
   - This step is safe because the LP already gives the *maximal* radii for
     those centres; SLSQP will simply correct potential numerical noise.
3. Return the final centres, radii, and sum.

The local SLSQP call is optional but usually helps attain numerical
consistency (`validate_packing` tolerances) and can sometimes squeeze a few
extra micrometres of radius.

### 5. Numerical details

* **LP solver**: `scipy.optimize.linprog` with `method='highs'`.  
  This backend is deterministic, supports large sparse constraints, and
  converges in microseconds for 26 variables.

* **Floating‑point tolerances**:  
  After the final LP or SLSQP, we reduce any radius that violates any
  constraint by a tiny epsilon (`1e-9`) to guarantee feasibility.

* **Random seeds**:  
  Set a single seed at the beginning (`np.random.seed(42)`) to make the
  process reproducible for grading.
