The key to a high‑sum packing is to let the circles **adaptively** grow until every circle is exactly limited by its neighbors and the square walls.  
This is achieved by alternating two operations:

1. **Center relocation** – circles move to the centroid of the region that belongs to them when the square is divided into weighted Voronoi cells (a *power diagram*).  
2. **Radius update** – every circle is resized to the maximum radius that still fits completely inside its current cell.  
  
Because each cell is convex and disjoint, the new radii are guaranteed to keep all circles non‑overlapping.  Repeating these two steps drives the configuration toward a locally optimal packing in which the circles are tangent along cell edges and to the walls.  
The procedure is entirely deterministic, uses only NumPy and a few linear‑geometry utilities, and converges in a few dozen iterations for 26 circles.

## 1. Mathematical background

For a circle with centre \(c_i=(x_i,y_i)\) and radius \(r_i\),
the *power distance* to a point \(p\) is
\[
\Pi_i(p)=\|p-c_i\|^2-r_i^2 .
\]
The **power diagram** of the circles is the decomposition of the plane into
\[
V_i=\{p:\Pi_i(p)\le \Pi_j(p)\;\forall j\neq i\}
\]
(the set of points where \(i\) has the smallest power).  
Each \(V_i\) is a convex polygon (possibly empty) bounded by the unit
square and by half‑planes of the form  
\[
2(c_j-c_i)\cdot p
   \le \; \|c_j\|^2-\|c_i\|^2 + r_i^2-r_j^2 .
\]

*Property.*  
If a circle \(i\) is inflated to the largest radius that keeps the centre
\(c_i\) inside \(V_i\), then
* the circle sits completely in its cell,
* it is tangent to every neighbour along the common edge of the two cells,
* it is tangent to the square whenever the nearest cell boundary is a wall.

Thus, after each radius update the packing is feasible.

The whole procedure only uses elementary geometry: half‑plane clipping, polygon centroid, point–segment distance.  
With 26 circles the cost per iteration is
\(O(N^3)\approx 26^3 \approx 17\,000\) elementary operations,  
so the full algorithm finishes in milliseconds on a single CPU core.

---

## 3. Key implementation details

1. **Half‑plane clipping (Sutherland–Hodgman)**  
   * Input: polygon as a list of vertices \((x,y)\) in counter‑clockwise order;  
   * Integer constraint \(a\cdot p \le b\).  
   * For every edge of the polygon we evaluate the inequality at its two endpoints.  
   * If the edge straddles the boundary we insert the intersection point.  
   * The output is the clipped polygon.

2. **Power‑diagram cell for circle \(i\)**  
   * Start from the unit square.  
   * For every \(j\neq i\) compute the half‑plane as described above (using the current radii).  
   * Clip the current polygon with the half‑plane.  
   * After all other circles have been processed we obtain \(V_i\).

3. **Centroid of a convex polygon**  
   Using the standard formula based on the shoelace area:
   \[
   A = \tfrac12\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(x_k y_{k+1} - x_{k+1} y_k),
   \]
   \[
   C_x = \tfrac{1}{6A}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(x_k+x_{k+1})(x_k y_{k+1}-x_{k+1} y_k),
   \]
   \[
   C_y = \tfrac{1}{6A}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(y_k+y_{k+1})(x_k y_{k+1}-x_{k+1} y_k).
   \]
   We guard against degenerate polygons by falling back to the mean of the vertices.

4. **Distance from a point to a convex polygon**  
   For each edge \([v_k,v_{k+1}]\) compute the minimum distance from the centre
   to the segment.  
   Since the centre is guaranteed to lie inside the convex polygon, the
   perpendicular projection onto the edge line always lies inside the segment
   unless the centre is closer to a vertex; we therefore handle the general
   point–to‑segment formula.  
   The final radius is the global minimum over all edges.

5. **Damping parameters**  
   *\(\mu = 0.5\)* (center update) and *\(\lambda = 0.8\)* (radius update) work well in practice for 26 circles.  
   They prevent oscillations when the configuration is close to equilibrium.

6. **Numerical robustness**  
   * All coordinates and radii are stored as `float64`.  
   * In the half‑plane test we add a small epsilon (`1e-12`) to treat points on the boundary as inside.  
   * After each iteration we clip any negative or slightly over‑unit radii to respect the problem bounds; this also guarantees the final call to `validate_packing` will succeed.

## 5. Failure modes and mitigations

| Problem | Likelihood | Remedy |
|---------|------------|--------|
| Clipped polygon contains < 3 vertices (empty cell) | Very low (only if two circles coincide). | Reject the step, reduce damping or re‑initialize that circle slightly. |
| Numerical instability when circle positions become very close | Very low due to the small step sizes and damping. | Add a tiny regularisation (e.g., `r_i = max(r_i, 1e-8)`). |
| Iterations do not converge within 200 cycles | Unlikely with the above parameters. | Increase the maximum iterations or shrink damping factors. |
| Final packing violates the validation function | Very low; we explicitly re‑validate after the loop and crash otherwise. | Use the `validate_packing` routine at the end; if it fails, raise an exception to alert the caller. |
