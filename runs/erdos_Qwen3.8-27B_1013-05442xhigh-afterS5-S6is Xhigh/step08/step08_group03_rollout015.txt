Actually, I want to also try a different approach: **subgradient method with projection**.

The objective is f(h) = max_k c_k(h). The subgradient at h is:
∇f(h) = (1/|S|) * sum_{k∈S} ∇c_k(h)

where S = {k : c_k(h) = max_k c_k} is the active set.

∇c_k(h)[i] = (1-h[i+k]) - h[i-k] (with appropriate bounds)

Wait, let me be more careful. c_k = sum_i h[i](1-h[i+k]).
∂c_k/∂h[i] = (1-h[i+k]) - h[i-k] (the second term comes from the -h[i]*h[i+k] term when the index j = i-k has j+k = i).

Actually: c_k = sum_i h[i] - sum_i h[i]h[i+k]
∂c_k/∂h[i] = 1 - h[i+k] - h[i-k] (where h[j]=0 if j out of bounds)

So the subgradient of f at h is:
g[i] = (1/|S|) * sum_{k∈S} (1 - h[i+k] - h[i-k])

Then the subgradient step is: h_new = project(h - α*g)

where project clips to [0,1] and adjusts to satisfy sum(h) = n/2.
