### Approach and Rationale

The core issue with the Erdős minimum overlap problem is to find a step function on [0,2] with integral 1 that minimizes the maximum overlap with its shifts. The previous solution achieved a bound of 0.380863, but we aim for 0.3808 to show improvement. Traditional methods are susceptible to local optima, so here we introduce a combinatorial structure combined with local search to explore the compact set of solutions. We model the function as a binary step function and use a geometric progression to search periods. This leverages the self-similarity and symmetry often seen in optimal functions, reducing the search space and escaping local traps. The key insight is that iterating periods at an optimal scale can yield global optima, with the parent solution providing a quality fallback.

### Mathematical Specification

- **Representation**: A step function on [0,2] represented as a binary sequence with exactly 200 ones (for n=400) or interpolated to meet the integral condition.
- **Variables**: Binary values at discrete points.
- **Objective**: Minimize C5, defined as the maximum of np.correlate(h, 1-h, mode='full') multiplied by dx (dx=2.0/n_points).
- **Constraints**: 
  - Integral condition: ∫h(x)dx = 1 → sum(h) = n_points/2 for even n_points.
  - Binary values: h(x) ∈ {0,1}.
- **Algorithm**:
  - Parent Handling: Use the parent representation and project it to binary feasibility.
  - Feature Detection: Generic step detection to find binary transitions.
  - Iterative Caching: Generate increasingly detailed binary patterns from the parent.
  - Final Refinement: Optimize in the continuous space to polish the solution.
