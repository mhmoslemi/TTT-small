### Bandit selection

The bandit is functioning exactly as intended operationally:

- 44 pilot programs per parent: `11 × 4 strategies`.
- 24 phase-2 programs per parent.
- Exactly 68 total per parent and 544 per step.
- It frequently concentrates all 24 on one strategy rather than silently reverting to equal allocation.
- The arm with highest initial expected improvement received maximum allocation in 19/24 completed parent decisions.

Actual phase-2 contribution:

| Step | Best pilot | Best phase 2 | Did phase 2 improve step best? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 0 | 0.380985889 | 0.381003260 | No |
| 1 | 0.380941399 | **0.380937100** | Yes, by 0.000004298 |
| 2 | **0.380910791** | 0.380921739 | No |

Across the 24 completed parent allocations, phase 2 improved the corresponding parent’s pilot best in **9/24 cases**.

More importantly, its step-1 gain was consequential. The bandit allocated 13 programs to strategy 1 even though strategy 3 held the best one-off pilot result. Strategy 1 then generated [the 0.380937100 child](/Users/mohammad/Downloads/TTT-small/runs/erdos_Qwen3.8-27B_1006-0453/step01/step01_group06_rollout057.meta.json), and that exact node became the parent of [the step-2 0.380910791 winner](/Users/mohammad/Downloads/TTT-small/runs/erdos_Qwen3.8-27B_1006-0453/step02/step02_group00_rollout026.meta.json). Therefore, the bandit added both a direct gain and an important successful lineage.

But its numeric posterior is overconfident:

- Projected gains reached 0.61 reward units.
- Realized gains were normally around `1e-5`–`2e-4`, except one weak-parent improvement of 0.00661.
- Projected versus realized gains had only weak association: Spearman ≈ 0.23 over these 24 decisions.
- Phase-2 aggregate validity was 64.24%, versus 64.58% for pilots; average reward was also essentially unchanged.

The cause is visible in [rollout_allocation.py](/Users/mohammad/Downloads/TTT-small/rollout_allocation.py:197): valid rewards use an unbounded Gaussian/NIG predictive distribution, and generated draws are clipped only at the lower endpoint. Rare optimistic upper-tail samples dominate expected-best calculations. This does not invalidate generated data or training, but it means the printed “projected phase-2 gain” is not trustworthy and the allocation may over-explore noisy arms.

So the bandit verdict is: **operationally correct, already useful once, but not yet proven superior to the old allocator and statistically miscalibrated.**

### Is the strategy actually affecting programs?

Yes—it is wired correctly and has a measurable effect.

- All 1,632 completed coder prompts contained the exact parsed strategy inside `<strategy>...</strategy>`.
- All 1,632 responses contained a Python block.
- All 1,632 generated programs were textually unique.
- The coder explicitly discussed the strategy in 1,145/1,632 reasoning outputs.
- Programs generated under the same strategy were more similar than programs from different strategies under the same parent: cosine similarity 0.2499 versus 0.2354. This occurred in 22/24 parents and is statistically strong.
- Specific strategy instructions propagated into code. For example:
  - Differential evolution: 17.0% under strategies requesting it versus 3.8% under sibling strategies.
  - Basin hopping: 50.0% versus 31.8%.
  - SLSQP: 75.9% versus 69.3%.

However, the reward effect is much weaker:

- Step 0 strategy identity explained about 11.7% of pilot reward variation and was statistically meaningful.
- Steps 1 and 2 showed no reliable strategy-level reward separation.
- Across all completed steps, the strategy reward effect was not statistically convincing.
- The winning coder often partially followed, corrected, or rejected the supplied plan. That matches the prompt’s intended “guidance, not authority” behavior.

Qualitatively:

- Step 0 winner: strategy proposed multi-resolution gradient refinement; coder implemented multi-resolution interpolation and SLSQP—good alignment.
- Step 1 winner: strategy proposed covariance-matrix evolution; coder considered it but mostly implemented basin-hopping/SLSQP—partial alignment.
- Step 2 winner: strategy proposed multi-resolution adaptive simplex; coder used multi-resolution, SLSQP, and coordinate refinement—partial alignment.

So the strategist is **not bullshit**, but it mostly provides diversity and directional ideas. The coder’s own reasoning remains the dominant component, and there is not yet evidence that strategies consistently improve reward versus a no-strategy control.

### One real strategy-pipeline defect

One strategy escaped validation with literal tokenizer artifacts such as `Ċ`, `Ġ`, and corrupted Unicode, and that corrupted text was passed unchanged into 13 coder prompts: [step02_group04_fold00_strategy01.txt](/Users/mohammad/Downloads/TTT-small/runs/erdos_Qwen3.8-27B_1006-0453/step02/step02_group04_fold00_strategy01.txt).

There were also two explicit safe-fallback strategy arms after malformed planner responses. Those did not crash anything, but the uncaught tokenizer-artifact case is a genuine issue.

Bottom line: **keep the run; training is healthy and results are real. Bandit selection has contributed genuine gain, but its posterior needs calibration. Strategies measurably steer programs, yet their reward value is inconsistent, and the strategy decoding sanitizer has one actual hole.**
